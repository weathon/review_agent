EXECUTIVE SUMMARY: REVIEWER WEAKNESS PATTERNS FOR SFT PAPER
================================================================================

PAPER: "SFT WITHOUT OVERFITTING: ANALYZING THE TRAINING DYNAMICS OF
        SUPERVISED FINE-TUNING"

KEY CLAIMS:
- Selective fine-tuning of Transformer modules (attention vs. feedforward)
  improves OOD generalization
- Two benchmarks: GeneralPoints (arithmetic) and V-IRL (navigation)
- Attention-only SFT claims better memorization-generalization trade-off

ANALYSIS SCOPE:
- Examined 500+ ICLR 2025 human reviews
- Focused on fine-tuning, memorization, OOD generalization, and evaluation
- Identified 11 concrete weakness patterns applicable to this paper
- Extracted specific review excerpts demonstrating each pattern

================================================================================
CRITICAL VULNERABILITIES (LIKELY REVIEWER CONCERNS)
================================================================================

TIER 1 - LIKELY TO RECEIVE REVIEWER CRITICISM:

1. NARROW EVALUATION SCOPE (2 benchmarks only)
   - Reviewers will question if results generalize beyond GeneralPoints & V-IRL
   - Need diverse reasoning tasks with clear task characteristics
   - Risk: "Task-specific findings, not general insight about SFT dynamics"

2. LACK OF MECHANISTIC INSIGHT
   - Why does attention-only tuning help OOD generalization?
   - Without explanation, results appear empirical coincidence
   - Risk: "No clear understanding of the mechanism underlying the improvement"

3. MISSING ABLATIONS & COMPARISONS
   - No comparison to LoRA, prefix tuning, adapter methods
   - Insufficient ablation of selective percentages and architectures
   - Risk: "Improvements may come from other factors, not selective attention"

4. MEMORIZATION VS GENERALIZATION NOT SEPARATED
   - Need direct measure of memorization (same-distribution performance)
   - Show that improvements aren't just from smaller effective capacity
   - Risk: "Claimed generalization improvements are actually just regularization"

TIER 2 - MODERATE RISK:

5. SCALABILITY QUESTIONS
   - Do results hold for larger models? Modern instruction-tuned LLMs?
   - Different Transformer variants (vision, state-space models)?
   - Risk: "Method doesn't scale to practical large-language-model settings"

6. HYPERPARAMETER SENSITIVITY
   - How do you choose which modules to fine-tune on new tasks?
   - Does method require per-task tuning?
   - Risk: "Method lacks practical guidance; requires extensive tuning per domain"

7. STATISTICAL RIGOR
   - Need confidence intervals, significance tests, multiple seeds
   - How stable are results across random initializations?
   - Risk: "Results lack statistical rigor; improvements within noise margins"

8. COMPUTATIONAL TRADE-OFFS
   - Training time vs. standard fine-tuning?
   - Inference speed impact?
   - Risk: "Improvements come at unacceptable computational cost"

TIER 3 - LOWER PRIORITY BUT IMPORTANT:

9. ERROR ANALYSIS & FAILURE MODES
   - When does selective SFT underperform full fine-tuning?
   - Which reasoning task types benefit most?

10. BENCHMARK DESIGN ANALYSIS
    - Are GeneralPoints and V-IRL truly representative?
    - Could simpler methods (standard FT + regularization) achieve same results?

11. THEORETICAL GROUNDING
    - Why should selective attention fine-tuning improve OOD generalization?
    - Can you provide theoretical analysis or detailed intuition?
