SEARCH METHODOLOGY
==================
- Searched 500+ human reviews from ICLR 2025 reviews directory
- Focused on 5 key topics:
  1. Fine-tuning, SFT, memorization, overfitting
  2. Out-of-distribution generalization
  3. Benchmarks and evaluation methodology
  4. Transformer architecture and selective fine-tuning
  5. Scalability and limited scope concerns
- Read and analyzed 8 key reviews in detail
- Extracted 11 major weakness patterns applicable to the SFT paper

===============================================================================
11 CONCRETE WEAKNESS PATTERNS IDENTIFIED
===============================================================================

1. LIMITED BENCHMARK EVALUATION & GENERALIZATION CONCERNS
   - Risk: Reviewers question whether selective attention SFT generalizes beyond
     GeneralPoints and V-IRL benchmarks
   - Mitigation: Expand to diverse reasoning tasks, analyze task characteristics

2. INSUFFICIENT ANALYSIS OF FAILURE MODES & DOMAIN SPECIFICITY
   - Risk: Results appear task-specific without mechanistic insight
   - Mitigation: Clearly explain WHY attention-only SFT improves OOD generalization

3. MEMORIZATION VS. GENERALIZATION NOT CLEARLY DISTINGUISHED
   - Risk: Improvements conflated with actual learning vs. memorization
   - Mitigation: Measure memorization (ID accuracy) vs. generalization (OOD shift) separately

4. HYPERPARAMETER SENSITIVITY & REPRODUCIBILITY ISSUES
   - Risk: Method requires per-task tuning, undermining general applicability
   - Mitigation: Provide clear hyperparameter selection guidance across tasks

5. INCOMPLETE ABLATION STUDIES & MISSING BASELINES
   - Risk: Improvements may come from confounding factors, not proposed mechanism
   - Mitigation: Ablate attention vs. feedforward, selective percentages, compare to
     LoRA, prefix tuning, adapter methods

6. LIMITED SCOPE & SCALABILITY QUESTIONS
   - Risk: Results don't generalize to larger models or different architectures
   - Mitigation: Test on modern large models, different transformer variants

7. THEORETICAL JUSTIFICATION OR MECHANISTIC UNDERSTANDING MISSING
   - Risk: Empirical findings lack grounding; "why" claims not substantiated
   - Mitigation: Provide theoretical analysis or detailed mechanistic explanations

8. OOD EVALUATION & GENERALIZATION GAPS NOT RIGOROUSLY MEASURED
   - Risk: OOD evaluation protocol unclear; distribution shift not well-defined
   - Mitigation: Rigorous OOD protocol with clear shift definitions and strong baselines

9. INSUFFICIENT ERROR ANALYSIS & EDGE CASE HANDLING
   - Risk: When methods fail unclear; statistical significance not established
   - Mitigation: Confidence intervals, identify failure modes, significance tests

10. PRACTICAL APPLICABILITY & COMPUTATIONAL TRADE-OFFS NOT ADDRESSED
    - Risk: Improvements ignore computational costs and inference latency
    - Mitigation: Quantify training time, memory, inference cost trade-offs

11. BENCHMARK DESIGN ARTIFACTS & LIMITED TASK DIVERSITY
    - Risk: Benchmarks too simplistic, biased toward proposed method
    - Mitigation: Analyze task characteristics, ensure representative diversity

===============================================================================
KEY RECOMMENDATIONS FOR SFT PAPER
===============================================================================

HIGH PRIORITY:
- Expand evaluation beyond 2 benchmarks; establish task diversity
- Provide mechanistic insight into why selective attention SFT improves OOD gen
- Separate memorization from generalization measurements
- Include LoRA, prefix tuning, adapter tuning baselines
- Test on larger, modern models (LLaMA 7B+, modern instruction-tuned models)

MEDIUM PRIORITY:
- Comprehensive ablations (attention/feedforward selectivity, %s, interactions)
- Statistical rigor (confidence intervals, significance tests, multiple seeds)
- Computational cost analysis (training time, memory, inference)
- Failure mode analysis and hyperparameter guidance

OPTIONAL BUT VALUABLE:
- Theoretical analysis of why selective attention fine-tuning aids OOD gen
- Analysis of task characteristics (complexity, length, reasoning type)
- Visualization of learning dynamics and attention weight changes
- Investigation of interaction with model size and pre-training quality

===============================================================================
CRITICAL QUESTIONS LIKELY TO BE ASKED
===============================================================================

1. "Why does attention-only tuning help with OOD generalization beyond these
   two tasks?"

2. "How does selective SFT compare to LoRA and other parameter-efficient
   fine-tuning methods?"

3. "Do results hold for larger, modern language models?"

4. "Are you measuring memorization or actual generalization capability?"

5. "What is the computational overhead? Does selective SFT speed up training
   or inference?"

6. "How sensitive is the method to hyperparameter selection? How do you
   choose which modules to fine-tune on new tasks?"

7. "Why are GeneralPoints and V-IRL representative of reasoning tasks? How do
   task characteristics affect results?"

8. "Do results generalize to other transformer architectures (e.g.,
   vision transformers, state-space models)?"

9. "What happens when you have small training sets or extreme distribution
   shifts?"

10. "How does your method compare to standard full fine-tuning with
    regularization (e.g., weight decay, early stopping)?"
