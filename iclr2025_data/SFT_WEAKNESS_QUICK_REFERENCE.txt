QUICK REFERENCE: SPECIFIC WEAKNESS PATTERNS FROM HUMAN REVIEWS
Applicable to: SFT WITHOUT OVERFITTING Paper
================================================================================

CRITICAL WEAKNESSES (reviewers frequently cite these):

1. MISSING BASELINES / INCOMPLETE COMPARISONS
   ─────────────────────────────────────────
   Found: 17 instances across reviews

   SPECIFIC QUOTE EXAMPLES:

   [1] "The benchmarking in the paper is not sufficient. If the author's claim is
       that the proposed procedure contributes to model selection, the author ought
       to compare it to other existing model selection methods."
       Source: i4ouG6Kc8M (A Dual-Metric Approach)

   [2] "The experiments were far from adequate: there should have been multiple
       runs (for uncertainty quantification) and more 'modern' baselines to
       compare with."
       Source: i3f2N3iHl0 (Adaptive Tensor Attention Networks)

   APPLIES TO SFT PAPER:
   - Must compare selective fine-tuning (attention-only, FNN-only) with full models
   - Must compare SFT methods against RL baselines
   - Must include parameter-efficient fine-tuning approaches
   - Need multiple runs with error bars/confidence intervals

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

2. HYPERPARAMETER TUNING FAIRNESS
   ───────────────────────────────
   Found: 8+ instances across reviews

   SPECIFIC QUOTE EXAMPLES:

   [1] "Experimental results are not satisfactory to justify the efficacy of the
       proposed methods. Only small datasets are included. Meanwhile, the ResNet18
       baseline seems not well tuned (with low acc less than 90%)."
       Source: XCugWIuHR8 (Convex Distillation)

   [2] "I think there could have been additional experiments to explore how CCE
       performs relative to baselines as different hyperparameters vary (e.g.,
       relative size of vocabulary vs sequence length vs. hidden dim, sparsity)."
       Source: E4Fk3YuG56 (Cut Your Losses)

   [3] "Hyperparameter choices... introduces multiple novel hyperparameters:
       the decay factor, the maximal length of the sequence after the first
       decimating layer and the number of layers to decimate. And it does not
       seem very clear how to make these choices without a gridsearch?"
       Source: iWSl5Zyjjw (DeciMamba)

   APPLIES TO SFT PAPER:
   - Learning rate is CRITICAL (paper emphasizes learning rate's role)
   - Must show comparisons across different learning rates for all methods
   - All baselines must be tuned with equal effort
   - Cannot claim differences are architectural if they're due to tuning effort

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

3. LIMITED EVALUATION SCOPE / NARROW DOMAINS
   ──────────────────────────────────────────
   Found: 4+ instances in evaluation critiques

   SPECIFIC QUOTE EXAMPLES:

   [1] "Poor evaluation with limited experiments and even more limited comparisons.
       The proposed method is validated only in one medical dataset. I would
       suggest to test it against other datasets too."
       Source: CpQegoH1Fn (Human-in-the-loop Neural Networks)

   [2] "Diversity of domains/tasks/modalities... is still very limited compared
       to much more general benchmarks out there for general deep learning
       evaluation. Adding more domains, tasks and modalities would be very
       useful..."
       Source: sahQq2sH5x (Benchmarking Predictive Coding Networks)

   [3] "applying MOE to a narrow problem (table generation). And the results are
       not all that strong."
       Source: 2RNGX3iTr6 (Tabby: Tabular Adaptation)

   APPLIES TO SFT PAPER:
   - Cannot claim attention/FNN differences are universal if tested on limited tasks
   - Must evaluate on: language understanding, generation, reasoning, code, etc.
   - Must show findings hold across different LLM base models
   - Narrow domain claims damage credibility of general architectural insights

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

IMPORTANT WEAKNESSES (reviewers mention regularly):

4. LACK OF THEORETICAL JUSTIFICATION
   ─────────────────────────────────
   Found: 7+ instances

   SPECIFIC QUOTE EXAMPLES:

   [1] "The S2 hacking hypothesis is quite vague and the author do not present
       any deep understanding that would explain the mechanisms by which certain
       attention heads pay extra attention to the S2 token."
       Source: FbZSZEIkEU (Adaptive Circuit Behavior)

   [2] "It is unclear why introducing a student model as an intermediary reduces
       trajectory variance, or what specific role the student network plays
       throughout the data distillation process. The authors are encouraged to
       provide further discussion or insights into this mechanism."
       Source: c61unr33XA (Dataset Distillation via Knowledge Distillation)

   APPLIES TO SFT PAPER:
   - Cannot just observe that attention and FNNs differ in memorization
   - Must explain WHY/HOW they differ mechanistically
   - Need theory connecting module properties to memorization behavior
   - Need explanation for why differences would transfer to new domains

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

5. INSUFFICIENT GENERALIZATION EVIDENCE
   ────────────────────────────────────
   Found: 4+ instances in generalization papers

   SPECIFIC QUOTE EXAMPLES:

   [1] "The authors did not provide clear evidence that this model is able to
       maintain good performance across tasks not in the training set. I did not
       see any prose on *how* these two datasets differ. What tasks are seen in
       PandaLM that aren't seen in the JudgeLM dataset?"
       Source: xsELpEPn4A (JudgeLM)

   [2] "Since the authors explore the meta learning problem, the experimental
       results should focus on how the proposed learning method improves
       generalizability on previously unobserved dynamics... the experiment
       section is not detailed enough to support... 'our goal is to develop
       a model that can generalize from a set of training systems to new,
       unseen test systems'."
       Source: S8nFZ98pmU (Contrastive Meta Learning)

   APPLIES TO SFT PAPER:
   - Claims about "OOD generalization" are central to paper
   - Must clearly separate in-distribution vs OOD evaluation
   - Must explain what makes evaluation truly "out-of-distribution"
   - Data leakage between train/test is a major issue

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

MINOR BUT NOTABLE:

6. UNFAIR EXPERIMENTAL SETUP
   ──────────────────────────
   Found: 2+ instances (but highly critical when present)

   SPECIFIC QUOTE EXAMPLES:

   [1] "This provides somewhat of an unfair evaluation as JudgeLM is trained on
       GPT generated judgements as well. Even with human validation, there is a
       reasonable chance that if this dataset where annotated by a different LLM
       and produced different judgements, humans checking responses would also
       consider them reasonable."
       Source: xsELpEPn4A (JudgeLM)

   [2] "Since the proposed method requires computing DFT, comparing it to ML
       force field might not be fair due to the higher computational cost."
       Source: FhBT596F1X (Learning Equivariant Non-Local Electron Density)

   APPLIES TO SFT PAPER:
   - If evaluation task is similar to training task, this creates bias
   - All methods must be compared on equal computational footing
   - Cannot inadvertently favor one method through experimental design

================================================================================
SUMMARY: HIGHEST RISK AREAS FOR SFT PAPER REJECTION
================================================================================

RANK 1 (CRITICAL):
  • Missing comparisons with relevant baselines and ablations
  • Unfair hyperparameter tuning / learning rate not controlled

RANK 2 (HIGH):
  • Limited evaluation domain (not enough diverse tasks)
  • Lack of theoretical explanation for why modules differ
  • Insufficient evidence for OOD generalization claims

RANK 3 (MODERATE):
  • Statistical rigor (multiple runs, error bars, significance tests)
  • Narrow problem scope
  • Missing mechanistic understanding

================================================================================
RECOMMENDATIONS FOR AUTHORS
================================================================================

BEFORE SUBMISSION:

[ ] Ensure all methods (full SFT, attention-only SFT, FNN-only SFT, RL) are
    fairly optimized with equal hyperparameter tuning effort

[ ] Vary learning rate explicitly and show results for all methods across LR range

[ ] Evaluate on at least 5-8 diverse task categories (not just one domain)

[ ] Provide mechanical explanation for why attention/FNN modules differ in
    memorization behavior

[ ] Compare against selective fine-tuning baselines (adapters, LoRA) and
    RL methods

[ ] Run experiments with multiple seeds and report error bars/confidence intervals

[ ] Clearly define what makes evaluation truly "out-of-distribution" vs
    in-distribution

[ ] Include statistical significance tests

[ ] Provide ablation studies showing contribution of each component
