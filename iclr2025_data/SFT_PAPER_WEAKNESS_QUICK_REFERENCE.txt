QUICK REFERENCE: SFT PAPER WEAKNESS PATTERNS WITH REVIEWER QUOTES
================================================================================

Paper: "SFT WITHOUT OVERFITTING: ANALYZING THE TRAINING DYNAMICS OF SUPERVISED FINE-TUNING"
Main claim: Selective attention-only fine-tuning preserves OOD performance; full/FNN-only FT causes memorization collapse on GeneralPoints + V-IRL benchmarks

================================================================================
WEAKNESS 1: LIMITED BENCHMARKS (2 DOMAINS ONLY)
================================================================================

Pattern: Two rule-based benchmarks insufficient to claim general SFT principle

Quote (Transformer Architecture, lXRDQsiP2v):
"if the method needs tuning for each specific task that definitely takes quite a
bit away from its appeal. Transformers are amazing (in part) because they require
little tuning from task to task."

Why it matters: GeneralPoints + V-IRL are both narrow, structured tasks. Different
reasoning types (language, commonsense, symbolic) may show opposite patterns.

Likelihood of criticism: 95%

Fix: Add 3+ benchmarks spanning different reasoning types (symbolic, commonsense,
language understanding)

================================================================================
WEAKNESS 2: NO MECHANISTIC EXPLANATION
================================================================================

Pattern: Empirical results without explanation of WHY selective attention helps OOD

Quote (Domain Adaptation, ijwYWoChN9):
"The foundational hypothesis that 'PLMs encapsulate multiple pieces of knowledge
as subnetworks' lacks supporting references or verification experiments. Although
empirical results support DST's effectiveness, the Introduction lacks a clear
causal rationale for these core design choices."

Why it matters: Without mechanistic insight, results appear task-specific luck
rather than genuine understanding of SFT dynamics. Reviewers want to know: Is it
attention modularity? Lower memorization capacity? Better gradient flow?

Likelihood of criticism: 90%

Fix: Provide mechanistic explanation via gradient analysis, representation study,
or capacity measurement

================================================================================
WEAKNESS 3: MEMORIZATION VS. GENERALIZATION CONFLATED
================================================================================

Pattern: Claimed "OOD improvements" may just be regularization from smaller capacity

Quote (Continual Learning, EDJ7cPZk7V):
"Most evaluations, including the ablation studies, focus on downstream tasks,
which are interesting and practically relevant but do not provide clear insight
into what the algorithm is doing... they also add confounding factors."

Why it matters: Must separately measure:
- ID accuracy (how well on training rules)
- OOD accuracy (how well on held-out rules)
- The GAP between them (actual generalization improvement)

If ID accuracy drops proportionally, the "improvement" is just regularization,
not true generalization.

Likelihood of criticism: 80%

Fix: Report ID and OOD performance separately. Show attention-only maintains ID
while improving OOD, not just learning less overall.

================================================================================
WEAKNESS 4: HYPERPARAMETER SENSITIVITY
================================================================================

Pattern: Method requires per-task tuning of which modules to fine-tune

Quote (Transformer Architecture, lXRDQsiP2v):
"if the method needs tuning for each specific task that definitely takes quite
a bit away from its appeal"

Quote (Continual Learning, EDJ7cPZk7V):
"If choosing hyperparameters via repetitive experiments, then it may defeat the
premise of continual learning"

Why it matters: If you must tune which modules to fine-tune for each new task,
the method isn't practical or general. Reviewers will ask: "How do you know to
use attention-only for a new task?"

Likelihood of criticism: 65%

Fix: Develop clear criteria for module selection OR show that same selection
(e.g., "always attention-only") works across diverse benchmarks

================================================================================
WEAKNESS 5: INCOMPLETE ABLATIONS & MISSING BASELINES
================================================================================

Pattern: Only 3 methods (full FT, FNN-only, attention-only) compared on 2 datasets

Quote (Domain Adaptation, ijwYWoChN9):
"L_KDL is not ablated to show its usefulness in this work"

Why it matters: Missing:
- Attention-only at different selectivity percentages (50%, 75%, 90% of layers)
- LoRA, BitFit, adapter-based fine-tuning comparison
- Full FT + regularization (L2, dropout, early stopping)
- Results across 2-3 model sizes

Likelihood of criticism: 85%

Fix: Add ablations on selectivity %, compare against LoRA and efficient FT methods,
test on multiple model scales

================================================================================
WEAKNESS 6: SCALABILITY UNKNOWN
================================================================================

Pattern: Results may not hold for larger models or diverse architectures

Quote (DPO Generalization, bGkPZtisSm):
"Llama 2-7B is relatively old, experiments on 3/3.1/3.2 would be better"

Quote (Continual Learning, EDJ7cPZk7V):
"The paper only explores ResNet and its smaller variants... For other architectures
such as transformers, VGG net, etc do the same conclusions stand?"

Why it matters: Attention/FNN specialization may be specific to model size/architecture
you tested. Modern large models may have different module properties.

Likelihood of criticism: 75%

Fix: Test on at least 7B and 13B models (or your equivalent scale). Show pattern
consistency across scales.

================================================================================
WEAKNESS 7: NO THEORETICAL JUSTIFICATION
================================================================================

Pattern: Theory-practice gap: Why SHOULD selective attention improve OOD?

Quote (Continual Learning, EDJ7cPZk7V):
"No explanation or intuition is provided as to why medium learning speed items
are the most useful for populating memory. It would be good if the authors provided
a rationale beyond the empirical results."

Why it matters: Paper feels shallow without theory. Reviewers expect either:
- Theoretical analysis of why attention is more modular, OR
- Detailed empirical analysis (gradient flow, representation changes, etc.)

Likelihood of criticism: 70%

Fix: Provide intuitive explanation of mechanism (attention is data-dependent?
Attention has different gradient properties? Attention encodes invariant patterns?)

================================================================================
WEAKNESS 8: OOD EVALUATION NOT RIGOROUS
================================================================================

Pattern: OOD settings unclear; no statistical significance testing

Quote (Time Series, nibeaHUEJx):
"A natural follow-up question is how well the guidance network performs in OOD
settings where the ground truth shifts are known but the time series were not
part of the training data"

Quote (Time Series, nibeaHUEJx):
"Based on the standard deviations computed there is a fair overlap between prior
methods and your method, it's possible that the advantage in those cases is not
so clear when there is a large variance."

Why it matters: Need:
- Clear definition of OOD (held-out rules? longer sequences? new compositions?)
- Multiple seeds (5-10) with statistical significance tests
- Confidence intervals, not just mean ± SD
- Analysis of hard OOD settings

Likelihood of criticism: 75%

Fix: Define OOD rigorously. Run 5-10 seeds. Report confidence intervals and
statistical tests (t-test, Mann-Whitney U).
