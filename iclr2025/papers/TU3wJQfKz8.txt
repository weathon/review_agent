# LAST-ITERATE CONVERGENCE OF SMOOTH REGRET MATCHING<sup>+</sup> VARIANTS IN LEARNING NASH EQUILIBRIA

###### Anonymous authors

Paper under double-blind review

## ABSTRACT

Regret Matching<sup>+</sup> (RM<sup>+</sup>) variants have been widely developed to superhuman Poker AIs, yet few studies investigate their last-iterate convergence. Their last-iterate convergence has been demonstrated only for games with strong monotonicity or two-player zero-sum matrix games. A primary obstacle in proving the last-iterate convergence for these algorithms is that their feedback is not the loss gradient of the vanilla games. This deviation results in the absence of crucial properties, *e.g.*, monotonicity or the weak Minty variation inequality (MVI), which are pivotal for establishing the last-iterate convergence. To address the absence of these properties, we propose a remarkably succinct yet novel proof paradigm that consists of: (i) recovering these key properties through the equivalence between RM<sup>+</sup> and Online Mirror Descent (OMD), and (ii) measuring the the distance to Nash equilibrium (NE) via the tangent residual to show this distance is related to the distance between accumulated regrets. To show the practical applicability of our proof paradigm, we use it to prove the last-iterate convergence of two existing smooth RM<sup>+</sup> variants, Smooth Extra-gradient RM<sup>+</sup> (SExRM<sup>+</sup>) and Smooth Predictive RM<sup>+</sup> (SPRM<sup>+</sup>). We show that they achieve last-iterate convergence in learning an NE of games satisfying monotonicity, a weaker condition than the one used in existing proofs for both variants. Then, inspired by our proof paradigm, we propose Smooth Optimistic Gradient RM<sup>+</sup> (SOGRM<sup>+</sup>). We show that SOGRM<sup>+</sup> achieves last-iterate convergence in learning an NE of games satisfying the weak MVI, the weakest condition in all known proofs for RM<sup>+</sup> variants. The experimental results show that SOGRM<sup>+</sup> significantly outperforms other algorithms.

## 1 INTRODUCTION

Nash Equilibrium (NE) is a fundamental concept in the field of game theory. Recent advancements in superhuman game AI, are largely attributed to NE learning (Moravčík et al., 2017; Brown & Sandholm, 2018; 2019; Pérolat et al., 2022). Despite these advancements, the most popular algorithms for learning an NE—no-regret algorithms, typically achieve only average-iterate convergence. Moreover, in two-player zero-sum matrix games, these algorithms are prone to divergence or cyclic behavior (Bailey & Piliouras, 2018; Mertikopoulos et al., 2018b; Pérolat et al., 2021). Average-iterate convergence requires strategy averaging. This averaging poses significant challenges in large-scale games where function approximation is used to represent the strategy since a new function has to be trained to represent the average strategy (Liu et al., 2023).

To address the challenges related to averaging, numerous studies consider the last-iterate convergence, ensuring iterates converge to NE (Mertikopoulos et al., 2018a; Daskalakis & Panageas, 2019; Tataranenko & Kamgarpour, 2020; Wei et al., 2021; Lee et al., 2021; Cen et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2023; Sokota et al., 2023; Abe et al., 2022a;b; 2023; Pérolat et al., 2021; 2022; Cai & Zheng, 2023). These algorithms are based on Online Mirror Descent (OMD) or Follow the Regularized Leader (FTRL). Despite their theoretical appeal, Regret Matching<sup>+</sup> (RM<sup>+</sup>) variants (Bowling et al., 2015; Farina et al., 2021; 2023), are more commonly utilized in solving real-world games. Precisely, they are widely used in superhuman Poker AIs (Bowling et al., 2015; Moravčík et al., 2017; Brown & Sandholm, 2018). The key distinction between RM<sup>+</sup> variants and FTRL/OMD based algorithms is that RM<sup>+</sup> variants update within the (subset of the) non-negative orthant, whereas FTRL/OMD based algorithms update within the original strategy space of the game.

Unfortunately, few studies investigate the last-iterate convergence of  $RM^+$  variants. To date, the only known results on the last-iterate convergence of  $RM^+$  variants are confined to a specific game with strong monotonicity (Meng et al., 2023) or two-player zero-sum matrix games (Cai et al., 2023). In contrast, the OMD/FTRL based algorithms achieve last-iterate convergence in a broader class of games, those that satisfy monotonicity. These games are also called as monotone games (Cai et al., 2022b; Cai & Zheng, 2023; Abe et al., 2023; Pérolat et al., 2021; 2022). They cover several common game types, such as two-player zero-sum matrix games and convex-concave games, along with significant applications like the training of Large Language Models (LLM) (Munos et al., 2023). Moreover, recent studies show that OMD/FTRL based algorithms even achieve last-iterate convergence in learning an NE of games satisfying the weak Minty variation inequality (MVI) (Cai & Zheng, 2022; Diakonikolas, 2020; Pethick et al., 2023). Weak MVI is weaker and covers more games than monotonicity. It includes applications like Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN) (Cai & Zheng, 2022). Therefore, a key question is:

*Do  $RM^+$  variants achieve last-iterate convergence in learning an NE of games satisfying monotonicity or even only the weak MVI?*

Compared to traditional no-regret algorithms, e.g., FTRL/OMD based algorithms, the primary challenge in proving the last-iterate convergence of  $RM^+$  variants is that their feedback is not the loss gradients of the vanilla games. This deviation results in the absence of crucial properties, e.g., monotonicity or weak MVI, which are pivotal for establishing the last-iterate convergence.

**Contributions.** (i) To address the absence of crucial properties, e.g., monotonicity or weak MVI, we introduce a novel proof paradigm. Firstly, it recovers these properties by leveraging the equivalence between  $RM^+$  and OMD in Liu et al. (2021). Secondly, it measures the distance of  $RM^+$  variants to NE via the tangent residual (Cai et al., 2022b) to show that this distance is related to the distance between accumulated regrets. Specifically, in  $RM^+$  variants, the feedback does not exhibit monotonicity or weak MVI. However, in their OMD equivalents, the feedback is the loss gradient of the vanilla games, which satisfies monotonicity or weak MVI. Then, We establish the last-iterate convergence of  $RM^+$  variants by demonstrating that the distance between accumulated regrets, rather than the strategies in OMD based algorithms, converges to 0. This convergence occurs since, in  $RM^+$  variants, the tangent residual converges 0 as this distance converges to 0. (ii) To show the practical applicability of our proof paradigm, we utilize this paradigm to establish that two existing smooth  $RM^+$  variants (Farina et al., 2023), Smooth Extra-gradient  $RM^+$  (SExRM<sup>+</sup>) and Smooth Predictive  $RM^+$  (SPRM<sup>+</sup>), achieve last-iterate convergence in learning an NE of games satisfying monotonicity. (iii) Inspired by our proof paradigm, we propose Smooth Optimistic Gradient  $RM^+$  (SOGRM<sup>+</sup>), which combines Optimistic Gradient (OG) (Cai & Zheng, 2022) and smooth  $RM^+$  variants. SOGRM<sup>+</sup> achieves last-iterate convergence in games satisfying the weak MVI. (iv) Experimental results show that SOGRM<sup>+</sup> significantly outperforms other algorithms. (v) Our proof paradigm yields explicit best-iterate convergence rates for SExRM<sup>+</sup>, SPRM<sup>+</sup>, and SOGRM<sup>+</sup> without any modifications.

## 2 PRELIMINARIES

### 2.1 SMOOTH GAMES AND TANGENT RESIDUAL

**Smooth games.** In this paper, we consider smooth games whose strategy space is simplex. We use  $x_i \in \mathcal{X}_i$  to denote the strategy of player  $i$  and  $x = \{x_i | i \in \mathcal{N}\}$  to represent the strategy profile, where  $\mathcal{X}_i$  is an  $(|A_i| - 1)$ -dimension simplex,  $|A_i|$  is the dimension of  $\mathcal{X}_i$ , and  $\mathcal{N}$  is the set of players. The utility of player  $i$  if all players follow strategy profile  $x$  is  $-\infty < u_i(x_i, x_{-i}) < +\infty$ , where  $-i$  is the players other than  $i$ . For any  $i$  and the fixed  $x_{-i} \in \mathcal{X}_{-i}$ ,  $u_i(x_i, x_{-i})$  is a concave function w.r.t.  $x_i \in \mathcal{X}_i$ . Also,  $\ell_i^x = -\nabla_{x_i} u_i(x_i, x_{-i})$  is loss gradient. In smooth games,

$$\|\ell^x - \ell^{x'}\|_2 \leq L\|x - x'\|_2, \forall x, x' \in \mathcal{X},$$

where  $\ell^x = [\ell_i^x : i \in \mathcal{N}]$ , and  $L > 0$  is a constant. In addition, we assume  $\|\ell_i^x\|_1 \leq P$  for each player  $i$  and strategy  $x$ , where  $P$  is a positive constant.

**Nash equilibrium (NE).** In NE, for any player, her strategy is the best-response to the strategies of others. The notation  $\mathcal{X}^*$  denotes the set of NE. As  $u_i(x_i, x_{-i})$  is a concave function w.r.t.  $x_i \in \mathcal{X}_i$ , then  $\forall x^* \in \mathcal{X}^*, x \in \mathcal{X}, \langle \ell_i^{x^*}, x_i^* - x_i \rangle \leq 0$  (Facchinei, 2003).

**Monotonicity.** Smooth games with monotonicity is called smooth monotone games, which include many common and well-studied classes of games, such as two-player zero-sum matrix games and convex-concave games. The most important property of monotone games is monotonicity

$$\langle \ell^x - \ell^{x'}, x - x' \rangle \geq 0, \forall x, x' \in \mathcal{X}.$$

Monotonicity is the most widely used assumption in existing works about the last-iterate convergence.

**Minty variation inequality (MVI).** From  $\langle \ell^{x^*}, x^* - x \rangle \leq 0$  and monotonicity, we get  $\langle \ell^x, x - x^* \rangle \geq 0, \forall x \in \mathcal{X}, \exists x^* \in \mathcal{X}^*$ , also called MVI. MVI is weaker than monotonicity as MVI holds if monotonicity holds, but not vice versa. We provide an example of games that satisfy the weak MVI but not monotonicity in Appendix G.

**Weak MVI.** Some recent works consider a weaker assumption than monotonicity and MVI called weak MVI (Cai & Zheng, 2022; Diakonikolas, 2020; Pethick et al., 2023; Cai et al., 2022a), which covers more game types. Formally, weak MVI with  $\rho \leq 0$  implies there exists  $x^* \in \mathcal{X}^*$  that ensures

$$\langle \ell^x + z, x - x^* \rangle \geq \rho \|\ell^x + z\|_2^2, \forall z \in \mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{X}}(x), x \in \mathcal{X},$$

where  $\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{X}}(x) = \{v \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{X}|} : \langle v, x' - x \rangle \leq 0, \forall x' \in \mathcal{X}\}$  is the normal cone of  $x$ . If  $\rho \rightarrow -\infty$ , intuitively, any smooth games satisfy the weak MVI. In Appendix G, we provide a smooth game that satisfies the weak MVI with  $0 > \rho > -\infty$  and does not satisfy the MVI. The relations between monotonicity, the MVI, and the weak MVI, is that  $\text{monotonicity} \subseteq \text{MVI} \subseteq \text{weak MVI}$ .

**Tangent residual.** To measure the distance from a strategy profile to the set of NE, we employ the tangent residual provided by Cai et al. (2022b). Formally,  $\forall x \in \mathcal{X}$ , its tangent residual is

$$r^{tan}(x) = \min_{z \in N_{\mathcal{X}}(x)} \|\ell^x + z\|_2.$$

If  $r^{tan}(x) = 0$ , then  $x$  is an NE in smooth games. Also, if  $x$  is an NE in smooth games,  $r^{tan}(x) = 0$ .

**Last-iterate convergence.** In this paper, this convergence refers to the behavior where the sequence of strategy profiles converges to the set of NEs. As previously discussed, if  $\lim_{t \rightarrow \infty} r^{tan}(x^t) = 0$ , then  $x^t$  is an NE, which implies that  $x^t$  converges to the set of NE. However, it is important to note that Cai et al. (2023) define a stronger concept of convergence, known as last-iterate convergence of the iterates. In contrast to the last-iterate convergence discussed in our paper, their definition implies that  $x^t$  converges to a specific point within the set of NE. Our results do not pertain to the last-iterate convergence of the iterates, whereas the results regarding SEXRM<sup>+</sup> and SPRM<sup>+</sup> in Cai et al. (2023) do.

### 2.2 REGRET MATCHING<sup>+</sup>

**Online convex optimization.** Each player  $i$  selects a decision  $x_i^t$  via the feedback in this framework. Such feedback is the loss gradient  $\ell_i^{t-1} = \ell_i^{x_i^{t-1}}$  in solving smooth games. No-regret algorithms are the algorithms, which ensures the regret  $R_i^T(x) = \max_{x_i \in \mathcal{X}_i} \sum_{t=1}^T (\ell_i^t, x_i^t - x_i)$  to grow sublinearly, where  $x_i^t$  is the decision at iteration  $t$ .

**Online mirror descent (OMD).** OMD is a traditional no-regret algorithm (Nemirovskij & Yudin, 1983). Let  $q_i^t(\cdot) : \mathcal{X}_i \rightarrow \mathbb{R}, \forall t \geq 0$ , OMD generates the decisions via the prox-mapping operator

$$x_i^{t+1} \in \arg \min_{x_i \in \mathcal{X}_i} \{ \langle \ell_i^t, x_i \rangle + q_i^t(x_i) + D_{q_i^{t-1}}(x_i, x_i^t) \},$$

where  $q_i^{0:t-1}(\cdot) = q_i^0(\cdot) + q_i^1(\cdot) + \dots + q_i^{t-1}(\cdot)$ , and  $D_{q_i^{t-1}}(x, y) = q_i^{0:t-1}(x) - q_i^{0:t-1}(y) - \langle \nabla q_i^{0:t-1}(y), x - y \rangle$  is the Bregman divergence associated with  $q_i^{0:t-1}(\cdot)$ . Notably, we employ the definition of OMD in Joulan et al. (2017) and Liu et al. (2021), which represents a generalization of the standard OMD, to demonstrate the equivalence between RM<sup>+</sup> and OMD as proposed by Liu et al. (2021). To recover the standard OMD, we can set  $q_i^0 = \phi(\cdot)/\eta$  and  $q_i^t = 0$  for all  $t \geq 1$ , where  $\phi(\cdot)$  is a 1-strongly convex regularizer with respect to some norm in the decision space  $\mathcal{X}$ , and  $\eta > 0$ .

**Blackwell approachability framework.** RM<sup>+</sup> variants are from this framework whose core insight lies in reframing the problem of regret minimization within  $\mathcal{X}_i$  as regret minimization within  $\text{cone}(\mathcal{X}_i)$  (Abernethy et al., 2011). Specifically, a regret minimization algorithm is instantiated in  $\text{cone}(\mathcal{X}_i)$ , where its output at iteration  $t$  is  $\theta_i^t$ . This corresponds to the strategy  $x_i^t = \theta_i^t / \langle \theta_i^t, \mathbf{1} \rangle$  within  $\mathcal{X}_i$ . Given the loss  $\ell_i^t$  at iteration  $t$ , the algorithm observes the transformed loss  $F_i(\theta_i^t) = \langle \ell_i^t, x_i^t \rangle \mathbf{1} - \ell_i^t$  and subsequently generates  $\theta_i^{t+1}$ .

**Regret Matching<sup>+</sup> (RM<sup>+</sup>) (Bowing et al., 2015).** RM<sup>+</sup> keeps track of the accumulated regret  $\theta_i^t$ . In RM<sup>+</sup>, the strategy  $x_i^t$  at each iteration  $t$  is denoted by  $x_i^t = \theta_i^t / \|\theta_i^t\|_1$ . It updates its accumulated regret  $\theta_i^t$  via the regret matching<sup>+</sup> operator (Bowing et al., 2015)

$$\theta_i^{t+1} = [\theta_i^t + \eta F_i^t(\theta_i^t)]^+,$$

where  $\eta > 0$  is the step-size,  $F_i(\theta^t) = \langle \ell_i^t, x_i^t \rangle \mathbf{1} - \ell_i^t$  ( $\theta^t = \{\theta_i^t : i \in \mathcal{N}\}$ ). As analyzed in Farina et al. (2021), RM<sup>+</sup> is closely connected to an OMD instance which updates in  $\text{cone}(\mathcal{X}_i)$  and faces a sequence of loss  $[F_i^t(\theta^t)]_{t \geq 1}$ . Formally, RM<sup>+</sup> can be rewritten as

$$\theta_i^{t+1} \in \arg \min_{\theta_i \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{|\mathcal{X}_i|}} \{ \langle -F_i(\theta^t), \theta_i \rangle + \frac{1}{\eta} D_\psi(\theta_i, \theta_i^t) \}, \quad x_i^{t+1} = \frac{\theta_i^{t+1}}{\|\theta_i^{t+1}\|_1},$$

where  $\psi(\alpha) = \|\alpha\|_2^2/2$  is the quadratic regularizer, and  $\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^d = \{y | y \in \mathbb{R}^d, y \geq 0\}$ .

**Equivalence between RM<sup>+</sup> and OMD in Liu et al. (2021).** The analysis in Farina et al. (2021) is the main approach for proving the last-iterate convergence of RM<sup>+</sup> variants (Meng et al., 2023; Cai et al., 2023). However, in this analysis, the feedback  $(-F(\theta^t) = [-F_i(\theta^t) : i \in \mathcal{N}])$  does not enjoy monotonicity or the weak MVI, crucial for proving the last-iterate convergence in existing works.

To recover monotonicity or the weak MVI, we use the equivalence provided by [Liu et al. \(2021\)](#) to rewrite  $\text{RM}^+$  as

$$\mathbf{x}_i^{t+1} \in \arg \min_{\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathcal{X}_i} \{(\ell_i^t, \mathbf{x}_i) + q_i^t(\mathbf{x}_i) + D_{q^{t-1}}(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_i^t), q_i^{0,t}(\mathbf{x}_i) = \frac{\|\theta_i^{t+1}\|_1}{\eta} \psi(\mathbf{x}_i)\}. \quad (1)$$

The feedback  $(\ell^{t+1} = [\ell_i^{t+1} : i \in \mathcal{N}])$  is the loss gradient of the vanilla game, which enjoys monotonicity or the weak MVI. Therefore, we recover monotonicity or the weak MVI via this equivalence. Notably, this equivalence indicates that, given  $\theta_i^{t+1}$  at iteration  $t$ , the update of  $\text{RM}^+$  can be expressed in the form of Eq. (1). However, utilizing Eq. (1) to derive  $\theta_i^{t+1}$  is impossible.

### 2.3 SMOOTH REGRET MATCHING<sup>+</sup> VARIANTS

Smooth  $\text{RM}^+$  variants ([Farina et al., 2023](#)) are designed to address the instability of Predictive  $\text{RM}^+$  ( $\text{PRM}^+$ ) ([Farina et al., 2021](#)). To do that, they enable the decision  $\theta_i^t$  in  $\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}^d$  instead of  $\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^d$  in other  $\text{RM}^+$  variants to obtain the smoothness of  $F_i(\theta^t)$ , where  $\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}^d = \{\mathbf{y} | \mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^d, \mathbf{y} \geq \mathbf{0}, \|\mathbf{y}\|_1 \geq 1\}$ . We consider two existing smooth  $\text{RM}^+$  variants, Smooth Extra-gradient  $\text{RM}^+$  ( $\text{SEXRm}^+$ ) and Smooth Predictive  $\text{RM}^+$  ( $\text{SPRM}^+$ ).  $\text{SEXRm}^+$  and  $\text{SPRM}^+$  are respectively related to instances of two OMD variants, Optimistic Gradient Descent Ascent (OGDA) ([Wei et al., 2021](#)) and Extra-Gradient (EG) ([Korpelevich, 1976](#)), which updates in  $\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}^d$ , the subset of  $\text{cone}(\mathcal{X}_i)$ . The update rule of  $\text{SEXRm}^+$  is

$$\theta_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}} \in \arg \min_{\theta_i \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}^d} \{(-F_i(\theta^t), \theta_i) + \frac{1}{\eta} D_\psi(\theta_i, \theta_i^t)\}, \mathbf{x}_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{\theta_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}}}{\|\theta_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}}\|_1}, \quad (2)$$

$$\theta_i^{t+1} \in \arg \min_{\theta_i \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}^d} \{(-F_i(\theta^{t+\frac{1}{2}}), \theta_i) + \frac{1}{\eta} D_\psi(\theta_i, \theta_i^t)\}, \mathbf{x}_i^{t+1} = \frac{\theta_i^{t+1}}{\|\theta_i^{t+1}\|_1},$$

and the update rule of  $\text{SPRM}^+$  is

$$\theta_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}} \in \arg \min_{\theta_i \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}^d} \{(-F_i(\theta^{t-\frac{1}{2}}), \theta_i) + \frac{1}{\eta} D_\psi(\theta_i, \theta_i^t)\}, \mathbf{x}_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{\theta_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}}}{\|\theta_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}}\|_1}, \quad (3)$$

$$\theta_i^{t+1} \in \arg \min_{\theta_i \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}^d} \{(-F_i(\theta^{t+\frac{1}{2}}), \theta_i) + \frac{1}{\eta} D_\psi(\theta_i, \theta_i^t)\}, \mathbf{x}_i^{t+1} = \frac{\theta_i^{t+1}}{\|\theta_i^{t+1}\|_1},$$

where  $\eta > 0$  and  $\psi(\cdot)$  is the quadratic regularizer.

## 3 OUR PROOF PARADIGM

We now introduce our proof paradigm that includes: (i) recovering monotonicity or the weak MVI by leveraging the equivalence between  $\text{RM}^+$  and OMD proposed by [Liu et al. \(2021\)](#), and (ii) measuring the distance of  $\text{RM}^+$  variants to NE via the tangent residual to show that this distance is related to the distance between accumulated regrets rather than the strategies in OMD based algorithms. We consider smooth  $\text{RM}^+$  variants, since other  $\text{RM}^+$  variants, e.g., vanilla  $\text{RM}^+$  and  $\text{PRM}^+$ , are experimentally shown to diverge in two-player zero-sum matrix games ([Cai et al., 2023](#)).

**Phase 1.** To recover monotonicity or the weak MVI of smooth  $\text{RM}^+$  variants via the equivalence in [Liu et al. \(2021\)](#), we prove this equivalence holds between smooth  $\text{RM}^+$  variants with OMD. To do that, it is sufficient to show the update rule in Eq. (4) can be written as the form in Eq. (5).

$$\mathbf{x}_i^{t_2} = \frac{\theta_i^{t_2}}{\|\theta_i^{t_2}\|_1}, \theta_i^{t_2} \in \arg \min_{\theta_i \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}^d} \{(-F_i(\theta^{t_1}), \theta_i) + \frac{1}{\eta} D_\psi(\theta_i, \theta_i^{t_0})\}, F_i(\theta^{t_1}) = \langle \frac{\theta_i^{t_1}}{\|\theta_i^{t_1}\|_1}, \ell_i^{t_1} \rangle \mathbf{1} - \ell_i^{t_1}, \quad (4)$$

$$\mathbf{x}_i^{t_2} \in \arg \min_{\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathcal{X}_i} \{(\ell_i^{t_1}, \mathbf{x}_i) + f_i(\mathbf{x}_i) + D_{h_i}(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_i^{t_0})\}, h_i(\mathbf{x}_i) + f_i(\mathbf{x}_i) = \frac{\|\theta_i^{t_2}\|_1}{\eta} \psi(\mathbf{x}_i), h_i(\mathbf{x}_i) = \frac{\|\theta_i^{t_0}\|_1}{\eta} \psi(\mathbf{x}_i), \quad (5)$$

where  $t_0, t_1, t_2$  refer to different iterations,  $\eta > 0$ ,  $\mathbf{x}_i^{t_0} = \theta_i^{t_0} / \|\theta_i^{t_0}\|_1$ ,  $\theta_i^{t_0}$  with  $\theta_i^{t_1} \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}^d$ ,  $\ell_i^{t_1}$  is the loss gradient of player  $i$  induced by  $\mathbf{x}_i^{t_1} = [\mathbf{x}_i^{t_1} = \theta_i^{t_1} / \|\theta_i^{t_1}\|_1 : i \in \mathcal{N}]$ , and  $\psi(\cdot)$  is the quadratic regularizer. As shown in Section 3.1 of [Liu et al. \(2021\)](#), Eq. (5) can be written as

$$\mathbf{x}_i^{t_2} = \frac{[\theta_i^{t_0} + \alpha \mathbf{1} - \eta \ell_i^{t_1}]_+}{\|\theta_i^{t_2}\|_1},$$

{5}------------------------------------------------

where  $\alpha$  is a unique constant to ensure  $\|\mathbf{x}_i^{t_2}\|_1 = 1$  ( $\alpha$  always exists). Then, considering Eq. (4), with the analysis in Section K of Farina et al. (2023), if

$$\|[\theta_i^{t_0} + \eta \langle \frac{\theta_i^{t_1}}{\|\theta_i^{t_1}\|_1}, \ell_i^{t_1} \rangle] \mathbf{1} - \eta \ell_i^{t_1} \mathbf{1} + \mathbf{1}\|_1 \geq 1,$$

$\theta_i^{t_2}$  in Eq. (4) can be obtained via

$$\theta_i^{t_2} = [\theta_i^{t_0} + \eta \langle \frac{\theta_i^{t_1}}{\|\theta_i^{t_1}\|_1}, \ell_i^{t_1} \rangle] \mathbf{1} - \eta \ell_i^{t_1} \mathbf{1} + \alpha \mathbf{1}.$$

Therefore, in this case,  $\alpha = \eta \langle \frac{\theta_i^{t_1}}{\|\theta_i^{t_1}\|_1}, \ell_i^{t_1} \rangle$ . Similarly, if

$$\|[\theta_i^{t_0} + \eta \langle \frac{\theta_i^{t_1}}{\|\theta_i^{t_1}\|_1}, \ell_i^{t_1} \rangle] \mathbf{1} - \eta \ell_i^{t_1} \mathbf{1} + \mathbf{1}\|_1 < 1,$$

$\theta_i^{t_2}$  in Eq. (4) can be obtained via

$$\theta_i^{t_2} = [\theta_i^{t_0} + \eta \langle \frac{\theta_i^{t_1}}{\|\theta_i^{t_1}\|_1}, \ell_i^{t_1} \rangle] \mathbf{1} - \eta \ell_i^{t_1} \mathbf{1} + \beta \mathbf{1} + \mathbf{1},$$

where  $\beta$  exists and is unique to ensure  $\|\theta_i^{t_2}\|_1 = 1$ . Therefore, in this case,  $\alpha = \eta \langle \frac{\theta_i^{t_1}}{\|\theta_i^{t_1}\|_1}, \ell_i^{t_1} \rangle + \beta$ .

As  $\alpha$  is unique, we have that

$$\alpha = \begin{cases} \eta \langle \frac{\theta_i^{t_1}}{\|\theta_i^{t_1}\|_1}, \ell_i^{t_1} \rangle, & \|[\theta_i^{t_0} + \eta \langle \frac{\theta_i^{t_1}}{\|\theta_i^{t_1}\|_1}, \ell_i^{t_1} \rangle] \mathbf{1} - \eta \ell_i^{t_1} \mathbf{1} + \mathbf{1}\|_1 \geq 1, \\ \eta \langle \frac{\theta_i^{t_1}}{\|\theta_i^{t_1}\|_1}, \ell_i^{t_1} \rangle + \beta, & \|[\theta_i^{t_0} + \eta \langle \frac{\theta_i^{t_1}}{\|\theta_i^{t_1}\|_1}, \ell_i^{t_1} \rangle] \mathbf{1} - \eta \ell_i^{t_1} \mathbf{1} + \mathbf{1}\|_1 < 1. \end{cases}$$

These complete the proof. Due to page limitations, a detailed proof is in Appendix A. This equivalence is the inherent property of smooth RM<sup>+</sup> variants and does not involve the game types. It also implies that smooth RM<sup>+</sup> variants can be represented by OMD based algorithms whose feedback is the loss gradient of vanilla games. We recover monotonicity or the weak MVI since the feedback in Eq. (5) is the loss gradient of the vanilla game.

**Phase 2.** As analyzed in Cai & Zheng (2022), if the loss gradient of the vanilla game enjoys monotonicity or the weak MVI, we can use the tangent residual to denote the distance to NE. Formally, from the first-order optimality of the prox-mapping operator in Eq. (5), we have

$$\begin{aligned} & \langle \ell_i^{t_1} + \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_i^{t_2}} f_i(\mathbf{x}_i^{t_2}) + \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_i^{t_2}} D_{h_i}(\mathbf{x}_i^{t_2}, \mathbf{x}_i^{t_0}), \mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{x}_i^{t_2} \rangle \geq 0 \\ \Leftrightarrow & \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}} \langle \ell_i^{t_1} + \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_i^{t_2}} h_i(\mathbf{x}_i^{t_2}) + \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_i^{t_2}} f_i(\mathbf{x}_i^{t_2}) - \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_i^{t_0}} h_i(\mathbf{x}_i^{t_0}), \mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{x}_i^{t_2} \rangle \geq 0 \\ \Leftrightarrow & \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}} \langle \ell_i^{t_1} - \frac{\theta_i^{t_0} - \theta_i^{t_2}}{\eta}, \mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{x}_i^{t_2} \rangle \geq 0 \Leftrightarrow -\ell^{t_1} + \frac{\theta^{t_0} - \theta^{t_2}}{\eta} \in \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}^{t_2}) \\ \Leftrightarrow & r^{tan}(\mathbf{x}^{t_2}) \leq \|\ell^{t_2} - \ell^{t_1} + \frac{\theta^{t_0} - \theta^{t_2}}{\eta}\|_2, \end{aligned} \tag{6}$$

where  $\mathbf{x}_i^{t_1} = \theta_i^{t_1} / \|\theta_i^{t_1}\|_1$ , where the third line comes from  $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_i^{t_2}} h_i(\mathbf{x}_i^{t_2}) + \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_i^{t_2}} f_i(\mathbf{x}_i^{t_2}) = \theta_i^{t_2} / \eta$  and  $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_i^{t_0}} h_i(\mathbf{x}_i^{t_0}) = \theta_i^{t_0} / \eta$ , the last line is from the definition of the tangent residual. Therefore, if we can prove  $\|\ell^{t_2} - \ell^{t_1}\|_2 \rightarrow 0$  and  $\|\theta^{t_2} - \theta^{t_0}\|_2 \rightarrow 0$ , we can get that  $r^{tan}(\mathbf{x}^{t_2}) \rightarrow 0$ , which implies  $\mathbf{x}^{t_2}$  is an NE. In smooth RM<sup>+</sup> variants, we have that  $\|\ell^{t_2} - \ell^{t_1}\|_2 \leq O(\|\theta^{t_2} - \theta^{t_1}\|_2)$  ( $\|\ell^{t_2} - \ell^{t_1}\|_2 \leq O(\|\theta^{t_2} - \theta^{t_1}\|_2)$  does not hold in other RM<sup>+</sup> variants, which is the reason why we consider smooth RM<sup>+</sup> variants). Thus, in smooth RM<sup>+</sup> variants, if we can prove  $\|\theta^{t_2} - \theta^{t_1}\|_2 \rightarrow 0$  and  $\|\theta^{t_2} - \theta^{t_0}\|_2 \rightarrow 0$ , we can get that  $\mathbf{x}^{t_2}$  converges to NE.

## 4 APPLICATION OF OUR PROOF PARADIGM: CONVERGENCE RESULTS OF SEXRM<sup>+</sup> AND SPRM<sup>+</sup>

To show the practical applicability of our paradigm, we use it to prove the last-iterate and best-iterate convergence of two existing smooth RM<sup>+</sup> variants, e.g., SEXRM<sup>+</sup> and SPRM<sup>+</sup>. Note that our convergence results in this section cover all games that existing works about the last-iterate convergence of RM<sup>+</sup> variants investigate.

{6}------------------------------------------------

**Theorem 4.1.** *SEXRm<sup>+</sup> with  $0 < \eta < \frac{1}{DL_u}$  or SPRM<sup>+</sup> with  $0 < \eta < \frac{1}{8DL_u}$  achieves asymptotic last-iterate convergence and  $O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}})$  best-iterate convergence rate in learning an NE of games satisfying monotonicity, where  $D = \max_{i \in \mathcal{N}} |A_i|$  and  $L_u = \sqrt{2P^2 + 4\bar{L}^2}$ . Specifically, if all players follow the update rule of SEXRM<sup>+</sup> or SPRM<sup>+</sup>, then  $r^{\tan}(\mathbf{x}^{t+\frac{1}{2}}) \rightarrow 0$  and  $\min_{\tau \in [t]} r^{\tan}(\mathbf{x}^{\tau+\frac{1}{2}}) \leq O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}})$  as  $t \rightarrow \infty$ .*

To prove Theorem 4.1, we introduce the Theorem 4.2, Theorem 4.3, and Lemma 4.4 (the proof of Theorems 4.2 and 4.3 are in Appendix B and C, respectively).

**Theorem 4.2.** *SEXRm<sup>+</sup> with  $0 < \eta < \frac{1}{DL_u}$  ensures  $\|\theta^{t+\frac{1}{2}} - \theta^t\|_2 \rightarrow 0$  and  $\|\theta^{t+1} - \theta^{t+\frac{1}{2}}\|_2 \rightarrow 0$  as  $t \rightarrow \infty$ , and  $\min_{\tau \in [t]} \left( \|\theta^{\tau+\frac{1}{2}} - \theta^\tau\|_2^2 + \|\theta^{\tau+1} - \theta^{\tau+\frac{1}{2}}\|_2^2 \right) \leq O(\frac{1}{t}), \forall t \geq 1$ .*

**Theorem 4.3.** *SPRM<sup>+</sup> with  $0 < \eta < \frac{1}{8DL_u}$  ensures  $\|\theta^{t+\frac{1}{2}} - \theta^t\|_2 \rightarrow 0$  and  $\|\theta^{t+1} - \theta^{t+\frac{1}{2}}\|_2 \rightarrow 0$  as  $t \rightarrow \infty$ , and  $\min_{\tau \in [t]} \left( \|\theta^{\tau+\frac{1}{2}} - \theta^\tau\|_2^2 + \|\theta^{\tau+1} - \theta^{\tau+\frac{1}{2}}\|_2^2 \right) \leq O(\frac{1}{t}), \forall t \geq 1$ .*

**Lemma 4.4.** *(Proposition 1 in Farina et al. (2023))*  $\forall a, b \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^d, \|a\|_1 \geq 1, \|b\|_1 \geq 1, \|\frac{a}{\|a\|_1} - \frac{b}{\|b\|_1}\|_2 \leq \sqrt{d}\|a - b\|_2$ .

*Proof.* Now, we start to prove the last-iterate and best-iterate convergence of SEXRM<sup>+</sup> and SPRM<sup>+</sup>. Firstly, from the analysis in Section 3 that Eq. (4) can be written as the form in Eq. (5), the update rule of SEXRM<sup>+</sup> can be written as (see details in Appendix F)

$$\begin{aligned} & \mathbf{x}_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}} \in \operatorname{argmin}_{\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathcal{X}_i} \left\{ \langle \ell_i^t, \mathbf{x}_i \rangle + q_i^{t-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{x}_i) + D_{q_i^{0:t-1}}(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_i^t) \right\}, \\ & \mathbf{x}_i^{t+1} \in \operatorname{argmin}_{\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathcal{X}_i} \left\{ \langle \ell_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}}, \mathbf{x}_i \rangle + q_i^t(\mathbf{x}_i) + D_{q_i^{0:t-1}}(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_i^t) \right\}, \\ & q_i^{0:t-1}(\mathbf{x}_i) = \frac{\|\theta_i^t\|_1}{\eta} \psi(\mathbf{x}_i) q_i^{0:t-1}(\mathbf{x}_i) + q_i^{t-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{x}_i) = \frac{\|\theta_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}}\|_1}{\eta} \psi(\mathbf{x}_i) q_i^{0:t-1}(\mathbf{x}_i) + q_i^t(\mathbf{x}_i) = \frac{\|\theta_i^{t+1}\|_1}{\eta} \psi(\mathbf{x}_i). \end{aligned} \quad (7)$$

Similarly, the update rule of SPRM<sup>+</sup> can be written as

$$\begin{aligned} & \mathbf{x}_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}} \in \operatorname{argmin}_{\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathcal{X}_i} \left\{ \langle \ell_i^{t-\frac{1}{2}}, \mathbf{x}_i \rangle + q_i^{t-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{x}_i) + D_{q_i^{0:t-1}}(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_i^t) \right\}, \\ & \mathbf{x}_i^{t+1} \in \operatorname{argmin}_{\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathcal{X}_i} \left\{ \langle \ell_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}}, \mathbf{x}_i \rangle + q_i^t(\mathbf{x}_i) + D_{q_i^{0:t-1}}(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_i^t) \right\}, \\ & q_i^{0:t-1}(\mathbf{x}_i) = \frac{\|\theta_i^t\|_1}{\eta} \psi(\mathbf{x}_i) q_i^{0:t-1}(\mathbf{x}_i) + q_i^{t-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{x}_i) = \frac{\|\theta_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}}\|_1}{\eta} \psi(\mathbf{x}_i) q_i^{0:t-1}(\mathbf{x}_i) + q_i^t(\mathbf{x}_i) = \frac{\|\theta_i^{t+1}\|_1}{\eta} \psi(\mathbf{x}_i). \end{aligned} \quad (8)$$

Monotonicity is recovered since the loss gradient  $\ell$  is a monotonic operator. Now, we prove the tangent residual of the strategy profiles  $\mathbf{x}^t$  converges to 0. From the analysis in Phase 2 of Section 3, according to the second prox-mapping operator in Eq. (7) and Eq. (8),  $\forall \mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{X}$ , we have

$$-\ell^{t+\frac{1}{2}} + \frac{\theta^t - \theta^{t+1}}{\eta} \in \mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathbf{x}^{t+1}).$$

From the definition of the tangent residual, we obtain

$$\begin{aligned} r^{\tan}(\mathbf{x}^{t+1}) & \leq \|\ell^{t+1} - \ell^{t+\frac{1}{2}} + \frac{\theta^t - \theta^{t+1}}{\eta}\|_2 \leq \|\ell^{t+1} - \ell^{t+\frac{1}{2}}\|_2 + \frac{1}{\eta} \|\theta^t - \theta^{t+1}\|_2 \\ & \leq L \|\mathbf{x}^{t+1} - \mathbf{x}^{t+\frac{1}{2}}\|_2 + \frac{1}{\eta} \|\theta^t - \theta^{t+\frac{1}{2}}\|_2 + \frac{1}{\eta} \|\theta^{t+\frac{1}{2}} - \theta^{t+1}\|_2, \end{aligned}$$

where the last inequality is from the smoothness of the smooth games. Then, using Lemma 4.4 with  $\mathbf{a} = \theta^{t+1}$  and  $\mathbf{b} = \theta^{t+\frac{1}{2}}$ , we have

$$r^{\tan}(\mathbf{x}^{t+1}) \leq L\sqrt{D} \|\theta^{t+1} - \theta^{t+\frac{1}{2}}\|_2 + \frac{1}{\eta} \|\theta^t - \theta^{t+\frac{1}{2}}\|_2 + \frac{1}{\eta} \|\theta^{t+\frac{1}{2}} - \theta^{t+1}\|_2.$$

{7}------------------------------------------------

From Theorem 4.2 and 4.3 ( $\|\theta^t - \theta^{t+\frac{1}{2}}\|_2 \rightarrow 0$  and  $\|\theta^{t+\frac{1}{2}} - \theta^{t+1}\|_2 \rightarrow 0$ ), we get  $r^{tan}(\mathbf{x}^t) \rightarrow 0$  as  $t \rightarrow \infty$ . Similarly, we get

$$\begin{aligned}
(r^{tan}(\mathbf{x}^{t+1}))^2 &\leq \|\ell^{t+1} - \ell^{t+\frac{1}{2}} + \frac{\theta^t - \theta^{t+1}}{\eta}\|_2^2 \leq 2\|\ell^{t+1} - \ell^{t+\frac{1}{2}}\|_2^2 + \frac{2}{\eta^2} \|\theta^t - \theta^{t+1}\|_2^2 \\
&\leq 2L^2 \|\mathbf{x}^{t+1} - \mathbf{x}^{t+\frac{1}{2}}\|_2 + \frac{2}{\eta^2} \|\theta^t - \theta^{t+1}\|_2^2 \\
&\leq 2L^2 D^2 \|\theta^{t+1} - \theta^{t+\frac{1}{2}}\|_2^2 + \frac{4}{\eta^2} \|\theta^t - \theta^{t+\frac{1}{2}}\|_2^2 + \frac{4}{\eta^2} \|\theta^{t+\frac{1}{2}} - \theta^{t+1}\|_2^2 \\
&\leq \left(2L^2 D^2 + \frac{4}{\eta^2}\right) \left(\|\theta^{t+1} - \theta^{t+\frac{1}{2}}\|_2^2 + \|\theta^t - \theta^{t+\frac{1}{2}}\|_2^2\right).
\end{aligned}$$

Therefore, from Theorem 4.2 and 4.3 ( $\min_{\tau \in [t]} (\|\theta^{\tau+\frac{1}{2}} - \theta^\tau\|_2^2 + \|\theta^{\tau+1} - \theta^{\tau+\frac{1}{2}}\|_2^2) \leq O(\frac{1}{t})$ ), we get that for  $\tau = \arg \min_{\tau \in [t]} (\|\theta^{\tau+\frac{1}{2}} - \theta^\tau\|_2^2 + \|\theta^{\tau+1} - \theta^{\tau+\frac{1}{2}}\|_2^2)$ ,

$$r^{tan}(\mathbf{x}^{\tau+1}) \leq \sqrt{O\left(\|\theta^{\tau+\frac{1}{2}} - \theta^\tau\|_2^2 + \|\theta^{\tau+1} - \theta^{\tau+\frac{1}{2}}\|_2^2\right)} \leq O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\right).$$

These complete the proof.  $\square$

## 5 OUR ALGORITHM: SOGRM<sup>+</sup>

By using our proof paradigm, we prove that SEXRM<sup>+</sup> and SPRM<sup>+</sup> achieve last-iterate convergence in games satisfying monotonicity. However, OMD/FTRL based algorithms even achieve last-iterate convergence in games satisfying the weak MVI, covering games satisfying monotonicity.

Inspired by our paradigm, we propose a new smooth RM<sup>+</sup> variant called Smooth Optimistic Gradient Regret Matching<sup>+</sup> (SOGRM<sup>+</sup>). We prove that SOGRM<sup>+</sup> achieves last-iterate convergence in games satisfying monotonicity via our paradigm. SOGRM<sup>+</sup> is connected to an OG instance which updates at  $\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}^d$ , the subset of  $\text{cone}(\mathcal{X}_i)$ . Note that the proof of SOGRM<sup>+</sup> needs additional techniques, such as transforming variables using the definition of the inner product to employ the weak MVI and tangent residual (details are in Eq. (11), (12), (35), (36), and (38)), rather than directly transforming variables using equalities as in OG. Formally, the update rule of SOGRM<sup>+</sup> at iteration  $t$  is

$$\theta_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}} \in \arg \min_{\theta_i \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}^d} \langle -F_i(\theta^{t-\frac{1}{2}}), \theta_i \rangle + \frac{1}{\eta} D_\psi(\theta_i, \theta_i^t), \quad \mathbf{x}_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{\theta_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}}}{\|\theta_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}}\|_1}, \quad (9)$$

$$\theta_i^{t+1} = \theta_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}} - \eta F_i(\theta^{t-\frac{1}{2}}) + \eta F_i(\theta^{t+\frac{1}{2}}).$$

**Theorem 5.1.** In smooth games satisfying the weak MVI with  $\rho > -\frac{1}{12\sqrt{3D L_u}}$ , there always exists  $0 < \eta < \frac{1}{2D L_u}$  that ensures SOGRM<sup>+</sup> achieves asymptotic last-iterate convergence and  $O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}})$  best-iterate convergence rate in learning an NE of these games, where  $D = \max_{i \in \mathcal{N}} |A_i|$  and  $L_u = \sqrt{2P^2 + 4L^2}$ . Specifically, if all players follow the update rule of SOGRM<sup>+</sup>, then  $r^{tan}(\mathbf{x}^{t+\frac{1}{2}}) \rightarrow 0$  and  $\min_{\tau \in [t]} r^{tan}(\mathbf{x}^{\tau+\frac{1}{2}}) \leq O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}})$  as  $t \rightarrow \infty$ .

From the first-order optimality of the first prox-mapping operator in Eq. (10),  $\forall \mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{X}$ , we have

$$\langle \ell_i^{t-\frac{1}{2}} + \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}}} q_i^{t-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{x}_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}}) + \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}}} D_{q_i^{0:t-1}}(\mathbf{x}_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}}, \mathbf{x}_i^t), \mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{x}_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}} \rangle \geq 0$$

$$\Leftrightarrow \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}} \langle \ell_i^{t-\frac{1}{2}} + \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}}} q_i^{0:t-1}(\mathbf{x}^{t+\frac{1}{2}}) + \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}}} q_i^{t-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{x}^{t+\frac{1}{2}}) - \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}}} q_i^{0:t-1}(\mathbf{x}^t), \mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{x}_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}} \rangle \geq 0.$$

Then, we have

$$\sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}} \langle \ell_i^{t-\frac{1}{2}} - \frac{\theta^t - \theta^{t+\frac{1}{2}}}{\eta}, \mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{x}_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}} \rangle \geq 0 \text{ and } \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}} \langle \ell_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}} - \frac{\theta^t - \theta^{t+1}}{\eta}, \mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{x}_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}} \rangle \geq 0, \quad (11)$$

where the left-hand side is from  $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}}} q_i^{0:t-1}(\mathbf{x}^{t+\frac{1}{2}}) + \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}}} q_i^{t-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{x}^{t+\frac{1}{2}}) = \ell_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}}/\eta$  with  $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}}} q_i^{0:t-1}(\mathbf{x}_i^t) = \theta_i^t/\eta$ , and the right-hand side is from Lemma 5.3. According to Eq. (11) and the definition of the normal cone, we have

$$-\ell^{t+\frac{1}{2}} + \frac{\theta^t - \theta^{t+1}}{\eta} \in \mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathbf{x}^{t+\frac{1}{2}}), \quad (12)$$

where  $\ell^{t+\frac{1}{2}} = [\ell_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}} : i \in \mathcal{N}]$ . From the definition of the tangent residual, we obtain

$$r^{tan}(\mathbf{x}^{t+\frac{1}{2}}) \leq \|\ell^{t+\frac{1}{2}} - \ell^{t+\frac{1}{2}} + \frac{\theta^t - \theta^{t+1}}{\eta}\|_2 \leq \frac{1}{\eta} \|\theta^t - \theta^{t+1}\|_2. \quad (13)$$

Combining Eq. (13), Theorem 5.2 ( $\|\theta^t - \theta^{t+1}\|_2 \rightarrow 0$ ), we get  $r^{tan}(\mathbf{x}^{t+\frac{1}{2}}) \rightarrow 0$  as  $t \rightarrow \infty$ . Similarly, from Theorem 5.2 ( $\min_{\tau \in [t]} \|\theta^{\tau+1} - \theta^\tau\|_2^2 \leq O(\frac{1}{t})$ ), we get that for  $\tau = \arg \min_{\tau \in [t]} \|\theta^\tau - \theta^{\tau+1}\|_2^2$ ,

$$r^{tan}(\mathbf{x}^{t+\frac{1}{2}}) \leq \frac{1}{\eta} \|\theta^\tau - \theta^{\tau+1}\|_2 \leq O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}).$$

□

## 6 EXPERIMENTS

We conduct experiments on randomly generated two-player zero-sum matrix games with sizes  $[10, 20, 50]$ , where learning an NE is defined as  $\min_{\mathbf{x}_0 \in \mathcal{X}_0} \max_{\mathbf{x}_1 \in \mathcal{X}_1} \mathbf{x}_0^\top A \mathbf{x}_1$ . Each element of the payoff matrix  $A$  is uniformly sampled from  $[-1, 1]$ . For each game size, we generate 20 instances and report the average duality gaps with variances. The duality gap,  $r^{dg}(\mathbf{x})$ , is used to evaluate the

{9}------------------------------------------------

distance to NE, defined as  $r^{dg}(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}} \max_{\mathbf{x}_i'} \langle \ell_i^g, \mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{x}_i' \rangle$ . As analyzed in Cai et al. (2022b), the duality gap involves a lower bound of the tangent residual,  $r^{dg}(\mathbf{x}) \leq C_0 r^{tan}(\mathbf{x})$ , where  $C_0$  is a game-dependent constant. Thus, if the tangent residual converges to 0, the duality gap also converges to 0. Due to the difficulty in precisely calculating the tangent residual, we do not use it as the metric. We compare smooth RM<sup>+</sup> variants (SEXR<sup>+</sup>, SPRM<sup>+</sup>, and SOGRM<sup>+</sup>) with existing RM<sup>+</sup> variants (ExRM<sup>+</sup>, PRM<sup>+</sup>, and RM<sup>+</sup>), as well as traditional last-iterate convergence OMD based algorithms—Optimistic Gradient Descent (OGDA) (Wei et al., 2021), Extra-Gradient (EG) (Korpelevich, 1976), and Optimistic Gradient (OG) (Hsieh et al., 2019; Cai & Zheng, 2022)<sup>2</sup>. For initialization, we set  $\theta_i$  to  $1/|\mathcal{X}_i|$  and 0 for smooth and other RM<sup>+</sup> variants, respectively. For OGDA, EG, and OG, the initial strategy is the uniform strategy. **For all tested algorithm, we use simultaneous updates since to the best of our knowledge, the theoretical analysis of existing work on last-iterate convergence is based on simultaneous updates.** All experiments are performed on a machine with an i9-13900K CPU and 128 GB of memory.

The convergence results are shown in Figure 1, smooth RM<sup>+</sup> variants generally achieve at least similar performance compared to other algorithms. Specifically, OGDA, EG, and OG underperform relative to their smooth RM<sup>+</sup> counterparts (SPRM<sup>+</sup>, SEXRM<sup>+</sup>, and SOGRM<sup>+</sup>, respectively) and are more sensitive to parameters. For larger  $\eta$  values ( $\eta = 1$  and  $\eta = 10$ ), OGDA, EG, and OG consistently diverge, while smooth RM<sup>+</sup> variants maintain last-iterate convergence. Additionally, we observe that SPRM<sup>+</sup> and SEXRM<sup>+</sup> consistently achieve comparable performance to their corresponding non-smooth RM<sup>+</sup> variants, namely PRM<sup>+</sup> and ExRM<sup>+</sup>, respectively. Under optimal parameter settings, SPRM<sup>+</sup> and SEXRM<sup>+</sup> significantly outperform PRM<sup>+</sup> and ExRM<sup>+</sup>, respectively. More importantly, we find that our algorithm, SOGRM<sup>+</sup>, exhibits the fastest convergence rate and shows the least sensitivity to parameter changes. Moreover, for the similar performance of SOGRM<sup>+</sup> under  $\eta = 1$  and  $\eta = 10$ , we hypothesize that when  $\eta \geq 1$ , the term  $\eta F_i(\theta^{t-\frac{1}{2}})$  becomes extremely larger than  $\theta_i^t$ , either positively or negatively. Consequently, the accumulated regret  $\theta_i^{t+\frac{1}{2}}$  heavily depends on the feedback  $\eta F_i(\theta^{t-\frac{1}{2}})$  from iterations  $\tau$  ( $\tau < t$ ) rather than  $\theta_i^t$ . Since the strategies are derived by normalizing the accumulated regret  $\theta^{t+\frac{1}{2}}$ , the resulting strategies exhibit only minor differences. Therefore, we can observe the similar performance of SOGRM<sup>+</sup> under  $\eta = 1$  and  $\eta = 10$ .

## 7 CONCLUSIONS

We study the last-iterate convergence of RM<sup>+</sup> variants in learning an NE for games that satisfy monotonicity or only the weak MVI. We introduce a novel proof paradigm to analyze the last-iterate convergence of RM<sup>+</sup> variants. Using this paradigm, we show that two existing variants, SEXRM<sup>+</sup> and SPRM<sup>+</sup>, exhibit last-iterate convergence in games with monotonicity. Building on this, we propose a new variant, SOGRM<sup>+</sup>, which achieves last-iterate convergence in games satisfying the weak MVI. To our knowledge, this is the first last-iterate convergence results for RM<sup>+</sup> variants in such games. Our paradigm stands out for its simplicity and innovation, and we believe this approach can extend to proving last-iterate convergence for additional RM<sup>+</sup> variants in broader game classes.
