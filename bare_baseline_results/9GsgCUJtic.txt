## Summary
This paper provides a comprehensive theoretical analysis of when GFlowNets correctly learn their target distributions. It makes three interrelated contributions: (1) TV bounds showing that balance violations near the root state have disproportionate impact, motivating a weighted detailed balance (WDB) loss; (2) representation-theoretic results on GNN-based GFlowNets (universality for trees, impossibility results for 1-WL GNNs on general graphs, and Look-Ahead GFlowNets to mitigate expressivity limits); (3) the FCS (Flow Consistency in Subgraphs) metric, a tractable and theoretically sound proxy for TV distance that is 3 orders of magnitude cheaper to compute than direct TV, along with a critical case study showing popular evaluation metrics can be misleading.

## Strengths
- **Theoretically rigorous with practical impact**: The paper delivers 5 theorems, 2 corollaries, and 1 proposition across three distinct angles—error propagation, expressivity limits, and evaluation. Each theoretical finding is paired with an empirical illustration and/or a methodological proposal, bridging theory and practice well.
- **Critical evaluation finding (Section 5.2)**: The demonstration that terminally-unrestricted LED/FL GFlowNets achieve 100% Shen accuracy while being provably incorrect is a genuinely important cautionary result for the community. Combined with FCS (Spearman 0.99 for sets, 0.90 for sequences), this contribution alone is highly valuable.
- **GNN expressivity transfer to GFlowNets**: Theorems 2 and 3 cleanly translate 1-WL GNN representational limits into GFlowNet failure modes. The construction is concrete, the result non-trivial, and the fix (LA-GFlowNets, Theorem 4) is elegant and provably more expressive.
- **FCS is well-motivated**: Definition 1, Theorem 5, Corollaries 1–2 collectively give FCS strong theoretical grounding (faithfulness to TV, PAC bound, role of β), not just ad hoc appeal.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **LA-GFlowNets only evaluated on toy settings**: The empirical validation in Section 4 uses synthetic 8-node graphs with exactly 2 terminal states. There is no evaluation on real-world graph generation tasks (e.g., molecule discovery) where GNNs are actually deployed, making it difficult to assess practical significance. This is a notable gap given how common GNN-based GFlowNets are.
- **WDB weighting is heuristically derived without comparative ablation**: The authors acknowledge the weighting γ(s,s')=1/|D_{s'}| is heuristic and that other choices may be better. The empirical benefits are inconsistent—no clear improvement for sequences and hypergrid—and there is no principled analysis of when WDB should help or ablation over γ choices. This weakens the contribution from "informed design" to "motivated but unresolved proposal."

### Minor
- **FCS computation in practice is under-explored**: The paper handles autoregressive/small SGs well, but the MC estimator for p_T(x) in general DAG-structured SGs may have high variance. How many backward trajectories are needed in practice? How does FCS scale with depth/branching factor? This is mentioned only briefly.
- **Theorem 1 involves intractable quantities**: The bounds in Eq. (4) depend on F(s₀) and Σ_{x ∈ D_{s*}} π(x), which are generally uncomputable. The gap between the general theorem and the practically usable Remark 1 / WDB loss is wide, and the connection is somewhat hand-wavy ("inspired by").
- **β-selection for FCS is not thoroughly studied**: Setting β = batch size is recommended but not justified beyond convenience; the interplay between β, TV approximation quality (Corollary 1), and PAC guarantees is only formally stated, not empirically profiled.

### Tiny
- Remark 1 is labeled as a remark rather than a lemma/theorem despite being a standalone formal result that Theorem 1 generalizes.
- The discussion of LED/FL-GFlowNet concerns may be perceived as somewhat adversarial toward prior works without fully ruling out whether constrained versions were also tested in those papers.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Empirical evaluation of LA-GFlowNets on a realistic graph generation task (e.g., small molecule generation) to establish practical relevance beyond 8-node toy examples.
- Ablation over γ variants for WDB, including principled alternatives like those inspired by SNR-based weighting from diffusion model literature cited in the paper.
- Profiling of FCS sensitivity to β and number of subsets m, particularly under the MC estimator regime.

## Novel Insights
The paper's most significant novel insight is that standard evaluation protocols for GFlowNets are fundamentally unreliable proxies for distributional correctness—specifically, terminally-unrestricted variants of LED and FL-GFlowNets can score perfectly on Shen accuracy and find many modes while being provably unable to sample from the correct distribution. This reframes a silent assumption in much of the GFlowNet evaluation literature and motivates FCS as a replacement. A secondary novel insight is the clean transplantation of 1-WL GNN representation limits into GFlowNet expressivity: the proof that regular-graph parent states force uniform action probabilities regardless of child-state rewards is a tight, self-contained impossibility result that clarifies an overlooked failure mode in graph-domain GFlowNet applications.

## Suggestions
1. Add at least one experiment with LA-GFlowNets on a realistic benchmark (e.g., molecule generation, phylogenetics) to validate beyond the n=8 toy case.
2. Provide an ablation of γ choices for WDB (e.g., depth-based, flow-magnitude-based, and constant) to give practitioners clearer guidance.
3. Include an empirical analysis of FCS robustness to β and number of sampled subsets m in the supplementary material.
4. Clarify (or probe) whether the original LED/FL experiments used terminally-restricted variants, as the paper currently says "significant reasons to believe" but doesn't confirm definitively—this is a critical claim for the field.
