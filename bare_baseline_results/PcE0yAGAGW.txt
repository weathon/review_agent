## Summary

FSL-MIC is a few-shot learning framework for EEG motor imagery classification consisting of three modules: a 1D CNN-based feature embedding module, a dot-product self-attention mechanism, and a relation module adapted from Relation Networks. The method is evaluated on BCI Competition IV 2a, 2b, and a small self-collected dataset (7 subjects) with K ∈ {1, 5, 10, 20} shot settings. The goal is to classify unseen subjects' EEG signals using only a handful of labeled examples, reducing the need for extensive BCI calibration.

---

## Strengths
- **Applied relevance**: The low-data BCI calibration problem is genuinely important, and framing cross-subject EEG classification as a few-shot task is a natural and motivated choice.
- **Multi-dataset evaluation**: Testing on two standard benchmarks (BCI 2a, 2b) plus an additional self-collected dataset adds breadth.
- **Ablation via shot count**: Reporting K = {1, 5, 10, 20} at least illustrates the expected trend of improving performance with more support examples.

---

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
- **The proposed FSL method consistently underperforms its own supervised baseline (CNN-attention-All).** On BCI 2a, the FSL (20-shot) achieves 72.6% while CNN-attention-All achieves 89.1%. On BCI 2b, 73.2% vs. 86.28%. This is presented without critical discussion — the paper's own results do not support the claim that FSL provides a "promising alternative." Crucially, 20 labeled support samples per class (40 total) is the same scale as the CNN-attention-Few baseline (~62.8–71.3%), yet FSL adds complexity without consistent benefit. There is no scenario where the proposed method wins.

### Major
- **No comparison to state-of-the-art FSL methods for EEG.** An et al. (2023) is mentioned but their actual numbers are never shown in Table 1. The paper claims "our model outperforms it" without a direct head-to-head number in the results table.
- **Paper is incomplete.** The last line reads: *"Rest of paper (reference and Appendix) is removed."* There are no references, no appendix, and large blocks of raw line numbers (lines 150–203, 288–341) are formatting artifacts left in the main text. This makes the work unreadable and unverifiable.
- **Overclaiming in abstract and conclusion.** The abstract states the framework "significantly outperforms traditional methods," but the FSL model's peak accuracy is 10–16 points below the CNN-all baseline on all datasets. The conclusion similarly states the FSL "significantly reduces training time" without any timing results.

### Minor
- **Self-attention formulation is non-standard.** The scaling factor 1/√d_k from Vaswani et al. is missing (S = QKᵀ without scaling). Applying tanh after the weighted sum M = WV is unexplained and non-standard; no ablation justifies it.
- **Oddly specific hyperparameters without justification.** A batch size of 164 and a decay rate of 0.033% per iteration are reported with no ablation or explanation.
- **Own dataset is very small (7 subjects)**, limiting conclusions about generalizability. Results on this dataset are also the weakest of the three.
- **Duplicate figure captions.** Figure 1 and Figure 2 captions appear verbatim 2–3 times each in the text.

### Tiny
- Section 4.4 is titled "Conclusion" but is embedded inside the "Experimental Results" section.
- Minor citation format issues (e.g., "Vaswani (2017)" instead of "Vaswani et al. (2017)").

---

## Nice-to-Haves
- A fair comparison: FSL vs. supervised CNN trained on the *same* K-shot budget (not all data), to isolate the benefit of the meta-learning component.
- Ablation of the attention module vs. plain RelationNet.
- Wall-clock training/inference timing results to back up efficiency claims.
- Interpretability analysis (mentioned in text but deferred to "next paper").

---

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions. The method is a straightforward application of Relation Networks + dot-product self-attention to 1D EEG signals, building explicitly on Lashgari et al. (2021) and Sung et al. (2018). There is no theoretical contribution, no new architectural insight, and the empirical finding — that more shots helps — is expected and not surprising.

---

## Suggestions
1. Fix the comparison: add a row for supervised CNN trained with K×2 samples (same as the FSL support set) to cleanly isolate meta-learning benefit.
2. Include timing experiments if computational efficiency is a claimed benefit.
3. Complete the manuscript (add references and appendix before submission).
4. Revise claims to accurately reflect results (FSL lags behind full supervised learning; acknowledge this as the expected low-data regime trade-off rather than a victory).
5. Add the scaling factor to the self-attention formula and justify or remove the tanh nonlinearity.

---

## Final Score

The paper applies existing components (RelationNet + self-attention + 1D CNN) to EEG motor imagery classification without producing results that outperform the supervised baseline. The manuscript is incomplete (no references/appendix), contains formatting artifacts, overclaims its results, and provides no meaningful novel insight. It falls well below the standard expected at ICLR.
