## Summary
Megalodon is a family of transformer-based generative models (19M and 40M parameters) for 3D molecule generation that augments DiT (Diffusion Transformer) blocks with EGNN-based equivariant layers and trains via a "modular co-design" objective—using independent noise schedules for continuous (coordinates) and discrete (atom/bond types, charges) modalities. The paper shows state-of-the-art results on standard 2D stability metrics and newly proposed xTB energy benchmarks for evaluating physical realism, and demonstrates superior generalization to molecules outside the training size distribution.

## Strengths

- **Empirical results are strong and consistent.** Megalodon outperforms prior models on 2D topological metrics (Mol. Stability 0.977, Validity 0.927 at 500 steps; 0.990 / 0.948 at 100 steps with FM), 3D distributional metrics (Bond Angle, Dihedral), and the new xTB energy benchmarks—all with the same training data and hyperparameters, isolating architecture/objective as the variable.

- **New energy-based benchmarks are a genuine contribution.** The xTB relaxation error (ΔE_relax) metrics ground evaluation in physically meaningful quantum mechanical quantities, addressing the gap in existing benchmarks that only evaluate 2D topological validity. Achieving ~3 kcal/mol median relaxation energy (close to the 2.5 kcal/mol thermodynamic threshold) is a meaningful milestone.

- **Generalization to large molecules is a compelling finding.** Figure 3 is striking: EQGAT-diff collapses to near-0 validity at 120 atoms while Megalodon-large stays above 40%, representing a 49x improvement. The analysis attributing this to transformers' superiority for discrete sequential data is plausible and well-motivated.

- **Dual objective training (diffusion + flow matching) reveals an interesting tradeoff.** The observation that diffusion excels at 3D structure energy while flow matching achieves better 2D topology with 25x fewer steps is an informative finding for the community.

- **The conditional structure generation result is impressive.** Prompting an unconditional model trained with the co-design objective to perform conditional generation without any fine-tuning—and achieving competitive performance with Torsional Diffusion—demonstrates the value of the joint discrete/continuous training.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Insufficient ablation of key contributions.** The paper identifies two key novelties: (1) the DiT-based architecture and (2) the independent time variable co-design objective. However, no ablation isolates the contribution of the co-design objective. The only architecture ablation is "EGNN + cross product" (Table 1), which collapses catastrophically (validity 0.223) and doesn't cleanly attribute gains to specific design choices. We don't know how much the co-design objective contributes independently of the architecture.

- **Conditional generation comparison is of limited scientific value.** Megalodon is compared against GeoDiff and Torsional Diffusion in Table 2 for conditional structure generation—but both are purpose-built conformer generation models, not unconditional 3DMG models. Megalodon outperforms GeoDiff and is competitive with Torsional Diffusion while doing a strictly harder task (full de novo generation). This is compelling but the comparison doesn't isolate architectural vs. task-formulation advantages. Comparing against SemlaFlow in this conditional setting would be more informative.

- **The flow matching energy results raise an unexplored issue.** The paper notes FM models have ~10x higher median energy than diffusion (Table 3: SemlaFlow 32.96 vs. Megalodon 3.17 kcal/mol) and attributes it to input structure normalization, but does not attempt to fix it. This leaves FM essentially unusable for the physical quality benchmarks introduced in the paper, making the paper's own energy benchmarks appear to favor the diffusion variant.

### Minor

- **The "cross product" claim.** The paper states "we emphasize that this cross-product term is critical for model performance" but provides no ablation of removing only the cross-product term from the full Megalodon model—only shows a fully reduced EGNN baseline. The "EGNN + cross product" baseline seems misconfigured (0.223 validity), making it hard to interpret.

- **Limited treatment of the co-design objective.** The description of the independent time variable training is introduced somewhat informally (Section 3). A clearer mathematical formulation and its relationship to prior multi-modal diffusion frameworks (e.g., MiDi's weighted cosine schedules) would strengthen the presentation.

- **Scalability claims are limited.** The paper presents only two model sizes (19M and 40M params) and notes these are "not that large compared to recent biological models." No scaling law analysis is presented. The claim that "scaling Megalodon produces up to 49x more valid molecules" conflates architectural improvements with scale.

### Tiny

- The paper's title "Applications of Modular Co-Design for De Novo 3D Molecule Generation" is somewhat generic and doesn't differentiate the work. The co-design framing is reasonable but "applications" implies a more systematic study than is presented.
- The appendix was removed from the provided text, making it impossible to verify several claims (model ablations in Table 5, Appendix E energy justification).

## Nice-to-Haves

- A direct ablation comparing: (a) Megalodon without co-design objective, (b) Megalodon with co-design but no transformer (pure EGNN), and (c) full Megalodon would cleanly attribute each contribution.
- Lipschitz analysis or theoretical grounding for why independent time variables improve the learning of structure–topology relationships.
- Attempting to fix the FM energy issue (e.g., by not normalizing coordinates to unit variance) would make the FM variant competitive on all benchmarks.

## Novel Insights

The most genuinely novel insight is the empirical observation that standard 3D molecule generation objectives (like EQGAT-diff) lead to a pathological training behavior: for the first half of the diffusion trajectory, models generate no bonds at all, causing the 2D graph to be uninformative and decoupled from structure prediction. The co-design solution—using independent time variables for discrete and continuous modalities—is simple but motivated by this diagnosis and produces measurable improvements in both unconditional stability and conditional structure generation. Additionally, the finding that a transformer-based backbone dramatically improves generalization to out-of-distribution (large) molecule sizes—even when >99.9% of training molecules are smaller—suggests that inductive biases suited for discrete sequential data (transformers) matter substantially for extrapolation in molecular generation, beyond what EGNN architectures can offer.

## Suggestions

- Provide a formal ablation table isolating: (i) architecture (DiT+EGNN vs. EGNN-only), (ii) co-design objective (independent vs. shared time variable), and (iii) self-conditioning.
- Include SemlaFlow in the conditional structure generation benchmark (Table 2).
- Investigate whether removing input coordinate normalization in the flow matching variant recovers energy quality.
- Consider reporting molecule-level energy statistics (e.g., 90th percentile ΔE_relax) rather than only mean/median, as high-energy tail molecules are particularly problematic in drug discovery.

## Final Score

The paper presents solid empirical improvements with physically meaningful new benchmarks, and the large-molecule generalization finding is genuinely striking. However, the architectural contribution is incremental (DiT + EGNN), the co-design objective modification is simple and underablated, and the FM-vs-diffusion comparison reveals an unresolved issue. This is a good-but-not-great paper.
