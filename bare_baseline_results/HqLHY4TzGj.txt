## Summary
This paper proposes Union-over-Intersections (UoI), a plug-and-play modification to the standard object detection pipeline. Instead of regressing proposals to the full ground truth bounding box, each proposal is trained to regress only to its intersection with the ground truth; then at inference time, rather than a winner-takes-all NMS, the union over all predicted intersection regions in a group forms the final detection. The method is demonstrated as a drop-in replacement across two-stage (Faster/Mask/Cascade R-CNN), single-stage (YOLOv3), and transformer-based (Deformable DETR) detectors on COCO and VOC.

## Strengths
- **Clear, principled motivation**: The decomposition of a hard extrapolation task (regress proposal → full GT) into an easier interpolation task (regress proposal → visible intersection) is well-reasoned and intuitive. The oracle experiment in Figure 3(d) cleanly shows that UoI's advantage widens as classification improves.
- **Broad applicability and consistent gains**: UoI is validated across five architectures spanning three detection paradigms, and shows consistent (if modest) improvements on every metric. Compatible with all NMS variants (Table 4) and IoU-based losses (Table 2), making it truly plug-and-play.
- **Thorough ablations**: Studies disentangle classification vs. localization contributions (Table 5), second regression stage necessity (Table 8), grouping sensitivity (Figure 3c), proposal quality robustness (Figure 3a), and training loss dynamics (Figure 3b). The LRP metric analysis adds depth.
- **Low overhead**: Adds minimal inference cost (< 2% FPS drop) and training time, making adoption practical.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Outdated baselines**: All detectors tested (YOLOv3, Faster R-CNN, Deformable DETR early-variant) are significantly behind the state of the art. There is no evaluation on strong modern models such as DINO, Co-DETR, YOLOv8/v9, or DETA. It is unclear whether UoI provides meaningful gains when proposals and features are already highly accurate — the oracle study hints the benefit may shrink as individual proposal quality improves.
- **Modest absolute gains**: Improvements of 0.5–1.1 mAP are easily within the range of training stochasticity for these models. No statistical significance testing or multiple-seed experiments are reported, making it hard to confirm reliability of the smaller deltas (e.g., YOLOv3: 33.7 → 34.5).
- **Crowded-scene limitation is severe**: The paper itself shows that closely overlapping same-class instances can be incorrectly merged (Figure 4, three parrots → one box). This is a critical failure mode for real-world applications (e.g., pedestrian detection, animal herds) and is only mentioned briefly without quantification or mitigation experiments (e.g., on OCHuman or CrowdHuman).

### Minor
- **"Minimal modification" claim overstated for DETR**: The paper describes splitting ground truth into quadrants and assigning queries to specific parts for Deformable DETR — this is a non-trivial architectural decision that contradicts the "minimal changes" narrative.
- **No direct head-to-head with box voting** at the core level (Table 7 compares TTA combinations, not plain inference). A cleaner ablation of "UoI post-processing vs. box voting post-processing, same regression targets" would clarify the individual contribution of each component.
- **Small-object cases unresolved**: AP_S gains are the smallest across all models (~0.3-0.7), and the paper acknowledges that with a single proposal, UoI degrades to baseline. No strategy is proposed for small objects.
- **Lambda sensitivity not ablated**: The refinement loss weight λ=0.5 is set without ablation.

### Tiny
- Only COCO and VOC datasets; LVIS or Objects365 would increase generality.
- Qualitative examples are limited; more failure cases would be informative.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Evaluate on modern strong detectors (DINO, Co-DETR) to assess whether UoI remains useful at higher mAP regimes.
- Quantitative crowded-scene analysis (e.g., CrowdHuman) to bound the failure mode.
- Statistical significance via multiple training seeds.
- Ablation of the number of quadrants for the Deformable DETR adaptation and sensitivity analysis of λ.

## Novel Insights
The key novel insight is treating bounding box regression as a *partial/intersection prediction problem* rather than a full-object alignment problem, and then recovering full-object coverage through set union — effectively turning a hard extrapolation problem into a simpler interpolation problem with collaborative merging. This framing is clean and generalizable. The oracle experiment showing that UoI's advantage *increases* with better classification (Figure 3d) is a genuinely useful forward-looking result: it positions UoI as a formulation that benefits from future progress rather than being made obsolete by it. The empirical verification that lower regression loss during training (Figure 3b) accompanies intersection-based targets supports the "easier task" hypothesis in a satisfying way.

## Suggestions
1. Add experiments on at least one modern strong baseline (e.g., DINO or YOLOv8) to show the approach scales to current practice.
2. Report results on CrowdHuman or OCHuman to quantify the crowded-scene limitation and explore remedies (e.g., adaptive group size based on predicted object density).
3. Report mean ± std over ≥3 seeds for at least one architecture to establish statistical significance of gains.
4. Provide a cleaner ablation separating: (a) intersection regression alone, (b) union post-processing alone, and (c) both together — the current tables conflate these for some architectures.
5. Quantify exactly what "dividing GT into quadrants" means for DETR vs. a true minimal modification, and consider a more principled DETR adaptation that preserves the "minimal change" property.
