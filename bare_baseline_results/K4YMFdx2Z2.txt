## Summary
This paper proposes **Unsolvable Problem Detection (UPD)**, a benchmark challenge for Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) that tests whether models can appropriately withhold answers when faced with three types of unsolvable visual questions: Absent Answer Detection (AAD — correct option removed from choices), Incompatible Answer Set Detection (IASD — answer choices are semantically irrelevant to the question/image), and Incompatible Visual Question Detection (IVQD — image and question are mismatched). The authors build the MM-UPD Bench on top of MMBench, introduce a "Dual accuracy" metric, evaluate ~20 LMMs, and explore CoT, self-reflection, and instruction tuning as baseline approaches.

---

## Strengths

- **Well-motivated problem**: The paper identifies a practically important and underexplored failure mode of LMMs — their tendency to blindly commit to an answer even when none is valid. This is directly relevant to real-world trustworthiness.
- **Systematic benchmark construction**: Built atop MMBench with a 3-step pipeline (filtering image-agnostic questions, constructing UPD variants, manual quality checks), enabling direct comparison with an established baseline and fine-grained ability-wise analysis across 18 ability dimensions.
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: ~20 state-of-the-art models (open- and closed-source) are evaluated across three prompt settings (Base, Option, Instruction), providing thorough empirical coverage.
- **Dual accuracy metric**: Measuring correctness on *both* a standard question and its UPD counterpart simultaneously is a natural and principled way to capture the tension between answering and abstaining.
- **Striking empirical finding**: The near-zero UPD performance for many high-performing open-source models (e.g., LLaVA-OV-7B at <6% in Base) and the very low correlation between MMBench and MM-UPD scores (max r=38.7%) is a concrete, surprising finding useful to the community.

---

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Incremental novelty over acknowledged prior work**: The paper explicitly cites Guo et al. (2024), Akter et al. (2024), and Qian et al. (2024) on related unsolvable VQA benchmarks. The claimed advances — expanded taxonomy, systematic evaluation protocol, unified metric — are meaningful but individually modest. The conceptual gap from prior work is not as large as presented. IVQD in particular is essentially the image-question mismatch task already studied by prior work.

- **Questionable ecological validity of benchmark construction**: All three subtasks are created by *mechanical perturbation* of MMBench. MM-AAD removes the correct option; MM-IASD shuffles answer sets to create entirely incompatible choices; MM-IVQD shuffles image-question pairs. These operations do not capture how unsolvable questions arise naturally in real interactions. In particular, IASD (completely unrelated answer choices) is an extremely artificial scenario that rarely arises in practice, making it arguably the weakest of the three subtasks.

- **Dual accuracy metric conflates two independent capabilities**: Because Dual accuracy requires *both* the standard and UPD question to be answered correctly, a model with any weakness on standard MCQ will mechanically score lower on Dual accuracy independent of its refusal capability. This confounds the measurement of UPD-specific ability with general VQA ability.

- **Instruction tuning shows non-trivial degradation on standard tasks**: Table 4 shows that after UPD instruction tuning, LLaVA-NeXT-13B drops from 76.7% → 68.9% (Original Standard), a ~8-point degradation. The paper acknowledges the trade-off but does not investigate it deeply or propose any mitigation strategy. This undermines the practical utility of the instruction tuning approach.

### Minor

- The instruction tuning experiments are limited to only LLaVA-NeXT-13B/34B. The generalizability of the recipe is unclear.
- No human baseline performance is reported, making it difficult to gauge how hard the benchmark truly is and whether the tasks have any inherent ambiguity.
- The distinction between AAD and IASD can be subtle in practice: AAD (related but incomplete options) vs. IASD (irrelevant options) exist on a continuum, yet they are treated as discrete.
- The paper positions itself around "trustworthiness" but does not connect to broader LMM safety/alignment frameworks beyond the narrow UPD task.

### Tiny

- Repeated image description alt-text makes the paper harder to read (artifact of the submission format).
- The paper says "proposing innovative approaches is left as future work" while most explored solutions are direct applications of existing methods — this should ideally not be a whole contribution for a venue like ICLR.

---

## Nice-to-Haves

- Include a human baseline to calibrate difficulty.
- Add open-ended QA variants even as preliminary experiments.
- Investigate the standard-performance trade-off in instruction tuning more carefully (e.g., with data mixing strategies).
- Motivate IASD with a realistic real-world scenario where such options could arise.

---

## Novel Insights

The paper's most genuinely novel and actionable observation is the extremely low correlation between standard benchmark performance (MMBench) and UPD performance across a wide range of models, combined with the finding that this gap is qualitatively different between open- and closed-source models due to RLHF-style safety training. The error analysis approach — providing the answer directly in the prompt to disentangle vision-side vs. language-side failures — is a clean and reusable methodological contribution. Together, these insights suggest that the LMM community's emphasis on accuracy benchmarks has left a systematic capability gap around *knowing when not to answer*, and that this gap requires dedicated training, not just better visual understanding.

---

## Suggestions

1. Add a human performance baseline to calibrate the actual difficulty and ambiguity of MM-UPD.
2. Provide a more rigorous justification for the ecological validity of IASD — either motivate with real-world deployment data or explicitly scope the claim.
3. Decouple standard accuracy from Dual accuracy in the main evaluation to make UPD-specific capability measurable independently.
4. Extend instruction tuning to at least one more model family (e.g., InternVL2) to test generalizability of the recipe.
5. Report correlation (Pearson/Spearman) more systematically for all three UPD subtasks and all three prompt settings.

---

## Final Score

The paper is a solid benchmark contribution addressing a practically important LMM reliability gap, with comprehensive experimentation and clear findings. It is weakened by incremental novelty, artificial benchmark construction, a conflated metric, and limited methodological contribution. It sits in the acceptable-but-not-exceptional zone for a top venue.
