## Summary
TiC-LM introduces a large-scale benchmark for continual pretraining of language models, centered around TiC-CommonCrawl (TiC-CC): 2.9T tokens from 114 monthly Common Crawl dumps spanning 2013–2024, which is over 100× larger than prior continual LM benchmarks. The paper additionally provides time-stratified evaluations from Wikipedia (TiC-Wiki), StackExchange (TiC-StackE), and code documentation (TiC-CodeDocs), and evaluates standard continual learning baselines — finding that data replay best reduces forgetting and that domain-specific temporal dynamics strongly influence which methods work best.

## Strengths
- **Scale and coverage**: TiC-CC is genuinely the largest CL benchmark for LMs (2.9T tokens, 114 timesteps, >10 years), enabling more realistic study of long-horizon continual learning than any prior work.
- **Multi-domain evaluation breadth**: Including CC, Wikipedia, StackExchange, and code docs captures domains with meaningfully different temporal dynamics, and the paper exploits this diversity to reveal interesting empirical contrasts (e.g., NumPy vs PyTorch, StackOverflow vs Stack-Math).
- **Domain-temporal analysis**: The insight that peak performance on TiC-WIKI-Diff months often comes *years after* those months are seen (because factual changes propagate through CC with a lag) is a genuinely interesting and counterintuitive observation.
- **Compute efficiency analysis**: The comparison against a "series of Oracles" (with realistic retrain cadence) and showing 62% compute savings is practically meaningful and goes beyond a simple oracle comparison.
- **Methodological rigor**: The regret-based formulation, hyper-parameter tuning on only the first 10 timesteps, and explicit handling of train/eval misalignment are all carefully thought out.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Only 3B models evaluated**: All continual learning experiments are conducted at 3B parameters. The key question — whether findings (especially the superiority of replay, the role of AR schedules, and the EWC advantage on TiC-WIKI-Diff) hold at 7B, 13B, or larger scales — is entirely unanswered. The paper motivates itself partly by analyzing 7B DCLM/Gemma models' staleness, but conducts no continual training at that scale.
- **No novel CL method proposed**: The paper is entirely a benchmark + baselines paper. All evaluated methods are direct adaptations from TiC-CLIP (Garg et al., 2024). The conclusion that replay is best is consistent with classical CL results and does not challenge or extend our understanding of *why* these methods behave as they do.
- **Arbitrary front-loading design**: Allocating half of the total 220B token budget to a single initial month (May-2013) is a strong design choice that biases the initialization heavily toward the oldest data. The authors note this is "at fault" for the gap to the Oracle on static evals (Section 6.2), but don't ablate over different initialization budgets.

### Minor
- **LwF appears entirely ineffective**: LwF results in Table 2 are identical to Cyclic Cosine across all TiC-CC metrics, suggesting either a trivially tuned KL weight or a near-degenerate implementation. This is not discussed critically.
- **Static evaluations show negligible differences**: CORE accuracy varies by only ~1.5% across all methods. It is unclear whether the perplexity differences on dynamic evals would actually translate to any measurable real-world model quality difference.
- **Within-month deduplication mismatch**: Fuzzy dedup only within each month diverges from production pipelines (which deduplicate globally). This choice is justified but means the training data distribution is not representative of how state-of-the-art LLMs are trained, potentially limiting applicability of findings.
- **Total token budget is small relative to modern LLMs**: 220B–440B tokens at 3B parameters is well below current practice (e.g., Llama 3 uses 15T+ tokens for 8B). Whether continual learning dynamics differ fundamentally at more common training regimes is unknown.

### Tiny
- The paper uses "TiC-CC-WiKi" and "TiC-Wiki" for two distinct evaluations; the naming is confusing.
- The description of `Cyclic Rept`/`Cyclic Rsqt` inconsistently uses both spellings.
- Standard deviations in Table 2 are all listed as exactly 0.000, which seems like a rounding artifact that could mislead readers about statistical significance.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Ablations over initialization token budget (e.g., 10%, 25%, 50% front-loaded) to understand how the initialization choice affects continual learning.
- At least one experiment at 7B scale to ensure findings transfer.
- Analysis of memory/storage cost of replay in realistic settings (current treatment is "we don't adjust token counts for this").
- A simple adaptive replay ratio that allocates based on measured domain drift rate rather than fixed α.

## Novel Insights
The most genuinely novel insight from the paper is the domain-temporal asymmetry finding: different knowledge domains evolve at fundamentally different rates, and this rate has a direct, systematic effect on which continual learning method is optimal. Slowly-evolving domains (Stack-Math, NumPy) benefit strongly from data replay of older dumps because relevant content peaked years ago; fast-evolving domains (StackOverflow, PyTorch) are *harmed* by replay because early data is not merely stale but misleading. A second notable observation is that factual changes visible in Wikipedia diffsets propagate into Common Crawl with a multi-year delay, so a model can still learn month-specific factual updates from later CC dumps without requiring online training — a finding with implications for how to evaluate and design continual learning systems.

## Suggestions
- Run at least a subset of key experiments at 7B scale to validate the transferability of findings.
- Include an ablation on the initialization budget (front-loading ratio) to understand the contribution of the biased initialization to the static eval gap.
- More carefully analyze and discuss LwF's near-zero effect; consider varying its regularization strength more aggressively.
- Report standard deviations with more decimal places or re-estimate using a different approach, since showing 0.000 for all runs obscures statistical significance.
- Release the TiC-CC dataset alongside the paper to maximize community uptake as an actual benchmark.
