## Summary
CAT Pruning is a training-free acceleration strategy for text-to-image diffusion models (SD3, Pixart-Σ) that selectively updates only a subset of tokens at each denoising step. The method combines three components: (1) *relative noise magnitude* (change w.r.t. a reference step t₀) to identify high-importance tokens, (2) a staleness-aware frequency monitor (EWMA-based) to ensure distributional balance, and (3) K-means spatial clustering with positional encodings to preserve spatial coherence. At α=0.3 (30% tokens updated), the method achieves 1.82×–2.15× end-to-end speedup with comparable CLIP scores on MS-COCO and PartiPrompts.

---

## Strengths
- **Practical speedups demonstrated on modern architectures**: 1.82× (SD3, 28 steps) to 2.15× (SD3, 50 steps) on real hardware with real-world 1024×1024 generation, not just theoretical MACs reductions.
- **Ablation study with clear motivation for each component**: Figures 4–6 walk through noise-only → noise+balance → noise+balance+cluster, empirically justifying each design choice.
- **Empirical correlation evidence for core assumption**: Figure 3 shows Pearson correlations of 0.82–0.89 between consecutive-step relative noise norms, providing a reasonable empirical basis for Proposition 1.
- **Two model families and two datasets**: Evaluation on SD3 and Pixart-Σ across PartiPrompts and MS-COCO adds some breadth.

---

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Evaluation metric limited to CLIP Score only**: CLIP Score is known to be a coarse semantic-level metric and does not capture fine-grained image fidelity. Standard image generation benchmarks require FID, IS, or at minimum SSIM/PSNR against the full-model outputs. The claim that performance is "preserved" cannot be substantiated with CLIP Score alone (e.g., Pixart-Σ COCO drops from 31.36 → 30.02 at 28 steps; this may mask visible degradation).
- **Single, unfairly adapted baseline**: AT-EDM was designed for U-Net-based SDXL, not DiT-based SD3/Pixart. The authors retrofit it with their own caching mechanism, making the comparison methodology questionable. AT-EDM's adapted CLIP scores crater to 24, 14, 17, 11 — results so poor they suggest the adaptation itself is broken, not that AT-EDM is intrinsically bad. No other relevant token-pruning or caching baselines (FORA, TGATE, DeepCache adapted to DiT) are compared.
- **No sensitivity analysis on key hyperparameters**: The choice of K=20 clusters, t₀ (step 8 out of 28), EWMA decay factor α, and the staleness-exploitation trade-off ratio are never ablated. It is unknown whether the method is robust or highly sensitive to these choices.

### Minor
- **Formatting issues and typos indicate low polish**: Line number artifacts appear in the middle of the PDF (e.g., lines 158–211, 237–290, 312–365), and the abstract contains "Our experiments demonstrate reveal a 50%-60% reduction," an obvious copyediting error. This raises concerns about paper quality.
- **Graph pooling layer underspecified**: Section 3.4 introduces a "1 light-weighted Graph Pooling Layer" but does not specify what graph is being constructed, the pooling operator, or any complexity analysis. It reads as a missing ablation component.
- **Computational overhead of KMeans not quantified**: The paper notes clustering is run only at step t₀+1, but the cost of KMeans over ~(h×w) tokens with d-dimensional features, even once, is not reported relative to total inference time.
- **No comparison to step-reduction baselines** (DPM-Solver, consistency models), which achieve similar or greater speedups with a different design axis and would contextualize the contribution.

### Tiny
- Proposition 1 is only proven at the simplest single-step case (t = t₀ + 1) in the appendix; generalization to arbitrary t is not addressed.
- Figure captions are duplicated verbatim in the paper text (alt-text artifacts) making the paper harder to read.

---

## Nice-to-Haves
- Per-token SSIM/PSNR maps between CAT outputs and full-model outputs would help visually ground quantitative claims.
- A combined experiment with step-reduction solvers (DPM-Solver++) to show orthogonality and joint speedup potential would significantly strengthen the paper.
- Sensitivity curves for K (number of clusters) and t₀ would make the method more practical for practitioners.

---

## Novel Insights
The paper's most original contribution is the use of a *reference-relative noise signal* (n_t − n_{t₀}) rather than absolute noise magnitude or feature similarity for scoring token importance, combined with an EWMA staleness monitor borrowed from the asynchronous SGD literature. The analogy between token staleness in diffusion and worker staleness in distributed optimization is creative, though not deeply developed. The spatial clustering approach (K-means with learned positional encodings on the relative noise features) is sensible but incremental. Overall, the novelty is at the component combination level rather than fundamental insight.

---

## Suggestions
1. Add FID (or sFID) computed against the full-model baseline outputs on a standard subset (e.g., 5K COCO captions) to substantiate quality claims.
2. Compare against a properly implemented FORA or adapted TGATE on the same DiT architectures, not only a broken AT-EDM port.
3. Provide an ablation table over t₀ ∈ {4,6,8,10} and K ∈ {10,20,40} to demonstrate robustness.
4. Quantify the overhead of the single KMeans call and graph pooling as a fraction of total inference time.
5. Correct all formatting artifacts and proof-read the abstract before re-submission.
