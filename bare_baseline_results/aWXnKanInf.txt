## Summary
TopoLM introduces a transformer-based language model augmented with an explicit 2D spatial arrangement of units and a spatial smoothness loss (derived from the vision-domain TDANN framework). By jointly optimizing next-token prediction with this spatial correlation loss, the model develops clustered, interpretable spatio-functional organization. Crucially, the emerging clusters match three key patterns documented in human fMRI: a distributed core language system, noun- and verb-selective clusters, and the concrete-vs.-abstract dissociation in lexical-category selectivity — all without directly fitting to any brain data.

---

## Strengths
- **Principled extension from vision to language.** The paper cleanly adapts the TDANN spatial smoothness loss (Margalit et al., 2024) to the transformer architecture, providing a direct test of whether this unifying principle generalizes across cortical systems. The architectural modifications (2D grid mapping, per-layer random permutations, neighborhood approximation) are well-motivated and clearly described.
- **Multiple, independent neuroscience validations.** Three separate experimental comparisons are made against established fMRI datasets (Fedorenko et al. 2010/2011 for core language system; Hauptman et al. 2024 for verb/noun clustering with quantitative Moran's I; Moseley & Pulvermüller 2014 for concrete-vs.-abstract dissociation). The replication of the *absence* of abstract-word clustering is a particularly strong validation.
- **Performance is largely maintained.** The model achieves competitive Brain-Score (−2 pts), a slight *improvement* on GLUE (+3 pts), and only modest degradation on BLiMP (−5 pts), addressing concerns that topographic constraints would cripple task utility.
- **Stimulus-computable and open.** The model is fully stimulus-computable, enabling future hypothesis generation for neuroimaging experiments. Code and data links are provided.

---

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Limited conceptual novelty — this is primarily a domain transplant.** The spatial loss (Eq. 1–2), the fMRI readout sampling simulation, and the overall validation pipeline are near-verbatim transfers from Margalit et al. (2024) and Lee et al. (2020) from vision to language. The paper's central question is whether the TDANN principle *generalizes*; the answer is yes, but the methodological contribution is modest. The paper reads more as a replication-in-a-new-domain rather than a fundamental advance.
- **Confounded and underpowered comparison with Topoformer-BERT.** The authors use Topoformer-BERT as a baseline but acknowledge it differs in corpus (BookCorpus vs. FineWeb-Edu, orders of magnitude smaller), architecture (single-head masked LM vs. multi-head causal LM), and topographic mechanism. Drawing conclusions about the superiority of the spatial smoothness approach over local-connectivity from this comparison is not warranted; a proper ablation would train both methods on matched data/architecture.
- **BLiMP drop of 5 points is non-trivial and under-discussed.** Grammaticality judgment tasks are one of the cleanest probes of core syntactic knowledge in LMs. A 5-point degradation (0.76 → 0.71) deserves more analysis: which sub-categories suffer? Is the trade-off consistent across α values? The paper attributes it to spatial loss without deeper ablation.
- **No spatial correspondence to actual brain anatomy.** The model's 28×28 grid is arbitrary with random permutations per layer, so clusters do not spatially correspond to any anatomical layout. Moran's I quantifies internal spatial smoothness, but there is no spatial registration between model maps and brain maps. The claim of "matching functional organization" is purely at the level of clustering statistics, not true topographic correspondence.

### Minor
- The sensitivity to fMRI simulation parameters (FWHM=2.0 mm, unit spacing=1.0 mm) is not explored — these choices can strongly affect how clustered the sampled maps appear.
- Only a single spatial loss weight (α=2.5) is used throughout; the ablation referenced in footnote 4 is described but not quantitatively shown in the main text.
- The model is GPT-2-small scale (~784 hidden dim, 10B tokens). Whether the spatio-functional organization persists at larger scales is unknown and potentially important for neuroscience claims.
- The evolutionary clustering algorithm for defining language-selective clusters is not a standard neuroscience tool; sensitivity to the threshold and minimum cluster size parameters is not reported.

### Tiny
- Figure captions are repeated (artifact of PDF extraction?) making the paper harder to read.
- The "non-topographic baseline" model has a slightly better validation loss (2.966 vs 3.075), yet this difference is brushed aside; it may partly explain Brain-Score differences.

---

## Nice-to-Haves
- An ablation showing the effect of α on BLiMP/GLUE/Brain-Score trade-offs would help readers understand where the Pareto front lies.
- Direct visualization of layer-by-layer cluster evolution during training would be a compelling addition.
- Testing the model's predictions on novel experimental stimuli (model-guided experiment, as suggested in the Discussion) would substantially strengthen the claim of generativity.

---

## Novel Insights
The most genuinely novel finding is that a single spatial smoothness objective, with no additional inductive bias, causes a transformer to develop both the *presence* of concrete-word lexical-category clustering and the *absence* of abstract-word clustering — a nuanced double dissociation found in human fMRI. This provides meaningful mechanistic evidence that the TDANN principle extends beyond the visual hierarchy into linguistic cortex, and that cortical spatio-functional organization across multiple levels (global language network, fine-grained lexical categories) may share a single organizing objective. The finding that Topoformer-BERT's local-connectivity constraint produces geometric smoothness (high Moran's I before thresholding) but not semantically meaningful clustering further argues for the importance of the *learning objective* over architectural constraints alone.

---

## Suggestions
- Add a properly controlled comparison: train a Topoformer-style (local-connectivity) model and a TopoLM-style (smoothness-loss) model on identical data, architecture, and objective, to isolate the effect of the topographic mechanism.
- Report BLiMP subcategory breakdowns for TopoLM vs. non-topo to understand what grammatical knowledge is degraded.
- Provide a sensitivity analysis of fMRI readout simulation parameters and α values.
- Include an analysis of whether spatial smoothness increases monotonically through training (learning curves for Moran's I) to support the mechanistic story.
