## Summary

This paper addresses non-smooth convex zeroth-order optimization and multi-armed bandits under heavy-tailed symmetric noise. The key advance over prior work (which required bounded κ-th moment with κ > 1) is extending heavy-tailed handling to any κ > 0, including noise distributions with unbounded expectation. The authors achieve this via a novel "median gradient estimate" that combines coordinate-wise median of gradient-difference samples with clipping, showing convergence rates matching the bounded-variance case (Õ(d²ε⁻²) iterations). They also achieve the optimal Õ(√dT) MAB regret under symmetric heavy-tailed rewards.

---

## Strengths

- **Extension to κ > 0 (including κ ≤ 1):** Prior zeroth-order methods under heavy tails (e.g., [19; 20]) required κ ∈ (1, 2], degenerating as κ → 1. This paper removes that restriction, with convergence guarantees for any κ > 0, including distributions with unbounded mean — a genuinely meaningful theoretical advance.

- **Non-degrading rates matching bounded-variance case:** The second-term iteration complexity in Table 1 is Õ((dM₂′/ε)²/b) regardless of κ, compared to the competitor's Õ((√dM₂′/ε)^(κ/(κ−1))/b) which blows up as κ → 1. This is a concrete and quantifiable improvement.

- **Optimal MAB regret:** Theorem 3 achieves Õ(√dT) matching the lower bound Ω(√dT) for stochastic MAB with bounded variance — and extends it to symmetric heavy tails with any κ > 0, including high-probability bounds.

- **Novel oracle assumption:** Assumption 3 explicitly conditions on the noise distribution for fixed (x, y), allowing symmetry and specific tail behavior (density bound in Eq. 4) to be exploited. The authors carefully discuss why this differs from prior assumptions and how it reduces to standard ones for κ ∈ (1, 2].

- **Unbiasedness of median estimator:** Lemma 1 establishes that the component-wise median gradient estimate is *unbiased* and has bounded second moment with m > 2/κ samples. This is a non-trivial technical result required for the whole framework.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Inconsistent experimental story in MAB (Figure 1):** The paper claims "HTINF and APE do not have convergence in probability, while our Clipped-INF-med-SMD does." However, Figure 1 (right) shows HTINF reaching probability ≈ 0.9 of best-arm selection and converging clearly, while the proposed method only reaches ≈ 0.6. The left graph (regret) shows HTINF also achieves lower final regret. The authors' narrative does not align with the empirical evidence shown. This is a major credibility issue.

- **ZO experiments do not highlight proposed method's advantage (Figure 3):** In all four subplots, the SGD-based variants (ZO-clipped-med-SGD, ZO-clipped-SGD) substantially outperform the SSTM-based variants (including the proposed ZO-clipped-med-SSTM) in terms of samples consumed. The paper's novel contribution is the "med-SSTM" method, but it is strictly worse than a simpler baseline in all shown settings. No explanation is given for why the theoretically accelerated SSTM underperforms SGD in practice.

- **Symmetry assumption limits scope:** While the authors discuss this limitation honestly, symmetry of the noise distribution is a strong condition not satisfied by many practical applications (e.g., financial losses, LLM gradient noise is often asymmetric). The experiments in §D.2.1 (not shown in main paper) are mentioned as showing competitive performance on asymmetric noise, but this evidence is relegated to the appendix.

### Minor

- **Known κ required:** The algorithm requires setting m = ⌈2/κ⌉ + 1, which requires knowing κ. For κ → 0, m → ∞, making the batch size prohibitive. The authors acknowledge this but only gesture at "future work" for adaptive schemes, while the existing literature (e.g., [18]) has addressed adaptive variants.

- **Cryptocurrency experiment (§5.2) is weak:** The real-world MAB experiment compares only against "hold ETH" and Efficient Frontier strategies, not against other bandit algorithms (HTINF, APE). There is no heavy-tailed noise analysis, no ablation, and no comparison that demonstrates the claimed advantages of the proposed method.

- **Density-based oracle assumption:** Assumption 3 requires a specific density bound of the form p(u|x,y) ≤ γᵏ|B|ᵏ/(|B|^{1+κ} + |u|^{1+κ}). While flexible, this requires the noise to have an absolutely continuous distribution with known density, which may not hold for discrete noise or noise from complex generative processes.

### Tiny

- Minor typographical/formatting issues: page numbers are scattered throughout the text (artifact of parsing), and a few redundant line-number sequences appear in the body.
- Remark 5 referenced in text but the visible portion doesn't contain it.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A lower bound specific to the symmetric heavy-tailed ZO setting would strengthen the claim of optimality, since current optimality comparisons are inherited from the bounded-variance setting.
- Comparison with median-of-means estimators (Catoni, etc.) and discussion of why coordinate-wise median is preferred would be informative.
- A practical guide for choosing m without knowing κ (cross-validation over small grid) would improve usability.

---

## Novel Insights

The core technical insight — that coordinate-wise median of gradient-difference estimates is *unbiased* for the smoothed gradient even under unbounded-moment heavy-tailed symmetric noise — is genuinely novel in the zeroth-order setting. Prior work exploited symmetry only in the first-order case [34]; extending this to zeroth-order differences (which combine two function evaluations with correlated noise) requires careful treatment of the joint distribution. The resulting "bounded second-moment from any κ > 0 symmetric noise" is a striking property that bridges the infinite-moment regime to standard stochastic optimization guarantees. The application of this idea to importance-weighted MAB estimators (where heavy tails are amplified by small mixing probabilities) is also an interesting and non-trivial extension.

---

## Suggestions

- Revise the narrative around Figure 1 to honestly reflect that HTINF appears to outperform the proposed method on this particular experiment, and provide a discussion of why (e.g., HTINF's UCB structure exploits the 2-arm case better).
- Include an experiment explicitly designed to demonstrate the proposed ZO method's advantage: choose κ ≤ 1, sufficient sample budget, and show that ZO-clipped-med-SSTM converges while ZO-clipped-SSTM fails (or diverges).
- Add the asymmetric noise robustness experiments from Appendix D.2.1 to the main paper body, given that symmetry is the primary limitation.
- Provide a discussion of the Oracle Calls complexity (not just iterations), since each iteration requires (2m+1)·b oracle calls and m = O(1/κ) can be large.

---

## Final Score

The paper addresses a genuine open problem and provides solid theory, but the experimental presentation is misleading (Figure 1 narrative contradicts the figure) and the ZO experiments do not demonstrate clear practical advantages for the proposed method. The assumptions are well-motivated but restrictive. Overall a borderline paper with meaningful theoretical contributions but under-supported empirical claims.
