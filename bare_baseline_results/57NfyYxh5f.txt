## Summary

This paper investigates how the training of a model's classification head (probe) affects the quality of post-hoc attribution methods for pre-trained visual models. The key finding is that the probe's training objective (BCE vs. CE loss) and complexity (linear vs. MLP) — involving less than 10% of total model parameters — has a much larger impact on explanation quality than the choice of pre-training paradigm itself. The paper provides a theoretical justification via the softmax shift-invariance property and recommends using BCE loss and B-cos MLP probes to produce more localized, stable, and compact model explanations.

---

## Strengths

- **Elegant theoretical grounding**: The softmax shift-invariance argument (Equations 2–3) clearly explains why CE-trained probes produce poorly calibrated attributions — infinitely many functionally equivalent probes exist, each producing wildly different saliency maps. Figure 4 makes this vivid and convincing.
- **Comprehensive experimental scope**: The study covers 5+ pre-training paradigms (supervised, MoCov2, BYOL, DINO, CLIP), two architectures (ResNet-50 and ViT-B/16), ~10 attribution methods (LRP, IntGrad, B-cos, IxG, GradCAM, ScoreCAM, LIME, Rollout, CGWI, CausalX), multiple datasets (ImageNet, VOC, COCO), and 4 evaluation families (GridPG, EPG, pixel deletion, compactness/complexity). This breadth is exceptional.
- **Large, consistent quantitative gains**: BCE over CE yields improvements of 18–40 p.p. in GridPG scores across diverse pre-training paradigms and attribution methods. This isn't a marginal effect.
- **Practical accessibility**: The "fix" (use BCE instead of CE for probe training) is a drop-in change that requires no architectural modifications, making the recommendations highly actionable.
- **First demonstration of B-cos compatibility with SSL**: Showing that inherently-interpretable B-cos models retain both accuracy and interpretability under self-supervised pre-training is a useful contribution for the XAI community.

---

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **The "surprising" framing is oversold.** BCE eliminating shift-invariance is well-understood (Srinivas & Fleuret, 2021 is even cited), and BCE producing more class-discriminative outputs is a direct consequence of its per-class sigmoid formulation. The insight is valuable, but presenting this as a deeply surprising finding overstates novelty. The community already knows BCE produces better-calibrated outputs — the extension to attribution quality is incremental rather than revelatory.

- **Localization ≠ faithfulness.** The evaluation almost entirely relies on localization metrics (GridPG, EPG, pixel deletion). These measure whether attributions point to the "right region," but not whether they faithfully reflect what the model actually computes. BCE probes may produce more localized maps simply because their class logits are better discriminated, not because the attributions more truthfully explain the network's computation. No sanity checks (e.g., model parameter randomization à la Adebayo et al., 2018) are performed, leaving open the question of whether improvements in localization represent improved faithfulness or just improved class-specificity of the probe's geometry.

- **Inconsistent results unexplained.** IxG and GradCAM show consistent BCE improvements only for B-cos models, not conventional ones. This exception is noted but not meaningfully explained. For a paper claiming broad generality of its findings, these carve-outs weaken the universality claim and suggest the phenomenon is more complex than presented.

### Minor

- The study is restricted to the frozen-backbone + probe regime. It is unclear whether the findings transfer to full fine-tuning settings, which are more commonly used in practice for high-stakes applications.
- No comparison to alternative fixes for the shift-invariance problem (e.g., weight normalization, L2 regularization on probe weights, bias removal). BCE is proposed as the solution without ablating simpler alternatives.
- The interplay between backbone quality (representation richness) and probe type is not teased apart — whether BCE helps more for weaker or stronger backbones is not systematically analyzed.
- Some key result tables are relegated to appendices, making the full picture difficult to assess from the main paper.

### Tiny

- "CGWI" is referred to as "CGW1" inconsistently across the paper.
- Figure captions sometimes duplicate the surrounding text verbatim.

---

## Nice-to-Haves

- Ablation comparing BCE to other regularization strategies addressing shift invariance (e.g., weight-normalized probes, centering logits).
- Faithfulness evaluation using model randomization or input-perturbation tests to verify that localization gains reflect genuine improvements in explanation quality.
- Extension of findings to full fine-tuning / partial fine-tuning regimes.
- Analysis in non-natural-image domains (e.g., medical imaging) where localization standards may differ.

---

## Novel Insights

The clearest novel contribution is the empirical demonstration that a single low-capacity classification head trained with a different loss function can cause dramatic divergence in saliency map quality — even when the backbone is completely frozen and identical. The shift-invariance argument is a clean, principled bridge between optimization theory and the behavior of attribution methods, and the magnitude of improvements (18–40 p.p. in GridPG) across an unusually broad range of backbones and explanation methods lends the finding practical weight. The implication — that post-hoc attribution papers benchmarking on CE-trained models may be underestimating explanation quality — is a genuinely useful observation for the XAI benchmarking community, even if the individual ingredients (BCE superiority, shift-invariance) are not individually new.

---

## Suggestions

1. Add at least one faithfulness-based evaluation (e.g., the Adebayo et al. randomization sanity check or a deletion/insertion benchmark with random baselines) to establish that BCE improvements are not merely cosmetic localization artifacts.
2. Provide an analysis of *why* IxG and GradCAM fail to improve for conventional models — even a brief theoretical or empirical investigation would strengthen the paper's claim to generality.
3. Include a table or figure that explicitly compares BCE to at least one alternative shift-invariance fix (e.g., softmax-free probes or L2-regularized CE) to motivate BCE as the preferred solution.
4. Clarify the scope: title the findings as pertaining to "frozen backbone + probe" settings rather than making broad claims about "post-hoc attribution methods" in general.

---

## Final Score

The paper is technically sound, well-motivated by a clear theoretical argument, and supported by an unusually broad experimental sweep. Its weaknesses are the incremental nature of individual components, reliance on localization metrics that conflate faithfulness with class-discriminability, and unexplained inconsistencies for certain attribution methods. It is a solid empirical contribution of practical value to practitioners using attribution methods with pre-trained models, but below the bar for a genuinely surprising or theoretically deep result.
