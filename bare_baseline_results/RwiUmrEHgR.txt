## Summary
The paper proposes a Cost-Sensitive Loss (CSL) function for long-tailed classification that dynamically adjusts per-class weights based on: (1) "semantic scale" — a measure of how well each class's features are learned; (2) per-class prediction entropy, used as a proxy for learning difficulty; and (3) a "reinforcement learning" term that provides an additive bonus to the loss depending on whether validation performance improved in the previous epoch. The CSL term is added to standard cross-entropy loss and claims SOTA results on CIFAR-10-LT, CIFAR-100-LT, ImageNet-LT, and Tiny ImageNet.

---

## Strengths
- **Practical modularity**: The CSL term can be appended to any existing loss function (CE, Focal, etc.) with minimal code changes, lowering adoption friction.
- **Interpretable motivation**: Using prediction entropy to distinguish Easy-to-Learn (ETL) from Difficult-to-Learn (DTL) classes is conceptually intuitive, and the class-91 (bicycle) case study (Figures 1–2) provides a concrete illustration.
- **Multi-dataset evaluation**: The paper evaluates on CIFAR-10-LT, CIFAR-100-LT, ImageNet-LT, and Tiny ImageNet across multiple imbalance ratios, which is standard practice in the field.

---

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
- **Contradictory formulas**: The formula for $\gamma_i$ is stated two different ways. In Algorithm 1 (line 19), it reads $\gamma_i \leftarrow \frac{S_i}{(1+\epsilon-\alpha+\max(S)\cdot H_i)}$, while the prose (Section 3) gives $\gamma_i = \frac{S_i}{(1+\epsilon)(H_i\cdot\max(S_i))}$. These are algebraically distinct expressions and neither hyperparameter $\alpha$ is introduced anywhere. It is impossible to reproduce the method as presented.
- **Suspect CIFAR-100 results**: The proposed method achieves 49.13% and 52.01% at $p\!=\!200$ and $p\!=\!100$, respectively, while the next-best baseline is 39.6% — a ~12 percentage-point gap with no explanation. This magnitude of improvement from a simple loss modification on a standard ResNet-32 is extraordinary and appears inconsistent with the literature, raising concerns about experimental validity.

### Major
- **Non-differentiable loss numerator**: $N_{pred,i}$ is a count of validation-time predictions (an integer, computed outside the training loop), while the denominator $\sum_k(z_k - e_i)^2$ depends on the current mini-batch. The gradient cannot meaningfully pass through $N_{pred,i}$. The authors claim the denominator makes the loss "differentiable," but it does not resolve the non-differentiability of the numerator, and the gradient signal it provides is questionable.
- **Epoch-level statistics mixed into mini-batch loss**: $N_{pred,i}$ is described as the validation count of class $i$ predictions per epoch, yet Algorithm 1 applies the CSL term inside the inner mini-batch loop (line 21). Using a fixed epoch-level quantity as part of the per-sample gradient update conflates batch-level and epoch-level computations.
- **The "reinforcement learning" claim is cosmetic**: The "reinforcement term" is a scalar constant $k$ chosen based on whether the epoch-level accuracy improved or declined — essentially a binary scalar. There is no policy, no state-action formalism, no discounting, and no RL algorithm. Calling this RL is misleading.
- **Missing recent baselines**: For ICLR 2025, comparisons only go up to ~2021 (IB loss, LDAM-DRW+SSP). Methods like PaCo (ICCV 2021), BCL (CVPR 2022), GCL (CVPR 2022), SADE (ICLR 2023), and many others are absent. Without these comparisons, claimed SOTA status is unsubstantiated.
- **No ablation study**: There is no experiment isolating the contribution of the entropy term, the $\gamma_i$ re-weighting, and the reinforcement term individually. It is impossible to know which component drives performance.
- **No statistical reporting**: All results are single runs without standard deviations. On CIFAR-10 at $p=100$, the gain over LDAM-DRW+SSP (78.00% vs 77.83%) is within typical variance for these benchmarks.
- **Abstract copy-pasted into Introduction**: Multiple consecutive sentences in the introduction are verbatim reproductions of the abstract, indicating poor writing discipline.

### Minor
- The ImageNet-LT improvement over Weighted Softmax is 49.3% vs 49.1% — a 0.2% gain presented as "outperforming state-of-the-art." This is not a meaningful result.
- The "semantic values" concept is attributed to Ma et al. (2023) without adequate explanation; whether it refers to feature norms or something else is unclear until late in the paper.
- Table 4 (Tiny ImageNet) shows only 0.52% gain over Focal loss (39.47% vs 38.95%), which does not justify the complexity overhead.
- The related work section appears *after* the experimental results (Section 6 follows Section 5), which is structurally unusual and disrupts the narrative.

### Tiny
- Footnote 1 defines imbalance ratio but it is already used before in the abstract and introduction without definition.
- The code link (`[iclr](#)`) is a broken placeholder.

---

## Nice-to-Haves
- A theoretical analysis (e.g., convergence guarantees or regret bounds) would strengthen the RL framing.
- Per-class accuracy breakdowns on CIFAR-100 would help verify the claimed improvements on tail classes.
- Runtime and memory overhead comparisons against baselines are missing.

---

## Novel Insights
The integration of prediction entropy as a proxy for per-class learning difficulty into a re-weighting scheme is a reasonable idea. However, the specific combination of entropy-based $\gamma_i$ and epoch-level prediction counts is undermined by the contradictory formula definitions and the non-differentiability concern. No genuinely novel insight emerges that is both clearly novel and technically sound beyond the paper's stated contributions.

---

## Suggestions
1. Reconcile and unify the two contradictory formulas for $\gamma_i$ with a single clean derivation, and explain where $\alpha$ comes from in Algorithm 1.
2. Provide a rigorous ablation: CE only → CE + entropy-$\gamma$ → CE + entropy-$\gamma$ + $N_{pred}$ → full CSL.
3. Add modern baselines (PaCo, BCL, GCL, SADE) and run experiments with 3–5 seeds reporting mean ± std.
4. Clarify the gradient flow: either treat $N_{pred,i}$ as a detached constant (and say so explicitly) or redesign the loss so it is genuinely differentiable end-to-end.
5. Independently verify the CIFAR-100 results — a ~12% gap over strong baselines needs careful validation and explanation.
