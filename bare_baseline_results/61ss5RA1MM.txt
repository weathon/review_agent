## Summary
OC-Flow frames training-free guided generation from pre-trained flow matching models as an optimal control problem, leveraging Pontryagin's Maximum Principle and the Extended Method of Successive Approximations (E-MSA). The paper delivers convergence guarantees in both Euclidean and SO(3) spaces, unifies prior backprop-through-ODE approaches (D-Flow, FlowGrad) as special cases, and validates the approach across text-guided image manipulation, conditional molecule generation on QM9, and all-atom peptide design.

## Strengths
- **Solid theoretical grounding**: Proposition 1 provides a clean KL-divergence bound via the running cost, and Theorem 2 / Theorem 5 give explicit monotone improvement guarantees under E-MSA for both Euclidean and SO(3)—something neither D-Flow nor FlowGrad possesses.
- **SO(3) extension is genuinely novel**: Extending E-MSA to the Lie group SO(3) with proper adjoint variable dynamics and convergence proofs is technically non-trivial and directly applicable to protein/peptide design pipelines where rotational degrees of freedom dominate.
- **Unification of prior art**: Cleanly showing D-Flow and FlowGrad as limiting cases of the OC-Flow update rule (γ→∞ or single control term) clarifies the design space and explains their empirical limitations.
- **Practical efficiency**: The adjoint/vector-Jacobian product approach reduces memory from O(ND²) to O(D²), and the asynchronous update scheme enables flexible compute–quality tradeoffs, with wall-clock time down from ~15 min (D-Flow) to ~216 s on images.
- **Multi-domain experiments**: The three distinct experimental domains (faces, small molecules, peptides) credibly demonstrate generality.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Modest empirical gains in peptide design**: Table 5 shows IMP improves from 14.3% → 15.0% and RMSD *worsens* from 1.645 → 2.127 for the combined (trans+rot) setting. Stability/affinity improvements exist but are small in absolute terms. Given that SO(3) guidance is the headline technical novelty, the flagship experiment's gains should be stronger.
- **Incomplete comparison in image experiments**: Table 2 does not include D-Flow as a baseline (only FlowGrad is compared against OC-Flow on the same prior). The omission is not adequately justified and weakens the empirical story.
- **Gap between theory and practice for SO(3)**: The practical implementation in Section 4.3 introduces approximations (decomposition onto canonical basis, asynchronous scheme) that deviate from the exact Algorithm 2 for which convergence is proved. The discretization error analysis for SO(3) is deferred to the appendix but the magnitude of the approximation gap is not characterized.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity not studied**: The weight decay β and learning rate η are free parameters whose joint interaction determines the effective γ; no systematic ablation of these across tasks is presented, making reproducibility harder.

### Minor
- The equivalence of FlowGrad to the γ→∞ limit involves taking dt→0 simultaneously; the two limits need not commute in practice with finite ODE steps, so the "special case" claim is approximate rather than exact.
- The QM9 D-Flow baseline uses L-BFGS with line search, while OC-Flow uses fixed-step gradient descent; the comparison is not entirely apples-to-apples.
- Table 3 shows D-Flow beating OC-Flow on Δε—this exception is not discussed.

### Tiny
- Figure 1 caption is duplicated verbatim in the PDF rendering.
- "square-shaped data" in Proposition 1 is informal terminology; a precise definition would help readers.

## Nice-to-Haves
- An ablation varying the number of ODE timesteps N vs. the number of control terms n (asynchronous ratio) would make the efficiency–quality tradeoff concrete.
- Comparing OC-Flow to posterior-sampling-based guidance (e.g., manifold-constrained gradients) on QM9 or peptides, not just backprop-through-ODE methods, would situate the work more broadly.
- Reporting running time for the SO(3) peptide experiment alongside memory usage.

## Novel Insights
The cleanest novel insight is the principled connection between the running-cost magnitude and KL divergence from the prior (Proposition 1), which gives a formal handle on the exploitation–faithfulness tradeoff that practitioners tune heuristically. The extension of E-MSA to SO(3) using left-invariant Hamiltonians and Lie-algebra-valued co-states is a technically meaningful generalization; prior work on guided generation on manifolds lacked convergence guarantees. The asynchronous update scheme—where the ratio of control-term updates to ODE steps is a free parameter—is a simple but useful engineering insight that lets practitioners trade memory for quality continuously.

## Suggestions
- Add D-Flow results to Table 2 (image manipulation) for completeness, even if the comparison requires adapting D-Flow to RF.
- Provide an empirical study on the SO(3) approximation quality (e.g., compare exact vs. approximate adjoint dynamics on a small system where both are feasible).
- Include error bars or significance tests for peptide design metrics, given the relatively small improvements over baseline.
- Characterize the discretization error on SO(3) (analogous to the O(Δt) result stated for Euclidean space in Appendix C.3).
