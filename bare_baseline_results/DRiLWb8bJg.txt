## Summary
The paper presents two complementary contributions: (1) **SAPO** (Soft Analytic Policy Optimization), a first-order model-based RL algorithm that combines analytic gradients from differentiable simulation with maximum entropy regularization (building on SHAC); and (2) **Rewarped**, a GPU-parallel differentiable multiphysics simulation platform built on NVIDIA Warp, supporting rigid bodies, articulations, elastic, plasticine, and fluid materials. Together, these address the bottleneck of applying RL to tasks involving deformable objects, where simulation is slow and sample complexity is prohibitive.

---

## Strengths
- **Clear ecosystem gap addressed**: Table 1 convincingly demonstrates that no existing parallel differentiable simulator supports all material types; Rewarped uniquely fills this void.
- **Strong empirical results**: SAPO consistently and substantially outperforms all baselines across 6 diverse tasks (e.g., HandFlip: 90 vs. 33 for SHAC, SoftJumper: 1820 vs. 853), with 10 random seeds and 95% CIs, demonstrating statistical rigor.
- **Informative ablations**: The ablation on HandFlip clearly separates the value of entropy-augmented actor training (w/o $\mathcal{H}_\pi$: +78.8%) from the soft critic (w/o $V_\text{soft}$: +133.3%), showing entropy in actor updates is the dominant factor.
- **Engineering contributions in Rewarped**: Parallelized MPM with gradient checkpointing via CUDA graph replay is technically nontrivial and enables practical scaling.

---

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Limited algorithmic novelty of SAPO**: SAPO is essentially SHAC + SAC-style entropy augmentation + several engineering tweaks from existing work (CrossQ batch normalization tricks, AdamW, SiLU, clipped double critic). The "main observation" (Eq. 19) is a straightforward substitution of entropy-augmented returns into SHAC's objective. The paper provides minimal theoretical analysis of *why* entropy regularization helps with differentiable simulation gradients beyond citing prior smoothing intuitions.
- **One-way coupling only**: Rewarped only supports one-way coupling from articulated rigid bodies to MPM particles (Section 5), meaning deformables cannot exert forces back on rigid bodies. This severely limits the fidelity of manipulation tasks (e.g., HandFlip, RollingFlat) and is a significant practical limitation that deserves more prominence.
- **Ablations on a single task**: All component ablations are conducted only on HandFlip. It is unclear whether the contributions (especially design choices III–V) generalize, particularly since the paper reports that design choices III–V have "minimal impact" on DFlex rigid-body tasks (Appendix F.3), suggesting the contribution may be task-specific.

### Minor
- **No wall-clock time comparison**: Results are presented only in environment steps, but the computational overhead of SAPO's entropy terms and additional critic relative to SHAC is not quantified. Practical usability depends on this.
- **Partial success on HandReorient**: SAPO only learns to catch and stabilize the cube rather than reorient it, yet this is listed as a success case. The claim of outperforming baselines is statistically valid (221.7 vs. −2.5) but functionally limited.
- **No comparison on DaXBench tasks**: The most closely related prior work on differentiable soft-body simulation for RL (DaXBench/Chen et al., 2022) is referenced throughout, but direct benchmarking on their tasks is absent; only DFlex rigid-body tasks appear in the appendix.
- **FluidMove marginal improvement**: The improvement over SAC/PPO on FluidMove (30.6 vs. 28.2/27.3) is marginal and may not be practically significant for a task that was explicitly designed for deformable RL.

### Tiny
- Lock-in to NVIDIA Warp as backend; portability/reproducibility on non-NVIDIA hardware is unclear.
- The target entropy heuristic $\bar{\mathcal{H}} = -\dim(\mathcal{A})/2$ is borrowed without sensitivity analysis.

---

## Nice-to-Haves
- Ablations extended to at least one deformable task other than HandFlip (e.g., SoftJumper).
- Analysis of gradient variance/magnitude comparing SAPO vs. SHAC to validate the "smoother optimization landscape" hypothesis empirically.
- Sim-to-real or cross-platform transfer experiments to assess how useful Rewarped policies are beyond the platform itself.

---

## Novel Insights
The key practical insight is that maximum entropy regularization — specifically the entropy term in the actor's FOBG objective — can substantially stabilize first-order MBRL with differentiable simulation on contact-rich and high-DoF deformable tasks, even when the critic's soft-value function alone provides smaller gains. This suggests that stochasticity injection via entropy, rather than just value function smoothing, is the more important stabilizer for differentiable policy gradients in non-smooth contact dynamics. This is actionable: future FO-MBRL works should likely incorporate entropy regularization regardless of other design choices.

---

## Suggestions
- Quantify one-way vs. two-way coupling fidelity impact, or at minimum clearly scope claims to one-way coupling regimes.
- Report wall-clock efficiency alongside sample efficiency to help practitioners understand total cost.
- Extend ablations to SoftJumper or RollingFlat to test if entropy's dominant role holds across different deformable material types.
- Provide direct comparison to DaXBench tasks/baselines for a more complete position of Rewarped in the landscape.

---

## Final Score

The paper offers a genuinely useful dual contribution (algorithm + platform) backed by strong experiments and careful evaluation. The weaknesses are real — SAPO's algorithmic novelty is modest, the one-way coupling limitation is significant, and ablations are narrow — but the combination of a well-motivated approach, clear gap in the ecosystem, and compelling empirical results make this a valuable contribution to the field.
