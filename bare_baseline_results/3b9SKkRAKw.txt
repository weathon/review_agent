## Summary
LeFusion is a lesion-focused diffusion model for background-preserving medical lesion synthesis. The core idea is to redesign the diffusion training objective to compute loss exclusively within lesion regions (lesion-focused loss), while at inference integrating real forward-diffused backgrounds with reverse-diffused foregrounds—similar to inpainting methods—to guarantee background fidelity. The paper further introduces histogram-based texture control for multi-peak lesions (e.g., lung nodules), multi-channel decomposition for multi-class lesions (e.g., cardiac MI/PMO), and a DiffMask module for controllable lesion mask generation. Validated on 3D lung nodule CT (LIDC) and cardiac MRI (Emidec), the synthetic data consistently boosts nnUNet and SwinUNETR segmentation performance.

## Strengths
- **Elegant decoupling of lesion and background generation**: The lesion-focused training loss (masking the objective to foreground voxels only) is a simple but important modification over standard inpainting diffusion (RePaint/Blended Diffusion), which use a global loss. This cleanly addresses background capacity waste in data-limited settings.
- **Practically important problem with strong baselines**: Two real-world 3D medical datasets tested with two SOTA segmentation downstream models (nnUNet, SwinUNETR), with rigorous ablations covering Hand-Crafted, Cond-Diffusion, Cond-Diffusion (L), and RePaint comparisons.
- **Consistent and meaningful downstream gains**: LeFusion-H+DiffMask achieves ~5% Dice improvement on LIDC over the baseline, and LeFusion-J+DiffMask achieves significant gains on the harder PMO class (e.g., +6.12% for nnUNet PMO Dice). Improvements are consistent across both segmentation architectures.
- **Comprehensive ablation**: The paper carefully decomposes contribution of each component (lesion-focused loss, histogram control, multi-channel decomposition, DiffMask), with clear qualitative and quantitative evidence.
- **No extra annotation required**: Histogram-based control is derived automatically from image-mask pairs without lesion type labels.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Novelty over RePaint/Blended Diffusion is incremental**: The inference-time background integration (Eq. 3) is directly from Lugmayr et al. (2022) and Avrahami et al. (2022). The core differentiator is simply masking the training loss to the foreground region (Eq. 4). While practically effective, this is a small modification. The histogram control, multi-channel decomposition, and DiffMask are reasonable but individually also modest.
- **Limited dataset breadth**: Only two lesion types (lung nodule CT, cardiac MRI) are validated. The generalizability of the approach—especially for more complex and irregular lesion distributions like brain tumors, liver lesions, or polyps—is not demonstrated, limiting confidence in the claimed broad applicability.
- **Histogram control requires knowing target distribution at inference**: While the authors mention sampling from the training histogram distribution, there is no rigorous protocol for how the control histogram should be selected in a real augmentation pipeline. This is an important practical concern that is underexplored.
- **Improvements in Table 2 are inconsistent for MI lesion**: For nnUNet MI Dice, gains over the baseline are small (~1–2.7%), and SwinUNETR MI improvements are modest. The method's advantages are more pronounced on PMO (smaller class), suggesting the benefit depends on class difficulty.

### Minor
- The comparison with RePaint is somewhat incomplete as an ablation. An experiment explicitly comparing: (a) RePaint inference with LeFusion-focused training loss, vs. (b) standard RePaint, would better isolate the contribution of the lesion-focused loss versus the training-free inpainting trick.
- Unpaired perceptual metrics (FID, KID, SWD) are dismissed in favor of downstream performance, but their correlation with downstream metrics is not shown—which makes the dismissal argumentative rather than empirically grounded.
- The claim that histograms prevent "mode collapse" (Sec. 3.2) is imprecise—the figure shows mode bias (toward healthy appearance) rather than mode collapse in the generative modeling sense.
- Concurrent related works (Lai et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2024) are cited but not compared due to "differences in research focus or unavailability of code," which is understandable but weakens positioning.

### Tiny
- Minor citation inconsistency: abstract says "Armato III et al., 2011" but Section 4.1 Setup says "Amato III et al., 2011."
- "SwinUNETR (Hatamizadeh et al., 2021)" is sometimes spelled "Hatanizadeh" in the paper.
- Eq. 4 uses $M_f \|\cdot\|_2$ without making explicit whether it is element-wise product followed by sum, or a masked average—the formulation could be clearer.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Validation on additional lesion/anomaly domains (e.g., liver tumors, polyps) to support the claimed generalizability to industrial/environmental settings.
- A study of the sensitivity to the choice of histogram bins and histogram sampling strategy at inference.
- Comparison of DiffMask diversity vs. simply augmenting with more copy-paste or morphological masks at higher volumes.

## Novel Insights
The most genuinely novel observation in this paper is that forcing the diffusion model to focus *only* on the lesion region during training—rather than applying the standard global noise-prediction loss—is more efficient and leads to better generative quality in data-limited medical settings. This insight, while simple, provides a principled justification for why lesion-focused synthesis should not merely rely on the inference-time inpainting trick from RePaint but should also shape the training objective. The histogram-based observation that without explicit texture conditioning, diffusion models bias toward generating "healthy-looking" lesions on normal backgrounds (mode shift, not collapse) is also an interesting empirical finding that could inform future work on conditional generation for out-of-distribution target regions.

## Suggestions
- Add an ablation that directly compares "RePaint inference + lesion-focused training loss (without histogram)" vs. "standard RePaint" to cleanly isolate the training objective's contribution.
- Provide a protocol (e.g., sample histogram from the empirical training distribution with replacement) for how histograms are selected at inference time, and report sensitivity to this choice.
- Consider testing on at least one additional lesion type (e.g., liver tumor or polyp) to strengthen generalizability claims.
- Clarify the FID/KID/SWD results: show whether they correlate with downstream performance across methods, or explain why they don't.
