## Summary
This paper proposes FGL_AC, a federated graph learning framework for graph classification that adds two components to standard federated learning: (1) spectral clustering to preprocess each client's local graph dataset before training, and (2) a GAT-style attention mechanism at the server to assign different aggregation weights to different clients' uploaded parameters. Experiments on MUTAG, ENZYMES, and PROTEINS datasets under four data split settings show modest improvements over FedAvg and FedProx baselines.

## Strengths
- **Practical motivation**: The problem of federated graph learning in IIoT settings with heterogeneous, distributed graph data is a real and important problem.
- **Multiple data split scenarios**: Testing under four conditions (balanced/unbalanced × overlap/no-overlap) is a reasonable attempt to simulate real-world heterogeneity.
- **Ablation study**: The paper does include ablation experiments separately removing the clustering (FGL_AC-C) and attention (FGL_AC-A) components to justify each contribution.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
- **Critical ambiguity in the attention mechanism**: Equation 8 copies the GAT attention formula (Veličković et al., 2018) but applies it to "client feature vectors c_i, c_j" without ever defining what these vectors are. Are they the uploaded model parameters? Some loss-derived embeddings? Gradient norms? This is the core technical contribution of the paper and it is left completely undefined, making the method unreproducible.
- **Unclear use of clustering outputs**: The spectral clustering groups graphs into clusters, but the paper never explains how these cluster assignments are used as "auxiliary information for downstream tasks." The algorithm (lines 3–6) computes clusters, then standard local model training follows with no explicit linkage to the clustering outputs.

### Major
- **Severely insufficient baselines**: The paper only compares against GCN-FedAvg and SAGE-FedAvg (and their FedProx variants). The federated graph learning literature has numerous relevant methods (GCFL, GCFL+, FedPub, SpreadGNN, FedStar, etc.) that are entirely absent. The comparison is thus not credible for a 2025 venue.
- **Tiny-scale experiments**: Only 3 clients and 1 server are simulated. MUTAG has only ~188 graphs; split three ways this yields ~60 graphs per client. Observed gains of 1–3% are well within noise for such small sample sizes. No confidence intervals or statistical significance tests are provided.
- **Notation collision**: In Table 1, both L (number of local iterations) and L (Laplacian matrix) are listed as separate symbols with the same letter — a basic correctness error that suggests insufficient proofreading.
- **No communication overhead analysis**: Spectral clustering on large graphs is expensive (O(n³) for eigendecomposition), and the attention mechanism requires the server to store all client parameters and compute pairwise attention. The claimed benefit of "reducing communication overhead" is never quantitatively validated.
- **Differential privacy mentioned but not analyzed**: Fig. 2 shows DP applied to model transfers, but there is no discussion of how DP noise affects the attention weight computation or whether the noise budget is calibrated anywhere in the paper.

### Minor
- Writing quality is poor throughout: numerous grammatical errors, awkward phrasing ("a graph structure with false edges"), repetitive sentences across sections.
- Figures 3 and 4 have near-identical alt-text descriptions and appear to be copy-paste duplicates — it is unclear whether the ablation experiment results are actually distinct across the two split conditions.
- Equation 9 is written for exactly 3 clients (α, β, δ) with no general formulation, contradicting the claim of handling K arbitrary clients.
- No convergence analysis or theoretical properties are provided for the attention-weighted aggregation update rule.
- The hyperparameter k for spectral clustering (number of clusters) is not analyzed or ablated.

### Tiny
- "European data" should read "Euclidean data" (Section 1).
- The notation `{0}---` appearing at the top is a formatting artifact.

## Nice-to-Haves
- A theoretical analysis showing that the proposed attention-weighted aggregation improves over FedAvg under some distributional assumptions would substantially strengthen the paper.
- Experiments with 10–100 clients on larger graph datasets (e.g., OGBG-molhiv, TUDataset larger splits) would make the scalability claims credible.
- Comparison with at least FedPub or GCFL+ is necessary for the work to be positioned fairly in the literature.

## Novel Insights
None beyond the paper's own contributions. The combination of spectral clustering preprocessing with attention-based server aggregation is an engineering assembly of well-known techniques (spectral clustering, GAT attention, federated averaging). The individual components are not novel, and the combination lacks theoretical motivation or surprising empirical findings that would constitute a genuine insight.

## Suggestions
- Clearly define how client feature vectors c_i are constructed for the attention computation in Equation 8, and provide pseudocode or an equation describing this process.
- Explicitly describe the path from spectral clustering cluster labels to their use in the GNN training pipeline (e.g., as node/graph features, as data selection criteria, etc.).
- Include at minimum FedPub and GCFL+ as baselines, and scale experiments to ≥10 clients.
- Report mean ± standard deviation over multiple runs to allow assessment of statistical significance.
- Resolve the L notation collision in Table 1 and fix the copy-paste errors in Figures 3 and 4.
