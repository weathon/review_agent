# Harsh Critic Review
## Section-by-Section Critical Review

**Title & Abstract**
The title is clear, attention-grabbing, and accurately reflects the paper's core claim. The abstract succinctly states the problem, the method (Calgacus), its key properties (same length, steerability, efficiency), and its broader implications (decoupling text from intent, AI safety concerns). All abstract claims are directly supported by the paper's content.

**Introduction & Motivation**
The introduction is exceptionally well-written and effectively motivates the work from a broad historical and philosophical perspective, framing it as a lens to understand the new reality of machine-generated text. The problem is compellingly introduced with vivid examples (political tweets, secret manuscripts). The contributions are clearly stated: the Calgacus protocol, its full-capacity property, efficiency, and the ensuing discussion on implications. The potential applications (censorship evasion, disguised unaligned chatbots) set high stakes.

**Related Work**
The section adequately covers steganography, generative steganography, and LLM-based steganography, correctly identifying the niche of "full capacity" (same length) as this paper's contribution. However, for an ICLR audience, the discussion is somewhat superficial. A more critical engagement with the **theoretical foundations of steganography** (e.g., the prisoner's problem, information-theoretic models, security definitions) is missing. The paper correctly notes the difficulty of formal guarantees but does not position its own protocol within or against these frameworks. This is a missed opportunity to deepen the technical contribution.

**Method**
*   **Clarity & Reproducibility:** The method description is outstandingly clear. The "recipe" format, Figure 3, and the provided demo make the protocol easily understandable and reproducible—a major strength.
*   **Soundness Metric:** Using LLM-assigned log-probability as a proxy for text "soundness" is standard and pragmatic. The handling of length dependence by comparing equal-length texts and the use of a real-text (Reddit) distribution as a baseline is methodologically sound.
*   **Key Analysis:** The "Low entropy token choices" paragraph provides a crucial and insightful explanation for the empirical probability gap between originals and stegotexts, elevating the work from mere demonstration to analysis.
*   **Limitations:** The stated limitations (dependency on `e`, `k`, LLM quality; need for identical compute) are appropriate. The appendices (A.1-A.5) offer valuable practical guidance and analysis of failure modes.

**Experiments & Results**
*   **Plausibility Demonstration:** The experiments in Figure 4 successfully demonstrate the core claim: stegotexts generated by Calgacus fall within the distribution of human-like text plausibility (as judged by an LLM). The use of a held-out Reddit corpus and testing with a different LLM (Phi-3, Figure 14) are good practices.
*   **Major Gap - Steganographic Evaluation:** The paper **lacks a rigorous quantitative evaluation of steganographic properties**, which is a critical shortcoming for ICLR.
    1.  **Security/Detectability:** The paper shows an LLM can distinguish originals from fakes via probability, which is an upper bound on vulnerability. However, it does not evaluate performance against **state-of-the-art steganalysis methods**. Can a dedicated detector (e.g., a classifier trained on real vs. Calgacus-generated text) reliably identify the stegotexts? This is essential to assess the practical "hiding" claim.
    2.  **Capacity Analysis:** While the "full capacity" property is highlighted, there is no analysis of the **effective bit rate**. The entropy of the rank sequence and the actual bits per token encoded should be quantified.
    3.  **Robustness Ablations:** Systematic studies on how stegotext quality varies with message length, prompt specificity, or different LLMs are absent. The qualitative examples are compelling but not a substitute for quantitative analysis.
*   **Qualitative Strengths:** The qualitative examples (Figures 1, 7, 8, 11-13) are excellent and powerfully illustrate steerability, domain adaptation, and the alarming chatbot application.

**Security Discussion (Section 3.1)**
The discussion of attack scenarios (unknown LLM, unknown key) is sensible and pragmatic. The argument for deniability, supported by the probability distribution overlap (Figure 4) and the toy example (Figure 15), is persuasive. However, the analysis remains informal. A more formal connection to concepts from deniable encryption or a quantitative analysis of the search space for key recovery would strengthen this section for ICLR.

**Discussion & Implications**
This section is the intellectual highlight of the paper and arguably its most significant contribution for ICLR's broader community. The "Unaligned chatbots disguised as aligned chatbots" scenario is a concrete, powerful, and timely illustration of a novel AI safety threat. The subsequent discussion on the nature of LLM knowledge, redefining hallucinations as a "lack of intention," and the difficulties of ascribing intent to LLMs is profound, original, and exceptionally well-argued. It successfully elevates the work from a technical protocol to a meaningful commentary on the foundations of LLMs.

**Writing & Clarity**
The writing is superb—clear, engaging, and rhetorically effective. The use of historical and literary references (Asterix, Oulipo, Tacitus) enhances the narrative without distracting from the technical content. The paper is a model of accessible yet deep scientific communication.

**Limitations & Broader Impact**
Technical limitations are adequately noted. The broader societal impact—the dual-use nature for censorship and deception—is front and center in the discussion, appropriately highlighting the risks. The paper convincingly argues that its very existence has an impact by demonstrating a fundamental decoupling.

### Overall Assessment

This is a thought-provoking, beautifully written paper with a simple yet powerful core idea. The Calgacus protocol is clearly described and demonstrated to work. The discussion on implications for AI safety and the nature of LLMs is outstanding and meets ICLR's interest in foundational and societal issues.

However, the paper's weakness is a **lack of rigorous quantitative evaluation from a steganography research perspective**. The absence of detection experiments against modern steganalysis methods and a deeper information-theoretic analysis of capacity and security is a significant gap for a top-tier machine learning conference. While the philosophical discussion is a major strength, the technical evaluation feels incomplete.

The contribution stands as a compelling demonstration and a profound think-piece, but its acceptance at ICLR hinges on whether reviewers weigh the innovative conceptual discussion more heavily than the missing empirical benchmarks typical of steganography research. In its current form, it is a borderline submission.

# Neutral Reviewer
## Balanced Review

### Summary
This paper introduces "Calgacus," a protocol for generative steganography that uses Large Language Models (LLMs) to hide an arbitrary text within a different, plausible text of exactly the same token length. The method leverages token ranks from the secret message to deterministically generate a stegotext from a secret prompt. The authors demonstrate the protocol's practicality with open-source LLMs and explore its broader implications for AI safety, trust in written communication, and philosophical questions about authorial intent and LLM knowledge.

### Strengths
1. **Novel and Elegant Core Idea:** The protocol's key innovation—achieving *full-capacity* steganography (identical input/output length) via simple rank-preserving transformation—is clearly presented and distinct from prior work. It represents a meaningful advance in generative steganography.
2. **Thorough Empirical Analysis:** The paper provides comprehensive experiments: quantifying stegotext plausibility against real text distributions (Fig. 4), analyzing the probability gap between originals and fakes (Figs. 5, 14), and examining dependencies on model quality (Fig. 8), message domain (Fig. 7), and prompt design (Figs. 9-10). The use of Reddit posts for evaluation is appropriate.
3. **Compelling Discussion of Implications:** The translation from technical result to broader impact is exceptionally well-executed. The AI safety scenario (disguising unfiltered LLMs), the re-framing of hallucinations as "void of intention," and the challenge to LLM "knowledge" are significant, thought-provoking contributions that align with ICLR's interdisciplinary scope.
4. **Strong Reproducibility and Practicality:** The method is shown to be efficient (seconds on consumer hardware) and workable with open-source models. The provision of a demo, companion notebook, and detailed appendix (best practices, handling vocabulary mismatches) strongly supports reproducibility and practical adoption.

### Weaknesses
1. **Insufficient Security Analysis:** The security discussion is relatively informal. While deniability and brute-force resistance are mentioned, there's no rigorous cryptographic treatment or threat modeling. The claim that adding a random string to the key suffices to prevent attacks is asserted but not proven against potential context-aware adversaries. A more formal security analysis would strengthen this critical aspect.
2. **Limited Evaluation of Undetectability (Stealth):** The evaluation focuses on *plausibility* rather than *undetectability*—a core requirement for steganography. The paper lacks systematic experiments (e.g., human studies or classifier-based detection) to assess whether a corpus of stegotexts can be distinguished from genuine texts beyond simple log-probability comparison.
3. **Methodological Details Somewhat Dispersed:** Important practical insights (e.g., rank inversion to handle short prompts, initial high-rank token phenomenon) are buried in the appendix. Integrating these into the main Method section would improve clarity and utility for implementers.
4. **Occasionally Overstated Rhetoric:** The philosophical introduction ("end of history itself") is engaging but may be perceived as hyperbolic. The discussion section grounds the implications more effectively; tempering the introductory claims would improve academic tone without diminishing impact.

### Novelty & Significance
**Novelty:** High. The full-capacity constraint and the rank-based protocol are novel contributions to LLM-based steganography. The interdisciplinary discussion linking the technique to intent, safety, and epistemology is also fresh and thought-provoking.
**Significance:** Very high. The work demonstrates a concrete, efficient method that exacerbates key societal concerns about LLMs: erosion of trust in text and novel AI safety vulnerabilities. The "Shibbolethian Theatre" scenario presents a tangible and urgent challenge for AI alignment. The paper successfully bridges technical innovation with meaningful philosophical and safety discourse.

### Suggestions for Improvement
1. **Formalize Security Analysis:** Develop a clearer threat model and provide a more rigorous security discussion. Consider analyzing the effective key space reduction if an attacker uses the stegotext context, and provide empirical or theoretical bounds on the feasibility of such attacks.
2. **Conduct Stealth Detection Experiments:** Perform human evaluations or train a detector to classify texts as stegotexts vs. genuine. Reporting detection accuracy (e.g., AUC) would provide stronger evidence for the undetectability claim central to steganography.
3. **Integrate Key Practical Insights:** Move the discussions on rank inversion (Fig. 9) and initial high-rank tokens (Fig. 10) from the appendix to the main Method section (Section 3) as important implementation considerations.
4. **Clarify and Temper Claims:** Refine the security claims (e.g., about random strings) with appropriate qualifications. Consider moderating the most dramatic historical assertions in the introduction to let the well-argued discussion stand out.
5. **Explore Additional Detection Countermeasures:** Briefly discuss potential mitigations or detection strategies from a defender's perspective, which would enrich the security discourse and practical relevance.

# Spark Finder Review
## How to Improve This Paper

### Missing Experiments (top 3-5 only)
1.  **Quantitative comparison against modern steganography baselines.** The paper claims novelty via "full capacity," but does not compare bit rate, statistical undetectability, or robustness to detection classifiers against state-of-the-art methods like Meteor or Wu et al. (2024). Without this, the practical significance of the contribution is not established.
2.  **Human evaluation of stegotext quality and stealth.** The core claim that generated texts are "coherent and plausible" to humans is supported only by cherry-picked examples and LLM log-probabilities. A controlled human study (e.g., Turing test or Likert-scale ratings) is essential to validate the method's practical utility for steganography.
3.  **Rigorous evaluation of the security claim against practical attacks.** The security discussion is theoretical. The paper should implement and test concrete attacks, such as training a classifier to distinguish stegotexts from genuine texts, or using an LLM to attempt to guess or constrain the key `k` from the stegotext `s` and its context.
4.  **Systematic evaluation of the "aligned chatbot disguise" application.** The gas meter example is a proof-of-concept. The paper should test this scenario at scale: measure the success rate of recovering hidden answers across a dataset of sensitive queries, and evaluate the quality and compliance of the surface answers.

### Deeper Analysis Needed (top 3-5 only)
1.  **Analysis of the capacity-quality-steerability trade-off.** The method has full capacity, but quality depends on the secret message `e`. A systematic study is missing: how do the entropy and domain of `e` affect the coherence and topical alignment of `s`? What is the effective, reliable capacity for a given quality threshold?
2.  **Theoretical analysis of the detectability via probability gap.** The paper identifies that stegotexts `s` are generally less probable than originals `e`, creating a detection surface. A deeper analysis is needed: Is this gap inevitable? Can it be reduced by selecting `e` with specific rank distributions? How effective is a simple detector based on this gap?
3.  **Formalization of security properties and threat models.** The paper explicitly avoids a formal model, but for ICLR, a clear definition of the adversary's knowledge (protocol, LLM, key space) and claimed security properties (key secrecy, deniability) is necessary to evaluate the contribution's rigor.

### Visualizations & Case Studies
1.  **Comprehensive failure case analysis.** To understand the method's limits, show and analyze stegotexts that fail to be coherent or steerable—e.g., from high-entropy `e` (like code), poor `k`, or weak LLMs. Annotate where high-rank tokens cause incoherence.
2.  **Visual trace of the encoding process.** A side-by-side visualization of a secret text `e`, its token-by-token rank sequence, and the resulting stegotext `s`, with annotations linking specific high-rank choices to potentially awkward phrases in `s`, would concretely demonstrate the mechanism and its constraints.

### Obvious Next Steps
1.  **Benchmark against existing steganography methods.** Direct comparison on standard metrics (encoding rate, statistical stealth, robustness) is a minimum requirement for a novel steganography paper at a top-tier conference. The related work is cited but not used for comparison.
2.  **Conduct a human evaluation study.** Even a small-scale user study is critical to support the claim that the generated texts are plausibly human-written. Relying solely on LLM probability as a proxy for human perception is a major methodological shortcoming.
3.  **Formalize the security argument.** Define a clear threat model and provide a more rigorous, information-theoretic or computational security argument for the key secrecy and deniability claims, moving beyond informal discussion and a single toy example.

# Final Consolidated Review
## Summary
The paper presents "Calgacus," a protocol for hiding an arbitrary text within a different, plausible text of the same token length using Large Language Models. The method is simple, efficient, and demonstrated to work with open-source models on consumer hardware. It further discusses profound implications for AI safety and the nature of authorial intent in machine-generated text.

## Strengths
- **Novel protocol achieving full-capacity steganography:** The core idea—using token ranks from a secret message to deterministically generate a stegotext of identical length—is elegant and clearly described, with a reproducible recipe, illustrative examples, and a provided demo.
- **Thorough empirical analysis of plausibility:** The paper quantitatively shows that generated stegotexts fall within the distribution of real text plausibility (as measured by LLM log-probability), analyzes the probability gap between originals and fakes, and systematically examines dependencies on model quality, message domain, and prompt design (e.g., Figures 4, 5, 7, 8).
- **Compelling discussion of broader implications:** The work translates the technical result into a concrete AI safety threat (the "Shibbolethian Theatre" scenario for disguising unaligned chatbots) and offers novel philosophical insights, re-framing hallucinations as a "void of intention" and challenging what it means for an LLM to "know" something.

## Weaknesses
- **Limited evaluation of undetectability (stealth):** While the paper demonstrates plausibility via LLM probability, it does not assess whether the stegotexts can be reliably detected by state-of-the-art steganalysis classifiers or human judges. This is a core requirement for steganography, and the shown probability gap suggests a detectable statistical signature that undermines the claim of perfect hiding.
- **Informal security analysis:** The security discussion, while pragmatic, lacks formal threat modeling or rigorous analysis of key recovery attacks beyond brute-force estimates. For a protocol making security claims (e.g., deniability, resistance to context-aware attacks), a more formal treatment would strengthen the contribution.
- **Absence of comparison to prior steganography methods:** The paper claims novelty in "full capacity" but does not benchmark against existing methods on standard metrics (e.g., effective bit rate, statistical stealth). This makes it difficult to assess the practical trade-offs and advantages of the proposed approach within the field.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Human evaluation of stegotext quality and steerability to complement the automatic plausibility metric.
- A more formal security definition and analysis connecting to existing cryptographic frameworks for deniable encryption.

## Novel Insights
The paper's discussion section provides genuinely novel observations: it uses the protocol as a lens to argue that hallucinations in LLMs might be better understood as a "lack of intention" rather than mere falsehoods, and it presents a concrete, alarming scenario where an aligned chatbot's responses can covertly encode answers from an unaligned model. This raises urgent questions about AI safety and challenges the entanglement of probabilistic text generation with authorial purpose.

## Suggestions
- Conduct detection experiments using a classifier trained to distinguish a corpus of Calgacus stegotexts from genuine texts, reporting accuracy metrics (e.g., AUC) to better evaluate stealth in practice.
- Include a comparative analysis with at least one recent steganography baseline (e.g., Meteor or Wu et al. 2024) on relevant metrics such as effective bit rate and statistical undetectability.
