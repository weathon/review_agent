# Harsh Critic Review
## Section-by-Section Critical Review

**Title & Abstract**
The title "Active Speech Enhancement: Beyond Passive Denoising Declipping and Dereverberation" is clear and reflects the proposed paradigm shift. The abstract succinctly states the core idea, contributions, and results. However, the claim of outperforming "existing baselines across multiple speech processing tasks" is vague. It is not specified whether these are state-of-the-art (SOTA) speech enhancement models or adapted ANC baselines. The abstract should more precisely define the comparison set to accurately frame the contribution's significance.

**Introduction & Motivation**
The introduction effectively contrasts traditional passive enhancement with ANC and motivates the unification into ASE. The four contributions are clearly listed. The problem is well-motivated. A minor point: the phrase "actively shapes the speech signal" could be clarified. Does "shaping" imply applying a frequency-selective gain? This relates to the core mechanism and should be explained more concretely early on.

**Background**
The background on feedforward ANC is standard and correctly sets up the notation. The transition to ASE (Eq. 4) is the key conceptual move: changing the objective from `d(n) - a(n)` to `d(n) + a(n)` to *enhance* rather than *cancel*. This is clearly explained. However, a critical conceptual gap emerges here. For tasks like dereverberation and declipping, the "disturbance" is not an additive external noise but a convolutional or non-linear distortion of the *speech itself*. The framework diagram (Figure 1b) and Eq. 4 imply `a(n)` is generated by a loudspeaker and acoustically added to `d(n)`. If `d(n)` is reverberant speech, how does adding an anti-signal `a(n)` result in dereverberation? The physical interpretation becomes unclear. The paper later defines the target `c(n)` for these tasks as the anechoic speech, sidestepping the physical setup. This disconnect between the motivating physical ANC framework and its application to non-additive distortions is a significant conceptual weakness that needs explicit discussion and justification.

**Method (ASE-TM Architecture & Optimization)**
The architecture is an incremental modification of SEmamba, replacing Mamba with Mamba2 and inserting an attention block. The description is reasonably detailed. The choice of Mamba2 is mentioned but not justified with evidence or citation regarding its "potential improvements in efficiency." The novelty of the architecture itself is limited.

The loss function is a complex combination of six terms, largely following prior work (MP-SENet, SEmamba). The modification of the magnitude loss to L1+L2 is noted but not motivated. The most significant element is the model's output: the complex spectrum of the cancelling signal `y(n)`. This is consistent with the ASE framework. However, the training process is described at a high level. A major omission is the lack of detail on **how the acoustic paths `P(z)` and `S(z)` are incorporated during training**. Are these simulated transfer functions applied to the signals within the training loop? Section 5.2 describes their simulation for dataset creation, but it is unclear if the model is trained end-to-end with these paths or if it learns a mapping from `x(n)` to `y(n)` assuming an identity secondary path. The reproducibility of the training procedure hinges on this detail.

**Experiments & Results**
*Datasets and Baselines*: The use of VoiceBank-DEMAND for denoising is standard. For dereverberation and declipping, generating data from VoiceBank is acceptable. The major concern is the choice of baselines. The paper compares only against **ANC methods adapted to the ASE framework** (THF-FxLMS, DeepANC, ARN). There is **no comparison to SOTA passive speech enhancement models** (e.g., SEmamba, diffusion models, etc.) on the same tasks and datasets. The claim that ASE is a "new paradigm" that "goes further" than passive enhancement is not validated if its performance is not compared to the pinnacle of passive methods. The outperformance of adapted ANC baselines is expected, as they were not designed for this task. This baseline selection severely undermines the paper's contribution assessment.

*Results*: The tables show ASE-TM significantly outperforming the adapted ANC baselines across all metrics and tasks. The results are internally consistent. The robustness analysis (Table 4) is good. The runtime analysis with future-frame prediction is commendable and important for the "active" claim. The ablation study (Fig 3a) shows the contribution of components, but it's unclear what "Mamba1 + Original loss" refers to—is this the original SEmamba? If so, this should be a primary baseline.

*Analysis*: Figure 4's power spectrum analysis for dereverberation/declipping is interesting but raises the earlier conceptual issue. For declipping, showing increased power in the enhanced signal makes sense if the anti-signal `a(n)` is "filling in" the clipped regions. For dereverberation, showing increased power is counter-intuitive, as dereverberation often involves *reducing* late-arriving energy. The interpretation of these spectra needs clarification in the context of the ASE physical model.

**Writing & Clarity**
The writing is generally clear. However, the central ambiguity regarding the physical realizability and training procedure for non-additive distortions (reverberation, clipping) creates confusion. Sections 3 and 5.2 (acoustic simulation) seem at odds with the application in Sections 5.1 and 6.2. This must be reconciled.

**Limitations & Broader Impact**
The limitations section is extremely brief and acknowledges only that baseline methods were adapted and that a unified multi-task model is future work. It fails to address the **core limitations**: (1) the lack of comparison to SOTA passive enhancers, (2) the unclear physical interpretation and potential training simplifications for non-additive distortions, and (3) the real-world feasibility (e.g., need for a reference microphone capturing the distorted speech `x(n)`, precise knowledge/calibration of secondary path `S(z)`). Broader impact is not discussed.

### Overall Assessment
The paper proposes a novel conceptual framing—Active Speech Enhancement—that merges ANC with speech enhancement. This is a potentially interesting direction. However, the experimental validation is critically flawed by the exclusive comparison to weak, adapted ANC baselines rather than state-of-the-art passive enhancement models. Furthermore, there is a significant conceptual and methodological gap in applying the physically-motivated ASE framework to non-additive distortions like reverberation and clipping, which is not adequately addressed. The architectural contributions are incremental. For ICLR, where novelty, rigor, and compelling evidence are paramount, these issues are substantial. The contribution does not currently stand on a solid foundation; major revisions in the experimental design and conceptual clarity are required before it could be considered acceptable.

# Neutral Reviewer
## Balanced Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a new paradigm called Active Speech Enhancement (ASE), which aims to unify goals from traditional passive speech enhancement and active noise cancellation (ANC). ASE actively shapes the speech signal by not only suppressing interference but also amplifying speech-relevant frequencies. The authors introduce a Transformer-Mamba-based model (ASE-TM) and a joint loss function, demonstrating improved performance on denoising, dereverberation, and declipping tasks compared to adapted ANC baselines.

### Strengths
1. **Novel Conceptual Framing**: The paper clearly motivates and formalizes the ASE paradigm, distinguishing it from passive enhancement and pure ANC. This provides a fresh perspective on integrating active control with speech signal improvement (Sections 1 & 3).
2. **Sophisticated Model Architecture**: The proposed ASE-TM model is technically sound, leveraging a modern hybrid Transformer-Mamba (Mamba2) design with time-frequency modeling and an integrated attention block. This builds thoughtfully on recent state-space model advancements (Sections 4.1, 4.2).
3. **Comprehensive Empirical Evaluation**: The paper provides extensive experiments across three distinct speech restoration tasks (denoising, dereverberation, declipping) using standard datasets and a suite of objective metrics (PESQ, STOI, CSIG, etc.). Results show consistent and significant gains over the chosen baselines (Section 5, Tables 1-3).
4. **Analysis of Practical Considerations**: The authors include a runtime analysis with a future-frame prediction strategy to address real-time constraints and evaluate robustness under varying acoustic conditions (e.g., different reverberation times and loudspeaker nonlinearities) (Sections 6.3, 6.4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited and Inappropriate Baselines**: The primary baselines are ANC methods (THF-FxLMS, DeepANC, ARN). For ICLR, a comparison against state-of-the-art *passive* speech enhancement models (e.g., recent diffusion models, strong Transformer-based enhancers) is crucial to justify the claim that "ASE goes further." The paper acknowledges this limitation but does not address it empirically (Section 7).
2. **High Computational Cost and Limited Efficiency Analysis**: The model requires ~10 days of training on an RTX A6000 GPU. While runtime for inference is discussed, there is no analysis of model size, parameter count, or FLOPs, which are important for assessing practicality (Section 5.3).
3. **Insufficient Analysis of the "Active" Mechanism**: The paper does not provide a clear ablation or analysis isolating the benefit of the "active" signal shaping (amplification of speech frequencies) versus the interference suppression component. It remains unclear how much of the gain comes from the novel paradigm versus simply using a more powerful neural architecture (Section 6).
4. **Lack of Theoretical Grounding**: The formulation of ASE is presented as an intuitive extension of ANC, but there is no theoretical analysis or discussion of its optimality, convergence properties, or fundamental limits compared to passive approaches (Section 3).
5. **Minor Clarity Issues**: The loss function description, while detailed, is complex and borrows heavily from prior work (SEmamba, MP-SENet). The specific contribution and tuning of the loss weights (`γ_i`) are not discussed, making reproducibility more challenging (Section 4.2).

### Novelty & Significance
**Novelty:** The core conceptual novelty of the ASE paradigm is moderate-to-high. While the architectural components (Transformer-Mamba hybrid) are incremental combinations of existing techniques, the framing of actively shaping speech within an ANC-like setup is underexplored.
**Significance:** The potential significance is high. If the ASE paradigm proves consistently superior to passive enhancement, it could influence the design of next-generation hearing aids, communication devices, and acoustic signal processing systems. However, the current evidence is not yet conclusive due to the weak baseline comparisons.

### Suggestions for Improvement
1. **Compare Against Strong Passive Enhancement Baselines**: For a credible claim of advancement, the authors must compare ASE-TM against top-performing passive models on the same tasks (e.g., recent diffusion models, CMGAN, or SepFormer-based enhancers). This is critical for ICLR.
2. **Conduct an Ablation Study on "Activeness"**: Design an experiment to decouple the effects. For instance, modify the loss to only suppress noise (like ANC) or only amplify speech, and compare the results to the full ASE objective. This would directly validate the paradigm's core premise.
3. **Include Model Efficiency Metrics**: Report the number of parameters, computational complexity (in GFLOPs), and real-time factor for the proposed model. Discuss trade-offs between performance and efficiency.
4. **Deepen the Theoretical Discussion**: Provide a more rigorous formulation. Could the ASE objective be derived from a principled optimization criterion (e.g., maximizing a perceptually-weighted SNR)? A brief theoretical discussion would strengthen the foundation.
5. **Clarify the Loss Function and Training Details**: Explicitly state the final values or tuning strategy for the loss weights (`γ_i`). Provide more details on the discriminator training for the metric-based adversarial loss to ensure reproducibility.
6. **Explore a Truly Unified Model**: The paper tests separate models for each task. A stronger demonstration of the ASE paradigm's versatility would be a single model trained on a multi-task objective (joint denoising, dereverberation, declipping), as hinted in the conclusion.

# Spark Finder Review
## How to Improve This Paper

### Missing Experiments (top 3-5 only)
1. **Compare against state-of-the-art speech enhancement methods.** The paper only compares to ANC baselines (THF-FxLMS, DeepANC, ARN). To substantiate the claim of a "new paradigm" that outperforms traditional speech enhancement, direct comparisons against recent strong SE models (e.g., SEmamba, diffusion models, CMGAN) on the same tasks and datasets are mandatory. Without this, the claimed superiority is unsupported.
2. **Evaluate on real-world acoustic recordings.** The entire evaluation uses simulated acoustic paths (RIRs, SEF). The core claim of active enhancement in an acoustic environment is undermined without validation on real data from physical microphone/loudspeaker setups. A real-world recorded test set is essential.
3. **Test multi-task/joint training performance.** The paper presents separate models for denoising, dereverberation, and declipping. A critical claim of ASE is unified enhancement, but there is no experiment showing a single model can handle multiple distortion types simultaneously or that multi-task training improves robustness. This is a fundamental gap.
4. **Ablation on the core "active" component.** An experiment is needed to isolate the benefit of the proposed "active shaping" (attenuating noise + amplifying speech) versus simply performing passive enhancement on the reference signal *x(n)*. Compare ASE-TM's output *e_h(n)* to the output of the same model architecture trained for standard, passive enhancement of *x(n)*.

### Deeper Analysis Needed (top 3-5 only)
1. **Analyze what the model is actually amplifying/attenuating.** The claim of "amplifying speech-relevant frequencies" requires analysis. Provide a frequency-band or formant-specific gain analysis comparing the enhanced signal *e_h(n)* to the clean target *c(n)* and the noisy input *d(n)*. Does it selectively boost? Or is it just a global gain?
2. **Phase analysis for dereverberation and declipping.** The results show good PESQ but poor NMSE for dereverberation (Table 2, NMSE ~0). This suggests possible phase misalignment or scale issues. A detailed analysis of phase reconstruction quality (e.g., using metrics like phase loss) is needed to understand this discrepancy and the method's true restoration capability.
3. **Sensitivity analysis to path estimation errors.** The method assumes known or perfectly simulated primary *P(z)* and secondary *S(z)* paths. Analysis is missing on how performance degrades with estimation errors in these paths (e.g., using mismatched RIRs during testing), which is crucial for real-world applicability.
4. **Interpret the attention mechanism's role.** The paper adds an attention block but provides only a cursory convergence plot. A qualitative analysis (e.g., visualization of attention maps) is needed to show what long-range dependencies it captures and how this relates to the ASE task.

### Visualizations & Case Studies
1. **Visualize the "anti-signal" *a(n)* for different tasks.** Show waveforms and spectrograms of the generated cancellation signal *y(n)* or anti-signal *a(n)*. Does it contain structured speech components (for enrichment) or just noise? This would directly validate the "active shaping" premise.
2. **Case studies on failure modes.** Show specific examples where the method fails (e.g., under extreme nonlinearity *λ²=0.1*, or very low SNR). Analyze the characteristics of these failures to bound the method's applicability and inspire future work.
3. **Time-aligned waveform comparisons.** For declipping, show waveform zooms on clipped regions to demonstrate how well the model reconstructs the clipped peaks, compared to baselines and the clean target.

### Obvious Next Steps
1. **Benchmark against modern speech enhancement models.** This is the most critical immediate step. Train and compare against at least 2-3 recent, strong SE models (not ANC models) on VoiceBank-DEMAND and the authors' reverberation/clipping datasets.
2. **Implement and test a multi-task model.** Train a single ASE-TM model on a mixture of all three distortions (noise, reverb, clip) and evaluate its performance on each task. This is a direct test of the framework's unifying potential.
3. **Profile computational efficiency for real-time.** Provide detailed metrics: number of parameters, FLOPs, and actual latency on a standard CPU/GPU, compared to baseline SE and ANC methods. The runtime analysis is insufficient.

# Final Consolidated Review
## Summary
This paper introduces Active Speech Enhancement (ASE), a paradigm that merges active noise cancellation with speech enhancement by not only suppressing interference but also actively amplifying speech-relevant frequencies. The authors propose a Transformer-Mamba-based architecture (ASE-TM) and a joint loss function, demonstrating improved performance on denoising, dereverberation, and declipping tasks compared to adapted active noise control baselines.

## Strengths
- **Novel Conceptual Framework:** The paper clearly formalizes the ASE paradigm, providing a fresh synthesis of active noise control and speech enhancement objectives. This is a well-motivated contribution that opens a new direction for the field (Sections 1 & 3).
- **Comprehensive Empirical Evaluation:** The method is evaluated across three distinct and challenging tasks (denoising, dereverberation, declipping) using standard datasets and a full suite of objective metrics (PESQ, STOI, CSIG, CBAK, COVL, NMSE). The results show consistent and significant gains over the chosen baselines (Tables 1-3).
- **Practical and Robustness Analysis:** The paper includes important analyses on real-time feasibility with future-frame prediction, and evaluates robustness under varying acoustic conditions (e.g., different reverberation times and loudspeaker nonlinearities), strengthening the claim of practical applicability (Sections 6.3, 6.4).

## Weaknesses
- **Inadequate Baseline Comparison:** The paper only compares against adapted Active Noise Control (ANC) methods (THF-FxLMS, DeepANC, ARN). To substantiate the claim that ASE is a superior *paradigm*, a direct comparison against state-of-the-art passive speech enhancement models (e.g., recent diffusion models, strong Transformer-based enhancers) on the same tasks is essential. The current comparisons do not establish whether ASE outperforms the best existing alternatives (Sections 5.4, 6).
- **Unclear Physical Interpretation for Non-Additive Distortions:** The ASE framework is physically motivated for additive noise cancellation. Its application to convolutional (reverberation) and non-linear (clipping) distortions, where the "disturbance" is not an additive external signal, creates a conceptual gap. The paper sidesteps this by redefining targets, but the physical realizability and interpretation of adding an anti-signal `a(n)` to achieve dereverberation or declipping remain unclear and insufficiently justified (Sections 3, 6.2, 6.5).
- **Limited Analysis of the "Active" Mechanism:** The core premise of ASE is the dual action of suppression and targeted amplification. The paper lacks an ablation study or analysis that isolates the contribution of this "active shaping" versus simply using a more powerful neural network architecture for enhancement. It remains unclear how much gain is attributable to the novel paradigm itself (Section 6).

## Nice-to-Haves
- Include standard model efficiency metrics (parameter count, FLOPs) to better assess practicality.
- Experiment with a single, multi-task model trained jointly on all distortions to more strongly demonstrate the framework's unifying potential.
- A preliminary evaluation on real-world recorded data would strengthen the claim of real-world applicability, though simulation is a standard first step.

## Removed Points
*These points are flagged to be removed, treat them with caution*
- **Strengths:** Generic statements like "the writing is clear" or "the architecture is sophisticated" without specific evidence.
- **Weaknesses:** Criticisms about the abstract being "vague" (it is sufficiently specific) and demanding a theoretical optimality proof (this is an empirical systems paper; such analysis is not standard).
- **Weaknesses:** The claim that the architectural novelty is "limited" (the paper's contribution is the paradigm, not solely the architecture; the model is adequately advanced for the task).
- **Weaknesses:** Nitpicks about the motivation of Mamba2 (the citation is provided, and efficiency claims are reasonable).

## Novel Insights
The paper's core novel insight is the formalization of Active Speech Enhancement as a unified framework that extends active noise cancellation from pure suppression to include targeted speech signal enrichment. This reframes enhancement as an active control problem where a generated anti-signal is designed to constructively and destructively interfere with the received signal to produce a perceptually superior output. The empirical demonstration that this framework can be effectively applied not only to additive noise but also to declarative tasks like dereverberation and declipping (despite the conceptual tension) is a provocative and potentially fruitful direction.

## Suggestions
- The most critical revision is to add comparisons against strong, modern passive speech enhancement baselines (e.g., SEmamba, a recent diffusion model) on all three tasks to properly contextualize the performance of the ASE paradigm.
- Design and include an ablation experiment that isolates the "active shaping" component. For example, compare the full ASE-TM against a version where the loss only enforces noise suppression (ANC-like) or a version trained for standard passive enhancement of the reference signal.
- Clarify the physical interpretation and training procedure for the dereverberation and declipping tasks. Explicitly discuss how the ASE setup (Fig 1b, Eq. 4) should be understood when the primary signal `d(n)` is not an additive mixture but a distorted version of the speech itself.
