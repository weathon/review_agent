# Harsh Critic Review
## Section-by-Section Critical Review

**Title & Abstract:** The title is descriptive and reflects the core contributions (Direct-Align and SRPO). The abstract clearly states the two key problems addressed (computational cost of multi-step denoising and need for offline reward tuning) and the proposed solutions. The claim of a "3x" improvement in human-evaluated realism/aesthetics is a strong, quantitative claim that must be robustly supported in the experiments. The abstract is well-structured but would benefit from briefly specifying the baseline for the 3x improvement (presumably vanilla FLUX.1.dev).

**Introduction & Motivation:** The introduction effectively motivates the problem by highlighting two key limitations of existing direct gradient methods: 1) restriction to late timesteps leading to reward hacking, and 2) lack of online reward adjustment. The contributions are clearly listed at the end. The related work in Section 2 is adequate, though the connection between "Optimization on Diffusion Timesteps" and the proposed Direct-Align method could be made more explicit. A minor point: the phrase "they lack an mechanism" in the first paragraph has a grammatical error.

**Method / Approach:**
*   **Direct-Align (Section 3.1):** The core idea—pre-injecting known noise to enable accurate single-step recovery from any timestep—is clever and addresses the stated computational/optimization instability problem. However, the presentation has significant clarity issues that hinder reproducibility and understanding.
    *   Equation (5) and the surrounding text are confusing. The term `K` is undefined. The description "combines ground-truth vectors and model predictions" and the weights `Δσ_t` and `σ_t - Δσ` are not adequately motivated or derived. Is `Δσ_t` a hyperparameter? How is it chosen? The relationship between Eqs. (4) and (5) needs a clearer step-by-step explanation.
    *   The "Reward Aggregation Framework" paragraph and Eq. (6) introduce a sequence `{x_k, ..., x_{k-n}}` and a discount factor `λ(t)`. The process for generating this sequence from a single `x_0` is not clearly described. Is `n` the number of timesteps optimized? How is `λ(t)` defined? This is a critical component for mitigating late-timestep hacking but is currently underspecified.
    *   Figure 2's "Direct-Align" pipeline shows 4 stages, but the text description mentions only three core steps. The alignment between the figure and text needs improvement.
*   **Semantic Relative Preference Optimization (Section 3.2):** This is a novel and interesting idea—using paired positive/negative prompt augmentations to derive a relative reward signal that cancels out text-agnostic biases.
    *   Equation (8) is clear in intent. However, the claim that "reward bias primarily originates from the image branch (as the text branch does not backpropagation gradient)" is stated without evidence and is non-trivial. If the reward model's text encoder is frozen during RL, this is true, but it should be explicitly stated.
    *   The "Inversion-Based Regularization" subsection is very brief and cryptic. Equation (9) reintroduces the undefined `K`. The claim that "the inversion process has the opposite effect" and that this decouples penalization is not sufficiently explained. How is inversion used? Is it an alternative to the SRP signal from Eq. (8), or used in conjunction? This needs elaboration to be credible.
    *   The practical implementation of control words (e.g., "realistic photo" vs. "CG Render") is sensible, but the choice of these specific words is not ablated or justified. Their effectiveness likely depends on the reward model's training data.

**Experiments & Results:**
*   **Setup & Baselines (Section 4):** The experimental setup is strong: a modern base model (FLUX.1.dev), a standard benchmark (HPDv2), and relevant baselines (ReFL, DRaFT, DanceGRPO). The inclusion of both automatic metrics and human evaluation is excellent. The huge efficiency claim (75x faster than DanceGRPO) is notable.
*   **Main Results (Section 4.2):**
    *   **Automatic Metrics (Table 1):** The results show SRPO leads in Aesthetic and PickScore, but is middling or last in ImageReward, HPS, and DeQA. The authors correctly interpret this as a sign of not overfitting to HPS (the training reward), which is good. However, the claim of "superior performance across multiple reward metrics" is an overstatement; performance is superior on *some* metrics. The trade-off should be discussed more neutrally.
    *   **Human Evaluation (Figure 4):** This is the most compelling evidence for the paper's claims. The bar charts show SRPO dramatically increasing the "Excellent" rate for Realism & Aesthetics and reducing "Fail" rates. However, critical details are missing: How were the 10 annotators trained? What was the inter-annotator agreement? The "3.7x/3.1x" improvement claims from the abstract need to be directly traceable to this figure or a stated calculation. Currently, they are not.
    *   **Qualitative Results:** Figures 3, 6, 7, and 12 provide good visual evidence of improved detail and realism. The comparison with FLUX.1.Krea in Fig. 7(A) is a strong result.
*   **Ablation Study (Section 4.3):**
    *   The "Denoising Efficiency" and "Optimization Timestep" studies (Fig. 5) effectively validate the need for early-step optimization and show Direct-Align's capability.
    *   The "Effectiveness of Direct-Align" ablation in Fig. 7(D) is crucial but poorly explained in the text. What does "w/o early timestep opt." mean technically? What does "w/o λ(t) discount" mean? The labels "Inversion" and "Direct Con." correspond to which methodological variants? This figure is central to validating the two core components but is currently incomprehensible without guessing.
    *   The study on control word frequency and effectiveness (Fig. 11, Section 4.3) is a good analysis of the method's limitations.
*   **Appendix & Cross-Reward (Section A):** The cross-reward generalization results in Fig. 8 and the extension to aesthetic models in Fig. 9 are valuable, demonstrating the method's robustness. The comparison to GRPO in Appendix B is useful for positioning.

**Writing & Clarity:** The paper is generally well-written and structured. However, as noted, the **Method section suffers from serious clarity issues**. Key variables are undefined (`K`, `Δσ_t`), the procedural description of Direct-Align is ambiguous, and the ablation study labels are cryptic. These issues must be resolved for the paper to be reproducible and for the contribution to be properly assessed. The figures are generally informative, though some captions could be more descriptive (e.g., Fig. 7D).

**Limitations & Broader Impact:** The limitation section correctly identifies two issues: 1) control word effectiveness depends on the reward model's knowledge, and 2) the latent space mapping may not align with human intent. These are fair. The societal impact discussion (Appendix D) is generic but acceptable. The LLM disclosure (Appendix E) is appropriate.

### Overall Assessment

The paper presents a novel and promising two-pronged approach (Direct-Align + SRPO) for efficient, hack-resistant alignment of diffusion models. The core ideas are conceptually sound and the experimental results, particularly the human evaluation, are compelling and suggest a significant advance. However, the current manuscript has a major flaw: the **Method section is not clearly or completely explained**, making the proposed techniques non-reproducible and their innovations difficult to fully evaluate. Key equations are confusing, procedures are underspecified, and the critical ablation study is not interpretable. If the authors can thoroughly rewrite the Method and Ablation sections with precise definitions, clear derivations, and step-by-step algorithms, this could be a strong ICLR contribution. As it stands, the contribution is obscured by presentation issues that must be fixed.

# Neutral Reviewer
## Balanced Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a novel online reinforcement learning framework for aligning diffusion models with human preferences. The method consists of two core components: (1) **Direct-Align**, a technique that enables gradient-based optimization across the full diffusion trajectory (including early timesteps) by using a predefined noise prior to recover clean images via a closed-form interpolation, and (2) **Semantic Relative Preference Optimization (SRPO)**, which reformulates rewards as text-conditioned signals and uses the relative difference between rewards from positive and negative prompt augmentations to mitigate reward hacking and enable online preference adjustment. The authors demonstrate state-of-the-art performance on the FLUX.1.dev model, reporting significant gains in human-evaluated realism and aesthetic quality with drastically improved training efficiency.

### Strengths
1.  **Novel and Effective Technical Contributions:** The core ideas of Direct-Align (leveraging the forward process equation for single-step recovery) and SRPO (using relative semantic preferences) are clearly motivated and address well-known, significant limitations in the field (reward hacking, expensive multi-step denoising, lack of online adjustment). The proposed closed-form solution for image recovery (Eq. 4-5) is a clever and practical insight.
2.  **Comprehensive and Rigorous Evaluation:** The paper includes an extensive experimental suite, comparing against multiple strong baselines (ReFL, DRaFT, DanceGRPO) using both automated metrics (Aesthetic, PickScore, HPS, GenEval) and a detailed, multi-dimensional human evaluation. The human study is particularly robust, involving multiple annotators and experts, and focuses on critical aspects like realism and artifact presence. The reported ~3.7x improvement in realism is a compelling result.
3.  **Exceptional Efficiency:** The reported training time of 10 minutes on 32 H20 GPUs (or 5.3 GPU hours) to fine-tune FLUX.1.dev, coupled with the claim of a 75x speedup over DanceGRPO, represents a major practical advancement. This addresses a key barrier to the widespread application of online RL for diffusion models.
4.  **Strong Ablation Studies:** The paper systematically ablates key components (early vs. late timestep optimization, the discount factor, inversion vs. direct reward, style control words), providing clear evidence for the necessity of each part of the proposed framework. Figure 5 effectively visualizes the denoising efficiency and reward hacking phenomena.

### Weaknesses
1.  **Limited Exploration of Reward Model Generalization:** While the cross-reward experiment in Appendix A shows consistent trends, the primary validation is heavily centered on the HPSv2.1 reward model. A more thorough analysis of SRPO's performance and potential limitations when integrated with a wider variety of reward models (especially non-CLIP-based or non-human-preference-tuned models) would strengthen the claim of general applicability. The extension to the unimodal aesthetic predictor is a good start but briefly described.
2.  **Clarity and Detail in Algorithmic Description:** Some parts of the method could be explained with greater precision. For instance, the exact process of "multiple noise injections" to create the sequence `{x_k, ..., x_{k-n}}` and the specific form of the decaying discount factor `λ(t)` in Eq. 6 are not fully detailed. The distinction and implementation between the "inversion-based regularization" and the direct SRP reward (Eq. 8) could be clearer.
3.  **Dependence on Specific Hardware and Model:** The efficiency claims are tied to NVIDIA H20 GPUs, which are not universally accessible, making direct reproducibility of the exact throughput challenging for some researchers. Furthermore, the entire study is built upon the FLUX.1.dev model; while this is a strong choice, demonstrating the method's efficacy on other open-source diffusion or flow matching architectures (e.g., SD3, Stable Diffusion) would better establish its generality.
4.  **Ambiguity in Baseline Comparisons:** The note that ReFL and DRaFT results are from the authors' own implementations (`*`) introduces a potential confound. While common, a more detailed justification of the implementation choices or a comparison against officially reported numbers (if available) would ensure a completely fair comparison.

### Novelty & Significance
**Novelty:** The work is highly novel. Direct-Align provides a fresh perspective on circumventing the computational graph of iterative samplers by exploiting the forward process, enabling full-trajectory optimization—a clear advancement over prior work limited to late timesteps. SRPO's formulation of reward as a text-conditioned, relative signal is a novel and intuitive way to achieve online preference steering without fine-tuning the reward model itself, differentiating it from concurrent works like ICTHP or HPSv3 that focus on dataset/reward model refinement.

**Significance:** The significance is substantial for the ICLR community. The paper tackles central, unsolved problems in aligning large generative models (reward hacking, efficiency). The demonstrated dramatic improvements in output realism and aesthetics, achieved with remarkable efficiency, represent a concrete step towards more reliable and controllable high-quality image generation. The method has clear practical implications for both research and industry applications.

### Suggestions for Improvement
1.  **Expand Reward Model Analysis:** Include a dedicated subsection or table quantitatively comparing SRPO's performance when fine-tuning with at least 3-4 distinct reward models (e.g., PickScore, ImageReward, Aesthetic Predictor, CLIP). Discuss any observed variations in convergence, stability, or final quality to better characterize the method's robustness.
2.  **Improve Methodological Clarity:** In a revised methodology section, provide pseudo-code for the full SRPO training loop. Explicitly define the construction of the timestep sequence and the functional form of `λ(t)`. Clearly delineate the two training modes (using inversion vs. direct SRP reward) and under what conditions each is used.
3.  **Demonstrate Architecture Generality:** To bolster claims of general applicability, add a small-scale experiment applying SRPO (even if just for a few hundred steps) to another popular base model (e.g., Stable Diffusion XL). This would convincingly show the method is not overly tailored to FLUX's specific architecture.
4.  **Clarify Baseline Implementation and Cost Metrics:** Provide a short description of the key modifications made in the author-implemented baselines. Furthermore, to make efficiency claims more universally interpretable, report computational cost in a hardware-agnostic metric like total FLOPs or a normalized metric (e.g., GPU-hours on A100 equivalents) alongside the specific H20 numbers.

# Spark Finder Review
## How to Improve This Paper

### Missing Experiments (top 3-5 only)
1. **Ablation of Direct-Align’s interpolation mechanism vs. standard multi-step denoising.** The paper claims Direct-Align avoids gradient explosion and enables early-step optimization, but it never compares gradient norms, training stability, or reward hacking when using standard DDIM/ODE solvers for backpropagation. Without this, it’s unclear whether the proposed interpolation is truly necessary or superior.
2. **Direct comparison with offline reward fine-tuning (e.g., ICTHP).** The paper claims SRPO reduces reliance on offline reward fine-tuning, but does not compare against a baseline that first fine-tunes HPSv2 on high-quality data and then applies a standard online RL method. This is critical to validate the claim of avoiding costly offline adaptation.
3. **Evaluation of diversity and mode collapse.** The paper reports gains in realism and aesthetics but does not measure diversity (e.g., FID, LPIPS) or text-image alignment (CLIPScore) on a broad, neutral benchmark like COCO. Overfitting to HPDv2’s narrow concepts could harm generalization; this omission undermines claims of state-of-the-art performance.
4. **Ablation on the choice of negative prompts in SRPO.** The method uses “CG Render” as a negative prompt for realism, but no study shows how sensitive results are to this choice or whether arbitrary negative prompts can introduce new biases. This is essential to trust the semantic relative preference mechanism.
5. **Comparison with preference optimization methods that do not require differentiable rewards (e.g., DPO, KTO).** The paper only compares to online RL methods with differentiable rewards. To position SRPO’s contribution, it should be benchmarked against state-of-the-art preference optimization techniques that also aim to mitigate reward hacking.

### Deeper Analysis Needed (top 3-5 only)
1. **Analysis of where and why reward hacking is reduced.** The paper claims SRPO mitigates reward hacking, but there is no analysis of reward score distributions (e.g., HPS) over training steps for SRPO vs. baselines. Showing that SRPO’s reward increases correlate with human judgment—while baselines’ rewards diverge—would substantiate the claim.
2. **Investigation of the inversion-based regularization’s effect.** The inversion mechanism is mentioned but not analyzed: does it actually penalize high-frequency artifacts? A frequency-domain analysis of images before/after inversion regularization would show whether it truly targets reward hacking at late timesteps.
3. **Breakdown of human evaluation results by prompt category.** The human evaluation aggregates scores across four HPDv2 subcategories. Reporting results per category (e.g., photo, painting) would reveal whether improvements are uniform or skewed by certain styles, which is critical given the method’s reliance on prompt augmentation.
4. **Quantification of the “online adjustment” capability.** The paper claims SRPO enables online reward adjustment via prompt augmentation, but there is no measure of how quickly or reliably the model adapts to new control words (e.g., a user-defined aesthetic). A study measuring convergence speed and success rate for unseen control words would validate this claim.

### Visualizations & Case Studies
1. **Visualization of gradient norms or optimization trajectories across timesteps.** To prove Direct-Align stabilizes early-step training, plot gradient norms vs. timestep for SRPO vs. ReFL/DRaFT. This would visually confirm the avoidance of gradient explosion.
2. **Side-by-side comparisons of failure cases for all baselines.** The paper shows successes but does not systematically display where SRPO fails (e.g., for low-frequency control words like “Cyberpunk”). Including a gallery of failures would honestly convey limitations and guide future work.
3. **t-SNE or PCA of image embeddings before/after SRPO training.** This would reveal whether the model’s output distribution has shifted meaningfully toward realism (or other target attributes) and away from artifact-prone regions of the latent space.
4. **Visualization of the effect of the discount factor λ(t).** Show how images change when λ(t) is removed or altered, illustrating how late-timestep discounting affects texture detail and reward hacking artifacts.

### Obvious Next Steps
1. **Apply SRPO to other state-of-the-art diffusion/flow matching models (e.g., SD3, Sora).** The paper only uses FLUX.1-dev; demonstrating effectiveness on other architectures would strengthen the claim of general applicability.
2. **Extend SRPO to video generation.** Given that reward hacking is also a critical issue in video models, applying SRPO to video diffusion would be a natural and impactful extension.
3. **Integrate SRPO with a learnable prompt embedding mechanism.** The paper notes that control words outside the reward model’s domain are less effective. A clear next step is to replace fixed prompts with learnable embeddings optimized alongside the diffusion model.
4. **Conduct a large-scale, crowd-sourced human evaluation (e.g., on MTurk).** The current human evaluation uses only 10 annotators and 500 prompts. Scaling to thousands of ratings from diverse raters would provide more statistically robust evidence of superiority.

# Final Consolidated Review
## Summary
This paper proposes a novel framework for aligning diffusion models with human preferences, comprising two main components. First, Direct-Align enables gradient-based optimization across the full diffusion trajectory by using a predefined noise prior to recover clean images via a closed-form interpolation, avoiding the computational cost of multi-step denoising. Second, Semantic Relative Preference Optimization (SRPO) reformulates rewards as text-conditioned signals and uses the relative difference between rewards from positive and negative prompt augmentations to mitigate reward hacking and enable online preference adjustment. The method demonstrates substantial improvements in human-evaluated realism and aesthetics when fine-tuning the FLUX.1.dev model, with exceptional training efficiency.

## Strengths
- **Novel and Effective Technical Contributions:** The core ideas are well-motivated and address significant, known limitations. Direct-Align's exploitation of the forward process for single-step recovery is a clever, practical insight. SRPO's use of relative semantic preferences provides a novel mechanism for online reward adjustment and bias mitigation without fine-tuning the reward model itself.
- **Compelling Empirical Results with Rigorous Human Evaluation:** The paper provides extensive experiments against strong baselines. Most notably, it includes a detailed, multi-dimensional human evaluation study that demonstrates dramatic improvements in perceived realism and reduction of artifacts, substantiating the paper's primary claims. The qualitative results (e.g., Figures 3, 7) visually support these gains.
- **Exceptional Training Efficiency:** The method achieves reported convergence in approximately 10 minutes on 32 H20 GPUs (5.3 GPU hours), representing a 75x speedup over a strong baseline (DanceGRPO). This addresses a major practical barrier to applying online RL for diffusion model alignment.

## Weaknesses
- **Critical Lack of Clarity in Method Description:** The methodology is not fully reproducible due to underspecified details and undefined variables. Key terms (e.g., `K`, `Δσ_t` in Equations 5 and 9) are introduced without definition. The process for generating the timestep sequence `{x_k, ..., x_{k-n}}` and the precise form of the discount factor `λ(t)` (Eq. 6) are not clearly explained. The ablation study in Figure 7(D) uses cryptic labels ("w/o early timestep opt.", "Inversion") that are not adequately defined in the text, making it impossible to interpret this crucial validation of the core components.
- **Incomplete Validation Against Offline Reward Adaptation Baselines:** A central claim is that SRPO "reduces the reliance on offline reward fine-tuning." However, the paper does not compare against a strong baseline that first fine-tunes the reward model on high-quality data (e.g., as done in concurrent works like ICTHP) and then applies a standard online RL method. This comparison is necessary to fully substantiate the claim of avoiding costly offline preparation.
- **Limited Analysis of Output Diversity and Generalization:** The evaluation focuses heavily on the HPDv2 benchmark. There is no assessment of how the fine-tuned model's diversity (e.g., via FID, LPIPS) or its text-image alignment on a broader, neutral dataset (like COCO) is affected. Overfitting to the four narrow visual concepts in HPDv2 could harm generalization, which is not measured.
- **Statistical Reporting for Human Evaluation Could Be Stronger:** While the human evaluation design is detailed, the paper does not report inter-annotator agreement statistics. The claim of "3.7x/3.1x improvement" from the abstract, while visually supported by the bar charts, is not explicitly derived or quantified in the results section, making it difficult to verify precisely.

## Nice-to-Haves
- **Demonstration on Additional Model Architectures:** Showing the method's efficacy on another popular open-source model (e.g., Stable Diffusion XL or SD3), even at a smaller scale, would strengthen the claim of general applicability beyond FLUX.1.dev.
- **Deeper Analysis of the SRPO Mechanism:** A quantitative analysis of reward score distributions over training for SRPO versus baselines could more concretely demonstrate how and when reward hacking is mitigated. Similarly, a frequency-domain analysis of images could help validate the claimed effect of the inversion-based regularization on high-frequency artifacts.
- **Hardware-Agnostic Efficiency Metric:** Reporting computational cost in a more universal metric (e.g., FLOPs or A100-equivalent GPU hours) alongside the H20-specific numbers would improve reproducibility and comparison for researchers with different hardware.

## Novel Insights
The paper introduces two genuinely novel insights. First, it recognizes that the standard diffusion forward process equation provides a closed-form solution for recovering a clean image from any noisy state if the ground-truth noise is known, enabling stable, full-trajectory optimization—a conceptual shift from prior work that struggled with early timesteps. Second, it reconceptualizes the reward signal not as an absolute score but as a text-conditional, *relative* measure between semantically opposed prompts. This reframing directly attacks reward model bias by constructing an optimization direction that cancels out text-agnostic artifacts (e.g., color casts) while preserving desired semantic attributes, offering a new pathway for online control.
