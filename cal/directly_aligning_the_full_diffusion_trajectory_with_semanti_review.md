# Harsh Critic Review
## Section-by-Section Critical Review

**Title & Abstract**  
The title accurately reflects the core contributions: aligning the full diffusion trajectory and semantic relative preference optimization. The abstract clearly states the two main challenges addressed and summarizes the proposed methods (Direct-Align, SRPO) and their benefits. However, the claim of “over 3x improvement” in human-evaluated realism and aesthetic quality is ambiguous—it is unclear what baseline this refers to and how the metric is defined (e.g., is it a multiplicative increase in the percentage of “Excellent” ratings?). This ambiguity should be resolved to avoid overstatement.

**Introduction**  
The introduction effectively motivates the problem of aligning diffusion models with human preferences and identifies two key limitations of existing methods: restriction to late diffusion steps (leading to reward hacking) and the lack of online reward adjustment. The contributions are clearly listed. However, the claim that existing methods suffer from “gradient explosion” when backpropagating through early timesteps is not supported by citations or evidence. Additionally, the description of Direct-Align as “injecting predefined noise into the clean image” is intuitive but lacks a precise explanation of how this avoids optimization instability.

**Related Work**  
This section adequately covers prior work on diffusion timestep optimization and reward model refinement. It correctly positions the paper’s contributions relative to existing literature. However, the discussion of why direct backpropagation struggles with early timesteps could be more thorough, perhaps referencing specific analyses from prior work.

**Method**  
*Direct-Align*: The idea of using a ground-truth noise prior to enable single-step recovery from any timestep is novel and well-motivated. However, the exposition has several unclear points. Equation (5) is ambiguous: the term “(σ_t − Δσ)ε” likely refers to ground-truth noise ε_gt, but the subscript is missing. The reward aggregation framework (Eq. 6) introduces a discount factor λ(t) that is not defined, and the summation limits are confusing (i from k to k-n?). The pipeline in Figure 2 suggests that a clean image x₀ is generated by the model, then noise is injected and recovered—this process needs clearer explanation, especially regarding how gradients flow and how this enables optimization at early timesteps.

*Semantic-Relative Preference Optimization (SRPO)*: The concept of using text-conditioned reward differences (C₁ - C₂) to steer preferences is innovative and addresses reward hacking. However, the method assumes the reward model is CLIP-based (i.e., reward = f_img(x)ᵀ ⋅ C); it should be clarified whether this holds for all reward models used (HPS, PickScore, etc.). The “inversion-based regularization” is mentioned briefly but lacks detail: how exactly are denoising and inversion directions used to decouple penalization and reward? The integration of Direct-Align and SRPO into a unified training objective is not explicitly presented.

**Experiments**  
*Evaluation Protocol*: The use of multiple automatic metrics (Aesthetic, PickScore, etc.) and a detailed human evaluation protocol (with criteria in Table 2) is commendable. However, including the proposed SRP metric (based on HPS) as an automatic evaluation may be circular, since it relies on the same reward model used for training. The human evaluation involves 10 annotators and experts, but inter-rater agreement statistics are not reported, which is important for assessing reliability.

*Main Results*: Table 1 shows that SRPO achieves the best scores on Aesthetic and PickScore but underperforms on ImageReward and HPS compared to some baselines. The authors interpret this as “immunity to HPS score inflation,” but the claim of “superior performance across multiple reward metrics” is selective. The human evaluation results (Figure 4) indicate significant improvements, with the “excellent” rate for realism increasing from 8.2% to 38.9% (a 4.7× increase, not 3.7× as stated elsewhere). This discrepancy should be clarified. The comparison with FLUX.1.Krea (Figure 7a) is interesting but not entirely fair, as Krea is a different model with potential architectural changes.

*Ablation Study*: The ablations on denoising efficiency, optimization timestep, and components of Direct-Align are informative but somewhat qualitative. For instance, the “hacking rate” study (Figure 5) asks annotators to mark the worst image, but the number of annotators and statistical significance are not provided. The style-control experiments demonstrate that SRPO can steer generation, but the effectiveness depends on the reward model’s familiarity with the style word—this is expected but not a strong ablation of SRPO’s core mechanism.

**Conclusion**  
The conclusion succinctly summarizes the contributions and acknowledges limitations (controllability dependent on reward model, interpretability issues) and future work. This is appropriate.

**Reproducibility Statement**  
The statement that source code and hyperparameters will be provided is good, but no link is included in the submitted text. ICLR expects code and data to be available for reproducibility; the authors should ensure this is provided upon submission.

**Appendices**  
The cross-reward experiments (Appendix A) show consistent improvements across different reward models, which strengthens the generalizability claim. The comparison to GRPO (Appendix B) highlights advantages but lacks quantitative convergence curves. The word-frequency statistics (Appendix C) help explain why some control words work better than others.

**Writing & Clarity**  
The paper is generally well-written, but the method section suffers from ambiguous notation and incomplete descriptions. Several equations have parsing artifacts or missing definitions (e.g., Eq. 5, Eq. 6). The flow of the algorithm could be presented more clearly, perhaps with a step-by-step training pseudocode.

**Limitations & Broader Impact**  
The limitations section appropriately notes that controllability depends on the reward model’s ability to recognize control words, and interpretability is limited due to latent-space manipulations. Societal impacts are not discussed; while not mandatory, a brief consideration of potential misuse (e.g., generating deceptive content) would be beneficial.

## Overall Assessment
The paper presents two novel contributions—Direct-Align, which enables optimization across the full diffusion trajectory, and SRPO, which allows online reward adjustment via prompt augmentation—that address significant limitations in existing preference alignment methods. The ideas are innovative and could have substantial impact on text-to-image generation. The experimental evaluation is extensive, with both automatic metrics and human studies, and shows promising results, particularly in improving realism and aesthetic quality.

However, the paper has notable weaknesses in clarity and empirical rigor. The method description is often ambiguous, with undefined terms and incomplete explanations of key mechanisms (e.g., inversion regularization, discount factor). The empirical claims are sometimes overstated or inconsistent (e.g., “over 3x” improvement, selective reporting of metrics). The human evaluation, while thorough, lacks statistical validation (inter-rater agreement, confidence intervals).

For ICLR, where clarity, reproducibility, and rigorous evaluation are paramount, these issues are significant. The core contribution is strong, but the paper requires major revisions to clarify the methodology, provide complete experimental details, and present claims more precisely. With these revisions, the paper could meet ICLR’s acceptance bar.

# Neutral Reviewer
## Balanced Review

### Summary
This paper introduces a novel online reinforcement learning (RL) framework for aligning text-to-image diffusion models with human preferences. The core contributions are: 1) **Direct-Align**, a method that enables efficient gradient-based optimization across the full diffusion trajectory (including early, noisy timesteps) by analytically recovering clean images via a fixed noise prior, and 2) **Semantic Relative Preference Optimization (SRPO)**, which formulates rewards as text-conditioned signals derived from the relative difference between positive and negative prompt augmentations, reducing reliance on offline reward model fine-tuning. The method is evaluated by fine-tuning the FLUX.1.dev model, reporting significant improvements in human-evaluated realism and aesthetic quality with dramatically reduced training time.

### Strengths
1.  **Strong Empirical Results:** The paper presents compelling quantitative and qualitative results. Human evaluation shows a ~3.7x improvement in realism and ~3.1x in aesthetic quality over the FLUX.1.dev baseline (Fig. 4, Sec. 4.2). The method also achieves high scores on automatic metrics (Aesthetic, PickScore) while avoiding reward score inflation, indicating it mitigates reward hacking (Table 1).
2.  **Notable Efficiency:** A key claimed advantage is computational efficiency. The method converges in "10 minutes" on 32 H20 GPUs, which is framed as a 75x speedup over the DanceGRPO baseline (Sec. 4.2, Table 1). This addresses a major practical bottleneck in diffusion model alignment.
3.  **Innovative Core Ideas:** The two main technical ideas are novel and address clear limitations in prior work. Direct-Align tackles the problem of unstable/expensive multi-step backpropagation, and SRPO offers a clever mechanism for online reward adjustment and bias mitigation without requiring new reward model training data.
4.  **Comprehensive Evaluation:** The evaluation is extensive, using multiple automatic metrics (Aesthetic, PickScore, ImageReward, HPS, GenEval, DeQA) and a detailed, multi-dimensional human evaluation protocol with trained annotators (Table 2, Sec. 4.1). The cross-reward experiments in the appendix (Fig. 8, 9) demonstrate generalization.

### Weaknesses
1.  **Limited Technical Depth and Justification:** The explanation of Direct-Align, while intuitive, lacks rigorous derivation or theoretical grounding. The choice of the discount factor λ(t) and the specific aggregation scheme in Eq. (6) are not well-motivated or ablated. The claim that inversion-based regularization "decouples penalization" (Sec. 3.2) is vague and not clearly demonstrated.
2.  **Controllability is Weak and Under-Analyzed:** The paper's second contribution is "online reward adjustment," but the controllability results are mixed. The method's effectiveness is explicitly stated to depend on the reward model's prior knowledge of the control words (Sec. 4.3, Fig. 11). For low-frequency styles (e.g., Cyberpunk), performance is limited, and for some styles, the control word must still be used at inference (Fig. 6, 7c). This limitation is acknowledged but not deeply analyzed, weakening the claim of flexible online control.
3.  **Insufficient Comparison and Positioning:** The comparison to Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)-based methods like DanceGRPO focuses heavily on efficiency but provides limited insight into qualitative trade-offs. The discussion in Appendix B is brief, and the claim that SRPO "eliminates the reliance on the diversity of the generative model" is not substantiated. A more detailed comparison of the optimization dynamics, sample efficiency, and final performance Pareto frontier would be more informative.
4.  **Clarity and Presentation Issues:** The paper is densely written and would benefit from clearer scaffolding. The relationship between Direct-Align and SRPO is sometimes confusing (e.g., Sec. 3.2 introduces "inversion-based regularization" without a clear connection to SRP). Figure 5 is critical (showing ablation of timestep optimization) but is poorly integrated into the main text narrative.

### Novelty & Significance
**Novelty:** The work is novel. Direct-Align's approach to full-trajectory optimization via a fixed noise prior and analytic recovery is a distinct solution to the computational challenge. SRPO's formulation of a relative reward from positive/negative text conditioning is a fresh take on mitigating reward model bias, moving beyond simple reward averaging or fine-tuning.
**Significance:** The results are significant if reproducible. Achieving substantial gains in human-perceived quality with orders-of-magnitude less compute directly addresses pressing problems in aligning large generative models. The potential to steer models with text prompts without costly reward retraining is highly desirable for practical applications.
**Clarity:** Moderate. The core ideas are understandable, but the methodological details and ablations require more careful explanation and visualization to be fully accessible.
**Reproducibility:** Likely high. The paper includes a reproducibility statement, promises code, uses open-source models (FLUX) and datasets (HPDv2). However, the reliance on the proprietary FLUX.1.dev as a base model and specific hardware (H20 GPUs) could be minor barriers.

### Suggestions for Improvement
1.  **Deepen the Methodological Analysis:** Provide a more formal justification for the Direct-Align update (Eq. 5) and the reward aggregation framework (Eq. 6). Include an ablation study systematically varying the discount schedule λ(t) and the number of aggregated steps `n` to justify the design choices.
2.  **Strengthen the Controllability Narrative:** Conduct a more systematic analysis of the controllability failure modes. Quantify the correlation between control word frequency in the reward model's training distribution (as hinted in Fig. 11) and the success rate of style application. Explore and discuss potential solutions, such as using ensembles of related words (as done for "Comic") more formally.
3.  **Enhance Comparison with GRPO:** Expand Appendix B into a proper subsection in the main paper. Directly compare sample efficiency (quality vs. number of gradient steps), final performance limits, and robustness to hyperparameters between SRPO and DanceGRPO, moving beyond just final metric scores and training time.
4.  **Improve Presentation and Clarity:** Re-structure the method section to more cleanly separate the two components. Use a diagram or clearer text to illustrate the "inversion-based regularization" concept. Integrate the critical ablation figure (Fig. 5) into the main experimental results flow, explaining what each panel demonstrates about the claims.

# Spark Finder Review
## How to Improve This Paper

### Missing Experiments (top 3-5 only)
1. **Cross-dataset evaluation for generalization.** The method is validated only on HPDv2 (a narrow, style-heavy dataset). To substantiate claims of broad applicability and reduced reward hacking, experiments on diverse datasets (e.g., COCO, DrawBench, Parti Prompts) are essential. Without this, the performance improvements may be dataset-specific.
2. **Comparison against modern Preference Optimization baselines.** The paper compares against older Online-RL methods (ReFL, DRaFT) but omits strong contemporary baselines like DPO/IPO for diffusion models or methods like DMD. For an ICLR claim of SOTA, benchmarking against the current preference alignment landscape is mandatory.
3. **Ablation on the `K` and discount factor `λ(t)`.** The core equations (5,6) introduce new hyperparameters (∆σ_t, λ(t)). A systematic ablation quantifying their impact on stability and reward hacking is missing. Their optimal settings are presented as a black box, undermining the technical contribution.
4. **Quantitative analysis of "reward hacking" mitigation.** The claim of mitigating hacking is supported by qualitative examples and a human "hacking rate." A quantitative analysis—e.g., measuring distribution shift in color histograms, texture spectra, or KL divergence from the base model—is needed for a convincing, evidence-based claim.

### Deeper Analysis Needed (top 3-5 only)
1. **Analysis of what the SRP reward actually optimizes.** The text-conditioned reward difference (`C1 - C2`) is central but its effect is not dissected. An analysis of the embedding space (e.g., visualizing how `C_realistic - C_CG` directions correlate with image features) is needed to trust that it captures semantic intent rather than arbitrary CLIP biases.
2. **Failure mode analysis for style control.** The paper notes reduced effectiveness for styles like "Cyberpunk" due to low frequency in the reward model's training data. A thorough analysis of the relationship between control word frequency, reward model sensitivity, and final controllability is critical to understand the method's real-world limits.
3. **Gradient stability and explosion analysis.** A key motivation is avoiding gradient explosion in early timesteps. However, there is no quantitative evidence (e.g., gradient norm plots across timesteps) comparing Direct-Align to baselines like ReFL. This leaves the core engineering claim unsupported.

### Visualizations & Case Studies
1. **Side-by-side trajectory visualizations.** To validate that Direct-Align enables effective early-timestep optimization, show a grid comparing image *trajectories* (from noise to clean) during optimization for SRPO vs. ReFL/DRaFT. This would visually demonstrate where and how gradients are applied effectively.
2. **Case studies of clear failures.** Include a panel of generated images where SRPO fails—either producing worse results than the base model or manifesting new artifacts. This is necessary to honestly bound the method's capabilities and for reviewers to assess its robustness.
3. **Visualization of the "negative gradient" effect.** For SRP, visualize the image regions/features that receive positive vs. negative gradients (e.g., via saliency maps) when using `C1` and `C2`. This would concretely show how the relative reward provides regularization.

### Obvious Next Steps
1. **Integrate a learnable prompt/prompt tuning.** The limitation regarding ineffective control words points directly to an obvious extension: making the control token(s) learnable embeddings, optimized alongside the model to better align with the reward's latent space. A preliminary experiment here would significantly strengthen the paper.
2. **Apply the framework to other reward model families.** The experiments use CLIP-based rewards. A logical next step is to apply SRPO to dense/reference-based rewards (e.g., SDXL-Refiner, MPS) or ensemble rewards to show generalizability beyond similarity-based scores.
3. **Formalize the connection to Contrastive Preference Optimization.** The SRP mechanism is reminiscent of contrastive learning. Explicitly framing it within existing preference optimization theory (e.g., as a form of contrastive loss) and comparing it theoretically to DPO/CPO would elevate the methodological contribution.

# Final Consolidated Review
## Summary
This paper introduces Direct-Align and Semantic Relative Preference Optimization (SRPO), a framework for aligning diffusion models with human preferences. Direct-Align enables gradient-based optimization across the full diffusion trajectory (including early, noisy timesteps) by analytically recovering clean images via a fixed noise prior. SRPO formulates rewards as text-conditioned signals derived from the relative difference between positive and negative prompt augmentations, reducing reliance on offline reward model fine-tuning. Experiments fine-tuning FLUX.1.dev show significant gains in human-judged realism and aesthetic quality with dramatically reduced training time.

## Strengths
- **Demonstrates strong empirical gains with high efficiency.** Human evaluation reports a ~3.7x improvement in realism and ~3.1x in aesthetic quality over the baseline FLUX.1.dev model. The method converges in ~10 minutes on 32 H20 GPUs, framed as a 75x speedup over the DanceGRPO baseline (Table 1, Sec. 4.2), addressing a major practical bottleneck.
- **Introduces two novel, well-motivated technical ideas.** Direct-Align tackles the computational challenge of full-trajectory optimization via a fixed noise prior and analytic recovery. SRPO's relative reward formulation from positive/negative text conditioning offers a fresh mechanism for online reward adjustment and bias mitigation without requiring new reward model training data.

## Weaknesses
- **Methodological clarity and justification need improvement.** Key components are ambiguously presented: Equation 5 has unclear notation (the term `(σ_t − Δσ)ε` lacks a subscript), and the discount factor `λ(t)` in Eq. 6 is not defined or motivated. The description of "inversion-based regularization" is vague and not clearly integrated with SRPO.
- **Controllability is limited and under-analyzed.** The effectiveness of SRPO's "online reward adjustment" is explicitly dependent on the reward model's prior knowledge of the control words (Sec. 4.3, Fig. 11). For low-frequency styles (e.g., Cyberpunk), performance is limited, and the paper lacks a systematic analysis correlating control word frequency, reward model sensitivity, and success rate, weakening the claim of flexible control.
- **Evaluation scope is narrow.** Validation is performed only on the HPDv2 dataset. To substantiate claims of broad applicability and reduced reward hacking, evaluation on more diverse datasets (e.g., COCO, DrawBench) is needed. Furthermore, comparisons are made against older Online-RL methods (ReFL, DRaFT) but omit strong contemporary preference optimization baselines (e.g., DPO/IPO for diffusion), leaving the SOTA claim incompletely substantiated.

## Nice-to-Haves
- A more formal justification for the Direct-Align update and reward aggregation framework, including an ablation study on the discount schedule `λ(t)` and the number of aggregated steps.
- Quantitative analysis of gradient stability (e.g., gradient norm plots across timesteps) to directly support the claim that Direct-Align avoids optimization instability in early timesteps.

## Novel Insights
The core novelty lies in the conjunction of two ideas: 1) using a fixed, ground-truth noise prior to analytically recover a clean image from any timestep, which decouples the reconstruction path from the computational graph and enables efficient, stable optimization across the full diffusion trajectory; and 2) reformulating the reward as the relative difference between text-conditioned embeddings (`C1 - C2`), which leverages the reward model's own text encoder to derive a semantic guidance signal that can be steered via prompt augmentation. This provides a pathway for online preference adjustment without costly reward model retraining.

## Suggestions
- Clarify the methodological presentation by defining all terms in Equations 5 and 6, and provide a clearer, step-by-step explanation of how Direct-Align and SRPO are integrated into a unified training objective.
- Conduct a systematic analysis of the controllability failure modes, quantifying the correlation between control word frequency (as hinted in Appendix C) and the success rate of style application, and discuss potential mitigations.
