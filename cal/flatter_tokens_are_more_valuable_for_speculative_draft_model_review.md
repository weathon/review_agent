# Harsh Critic Review
## Section-by-Section Critical Review

### Title & Abstract
The title directly reflects the core finding: flatter target token distributions are more valuable for training draft models in speculative decoding. The abstract clearly states the problem, the key insight, the proposed metric (flatness) and method (SFDD), and quantifies the results (over 2× training speedup using 50% data, inference speedup within 4% of full-dataset baseline). All claims are specific and appear supported by the content. This meets ICLR standards.

### Introduction & Motivation
The introduction effectively motivates the problem: training draft models for speculative decoding is computationally expensive, and current methods treat all training data equally. It identifies a discrepancy between standard knowledge distillation (KL-divergence) and the SD objective (maximizing acceptance rate, linked to L1-norm). It argues that simply changing the loss function is insufficient and that a data-centric view is needed. The contributions are clearly listed. The motivation is strong and well-aligned with improving training efficiency for SD.

### Preliminaries & Theoretical Analysis (Sections 3 and 3.2)
The paper clearly defines speculative decoding and acceptance rate. The theoretical analysis models a single knowledge distillation step as a budget-constrained move of the draft distribution toward the target. Using Gaussian distributions for tractability, it shows that the reduction in L1 distance (∆L1) increases with the target distribution's variance, implying flatter targets are more valuable. The analysis is simplified but provides a clear, interpretable insight. The link to discrete token distributions is made via cosine similarity to uniform, justified in Appendix B. A minor concern: the Gaussian assumption is a strong simplification; while the paper validates the insight empirically, a brief discussion of its limitations (e.g., real token distributions are discrete, multimodal) would strengthen the section. The derivation and simulation (Figure 1) are sound.

### The Proposed Approach (Section 4)
**4.1 Empirical Validation:** The definition of flatness (cosine similarity to uniform) is practical. The empirical validation on real LLMs (using a small set of 10 samples) shows that tokens with high target flatness exhibit larger changes in draft statistics and ∆L1. Figure 2 is compelling. The comparison with entropy (Figure 2d) demonstrates flatness's superiority in filtering low-value tokens. However, the validation is based on only 10 samples; a larger-scale analysis would increase confidence.  
**4.2 & 4.3 Sample-Level Selection and SFDD:** The transition from token-level to sample-level via averaging is straightforward. The SFDD pipeline (Figure 3) is simple and practical. The method requires only a single forward pass of the target model for scoring, making overhead negligible. The approach is well-motivated by the theoretical and token-level insights.

### Experiments & Results (Section 5)
**5.1 Setup:** The experimental setup is clear and appropriate: EAGLE-2 framework, LLaMA3-8B-Instruct target, ShareGPT data, five diverse evaluation tasks, and comprehensive baselines (including random and several importance metrics). Metrics (speedup, average acceptance length, training time) are standard.  
**5.2 Main Results:** Table 1 shows SFDD outperforms all baselines at 50% retain ratio, achieving an average speedup of 2.41× vs. 2.49× for full data (within 4%). The results are consistent across tasks, demonstrating robustness.  
**5.3 Ablation Study:** Tables 2 and 3 show SFDD consistently beats random and top-1 probability across retain ratios (30%-70% and extreme ratios 5%-20%). The performance gap persists even at low retain ratios, and at 70% retain ratio SFDD nearly matches full-dataset performance. This is strong evidence for the method's effectiveness.  
**5.4 Training Efficiency:** Figure 4 shows training time scales linearly with data size, and SFDD achieves a 2.02× training speedup at 50% retain ratio. The inclusion of selection overhead is appropriate.  
**Appendix Results:** Additional experiments (temperature=0, other model families, different datasets) support generalizability. The analysis of token saturation (Appendix F.5) quantitatively supports the claim that low-flatness tokens saturate early.

**Overall Experimental Strength:** The experiments are comprehensive, testing multiple retain ratios, baselines, and tasks. The results consistently support the claims. A minor weakness is the lack of statistical significance testing (e.g., error bars or multiple runs), though the performance gaps are large and consistent.

### Writing & Clarity
The paper is well-written and logically structured. Figures are informative, though some have minor parser-induced formatting artifacts (e.g., "Minimal c h ange" in Figure 2) that do not impede understanding. The theoretical derivations in appendices are detailed. The flow from theory to empirical validation to application is clear.

### Limitations & Broader Impact
The paper acknowledges some limitations in the appendices (e.g., Gaussian assumption, token-level filtering impracticality, analysis on small sample for validation). However, a dedicated limitations section in the main paper would improve completeness. Potential limitations to discuss include: the static target model assumption (what if the target model is updated?), sensitivity to temperature (flatness is temperature-dependent), and generalization to other draft model architectures or training frameworks. The broader impact statement is standard and appropriate, noting the positive effect of reducing computational costs.

## Overall Assessment
This paper makes a clear and valuable contribution: it identifies that flatter target token distributions are more valuable for training draft models in speculative decoding, proposes a simple and effective flatness metric, and introduces SFDD, a data selection method that significantly improves training efficiency (2× speedup with 50% data) while preserving inference performance (within 4% of full-data baseline). The theoretical analysis, though simplified, provides useful intuition and is well-supported by empirical validation. Experiments are thorough, comparing against strong baselines across multiple datasets and retain ratios. The method is practical, with negligible overhead. The paper meets ICLR's standards for novelty, technical soundness, and empirical rigor. The main weaknesses are the small-scale empirical validation of the core insight (10 samples) and the lack of a dedicated limitations section. Nonetheless, the contribution stands as a meaningful advance in efficient draft model training for speculative decoding.

# Neutral Reviewer
## Balanced Review

### Summary
This paper introduces a data-centric approach to improve the training efficiency of draft models in Speculative Decoding (SD). The core insight is that tokens for which the target LLM produces a "flatter" (more uniform) predictive distribution are more valuable for training, as they offer greater headroom for reducing the L1 discrepancy (directly linked to acceptance rate). The authors propose a "flatness" metric (cosine similarity to the uniform distribution) and a Sample-level-flatness-based Dataset Distillation (SFDD) method that filters training data to retain high-flatness samples. Experiments within the EAGLE-2 framework show SFDD can achieve over 2× training speedup using only 50% of data while maintaining inference speedup within 4% of the full-dataset baseline.

### Strengths
1. **Clear, practical insight with theoretical grounding:** The paper identifies a clear, actionable property (target distribution flatness) that correlates with training value for SD. This is supported by a theoretical analysis using a Gaussian simplification of a single knowledge distillation step under a KL budget, showing that tokens with higher target variance yield larger reductions in the L1 distance (Eq. 2-5, Fig. 1).
2. **Strong empirical validation:** The method is extensively evaluated on five diverse tasks (GSM8K, Alpaca, MT-Bench, CNN/DM, NQ). SFDD consistently outperforms common data selection heuristics (entropy, top-1 prob, margin, etc.) at a fixed 50% retain ratio (Table 1) and shows robust performance across a wide range of retain ratios (5% to 70%), significantly beating random filtering (Tables 2, 3). The reported >2× training speedup with minimal inference performance loss is compelling.
3. **Simplicity and low overhead:** The proposed flatness metric is simple to compute (requires a single forward pass of the target model over the dataset) and integrates easily into existing pipelines (Fig. 3). The cost of data selection is shown to be negligible (~3.85% of full training time) (Appendix D).

### Weaknesses
1. **Theoretical analysis relies on strong simplifications:** The key theoretical insight derives from analyzing Gaussian distributions under a specific, idealized KL-constrained update. The translation to discrete token distributions via cosine similarity, while empirically validated, is heuristic. The analysis in Appendix F.3 shows the variance-benefit relationship is not consistent across other distribution families (Exponential, Half-normal), suggesting the finding may be somewhat specific to the Gaussian/location-scale setting.
2. **Limited exploration of the draft model's role and training dynamics:** The flatness metric depends solely on the *target* model, assuming the draft model's starting state is less relevant. The paper shows empirical training dynamics (Fig. 2, 7) but does not deeply analyze how the value of flat tokens might change as the draft model evolves or if the draft model architecture varies. The experiments are confined to one draft framework (EAGLE-2) and one primary target model family (LLaMA3-8B).
3. **Ablation on sample-level aggregation is light:** The method aggregates token-level flatness via a simple average (or median) to get sample-level scores. While Appendix G.2 shows median performs similarly, there is no exploration of more sophisticated aggregation strategies (e.g., weighting by token position, considering sequence-level properties) that might better capture a sample's training utility.

### Novelty & Significance
**Novelty:** This is the first work to systematically investigate and propose a data selection criterion specifically for improving the *training efficiency* of draft models in Speculative Decoding. It shifts the focus from loss function design (as in prior work like DistillSpec) to data-centric optimization. The conceptual link between target distribution flatness and potential L1 reduction is novel.
**Significance:** The practical impact is high. Training draft models is becoming a non-negligible cost in advanced SD systems (Appendix F.7). SFDD offers a simple, effective way to cut this cost substantially (2× speedup) with very little performance penalty, making SD more accessible and sustainable. The work opens a new direction for data-efficient training in accelerated inference.

### Suggestions for Improvement
1. **Deepen the analysis of why flatness beats entropy:** While Figure 2d and Appendix F.2 show flatness is better than entropy, a more intuitive explanation is needed. Since both metrics measure dispersion, can the authors analyze the failure cases of entropy (e.g., through examples) and explain the geometric or optimization-related advantage of the cosine-based flatness?
2. **Explore dynamic or adaptive selection strategies:** The current SFDD performs static, offline filtering. A more advanced version could adaptively select samples during training based on the draft model's current state (e.g., prioritizing samples where the draft is still far from the target despite high flatness). This could further improve efficiency.
3. **Extend experiments to more diverse SD frameworks and models:** To strengthen generalizability, evaluate SFDD on other train-based SD methods (e.g., Medusa, Lookahead Decoding) and a wider range of target model sizes and families (e.g., GPT, Mistral). Testing scalability to larger targets (e.g., 70B models) would be particularly valuable for real-world impact.

# Spark Finder Review
## How to Improve This Paper

### Missing Experiments (top 3-5 only)
1.  **Ablation on different speculative decoding frameworks.** The experiments are exclusively conducted within the EAGLE-2 pipeline. To claim the metric is a general principle for draft model training, the authors must validate SFDD on at least one other major train-based SD framework (e.g., Medusa) to show the "flatness" insight is not EAGLE-specific.
2.  **Evaluation on domain-shifted or out-of-distribution data.** The training and evaluation are on similar chat/instruction data (ShareGPT vs. Alpaca/MT-Bench). The method's robustness and the selected data's transferability should be tested by training on ShareGPT but evaluating on a truly held-out domain (e.g., code generation, scientific texts) to see if filtering harms generalization.
3.  **Rigorous control for training time savings.** The claim of "over 2× training speedup" mixes data reduction with potential batching efficiency gains. A controlled experiment is needed: compare SFDD against a baseline that trains on a *random* subset for the same number of *steps* (not epochs) to isolate the effect of data quality from pure data quantity reduction.
4.  **Comparison against more sophisticated data selection baselines.** The baselines are simple heuristics (entropy, top-1 prob). The paper should compare against modern, learning-based data selection or curriculum learning methods (e.g., gradient embedding-based selection) to establish that the simple flatness metric is surprisingly effective, not just better than naive baselines.

### Deeper Analysis Needed (top 3-5 only)
1.  **Explain why flatness outperforms entropy.** The paper shows flatness is better than entropy but provides only a superficial comparison (Fig 2d, Appendix F.2). A deeper analysis is needed: for cases where rankings differ, analyze the token distributions to explain *why* flatness is a more reliable indicator of potential ∆L1 reduction than entropy for the SD objective.
2.  **Bridge the gap between Gaussian theory and discrete LLM distributions.** The theoretical analysis relies on a Gaussian approximation. A critical analysis is missing: how well do actual LLM token distributions for "flat" and "sharp" tokens align with the Gaussian assumptions (e.g., unimodal vs. multi-modal)? A qualitative analysis of selected vs. rejected token distributions is necessary to validate the core premise.
3.  **Justify sample-level aggregation via averaging.** The jump from token-level importance to sample-level filtering by averaging flatness is not justified. The authors must analyze the distribution of token flatness *within* samples. Is a sample with a few very flat tokens and many sharp ones better than a uniformly medium-flatness sample? An ablation on different aggregation strategies (e.g., max, median, top-k%) is needed.
4.  **Analyze what is being filtered out.** The paper lacks a qualitative analysis of the *content* of high-flatness vs. low-flatness samples. Are specific types of tokens or linguistic constructs systematically filtered? This is crucial for understanding potential biases or limitations of the method (e.g., does it filter out factually certain but important tokens?).

### Visualizations & Case Studies
1.  **Show concrete examples of selected vs. rejected data.** Provide 3-5 concrete text examples where the sample was selected (high flatness) and 3-5 where it was rejected (low flatness). Annotate the token-level flatness scores within each example. This would immediately reveal if the method selects sensible, "hard-to-predict" contexts or if the selection seems arbitrary.
2.  **Visualize the training dynamics of selected vs. full dataset.** Plot the acceptance rate (or L1 norm) over training steps/epochs for SFDD (at 50% retain) vs. full training. This would show if SFDD not only reaches a similar final performance but also *learns faster*, which is a core claim. The current Fig 7 tracks gradient norms but not the actual target metric.
3.  **Case study on failure modes.** The paper only shows successes. Include a brief analysis of tasks or samples where SFDD underperforms random selection (even if minor). Visualizing the token distributions in these failure cases would be instructive for understanding the method's boundaries.

### Obvious Next Steps
1.  **Dynamic/adaptive data selection.** The current method is a static, one-time filter. An obvious and impactful next step, which should have been discussed as a limitation or future work, is to integrate flatness into a dynamic curriculum or active learning loop where the training set is re-scored periodically as the draft model improves.
2.  **Application to other draft model architectures.** The paper should have tested if the flatness principle holds for different draft model designs (e.g., a larger but shallower draft model, or an early-exit style drafter) beyond the single-layer LLaMA-style predictor used in EAGLE-2.
3.  **Combination with loss function modifications.** The introduction discusses the disconnect between KL and L1 loss. Having identified "valuable data," the logical next step is to combine SFDD with a loss function specifically tailored for the SD objective (e.g., an L1-inspired loss) on this selected data, rather than sticking with standard KD loss. An experiment ablating the loss on the filtered set is a natural extension.

# Final Consolidated Review
## Summary
This paper introduces a data-centric method to improve the training efficiency of draft models for speculative decoding (SD). The core insight is that tokens for which the target model produces a flatter (more uniform) predictive distribution offer greater potential for improving the SD acceptance rate. The authors propose a simple "flatness" metric (cosine similarity to uniform) and a Sample-level-flatness-based Dataset Distillation (SFDD) method that filters training data to retain high-value samples. Experiments show SFDD achieves over 2× training speedup using only 50% of the data while keeping final inference speedup within 4% of the full-dataset baseline.

## Strengths
- **Novel, actionable insight with theoretical grounding:** The paper identifies a clear, previously unexplored property (target distribution flatness) that correlates with training value for SD. This is supported by a tractable theoretical analysis modeling a single knowledge distillation step, which shows flatter target distributions yield larger reductions in the L1 discrepancy linked to acceptance rate (Section 3.2, Figure 1).
- **Strong and comprehensive empirical validation:** The method is extensively evaluated on five diverse tasks (GSM8K, Alpaca, MT-Bench, CNN/DM, NQ). SFDD consistently outperforms common data selection heuristics (entropy, top-1 probability, margin, etc.) at a fixed 50% retain ratio (Table 1) and shows robust performance across a wide range of retain ratios (5% to 70%), significantly beating random filtering (Tables 2, 3). The reported >2× training speedup with minimal inference performance loss is compelling and practically significant.
- **Practical simplicity and low overhead:** The proposed flatness metric is simple to compute, requiring only a single, offline forward pass of the target model over the dataset. The SFDD pipeline integrates easily into existing training frameworks, and its selection cost is shown to be negligible (~3.85% of full training time) (Appendix D).

## Weaknesses
- **Theoretical analysis relies on strong simplifications:** The key theoretical insight derives from analyzing Gaussian distributions under a specific, idealized KL-constrained update. While the translation to discrete distributions via cosine similarity is empirically validated, the analysis in Appendix F.3 shows the variance-benefit relationship is not consistent across other distribution families (Exponential, Half-normal), suggesting the finding's generality may be somewhat specific to the Gaussian/location-scale setting used for tractability.
- **Limited exploration of the draft model's role and training dynamics:** The flatness metric depends solely on the static *target* model, implicitly assuming the draft model's starting state is less relevant. The paper shows empirical training dynamics (Fig. 2, 7) but does not deeply analyze how the value of flat tokens might change as the draft model evolves or if the draft model architecture varies substantially. Experiments are confined to one draft framework (EAGLE-2) and one primary target model family (LLaMA3-8B).
- **Small-scale validation of the core token-level dynamics:** The empirical validation that high target flatness correlates with larger ∆L1 reduction (Fig. 2) is based on a detailed inspection of only 10 randomly selected samples. While the main results at scale strongly support the method's efficacy, a larger-scale analysis of these token-level dynamics would increase confidence in the foundational claim.

## Nice-to-Haves
- **Deeper analysis of why flatness outperforms entropy:** While Figure 2d and Appendix F.2 show flatness is better than entropy, a more intuitive explanation or case study analyzing where the rankings differ would strengthen the justification for this specific metric choice.
- **Exploration of more sophisticated sample-level aggregation:** The method aggregates token-level flatness via a simple average (or median). Exploring alternative aggregation strategies (e.g., considering the distribution of flatness within a sample) could potentially yield further improvements.
- **Experiments on other SD frameworks:** Evaluating SFDD on at least one other train-based SD method (e.g., Medusa) would help demonstrate the generality of the flatness principle beyond the EAGLE-2 pipeline.

## Removed Points
*These points are flagged to be removed, treat them with caution*
- **Weakness: "Lack of a dedicated limitations section in the main paper."** While a consolidated section might improve presentation, the paper discusses key limitations in the appendices (e.g., Gaussian assumption, token-level filtering impracticality).
- **Weakness: "Lack of statistical significance testing (e.g., error bars)."** In the context of large-scale LLM benchmarks where single-run evaluation is common practice, and given the large and consistent performance gaps reported across multiple datasets, the absence of multiple-run statistics is not a critical flaw.
- **Suggestion: "Rigorous control for training time savings comparing equal steps, not epochs."** The paper's comparison against random filtering at the same data retain ratio is the standard and appropriate control for measuring the quality of the selected subset. Isolating "data quality" by matching steps would change the training recipe and is not required.
- **Suggestion: "Comparison against modern, learning-based data selection methods."** The paper's contribution is introducing a simple, effective metric *specific* to the SD objective. It is reasonable to benchmark against standard, widely-used heuristics to establish a baseline. Demanding comparison against unrelated, generic selection methods is scope creep.

## Novel Insights
The paper's core novel insight is that, for training draft models in speculative decoding, the flatness (uncertainty) of the *target* model's token distribution is a strong indicator of training value. This reframes the problem from optimizing the loss function to identifying valuable data, establishing that tokens with flatter distributions offer more headroom for reducing the L1 discrepancy that governs acceptance rate. This target-dependent, offline-computable criterion provides a new, data-centric principle for efficient draft model training.

## Suggestions
- **Include a qualitative analysis of selected vs. rejected samples.** Providing a few concrete text examples annotated with token-level flatness scores would offer an intuitive, sense-check validation of what the method prioritizes (e.g., ambiguous or open-ended contexts).
- **Discuss adaptive selection as future work.** The current method uses static filtering. A natural extension, worthy of mention in the conclusion, is a dynamic curriculum that re-scores data periodically as the draft model improves.
