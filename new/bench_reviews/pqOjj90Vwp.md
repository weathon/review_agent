This paper proposes a unified logical framework for characterizing the expressive power of GNNs that can be decomposed into aggregation (AGG) and combination (COM) operations—termed General Aggregate-Combine Neural Networks (GACNNs). The central results establish a recursive correspondence between GACNN computation structures and equivalent logic sets in first-order logic with counting quantifiers (FOC), mapping AGG to ∃≥N and COM to Boolean connectives ∧ and ¬. The framework is applied to derive the logical expressivity of several prominent GNN classes (MPNNs, Subgraph GNNs, NBFNet, SEAL, Local 2-GNN, 2-FGNN), provides a general method for computing homomorphism expressivity of any GACNN, and yields WL upper bounds via variable counting.

## Strengths

- **Unified generalization:** The GACNN abstraction and the mapping AGG↔∃≥N, COM↔¬/∧ naturally extends the Barceló et al. (2020) characterization from MPNNs to a broad class of GNN architectures. Proposition 7 concisely summarizes the logical expressivity of eight GNN classes in a common notation, enabling direct structural comparisons (e.g., how Subgraph GNN strong extends the weak variant by allowing cross-source aggregation ψ(x,y) := φ(y)).

- **Resolution of a conjecture:** Theorem 8 resolves the conjecture from Zhang et al. (2024) about when a GNN's homomorphism expressivity exists and how to construct it, extending prior results by Dell et al. (2018) to arbitrary GACNNs. The constructive procedure—removing negation and high-count quantifiers from the equivalent logic set, then mapping variables to nodes and atomic predicates to edges—is elegant and intuitive.

- **Conceptual clarity:** The interpretability of the logical characterizations is a genuine advantage over coarse WL-based comparisons. The framework makes precise, for instance, why 2-GNNs cannot express GrandParent(x,y) while NBFNet can—a distinction that is less transparent from WL analysis alone.

## Weaknesses

### Major:

- **Under-specified assumptions on AGG/COM for Theorem 3 / Proposition 5.** The paper states that COM and AGG are "arbitrary" functions (Section 4), but the logical correspondence in Theorem 3 requires that AGG on multisets of binary outputs can realize any counting threshold (∃≥N) and COM on two values can realize any Boolean function (via ¬ and ∧). For real-valued intermediate representations, which is the standard setting for GNNs, these properties depend on the activation functions and architecture details (e.g., width, depth). Barceló et al. (2020) established the MPNN correspondence under specific assumptions (sufficiently wide MLPs with ReLU activations for universal Boolean approximation), but the current paper does not explicitly state what conditions on AGG/COM are needed. This matters because the paper's claimed generality ("arbitrary computation procedure built upon aggregation and combination functions") implicitly requires more than just structural composition—it requires functional universality of the constituent operations. The paper should clearly state these assumptions and discuss where they may fail.

- **The gap between the full equivalent logic set and the positive fragment for homomorphism expressivity.** Theorem 8 constructs homomorphism expressivity by first stripping negation and ∃≥N for N≥2 from the equivalent logic set Φ, then mapping the remaining formulas to pattern graphs. The equivalence claim (Hom(F,G) = Hom(F,H) for all F ∈ ℱ ⇔ GACNNs don't distinguish G,H) requires that the *full* discriminating power of the GACNN class is captured by the positive existential counting fragment. While this holds for classic WL-like models where the CFI theorem ensures that positive formulas with counting suffice for isomorphism testing, the paper does not provide a proof or even a proof sketch that this reduction is valid for arbitrary GACNNs. Theorem 8 is stated for general GACNNs without restricting to unlabeled graphs or positive fragments, but the construction explicitly discards parts of Φ. A formal justification for this "projection" step is needed, especially since the paper claims to solve an open problem from Zhang et al. (2024).

- **No empirical validation.** The paper is entirely theoretical. Like similar frameworks in the GNN expressivity literature (e.g., "Beyond WL" (Zhang et al., 2024) included extensive synthetic and real-world experiments; "Homomorphism Expressivity of Spectral Invariant GNNs" also provided experiments verifying that predicted expressivity hierarchies hold in practice), empirical experiments demonstrating that the predicted expressivity differences between GNN architectures manifest on benchmark tasks would significantly strengthen the contribution. Even small-scale experiments confirming that the logical characterizations correctly predict GNN separability on specific graph pairs would provide evidence for the framework's correctness.

- **Equalities and strict inequalities in Corollary 10 are not fully justified.** The paper derives claims such as "FGNN = 3-WL," "Subgraph GNN (weak) = NBFNet," and the strict ordering chain from variable counts in the grammar. While Proposition 9 correctly states that variable counting gives *upper* bounds on WL power, the paper does not independently prove *lower* bounds for each model class. Some equalities (e.g., FGNN = 3-WL) may follow from prior work, but this is not made explicit, and some strict inequalities (e.g., "Subgraph GNN (strong) < Local 2-FGNN") require constructing separating graph families, which are not provided. The paper should clearly distinguish which claims follow from established results versus which are new and require independent proof.

## Removed Points

- **"Theorem 3/Proposition 5 is fundamentally incorrect for arbitrary neural networks."** The harsh critic argued that arbitrary real-valued neural networks can encode operations not expressible in FOC. While the concern about under-specified assumptions is valid (and kept above), the claim that the theorem is *incorrect* goes too far. The paper works with binary-output GNNs and defines the equivalent logic set in terms of *what Boolean functions the GNN class can compute*, not deriving specific formulas from specific weight configurations. Under reasonable universality assumptions (wide enough networks), the structural correspondence between AGG/COM and ∃≥N/∧/¬ is sound, as established by Barceló et al. (2020) for MPNNs. The correct criticism is that these assumptions should be made explicit, not that the approach is fundamentally flawed.

- **"Bullet 5 of Definition 2 makes separation equivalence tautological."** While bullet 5 does define the equivalent logic set to include this property, this is how mathematical definitions work: the definition specifies what it means for Φ to be the equivalent logic set of X, and then one must prove that a specific construction satisfies this definition. This is not circular—it's analogous to defining "basis" to mean "linearly independent spanning set" and then proving a specific set is a basis.

- **"Bullet 2 vs. Bullet 4 inconsistency invalidates the definition."** The asymmetry is explicitly acknowledged and justified by the paper's footnote about GNNs not captured by logic formulas. This is a standard approach in finite model theory: complete capture for each direction is not always achievable, and approximate capture (for bounded sizes) is the accepted relaxation. This does not invalidate the definition but does limit its strength—appropriately flagged above.

- **Requests for proof sketches in the main body** are formatting/preference suggestions that don't constitute substantive weaknesses; the proofs are in the appendix.

- **Demanding comparison with "the full landscape of subgraph GNN taxonomies"** is a scope concern—the paper already provides characterizations for several major classes.

- **Concerns about "homomorphism expressivity disregards features"**, while valid for practical applicability, is a general limitation of structural expressivity frameworks (including "Beyond WL") and not specific to this paper's contributions.

- **Demanding "computational complexity analysis"** of checking formula membership or computing homomorphism expressivity sets is outside the paper's stated scope of providing a theoretical characterization framework.

## Novel Insights

The most insightful observation from the review synthesis is that the paper's AGG↔∃≥N, COM↔¬/∧ correspondence provides a *structural* explanation for why higher-order GNNs are more expressive: not merely because they use more variables, but because they allow richer *logical composition patterns* (single-source vs. multi-source aggregation, cross-source combination). For instance, the distinction between NBFNet (single-source) and Local 2-GNN (separate dual-source) vs. 2-FGNN (joint dual-source) mirrors the difference between Bellman-Ford and Floyd-Warshall algorithms, a connection the paper briefly makes but that deserves deeper elaboration. This structural-logical view is more interpretable than WL hierarchy comparisons and could inform principled GNN design.
