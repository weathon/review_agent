## Summary

IncentRL proposes augmenting standard RL rewards with a KL-divergence term between predicted outcomes p(o|s,a) and preferred outcomes q(o|s), weighted by a parameter β. The paper's central claimed novelty is treating β as a Bayesian random variable updated online to eliminate manual trade-off tuning between extrinsic and intrinsic rewards. Experiments on a 2-state toy MDP, MountainCar-v0, and MiniGrid DoorKey 8×8 show improved performance with certain fixed β values, and Bayesian β adaptation is claimed to match the best fixed values.

## Strengths

- **Clear conceptual framing:** The idea of using KL divergence between predicted and preferred outcome distributions as an intrinsic exploration signal is a clean, well-motivated formulation that naturally connects to both intrinsic motivation and variational inference perspectives.
- **Transparent failure mode analysis:** The paper honestly acknowledges and demonstrates that large β values cause severe degradation (MountainCar: −53% at β=0.3, −75% at β=1.0). This transparency about when the method fails is valuable.
- **Theoretical limiting behavior:** Propositions 1 and 2 correctly characterize that small β preserves optimal policies and β→∞ collapses to pure KL minimization, providing interpretable bounds on the framework's behavior.
- **Relevance to sparse-reward settings:** The MiniGrid DoorKey experiment addresses a genuinely hard exploration problem where intrinsic signals are most needed, and the 98% success rate vs. 90.5% baseline is a meaningful improvement.

## Weaknesses

### Major:

- **The Bayesian adaptation mechanism—the paper's central novelty—is never specified.** The title, abstract, and introduction foreground Bayesian adaptation of β as the main contribution, yet the paper never provides the prior, likelihood, posterior update rule, or algorithmic procedure. The Appendix only reports summary statistics of β values over 4 "rounds" (undefined), and Figs. 3–4 show posterior evolution without any mathematical specification. This makes the core claim unverifiable and unimplementable from the paper alone. Even a brief algorithm box or pseudo-code for the Bayesian update is absent. For a contribution centered on "Bayesian adaptation," this is a critical omission.

- **The empirical validation of Bayesian adaptation is anecdotal and incomplete.** The most detailed experiments (toy MDP, MountainCar) use *fixed* β sweeps only—no Bayesian β results are reported. For MiniGrid, the paper states only that "in additional runs (not shown), the Bayesian adaptation of β achieved performance comparable to the best fixed value." No learning curves, final performance numbers, or statistical comparison between Bayesian β and the best fixed β are provided. The claim that Bayesian adaptation "eliminates the need for manual trade-off tuning" is therefore unsupported by the presented evidence.

- **The outcome distributions p(o|s,a) and q(o|s) are underspecified in the main experiments.** In the toy example, p and q are clearly defined as discrete distributions. But for MountainCar (continuous state space), the paper states that "q(o|s) is a preferred outcome assigning all probability to the goal (x ≥ 0.5)" without specifying what the outcome variable o is (full state? position only? discretized?), or how p(o|s,a) is obtained (learned forward model? environment dynamics?). For MiniGrid, no specification is given at all. This is not merely a missing hyperparameter—without knowing what these distributions are, the reader cannot assess whether the KL term is doing meaningful outcome prediction or simply biasing toward the goal (which reduces to a form of potential-based shaping with prior knowledge).

- **No comparison with established intrinsic motivation baselines.** The paper claims to address "a core limitation of intrinsic motivation methods" but only compares against unshaped baselines (β=0). There are no comparisons with any standard exploration method (ICM, RND, count-based bonuses, VIME, etc.) even though these are the natural competitors. This makes it impossible to assess whether IncentRL offers advantages over existing approaches to the same problem.

- **The preferred distribution q encodes strong goal knowledge, undermining the sparse-reward motivation.** In all experiments, q(o|s) is set to a point mass at the goal state. This means the agent already knows where the goal is—a substantial form of domain knowledge. The paper's own motivation is addressing sparse-reward settings, yet the method requires specifying where the reward is. An ablation comparing with an equivalent dense reward without the KL term would clarify whether the gains come from the KL mechanism itself or simply from providing goal-directed information.

### Minor:

- **Theoretical propositions are limited.** Propositions 1 and 2 establish continuity and dominance behavior that are straightforward consequences of standard perturbation theory; they do not provide convergence guarantees, suboptimality bounds, or characterization of intermediate β behavior. This is acknowledged as toy analysis but the writing implies stronger grounding than delivered.

- **Connections to neuroscience (dopamine RPE) and FEP are motivational, not formal.** The paper states that IncentRL "connects to" these frameworks but does not derive testable predictions or formal equivalences. The connection is that both use KL divergences. This is appropriate as inspiration but the language overclaims the theoretical grounding.

- **Only 3 seeds for MountainCar and MiniGrid.** With standard deviations of 472–908 in MountainCar, the 18% improvement at β=0.1 may not be statistically significant. 

### Trivial

- **Section organization issues:** Section 3.3 and Section 4 are merged confusingly, with the "Theoretical Analysis" header appearing mid-section.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Formalize the Bayesian β scheme (prior, likelihood, update) and include an algorithm box; this is essential for reproducibility and would transform the paper.
- Compare against at least 2–3 standard intrinsic motivation baselines (ICM, RND, count-based) on the same domains.
- Test with a non-oracle preference q(o|s) (e.g., learned from demonstrations or subgoal-based) to demonstrate generality beyond goal-biasing.
- Include an ablation that compares the KL incentive against an equivalent manual dense reward to isolate the mechanism's contribution.

## Novel Insights

The paper identifies an important tension: KL-based shaping with a well-chosen β can help exploration, but β tuning is itself a hard problem—and the MountainCar results vividly show this (18% improvement at β=0.1 vs. −75% at β=1.0). This makes the *idea* of adaptive β compelling, even though the paper's execution of that idea is incomplete. The observation that IncentRL effectively biases agents toward the goal via preference distributions is simultaneously its strength (it works) and its limitation (it requires knowing the goal), suggesting the method is better understood as a form of informed reward shaping than as a general-purpose exploration mechanism.

## Suggestions

1. **Formalize the Bayesian adaptation algorithm.** Provide a complete specification: prior p(β), likelihood linking β to observed outcomes, and the posterior update rule. Include pseudo-code. Without this, the paper's central contribution is incomplete.
2. **Report Bayesian β results on all environments**, not just MiniGrid, and present learning curves that directly compare Bayesian adaptation against fixed β sweeps and simple annealing schedules.
3. **Clarify p(o|s,a) and q(o|s) for each experiment**, including the outcome representation, prediction model, and any discretization/parametric assumptions made. An ablation comparing the KL incentive to a simple dense reward toward the goal would isolate whether the gains come from the KL mechanism or from goal knowledge.
4. **Add comparisons with at least ICM and RND** on MiniGrid to establish whether IncentRL is competitive with established exploration methods, not just with an unshaped baseline.
5. **Moderate the claims about "eliminating manual trade-off tuning" and "cognitive grounding"** until the Bayesian algorithm is fully specified and empirically validated. The current writing significantly overclaims relative to what is demonstrated.
