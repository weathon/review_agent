## Summary

AutoCoder introduces AIEV-INSTRUCT, a pipeline that augments code instruction data with multi-turn agent interaction and execution feedback. Models trained on the resulting 169K-sample dataset achieve 90.9% Pass@1 on HumanEval (33B), surpassing GPT-4 Turbo and GPT-4o, and show competitive results on MBPP, DS-1000, MultiPL-E, and LiveCodeBench. The paper also proposes a two-stage teaching/self-learning paradigm and claims a unique capability for automatic external-package installation in code interpreters.

## Strengths
- **Execution-feedback data generation is sensible and empirically supported.** The core AIEV-INSTRUCT pipeline—using a questioner/programmer agent loop with Docker-based execution and stderr feedback—is well-motivated, and the ablation in Figure 6 shows consistent gains from adding execution feedback across HumanEval, MBPP, and DS-1000 for both 6.7B and 33B models.
- **Broad and contemporary evaluation suite.** The authors test on HumanEval+, MBPP+, DS-1000, MultiPL-E, and LiveCodeBench (a regularly updated competitive-programming benchmark), which demonstrates awareness of contamination risks and provides a more robust picture than HumanEval alone.
- **Transparent training details.** The paper reports hardware (40× A100), framework (DeepSpeed ZeRO-3), hyperparameters (learning rate 5e-5, bf16, 2 epochs, full-parameter tuning), and model sizes with enough specificity to support reproducibility.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **The Self-Learning Stage is described as a core contribution but never demonstrated.** Section 3.1 and Figure 3 present a two-stage pipeline in which the student model eventually replaces GPT-4 Turbo as the annotation agent, and the abstract/introduction frame this autonomy as a key innovation that reduces reliance on proprietary models. However, the paper never states that the transition actually occurred, what fraction of the 169K dataset—if any—was generated by the student, or whether the final model was trained on any self-generated data at all (Section 3.2 explicitly says “The gpt-4-turbo-2024-04-09 is used as the teacher model”). Without ablations comparing models trained with and without self-learned data, the second research question in the introduction is left entirely unanswered, and a prominently advertised contribution lacks evidence.
- **The automatic external-package installation claim is asserted without quantitative evaluation.** The abstract states that “AutoCoder is the only model that supports automatically installing external packages in the Code Interpreter,” and Section 4.1 describes the post-processing approach. Yet there is no benchmark, success-rate metric, or controlled comparison against GPT-4, OpenCodeInterpreter, or other agents. A distinctive capability claimed as a contribution cannot be accepted without measurement.

### Minor
- **Benchmark-specific gain pattern warrants explicit discussion.** AutoCoder-33B gains +9.8 points over its DeepSeek-Coder-Instruct-33B baseline on HumanEval (90.9 vs. 81.1) but only +3.0 on HumanEval+ (78.0 vs. 75.0), +2.1 on MBPP+ (70.6 vs. 70.1), and +2.0 on LiveCodeBench (25.4 vs. 23.4). The ablation in Figure 6 is even more pronounced: adding execution feedback yields a +10.1 jump on HumanEval (81.3 → 91.4) but only +2.3 on MBPP and +2.8 on DS-1000. Because HumanEval is a small, widely distributed benchmark, this skew raises legitimate concerns about format overfitting or weak test-case exploitation that the authors do not address. The decontamination method (Levenshtein distance >90%) is coarse and would not catch semantic paraphrasing; the paper should report semantic-similarity audits or embedding-based overlap analyses to bolster confidence.
- **Figure 6 and Table 1 report inconsistent HumanEval scores.** Figure 6 lists 91.4% (33B) and 79.2% (6.7B), whereas Table 1 reports 90.9% and 78.7%. The discrepancies are small but unexplained and should be reconciled.

### Trivial
- **Unit-test verification gap.** Both the questioner and programmer agents are instantiated from the same teacher model (GPT-4 Turbo), so an incorrect teacher could generate incorrect unit tests that nonetheless execute without error. The paper does not discuss this failure mode, though it is partially mitigated by the multi-turn execution loop.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Provide semantic contamination statistics (e.g., embedding similarity, n-gram overlap) between the training data and all evaluated benchmarks.
- Explicitly state whether the Self-Learning Stage was reached; if not, remove the autonomy claim or relegate it to future work.
- Design a small benchmark for external-package installation tasks and report success rates against at least one baseline.
- Include case studies of LiveCodeBench Medium/Hard failures to contextualize the modest gains there.

## Removed Points
These points are flagged to be removed, treat them with caution.
- **Criticism that the HumanEval result “exhibits the classic signature of test-set contamination.”** While the benchmark-specific gain pattern is genuinely concerning and should be discussed, the harsh reviewer treats contamination as proven rather than hypothesized. The paper evaluates on multiple benchmarks (including the uncontaminated LiveCodeBench) and shows gains everywhere; without direct evidence of leakage, this should be framed as a suspicious pattern, not confirmed contamination.
- **“Cost figure cited for GPT-4 Turbo annotation is unsourced and unverifiable.”** The paper explicitly attributes the $1,000/6,500 entries figure to their own tests (“According to our tests”), so it is sourced.
- **“Special tokens added around bash commands are never specified, making reproduction difficult.”** The paper states that sample demonstrations are in Appendix D and code is in supplementary materials; because appendices are stripped by the parser, this information may exist in the original submission.
- **Pure formatting/style nitpicks and typos.** These are parser artifacts, not author errors.

## Novel Insights
The paper’s most genuinely novel observation—beyond its own contributions—is the tension between execution-verified training and benchmark-specific overfitting. The fact that adding execution feedback produces a ~10-point jump on HumanEval but only ~2–3 points on MBPP, DS-1000, and LiveCodeBench suggests that the training signal may be optimizing for superficial test-case patterns on small benchmarks rather than robust reasoning. This pattern deserves deeper investigation in future work on code-training data generation.

## Suggestions
- Either demonstrate the Self-Learning Stage with data-fraction statistics and an ablation, or remove it from the list of contributions and reframe it as future work.
- Add a controlled evaluation of the code-interpreter package-installation capability, even on a small hand-crafted set of tasks requiring `pip install`.
- Reconcile the numerical discrepancies between Figure 6 and Table 1, and add a paragraph explicitly discussing why HumanEval gains are so much larger than those on stricter benchmarks.
