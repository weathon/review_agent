## Summary

The paper identifies that LLM-based molecular generation models produce structurally similar molecules even when using diversity-promoting decoding schemes (because textual diversity in SMILES does not correspond to molecular structural diversity). To address this, the authors propose a two-stage fine-tuning approach: (1) supervised fine-tuning (Div-SFT) to adapt LLMs to autoregressively generate multiple molecules in a single sequence, and (2) multi-stage reinforcement learning (Div-SFT+RL) that decomposes the generation of each molecule into a separate RL stage with per-molecule credit assignment, using a Tanimoto-based diversity reward and a BLEU-based description-matching reward.

## Strengths

- **Important observation that textual diversity ≠ molecular structural diversity** (Figure 3b): The paper provides direct empirical evidence that diverse beam search improves textual J-distinct but fails to improve molecular J-distinct. This is a genuine, clearly illustrated, and underappreciated insight in the LLM-for-chemistry literature that strongly motivates a training-based approach over decoding-based alternatives.

- **Multi-stage RL formulation effectively addresses credit assignment**: Table 4 provides compelling evidence—multi-stage RL (NCircles_h=0.65 = 14.35) more than doubles single-stage RL (6.49) and hard-filtered SFT (6.15), validating the per-molecule reward decomposition argument from Section 3.2.

- **Consistent improvements across multiple base models**: Figure 5 shows Div-SFT+RL consistently achieves the highest NCircles across both BioT5+ and MolT5, and Figure 7 demonstrates improvements on DrugAssist (Llama-7B based), including generalization to unseen QED properties (Figure 7d).

- **Large performance gap over existing LLMs**: Table 2 shows BioT5++ours achieves NCircles_h=0.65 = 14.35, more than double the next-best existing model (BioT5 at 6.07), and far surpassing GPT-4o (3.53) and o1-preview (2.36), establishing that the diversity problem is real across current LLMs and the proposed method effectively addresses it.

- **Self-improvement design requires no external diverse datasets**: Both SFT and RL stages use samples generated by the model itself (Sections 3.1–3.2), making the approach broadly applicable without curated diverse molecular datasets.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **BLEU on SMILES strings is a structurally inappropriate quality metric for molecular generation, and it underpins both the acceptance criterion and the RL reward**: The paper uses BLEU > 0.7 between a generated SMILES string and a ground-truth example as the criterion for "accepted & unique" molecules (Section 4.1, line 290), and BLEU as the description-matching reward r_match (line 295). BLEU measures n-gram string overlap, not whether a generated molecule satisfies a chemical description. Two structurally different molecules can have high BLEU (e.g., stereoisomers with near-identical SMILES), while two molecules satisfying the same description can have low BLEU (structurally diverse valid matches). This is particularly problematic because the "Accepted & Unique" count gates which molecules enter the NCircles computation—if BLEU incorrectly accepts or rejects molecules, it propagates errors into the primary metric. The paper acknowledges this in footnote 6 ("the BLEU score has a limitation by just measuring the textual similarity") and reports Tanimoto/Dice experiments in Appendix D.2, but these remain in the appendix rather than the main results. Moving those experiments to the main paper would substantially strengthen the evaluation. This is a major (not fatal) concern because (a) the diversity metrics themselves (NCircles, IntDiv) are Tanimoto-based and thus chemically meaningful, (b) qualitative results (Table 1, Figure 6) suggest the generated molecules are reasonable, and (c) the paper follows prior work (Edwards et al., 2022; Pei et al., 2024) in using BLEU.

- **RL diversity reward and evaluation metrics share the same Tanimoto similarity measure, with no orthogonal diversity validation**: The diversity reward r_div is defined as the complement of max Tanimoto similarity on structural fingerprints (Section 3.2), and the evaluation metrics NCircles and IntDiv are computed from the same Tanimoto similarity (Section 4.1). The model is thus directly optimized for the test metric. Without evaluation on an orthogonal notion of chemical diversity—e.g., Bemis-Murcko scaffold diversity, pharmacophore fingerprint diversity, or even a different fingerprint type—it is unclear whether the learned diversity generalizes beyond the specific fingerprint metric. This does not invalidate the results (Tanimoto on Morgan fingerprints is a reasonable diversity measure), but it limits confidence that the method captures chemically meaningful diversity rather than fingerprint-specific diversity.

### Minor

- **The comparison between fine-tuning and decoding schemes (Figure 5) does not isolate the contribution of fine-tuning from the autoregressive generation paradigm**: Div-SFT+RL is a fine-tuned model generating autoregressively, while baselines are the original model with various decoding schemes. Applying decoding schemes (e.g., diverse beam search) to the fine-tuned Div-SFT model would isolate whether fine-tuning alone or the interaction of fine-tuning with autoregressive generation drives the improvement. The paper's conclusion that "fine-tuning outperforms decoding schemes" is slightly overstated—what is shown is that the full pipeline outperforms decoding on the unmodified model.

- **Section 4.2 evaluates only 500 of the 3300 test descriptions, with no justification**: Section 4.1 uses all 3300 descriptions, but Section 4.2 (comparison with existing LLMs) uses only the first 500 (line 335). While likely due to API costs for GPT models, this discrepancy should be acknowledged and its potential impact discussed.

- **The "first to explore" claim (Section 1, line 87) is overclaimed**: "We are the first to explore the use of LLMs for generating diverse molecules" overstates novelty—diverse molecule generation is a well-studied problem in computational chemistry. The paper's specific contribution (fine-tuning LLMs for structural diversity via RL) is novel, but the claim should be scoped more precisely.

## Novel Insights

The observation that textual diversity and molecular structural diversity are orthogonal dimensions—and that standard decoding schemes for LLMs optimize the wrong one—is a genuinely important insight that extends beyond this paper. It suggests a broader principle: whenever LLMs generate outputs in a formal language (code, SMILES, mathematical notation), diversity in the string representation may not correspond to diversity in the semantic/structural space. The multi-stage RL decomposition for credit assignment is also a clean methodological contribution that could generalize to other sequential generation settings where individual elements contribute differently to a global objective.
