Let me now write the consolidated review.</think>## Summary
NarrativeBridge introduces a Causal-Temporal Narrative (CTN) benchmark dataset generated via LLM prompting from existing MSR-VTT and MSVD captions, and a Cause-Effect Network (CEN) architecture with separate cause and effect visual encoders trained on the CTN captions. The paper reports that CEN significantly outperforms existing video captioning methods and VLMs on the new benchmark in terms of CIDEr, ROUGE-L, and SPICE.

## Strengths
- **Clear task motivation:** The paper identifies a real gap—existing video captioning benchmarks and models lack explicit causal-temporal narrative structure—and proposes both a dataset creation pipeline and an architecture to address it. The examples in Figures 1 and 6 effectively illustrate how standard captions describe isolated events without causal linkage.
- **Human validation of reference captions:** The human evaluation study (5 raters, 100 videos, 3 dimensions) with a mean quality score of 4.8/5 and ICC of 0.87 provides evidence that the LLM-generated CTN captions are perceived as coherent and relevant by domain experts.
- **Comprehensive ablation study:** Table 2 provides useful ablations (E_combined, w/o FT variants, Only E_cause, Only E_effect, cross-dataset evaluations) that demonstrate the contribution of each CEN design choice, showing meaningful drops when components are removed.
- **Cross-dataset generalization:** The Zero-Shot X and Fine-tune X experiments (Table 2) show that models trained on one CTN dataset can transfer to the other, providing some evidence of generalization beyond a single training distribution.

## Weaknesses

### Major:

- **The CTN benchmark does not reliably encode genuine causal structure.** The CTN captions are generated by an LLM (Mixtral) from original textual captions—without access to the video. Research on LLM causal reasoning has shown that LLMs tend to rely on temporal ordering heuristics (earlier events → cause) and parametric knowledge rather than genuine causal inference from context. The paper's own flagship example (Figure 1) illustrates this concern: the cause "a car drove recklessly through an open field flipping over" is linked to the effect "a group of guys started playing beer pong," but there is no causal relationship between these events—they are merely temporally successive. The prompt constrains output to exactly one Cause/Effect pair with ≤15 words each, creating a highly templated and restricted "narrative" format. While the human evaluation confirms the captions are coherent and relevant, it evaluates only the LLM-generated references on dimensions like "relevance" and "temporal coherence," not whether the identified cause-effect links are genuinely causal as opposed to merely sequential or inferred from commonsense priors. This is a fundamental concern because the benchmark's claim to represent causal-temporal narrative is its primary justification.

- **Standard captioning metrics (CIDEr, ROUGE-L, SPICE) do not evaluate causal-temporal understanding.** The core claim is that CEN "significantly outperforms SOTA in articulating causal-temporal narrative," but all automatic evaluations use n-gram overlap and scene-graph similarity metrics that cannot distinguish between "A caused B" and "A and B happened." The paper explicitly reinterprets these metrics as capturing causal-temporal capability (Section 4.1), but this is not what they measure. A model that learns the syntactic pattern "Cause: X, Effect: Y" without understanding causality would score well on these metrics. Without targeted evaluation—e.g., checking whether generated causes temporally precede effects, whether causal direction is correct, or whether effect events are truly entailed by cause events—the claim that CEN understands causal-temporal narrative is not substantiated by the evidence presented.

- **Near-complete overlap of cause and effect feature representations undermines the architectural motivation.** Figure 4(b) shows that the UMAP visualizations of cause (black) and effect (orange) features are nearly completely overlapping. This directly challenges the rationale for having two separate CLIP-ViT encoders: if both encoders produce essentially indistinguishable representations from the same video input (without temporal segmentation), the dual-encoder design may be achieving gains through increased model capacity rather than through specialized cause/effect learning. The paper interprets this overlap as "aligning with the inherent structure of causal-temporal narratives," but in causal modeling, distinct roles (cause vs. effect) typically require distinguishable representations. No quantitative measure of representational divergence (e.g., CKA, CCA) is provided. The important ablation—shuffling cause/effect text labels during training—is also missing, which would directly test whether the encoders learn specialized representations.

- **Baseline comparison fairness concerns.** The SOTA methods (SEM-POS, AKGNN, GIT) are general-purpose video captioning models not designed for the cause/effect caption format. The paper states "we follow the hyperparameter settings specified in their respective methods" but does not clarify whether these models were trained from scratch on CTN data, how the cause+effect concatenated target was formatted for them, or what parameter counts they have relative to CEN. For VLMs (VideoLLaVA, ShareGPT4Video), the paper says it uses "recommended hyperparameters" for fine-tuning but provides minimal detail. Since CEN is explicitly designed for and trained on the CTN format, its advantage on CTN benchmarks may reflect format-matching advantage rather than genuine causal-temporal understanding. A more informative comparison would include an ablation where a standard single-encoder model with comparable parameters is trained on the same CTN data.

### Minor:

- **The EMScore threshold θ=0.2 is not well justified.** The paper states that 66.4% of initially generated captions pass this threshold, and only the distribution (Figure 5) is shown. No sensitivity analysis or threshold ablation is provided, and no qualitative examples of captions above vs. below the threshold are given to demonstrate that the filter meaningfully removes low-quality or spurious causal claims.

- **Human evaluation covers only 0.8% of the dataset.** With 100 videos out of 11,970 and 5 raters evaluating only the LLM-generated reference captions (not model outputs), the study validates reference quality but does not validate whether models trained on this data produce causally accurate captions. This is acknowledged but represents a notable gap for a benchmark paper.

- **Both encoders see the entire video without temporal segmentation.** Since E_cause and E_effect process the same complete video frames, the distinction between cause and effect representations is solely driven by the different text alignment targets, not by any architectural inductive bias linking early frames to causes and later frames to effects. The paper does not discuss this design choice or experiment with temporal segmentation.

### Trivial:
- The limitations section (Section 5) is brief and does not address the concern that CTN captions may reflect LLM priors rather than genuine video-grounded causality, or that the evaluation metrics cannot assess causal accuracy.

## Novel Insights
The near-complete feature overlap between cause and effect encoders (Figure 4b) is a genuinely surprising finding that challenges the paper's own architectural narrative. If both encoders learn similar feature spaces from the same video input, the performance gains from the dual-encoder design likely stem from increased model capacity (roughly 2× CLIP-ViT parameters in Stage 1) and the implicit regularization of having two separate contrastive losses, rather than from learning genuinely distinct cause and effect dynamics. This suggests that a more parameter-efficient architecture (e.g., a shared backbone with lightweight adapters) might achieve similar performance, which would be a more practical design. The paper misses an opportunity to investigate this, particularly through the shuffled-label ablation.

## Suggestions
- **Most critical:** Add a shuffled-label control experiment where cause and effect text labels are swapped during CEN training. If performance remains similar, it would fundamentally challenge the claim that the dual encoders capture cause/effect semantics.
- Add at least one evaluation metric that specifically measures causal/temporal accuracy of generated captions (e.g., manual evaluation of whether generated causes precede effects and whether effects are entailed by causes).
- Justify the EMScore threshold with a sensitivity analysis and provide examples of captions at different EMScore levels.
- Discuss the distinction between temporal succession and genuine causation in the dataset, and acknowledge that many CTN caption pairs may reflect temporal ordering rather than true causal relationships.
