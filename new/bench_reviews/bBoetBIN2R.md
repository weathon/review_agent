## Summary

NarrativeBridge introduces a novel video captioning task focused on causal-temporal narrative (CTN) and proposes the Cause-Effect Network (CEN), a two-stage architecture with separate cause and effect encoders. The paper also presents a synthetic CTN benchmark generated via few-shot prompting of Mixtral from existing captions, filtered with EMScore. Empirically, CEN outperforms strong baselines—including fine-tuned vision-language models—by large margins on standard captioning metrics (CIDEr, ROUGE-L, SPICE).

## Strengths

- **Addresses a meaningful gap** in video captioning by moving beyond isolated event description toward structured cause-effect narrative, a worthwhile research direction.
- **Complete experimental pipeline** with extensive baselines (SEM-POS, AKGNN, GIT, VideoLLaVA, ShareGPT4Video), ablations (Table 2), and qualitative comparisons (Figure 6).
- **Large quantitative improvements** on the proposed benchmark: CEN achieves 63.51 CIDEr on MSVD-CTN and 49.87 on MSR-VTT-CTN, substantially exceeding prior specialized methods.
- **Dataset quality validation** includes an automatic EMScore filtering loop and a human evaluation on 100 sampled videos reporting high inter-rater reliability (ICC = 0.87).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **Evaluation metrics do not assess causal-temporal narrative understanding.** The paper claims CEN is superior at “articulating the causal and temporal aspects of video content” (Abstract) and that CIDEr/ROUGE-L/SPICE “captur[e] the ability to reproduce salient causal-temporal narrative elements” (Section 4.1). These are standard n-gram and scene-graph overlap metrics; they measure lexical conformity to the LLM-generated reference, not causal validity, temporal coherence, or narrative structure. Since the CTN references follow a rigid cause-effect template, high scores primarily reflect template matching rather than demonstrated causal understanding. No causal-specific metric or human evaluation of *model outputs* is provided to validate the core claim.

- **Benchmark is not visually grounded in causal annotation.** The CTN captions are generated by prompting Mixtral with *original human captions only*; the prompt explicitly states “you cannot directly access the video itself” (Prompt 1). The “causal” relationships are therefore inferred from existing text, not visual evidence. The automatic filtering uses EMScore (threshold 0.2), an unvalidated proxy for causal fidelity, and the human evaluation covers only 100 of 11,970 videos. Because the LLM may recombine or hallucinate cause-effect links not present in the video (e.g., Figure 1 ambiguously frames beer pong as an effect of a car crash), the benchmark may not represent true video-grounded causality, undermining the claim that the dataset enables learning causal-temporal dynamics *from video*.

- **Feature overlap undermines the architectural claim of specialized encoders.** The paper claims that $E_{\text{cause}}$ and $E_{\text{effect}}$ “learn specialized video representations” (Figure 3 caption, Section 3.2.1). However, Figure 4(b) shows the UMAP embeddings of cause and effect video features are nearly perfectly overlaid, indicating the two encoders are not capturing distinct subspaces. The authors interpret this overlap as “strong correlation … supporting the design,” but near-complete overlap is more consistent with redundancy than specialization. Table 2 corroborates this: single-encoder ablations (Only $E_{\text{cause}}$: 56.42 CIDEr; Only $E_{\text{effect}}$: 57.14 CIDEr) remain within ~7 points of the full model (63.51) on MSVD-CTN, and the “Fine-tune X” cross-dataset baseline actually outperforms the full CEN (65.60 vs. 63.51). The central claim that dedicated separate encoders are necessary for causal-temporal narrative is therefore not convincingly supported by the evidence.

### Minor

- **Abstract misreports CIDEr margins as absolute scores.** The abstract states “17.88 and 17.44 CIDEr on the MSVD-CTN and MSR-VTT-CTN datasets, respectively.” These are margins over the next-best method (GIT), not the absolute scores (63.51 and 49.87). The phrasing is ambiguous and likely to mislead readers skimming the abstract.

- **Training procedure appears inconsistent with the stated loss.** Section 3.2.2 states that “the parameters of stage 1 encoders are frozen at this stage,” yet Equation 9 defines the total loss as $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{cause}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{effect}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{caption}}$. If the Stage 1 encoders ($E_{\text{cause}}$, $E_{\text{effect}}$, $T_{\text{cause}}$, $T_{\text{effect}}$) are frozen, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{cause}}$ and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{effect}}$ would not contribute gradients to the trainable Stage 2 parameters, making their inclusion in the total loss unexplained.

- **VLM comparisons may be inflated by format sensitivity.** CIDEr and ROUGE-L are sensitive to lexical and syntactic alignment. The fine-tuned VLMs in Figure 6 produce free-form descriptive sentences rather than consistently matching the rigid “Cause: … Effect: …” template used in CTN references. While the qualitative examples do show content errors by VLMs, the magnitude of the reported CIDEr gaps may partially reflect a format mismatch rather than purely inferior causal understanding.

### Trivial

- Figure 1’s CTN example frames beer pong as an effect of a car crash based solely on temporal adjacency in a compilation video, illustrating the risk of LLM-generated causality from text alone.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A comparative human evaluation of CEN and strong baselines (e.g., GIT, VideoLLaVA) on causal accuracy and temporal coherence, since n-gram overlap with synthetic references does not establish causal understanding.
- Attention-weight or gradient-based analyses showing that $E_{\text{cause}}$ and $E_{\text{effect}}$ attend to different temporal regions, to reconcile Figure 4(b) with the architectural claim.
- Evaluation using metrics robust to syntactic variation (e.g., BERTScore) or structured cause/effect extraction accuracy, to disentangle content understanding from template matching.

## Removed Points

These points are flagged to be removed, treat them with caution:
- **Strength: “Feature-space analysis validating … the dual-encoder architecture is well-motivated by the data geometry.”** The near-complete overlap in Figure 4(b) does not support the dual-encoder claim; if anything, it contradicts it. This strength was dropped because it conflicts with the verified major weakness about feature overlap.
- **Strength: “Qualitative evidence of nuanced causal reasoning.”** While Figure 6 shows CEN outperforming baselines on cherry-picked examples, the ground-truth captions themselves may contain spurious causal inferences, and qualitative examples alone cannot override the metric validity concerns. Moved to Removed Points.

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions.

## Suggestions

- Conduct a human evaluation directly comparing model outputs on causal accuracy and temporal coherence.
- Analyze temporal attention weights to verify whether the encoders attend to different video segments.
- Add BERTScore or similar paraphrase-robust metrics to supplement CIDEr/ROUGE.

## Score and Decision

**Calibration anchors used:**
- *High:* `MLBdiWu4Fw.md` (InternVid, avg 7.00, Accept spotlight): Large-scale, web-crawled video-text dataset with multi-scale LLM captioning and thorough downstream validation. NarrativeBridge is smaller in scale, uses text-only LLM generation without direct video grounding, and lacks the rigorous validation of InternVid.
- *High:* `fCi4o83Mfs.md` (TOMATO, avg 6.75, Accept poster): Human-annotated benchmark with principled metrics for temporal reasoning. NarrativeBridge uses synthetic LLM-generated captions and standard metrics that do not measure its claimed causal contribution, falling well below TOMATO’s rigor.
- *Medium:* `IryGDUHxDE.md` (avg 5.25, Reject): Interesting idea with mixed reviews; suffered from presentation and reproducibility concerns. NarrativeBridge has clearer presentation and more extensive baselines, but its core empirical claims are more seriously undermined by metric and dataset validity issues.
- *Low:* `FFUmPQM8c5.md` (AVCaps, avg 4.00, Withdrawn): Small-scale LLM-augmented dataset with weak experimental validation. NarrativeBridge is stronger experimentally but shares the weakness of relying on LLM-generated captions without sufficient visual grounding.
- *Low:* `U17KoLrXE8.md` (ObjectNet Captions, avg 5.25, Reject): Demonstrated that CIDEr overstates machine performance and does not reflect human-judged quality. NarrativeBridge exemplifies this problem by leaning heavily on CIDEr to claim causal understanding.

NarrativeBridge identifies a sensible direction and assembles a complete pipeline, but its two central empirical claims are seriously weakened by the evidence. The architectural contribution—separate specialized encoders—is contradicted by near-complete feature overlap and the competitiveness of single-encoder ablations. The evaluation protocol uses generic n-gram metrics to claim superior “causal-temporal narrative” understanding, but these metrics measure conformity to an LLM-generated template, not causality. The dataset itself is derived from text alone, raising questions about visual grounding. Relative to the anchors, the paper sits below the medium band due to these unvalidated core claims.
