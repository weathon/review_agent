## Summary

NarrativeBridge introduces a framework for causal-temporal narrative video captioning, comprising (1) the Causal-Temporal Narrative (CTN) benchmark dataset, generated via LLM few-shot prompting that reformulates existing MSR-VTT/MSVD captions into Cause/Effect structured captions filtered by EMScore, and (2) the Cause-Effect Network (CEN), a two-stage architecture with separate CLIP-ViT encoders trained contrastively on cause and effect text portions, followed by a transformer decoder for caption generation. CEN achieves substantial improvements over SOTA methods and VLMs on the proposed CTN benchmark across CIDEr, ROUGE-L, and SPICE metrics.

## Strengths

- **Identifies a meaningful gap in existing benchmarks.** The observation that MSR-VTT and MSVD captions lack causal-temporal narrative structure is well-motivated and illustrated effectively (Figure 1). This gap is real and underexplored.
- **Comprehensive dataset pipeline with quality control.** The CTN caption generation includes automatic evaluation (EMScore filtering with iterative regeneration) and human evaluation (5 raters, ICC=0.87, 93% scores ≥4). This dual validation demonstrates effort toward ensuring dataset quality.
- **Strong empirical improvements on the proposed benchmark.** CEN outperforms next-best (GIT) by +17.88 CIDEr on MSVD-CTN and +17.44 on MSR-VTT-CTN (Table 1), with consistent gains across all metrics and datasets.
- **Thorough ablation study.** Six ablation variants (Table 2) systematically validate contributions from separate encoders, fine-tuning, and individual cause/effect components. Cross-dataset zero-shot and fine-tuning evaluations demonstrate generalization.

## Weaknesses

### Major:

- **LLM-generated ground truth does not reliably encode causality; creates self-referential evaluation.** The CTN captions are generated by Mixtral from text-only original captions, never from the actual video. The LLM is forced to produce a "Cause" and "Effect" pair regardless of whether a genuine causal relationship exists in the video. The only quality gate is EMScore, which measures video-caption relevance — not causal correctness. This means the LLM may fabricate causal structure from mere temporal sequence (e.g., Figure 1's example implausibly implies the car crash caused beer pong playing). Since models are trained and evaluated against this same synthetic ground truth, improvements in CIDEr/SPICE measure how well a model reproduces the LLM's text reformulation pattern — not whether it has learned genuine causal reasoning from video. This is the paper's most fundamental limitation: the "causal" label is primarily a formatting convention, not a validated annotation.

- **Standard captioning metrics cannot evaluate causal-temporal understanding.** The paper uses CIDEr, ROUGE-L, and SPICE to measure performance on a benchmark explicitly claiming to capture cause-effect dynamics. These are n-gram overlap and scene graph metrics that cannot distinguish correct vs. incorrect causal direction (A causes B vs. B causes A), nor causal vs. mere temporal or correlational relations. The paper asserts these metrics "capture causal relationships and event sequences" (Section 4.1), but provides no evidence they are sensitive to causal correctness. This creates a fundamental mismatch between the claimed capability (causal-temporal narrative understanding) and what is actually measured (textual similarity to LLM-generated references).

- **Separate cause/effect encoders appear to learn redundant representations, undermining the architectural motivation.** Figure 4(b) shows near-complete overlap of cause and effect feature embeddings in UMAP space, and the ablation (Table 2) shows `Only E_cause` (56.42 CIDEr) and `Only E_effect` (57.14 CIDEr) perform surprisingly close to full CEN (63.51 CIDEr) on MSVD-CTN. Since both encoders process the identical full video without any temporal segmentation into cause/effect segments, and are only differentiated by which text fragment they are contrastively aligned with, they appear to learn largely similar video representations. The near-overlap in Figure 4(b) contradicts rather than supports the claim of "strong correlation ... aligning with the inherent structure of causal-temporal narratives" — it suggests the encoders are not specializing in distinct aspects of the video.

### Minor:

- **Single reference caption per video limits evaluation robustness.** With only 1 CTN caption per video, reference-based metrics like CIDEr (designed for multiple references) may produce less reliable scores and make the evaluation more sensitive to idiosyncratic LLM wording choices.

- **EMScore threshold (θ=0.2) is low and unjustified.** Figure 5 shows 66.4% of unfiltered captions already exceed this threshold, suggesting it may be too permissive. No sensitivity analysis is provided to justify the choice.

- **Many CTN examples conflate temporal sequence with causation.** Examples like "a soccer player kicked the ball → the ball went into the goal" or Figure 1's car crash → beer pong represent temporal succession rather than genuine causal inference. The paper does not acknowledge or analyze this conceptual limitation.

- **Human evaluation does not compare CTN captions against alternatives.** Raters evaluated only CTN captions in isolation. Without comparison to original captions or alternative causal annotations on the same videos, the study cannot establish that CTN captions are causally superior — only that they are fluent and broadly relevant.

## Novel Insights

The paper reveals an interesting tension at the heart of LLM-generated benchmarks: when ground truth is itself LLM-produced from text-only inputs, models trained on it may learn to reproduce linguistic patterns (the "Cause/Effect" template) rather than genuinely grounding causal structure in visual evidence. The UMAP overlap in Figure 4(b) is actually more informative than the authors acknowledge — it empirically demonstrates that without temporal segmentation or explicit causal supervision signals from the video itself, dual-encoder architectures converge to similar representations, suggesting that "causal specialization" in video encoders requires stronger inductive biases than just different text alignment targets.

## Suggestions

- Reframe claims from "causal-temporal narrative understanding" to "structured narrative caption generation" to better align claims with what the benchmark and evaluation actually support.
- Add a targeted evaluation that probes causal directionality (e.g., swap cause/effect in reference and measure performance drop) to demonstrate that CEN is sensitive to causal structure rather than just template matching.
- Analyze temporal attention patterns of E_cause vs. E_effect to determine whether specialization exists despite the UMAP overlap, or provide quantitative representation similarity analysis (CKA, CCA).
