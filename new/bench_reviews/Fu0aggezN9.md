## Summary

This paper proposes graph-based Document Structure Analysis (gDSA), a new task that extends traditional Document Layout Analysis (DLA) by requiring models to predict both spatial and logical relations among layout elements as a graph. To support this task, the authors construct the GraphDoc dataset (80K images, 4.13M relation annotations across 8 types) built atop DocLayNet, introduce the DRGG (Document Relation Graph Generator) plug-and-play architecture for encoder-decoder detectors, and propose new threshold-based evaluation metrics (mR_g, mAP_g). The best reported configuration (InternImage + RoDLA + DRGG) achieves 57.6% mAP_g@0.5.

---

## Strengths

- **Novel and well-motivated task formulation.** The unification of spatial and logical relations (including non-textual elements) into a single graph framework is a genuine conceptual step forward over existing DLA datasets. Table 1 demonstrates that no prior dataset simultaneously covers graph modality with non-textual instances at scale.

- **Large-scale dataset.** 80K document images and 4.13M relation annotations across 8 categories is a substantive contribution. Building upon the reputable DocLayNet license (CDLA 1.0) is legally sound.

- **Plug-and-play architecture.** DRGG integrates with multiple detectors (DETR, Deformable DETR, DINO, RoDLA) without modification, and consistently improves DLA performance (e.g., RoDLA 80.5% → 81.5% mAP with DRGG). This practical design choice adds value to the research community.

- **Custom evaluation metrics.** The paper correctly identifies why standard SGG top-k metrics are inappropriate for documents (variable number of relations, class imbalance). The threshold-based mR_g / mAP_g approach with explicit instance-matching (Algorithm 1) is a justified and appropriate methodological contribution.

- **Per-category analysis** (Table 3) provides genuine insight into the differential difficulty of relation types.

---

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
*None that fully invalidate the contribution, though the combination of Major issues below is serious for a benchmark paper.*

### Major

- **Annotation quality is unvalidated.** The entire GraphDoc ground truth is generated by a heuristic rule-based pipeline (Tesseract OCR + PDF parsing + XY-Cut reading order + hand-crafted grouping rules). The paper asserts "most of the results have been manually verified and refined," yet provides no quantification: no inter-annotator agreement, no error analysis on the automatic labeling, no sampled precision/recall audit of the rule system. For a paper whose primary contribution *is* a benchmark, the absence of quantified annotation quality is a critical gap. Readers cannot assess whether DRGG is learning meaningful structure or reproducing pipeline artifacts. The paper should report even a small-scale (e.g., 500-sample) IAA or precision/recall analysis of the heuristic labeling.

- **Evaluation metric specification is incomplete.** Algorithm 1 terminates by calling $f_{\text{mR}}(X_T, G)$ and $f_{\text{mAP}}(X_T, G)$ which are never formally defined. The paper states "The remaining settings align with standard SGG evaluation metrics," but given that this paper explicitly argues standard SGG metrics are *not* appropriate for this task, a benchmark paper must fully specify its own metric. Multi-label edge handling, how AP is accumulated across relation categories when a pair can have multiple simultaneous relations, and confidence score aggregation are all left underspecified. This makes reproducibility impossible.

- **Suspicious per-category AP patterns suggest evaluation irregularities.** In Table 3, some configurations achieve exactly 99.0% AP on Left or Right while performing poorly on other spatial relations in the same experiment (e.g., Deformable DETR achieves Left=99.0% but Right=11.9%, Up=16.8%, Down=19.8%). Meanwhile, DETR achieves only 8.9% on Left and Right. If Left/Right were simply coordinate comparisons, all models should perform well; if they require learned representations, all models should be similarly capable. This pattern is internally inconsistent and not explained anywhere in the paper. It may indicate an evaluation artifact or imbalanced prediction behavior that inflates the aggregate headline metric.

- **DRGG's contribution is not isolated.** Table 2 compares different detector+backbone+DRGG combinations, but the only non-DRGG row is "InternImage + RoDLA" (DLA only, no gDSA baseline). There is no ablation comparing DRGG vs. a simpler pairwise relation classifier, no ablation of the weighted token aggregation, no ablation of the multi-layer feature combination (Eq. 5). Without these, it is unclear whether DRGG's architecture is the source of gains or whether any relation head on top of RoDLA+InternImage would work similarly.

- **No comparison with relation prediction baselines.** The paper positions itself relative to SGG literature in the introduction and related work, but provides no SGG-adapted baseline (e.g., a Transformer-based relation head, Neural Motifs, or even a coordinate-only heuristic). Without this, neither the task difficulty nor DRGG's relative contribution can be assessed.

### Minor

- **Overclaiming in the abstract/introduction about ROP and HSA.** The abstract states GraphDoc enables "training models to complete multiple tasks like reading order, hierarchical structures analysis." No experiments directly evaluate reading order as a sequence task or hierarchy reconstruction. The paper establishes a relation-prediction benchmark; claiming it enables the full breadth of these tasks requires direct evaluation.

- **Reliance on visual-only features for Reference relations.** The paper achieves only 16.8% AP on Reference relations, which are fundamentally text-defined (e.g., "see Figure 1"). This is acknowledged in the limitations section and is a legitimate design choice, but the paper does not explore even a simple text-matching baseline to contextualize this low number.

- **Spatial relations dominate aggregate metrics.** With 64% of all 4.13M annotations being spatial relations—and some configurations achieving 99% AP on Left/Right—aggregate mAP_g scores are heavily driven by the easiest categories. A separate mAP_g for logical-only relations would provide a more meaningful signal for the harder (and more interesting) portion of the task.

### Trivial

- The training/evaluation split is described ambiguously: "All experiments were conducted using the GraphDoc dataset for both training and validation" does not clarify whether the DocLayNet standard train/val/test partition is preserved.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Evaluate DRGG (or a multimodal variant incorporating OCR tokens) on Reference relations specifically, to establish whether the visual-only gap is closeable.
- A downstream task evaluation (e.g., using predicted graphs to aid document QA) would substantiate the broader claims about the utility of gDSA outputs.

## Novel Insights

The per-category performance patterns in Table 3 reveal a striking inconsistency: certain backbone–detector combinations achieve near-perfect AP on Left (e.g., Deformable DETR at 99.0%) while achieving low AP on Right (11.9%) within the *same* model. This is not explained by the paper and suggests potential artifacts in either the evaluation pipeline, the ground-truth annotation heuristic, or the model's prediction mechanism. This observation—raised across multiple reviewers independently—goes beyond generic weakness and points to a possible methodological flaw that deserves focused investigation. If confirmed, it would substantially affect the interpretation of aggregate headline numbers.

## Suggestions

1. Report inter-annotator agreement (even on 500 randomly sampled document pages with 2+ annotators) to establish the validity of the heuristic-generated ground truth.
2. Fully specify $f_{\text{mR}}$ and $f_{\text{mAP}}$ in Algorithm 1 or a formal appendix, including multi-label edge handling.
3. Investigate and explain the Left/Right AP asymmetry in Table 3; either correct an evaluation bug or provide a detailed explanation.
4. Add at least one ablation: DRGG vs. a simple pairwise MLP relation head on the same backbone/detector, to isolate the architectural contribution.
5. Report separate aggregate mAP_g for spatial-only and logical-only relation subsets.
6. Clarify the train/val/test split in the experiments section.
