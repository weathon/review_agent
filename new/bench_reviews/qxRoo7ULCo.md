## Summary

4K4DGen proposes a pipeline for generating panoramic 4D dynamic scenes at up to 4K resolution from a single static equirectangular image. The two main contributions are: (1) a **Panoramic Denoiser** that adapts 2D perspective video diffusion priors to the spherical domain through an iterative project-denoise-fuse loop over spherical latent codes, and (2) **Dynamic Panoramic Lifting** that converts the generated panoramic video into a set of time-indexed 3D Gaussians using monocular depth alignment and spatial-temporal regularization. The paper claims this is the first system capable of 4K omnidirectional 4D generation without annotated 4D panoramic training data.

---

## Strengths

- **Novel and practically relevant problem**: Generating 4D immersive panoramic content from a single image directly addresses a bottleneck in VR/AR content creation and has not been addressed at this scope before.
- **Creative technical approach**: The spherical latent project-denoise-fuse mechanism (Eq. 3, Sec. 3.3) is a non-trivial adaptation of I2V diffusion priors to the panoramic domain. It avoids the need for panoramic video training data by jointly denoising 20 overlapping perspective projections and fusing them at each denoising step.
- **Ablations support the core design choices**: Table 2 clearly shows that animating the full panorama produces limited motion (due to resolution and domain mismatch), and animating perspective views independently produces inconsistencies. The proposed joint denoising substantially outperforms both, providing concrete empirical validation of the main animating-phase contribution.
- **Comprehensive ablation of the lifting phase**: Fig. 6 qualitatively demonstrates the value of temporal loss (removing it causes visible flashing artifacts) and spatial-temporal alignment (removing it degrades depth consistency in complex regions like smoke).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
*None that directly invalidate the core animating-phase contribution.*

### Major

- **Evaluation does not substantiate the 6-DoF / free-viewpoint claim.** The paper's headline promise (Abstract, Sec. 1, Fig. 1) is "free-viewpoint," "6-DoF virtual tours," and "arbitrary angles." Yet the evaluation in Sec. 4.1 tests only cameras with p=0 (origin-centered rotation), plus perturbations of α=0.05. The perturbed distance of 0.05 units is tiny and constitutes essentially viewpoint rotation, not genuine camera translation. No metric evaluates parallax correctness, cross-view depth agreement, or rendering quality under meaningful camera displacements. The claim of 4D/6-DoF capability is thus asserted but not demonstrated empirically.

- **Only one weak baseline is evaluated.** The sole comparator is 3D-Cinemagraphy, an optical-flow-based dynamic image technique that is neither a 4D generation method nor designed for panoramic output. The paper justifies excluding SDS-based 4D methods because they are "object-centered," which is fair; however, a more competitive baseline like DreamScene360 (cited in Sec. 2 as a panoramic 3DGS generator) combined with an off-the-shelf I2V animation model would be far more relevant and would still be likely to underscore the proposed method's advantages. As-is, Table 1 only shows the proposed method beats a mismatched older baseline on no-reference perceptual scores.

- **The 4K claim is partially misleading.** The title, abstract, and contributions repeatedly emphasize "4K" output. However, perspective views used during the animating phase are only 512×512 (Sec. 4.1). The actual dynamic content is generated at low resolution and lifted via Gaussians; the 4K label refers to the input/output panorama resolution, but detailed high-frequency content in the animated regions is generated at sub-4K scale. The paper does not measure whether the dynamic regions in the 4K output contain 4K-level detail or merely inherit the static input's resolution.

- **Small, synthetic evaluation dataset.** The benchmark consists of only 16 panoramas, all generated by a text-to-panorama diffusion model (Sec. 4.1). This is extremely small by generative model standards and fails to assess robustness on real captured panoramas, which are the primary motivating use case.

### Minor

- **Geometric regularization may have an inconsistency.** In Eq. (5), the geometric regularization term is: L_geo = 1 − Cov(R_D(G_t, 0, d_i), Θ(γ(I, d_i))). The depth reference Θ(γ(**I**, d_i)) uses the **static input panorama** I, not the t-th video frame I_t. This means all time-indexed Gaussians are regularized toward the depth of the original static scene, which may suppress legitimate dynamic depth changes (e.g., billowing smoke or flowing water that changes depth over time). This may be intentional to prevent drift, but the choice is not motivated and could harm the quality of the dynamic representation.

- **Eq. 4 formulation is ambiguous.** The depth alignment objective contains an outer argmin over S and an inner min over views i. Minimizing over the single best-matching view rather than summing over all views would be geometrically problematic. This likely represents shorthand notation, but as written it creates ambiguity about how depth from multiple views is fused. The paper should clarify whether this is a sum, expectation, or true min.

- **Missing runtime measurements.** The paper claims "real-time rendering" as a key practical advantage, and the Panoramic Denoiser runs 20 perspective denoising passes per denoising step. Neither rendering FPS nor wall-clock time for either pipeline stage is reported anywhere.

- **Evaluation relies entirely on no-reference metrics.** Q-Align scores measure perceptual plausibility but are insensitive to cross-view geometric consistency, temporal stability under view change, or correct 3D structure—which are the properties that matter most for the claimed 6-DoF immersive output. No consistency-specific metric (e.g., cross-view feature matching or flicker metrics at translated novel views) is used.

### Trivial

- **Text prompts in conclusion**: The conclusion states users can create 4D content "using text prompts," but the pipeline is single-panorama-input + mask. Text prompts appear only informally in Figure examples. This minor claim mismatch should be corrected.
- **Notation ambiguity in Sec. 3.3**: S^0 is used to denote both the initial noisy spherical latent and the final clean latent, creating minor ambiguity in the description of the denoising process.

## Novel Insights

The most genuinely novel insight in the paper is the connection between multi-view denoising consistency (previously exploited in MultiDiffusion/SyncDiffusion for static panoramic images) and the temporal animation domain: by simultaneously denoising overlapping perspective projections of a spherical latent at each diffusion step, the method achieves cross-view temporal consistency without requiring panoramic video training data. The ablation (Table 2) makes a concrete, measurable case that this outperforms both naive baselines. The paper demonstrates that the "overlap alignment" property of joint multi-view denoising extends naturally from the spatial panorama generation setting to the temporal video animation setting.

## Suggestions

1. **Replace p=0-only evaluation with meaningful camera translation experiments**: Render from cameras at α=0.1, 0.3, 0.5 and report how quality degrades — this directly tests the 6-DoF claim.
2. **Add a DreamScene360 + I2V baseline**: Even if it fails on 360° consistency, showing *how* it fails strengthens the paper's justification.
3. **Separate the "4K" claim into resolution tiers**: Report metrics separately for dynamic regions (which are animated at lower effective resolution) vs. static background, or add a super-resolution stage to justify the headline claim.
4. **Clarify the depth regularization index**: Explicitly state whether L_geo in Eq. (5) uses I (static input) or I_t (animated frame at time t) and motivate the choice.
5. **Specify the fusion rule in Eq. (3)**: State explicitly whether the argmin is solved by weighted averaging of back-projected latents, gradient descent, or another mechanism, and report the cost per denoising step.
6. **Report FPS and total pipeline time** so practitioners can assess practical VR/AR suitability.
