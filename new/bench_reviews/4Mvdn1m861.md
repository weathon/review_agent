## Summary

The paper proposes TokenCount, a training-free object counting framework that leverages SAM's decoder output tokens (rather than image encoder embeddings) for instance verification, combined with an iterative probabilistic prompt generation strategy that uses image embedding similarity to guide prompt sampling. The key idea is that SAM's output tokens encode both semantic and positional information through bidirectional attention, enabling better discrimination between positive and negative instances than raw image embeddings. The method achieves MAE 16.25 on FSC-147 (best among SAM-only training-free methods) and MAE 4.68 on CARPK (outperforming all listed supervised methods).

## Strengths

- **Novel insight of using SAM's output tokens for verification**: The core idea of repurposing SAM's decoder output tokens—which encode both semantic and positional information through bidirectional attention—for instance verification is genuinely novel and well-motivated. The PCA visualization in Figure 5 provides compelling qualitative evidence that output tokens form clearly separable positive/negative clusters where image embeddings overlap. This is supported by the ablation in Table 5 (output token: MAE 16.25 vs. image embedding: MAE 16.94).

- **Best performance among SAM-only training-free methods on FSC-147**: On FSC-147, the method achieves MAE 16.25, substantially outperforming all SAM-only training-free baselines—TFOC (19.95), Count-Anything (27.97), and plain SAM (42.48)—and even the training-based Counting-DETR (16.79). It also outperforms SAM+α methods OmniCount (18.63) and TFCounter (18.56) without requiring auxiliary models (Table 1).

- **Impressive CARPK results**: The method achieves MAE 4.68 and RMSE 6.13 on CARPK, outperforming every listed training-based method including BMNet+ (5.76), SAFECount (5.33), and LOCA (9.97). This demonstrates the approach excels in structured, homogeneous counting scenarios (Table 1).

- **Practical advantage of training-free, SAM-only deployment**: The method requires no additional foundation models (unlike TFCAC which needs DINO), no training, and no architectural modifications to SAM, making it a meaningful practical contribution for deployment scenarios with limited resources.

- **Principled probabilistic prompt generation with iterative expansion**: The probability distribution update mechanism—zeroing verified positive regions and decreasing negative regions (Figure 3)—is a well-designed strategy that prevents redundant sampling while expanding search coverage. Table 4 shows consistent MAE improvement as prompts per iteration increase.

### Fatal

### Major

- **Abstract overclaims: "outperforming existing training-based and training-free counting methods" is false by the paper's own data**: The abstract states the method achieves "superior accuracy with an MAE of 16.25, outperforming existing training-based and training-free counting methods." Table 1 directly contradicts this: at least four training-based methods achieve lower MAE—LOCA (10.79), PseCo (13.05), SAFECount (14.32), BMNet+ (14.62)—and the training-free TFCAC achieves MAE 12.26. The defensible claim is much narrower: best among SAM-only training-free methods. The body text (Section 4.2) is appropriately cautious ("our proposed method achieved the best performance among training-free SAM only methods"), making the abstract's broader claim particularly misleading. This matters because the overclaiming inflates perceived impact and misrepresents the method's actual standing.

- **High RMSE relative to MAE indicates severe outlier failures that the paper ignores**: On FSC-147, the proposed method has MAE 16.25 but RMSE 133.11—a ratio of ~8.2, far exceeding comparable methods (LOCA: ~5.3, BMNet+: ~6.3, TFCAC: ~4.6). More critically, the RMSE of 133.11 is *worse* than TFOC (132.16) and Count-Anything (131.24), methods the paper claims to outperform. This implies the method has catastrophic counting errors on some images that dominate the RMSE, while the narrative exclusively highlights MAE improvements. The paper's claim that output tokens provide "reliable" verification is directly contradicted by these extreme outliers. The paper should acknowledge this discrepancy, analyze failure cases, and explain why such extreme errors occur.

### Minor

- **TS-SS metric shows marginal improvement over cosine similarity**: Table 3 shows Cosine Similarity MAE = 16.48 vs. TS-SS MAE = 16.25, an improvement of only 0.23 MAE. The paper devotes substantial theoretical justification (Section 3.2) to arguing that standard metrics are "inadequate for reliably verifying these dual-natured tokens" because cosine similarity "discards all vector magnitude information." Yet cosine similarity performs nearly identically. While the paper argues that the near-parity of non-hybrid metrics motivates a hybrid approach, the empirical gain is modest relative to the theoretical buildup. The contribution of TS-SS as a "key component" is overstated.

- **Method relies on image embedding similarity for prompt generation despite criticizing it**: Equation (1) computes the initial probability distribution using cosine similarity between image embeddings. The introduction explicitly criticizes "the inherent uncertainty of the similarity metric derived from [SAM's] image encoder." While using image embeddings for coarse localization and output tokens for fine verification is a defensible two-stage design, the paper does not analyze how often the image-embedding-based distribution fails to cover all target regions, which determines the method's ceiling performance. A failure mode analysis of the prompt generation stage would strengthen the paper.

- **Incomplete ablation of critical hyperparameters**: The temperature parameter τ is specified as a "t-scale factor of 0.066" but its relationship to the τ in Equation (1) is ambiguous. The verification threshold (300) is stated but only tested at one value. The early stopping criterion (IoU > 0.95, patience 5) is not ablated. Without sensitivity analysis for these parameters, it is difficult to assess robustness.

- **Efficiency concern with high prompt count**: Table 4 shows the best configuration uses a mean of 1438.22 prompts, requiring many SAM forward passes. The paper acknowledges this but does not quantify the efficiency-accuracy tradeoff or compare FLOPs/inference time systematically with other methods (only reporting 1.69s/image for the proposed method).

- **CARPK evaluation uses 12 exemplars without clarifying parity across methods**: The paper states "we randomly selected 12 objects from the training set to use as exemplars" for CARPK. It is unclear whether other compared methods use the same number of exemplars on this dataset. If the proposed method benefits from more exemplars, the favorable CARPK comparison may be partially confounded.

## Novel Insights

The PCA visualization in Figure 5 reveals an interesting structural property of SAM's internal representations: the decoder's output tokens naturally separate positive and negative instances into distinct clusters, while the encoder's image embeddings produce overlapping distributions. This suggests that SAM's cross-attention mechanism effectively injects prompt-specific semantic-positional binding into the output tokens, creating a representation space that is inherently more discriminative for instance matching than the encoder's features. This observation has implications beyond counting—it suggests that output tokens from promptable foundation models may generally serve as better feature spaces for downstream verification/retrieval tasks than the more commonly used encoder embeddings.

- **Moderate the abstract claims**: Replace "outperforming existing training-based and training-free counting methods" with the defensible claim: "outperforming existing SAM-only training-free counting methods and achieving competitive performance with methods requiring additional models."

- **Clarify the relationship between the "t-scale factor" and τ**: Make explicit whether the 0.066 value corresponds to the τ in Equation (1), and ablate at least the verification threshold to demonstrate robustness.
