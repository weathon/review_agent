## Summary

The paper proposes the Super Robot View Transformer (S-RVT), a framework that augments virtual view-based manipulation methods (RVT and RVT-2) with three components: a Super Point Renderer (S-PR) to handle occlusion artifacts, a Super-resolution Multi-View Transformer (S-MVT) to produce higher-resolution heatmaps, and a Hierarchical Sampling Policy (HSP) for coarse-to-fine 3D pose estimation. S-RVT2 (S-RVT applied to RVT-2) achieves 87.8% average success on RLBench's 18 tasks, surpassing RVT-2's 81.4%, with particularly dramatic gains on high-precision tasks like Insert Peg (86% vs 40%).

## Strengths

- **Strong empirical improvements on an established benchmark**: S-RVT2 achieves a 6.4% absolute improvement over RVT-2 on the standard 18-task RLBench benchmark (Table 1), with striking gains on precision-demanding tasks: Insert Peg (40%→86%), Sort Shape (35%→71.3%), and Stack Cups (69%→90%). These are substantial and clearly demonstrated improvements.
- **SPR is a well-motivated, effective contribution**: The insight that occlusion from the robot arm and tabletop can be addressed by preprocessing the point cloud before rendering (using DBSCAN color-space clustering to remove the tabletop while retaining the arm) is simple, practical, and yields the largest ablation contribution for S-RVT2 at 5.5% (Table 2, Row 2). Figure 3 provides clear visual evidence of its effect.
- **Systematic and comprehensive ablation design**: Table 2 tests each component (SPR, HSP, focal loss, uncertainty weighting), the number of views, and the upsampling factor for both S-RVT and S-RVT2. This provides useful practical guidance, e.g., 4 views is optimal (Rows 6–7, 16–17), and super-resolution factor matters more for S-RVT than S-RVT2 (Rows 8–9, 18–19).
- **Generality as a boosting framework**: The approach successfully enhances both RVT (S-RVT: +10.5%) and RVT-2 (S-RVT2: +6.4%), demonstrating it is not tailored to a single base architecture.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **The ablations reveal that two of three proposed components (HSP and focal loss) contribute minimally to S-RVT2**: For S-RVT2, removing HSP costs only 0.9% (Row 3) and removing focal loss only 0.6% (Row 4), while SPR contributes 5.5% (Row 2). The paper's narrative presents three equally important innovations, but in the headline model, SPR alone plus the inherited RVT-2 zoom-in mechanism carries the improvement. The paper acknowledges that "HSP is crucial for accurate 3D pose estimation" based on the S-RVT ablation (6.5% drop) but does not discuss why HSP becomes near-redundant in S-RVT2, which strongly suggests the zoom-in mechanism subsumes most of HSP's function. This asymmetry should be analyzed and discussed honestly.

- **The "nearly two-fold improvement over existing methods" claim in the abstract is misleading in scope**: The abstract broadly states "S-RVT exhibits nearly a two-fold improvement over existing methods," but this 2× claim applies only to selected high-precision tasks (Insert Peg: 40%→86%, Sort Shape: 35%→71.3%), not to the overall benchmark. The "115% relative improvement" in Section 1 cherry-picks RVT-2's weakest task (Insert Peg at 40%) as the baseline. Other methods achieve 27% on Insert Peg (Act3D), so the 2× claim is accurate relative to RVT-2 but conflated with "existing methods" in a way that inflates the contribution. The improvement on the overall metric is 6.4%, which is still meaningful but far from "nearly two-fold."

### Minor

- **S-RVT2's zoom-in mechanism is not discussed beyond stating it is inherited from RVT-2**: While the paper is transparent that "S-RVT2 only adds a zoom-in process" (Section 3), the interaction between the zoom-in and HSP (both coarse-to-fine mechanisms) is never analyzed. The near-zero contribution of HSP in S-RVT2 (0.9%) vs. its large contribution in S-RVT (6.5%) directly points to redundancy, but this is not explored. The paper would be strengthened by discussing whether zoom-in subsumes HSP or what the architectural implications are.

- **Real-world evaluation is anecdotal**: Only 10 test episodes per task with no baseline comparison (Table 3). For "plug charger" with 1 variation, 60% success means 6/10 — essentially a coin flip given the statistical noise. The claim of demonstrating "generalization capability" is overstated for this sample size. However, real-world robotics experiments are inherently resource-constrained, and simulation results remain the primary evidence.

- **The epistemic/aleatoric uncertainty framing adds conceptual overhead without analytical rigor**: SPR addresses occlusion, which the paper terms "aleatoric uncertainty," but robot arm occlusion is a deterministic artifact of the rendering setup rather than inherent data variability. The mapping is somewhat loose and does not guide the technical choices beyond labeling. This is a framing concern, not a technical one.

## Removed Points

- **Claim that S-RVT2 is "under-specified" and "irreproducible"**: The paper explicitly states S-RVT2 = S-RVT + RVT-2's zoom-in, and the zoom-in mechanism is published in the cited RVT-2 paper. This is not an unspecified mystery component — it is a clearly stated combination of the proposed modules with an existing baseline mechanism. The concern about interaction with HSP is valid (moved to Minor), but the "under-specified" framing is overstated.

- **Cross-entropy notation issue**: The harsh critic flagged that Eqs. 2 for rotation and gripper state "appear in a non-standard binary cross-entropy form despite these being multi-class classification problems." This is a notation/clarity concern at best — the mathematical content is correct (cross-entropy for classification). This is trivial and removed.

- **Missing hyperparameters for HSP (coarse/fine resolution, search radius)**: These implementation details may be in the Appendix (which was stripped). The conceptual description of HSP is clear (coarse sampling then fine refinement), and the paper provides Figure 4 and algorithmic explanation. Missing exact numbers is a minor reproducibility detail, not a fundamental issue.

- **Row 20 (60.2%) vs. RVT (62.9%) discrepancy**: The "all off" S-RVT configuration retains structural differences from the original RVT (e.g., the EUCB upsampling block, conditional rotation/gripper sampling), which are not among the ablated components and are part of the new S-MVT backbone. The 2.7% gap is expected and not a discrepancy.

- **DBSCAN failure modes**: The concern about color similarity between objects and tabletop is reasonable but speculative — RLBench environments have distinct colors for tabletops and task objects, and the paper demonstrates this works empirically across 18 tasks. Acknowledging limitations would be a nice-to-have, not a weakness.

- **Act3D input resolution difference**: The paper already notes Act3D uses 256×256 vs. 128×128 for other methods. This is transparent to the reader and S-RVT2 outperforms Act3D (87.8% vs 65.0%) by a large margin regardless.

## Novel Insights

The ablation reveals a striking asymmetry: the three proposed components (SPR, HSP, focal loss) are presented as a unified triple innovation, yet in the headline model S-RVT2, only SPR provides meaningful improvement (5.5%). HSP (0.9%) and focal loss (0.6%) are largely subsumed by the inherited zoom-in mechanism. This suggests that for the model achieving the best overall results, the paper's core contribution simplifies to: (1) SPR for occlusion handling + (2) building on RVT-2's zoom-in. In contrast, for S-RVT (without zoom-in), all three components matter substantially (3.4%, 6.5%, 5.3% drops respectively). The paper would be more honest by foregrounding SPR and presenting HSP/focal loss as valuable specifically when zoom-in is unavailable.

## Suggestions

- Revise the abstract/introduction to accurately scope the "two-fold improvement" claim to "high-precision tasks" rather than "existing methods" broadly, and report the overall 6.4% improvement prominently.
- Add explicit analysis of why HSP and focal loss have reduced contribution in S-RVT2, discussing the interaction/overlap with the zoom-in mechanism.
- Consider whether HSP can be removed from S-RVT2 in practice to reduce computational cost, given its marginal 0.9% contribution.
