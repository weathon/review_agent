## Summary
The Super Robot View Transformer (S-RVT) is a framework that augments virtual view-based manipulation methods (RVT, RVT-2) with three components: a Super Point Renderer (S-PR) that adds a filtered fourth downward camera view using DBSCAN-based occlusion removal, a Super-resolution Multi-View Transformer (S-MVT) with 4× heatmap upsampling, and a Hierarchical Sampling Policy (HSP) for memory-efficient coarse-to-fine 3D pose estimation. The method is evaluated on 18 RLBench tasks, lifting RVT-2 from 81.4% to 87.8% average success, with especially large gains on high-precision tasks like Insert Peg (40% → 86%).

---

## Strengths

- **Consistent improvement across both base models and all 18 tasks**: S-RVT lifts RVT from 62.9% to 73.4% (+10.5%) and S-RVT2 lifts RVT-2 from 81.4% to 87.8% (+6.4%), demonstrating that the proposed components function as a general booster rather than a task-specific patch (Table 1).
- **Dramatic high-precision task gains**: Insert Peg improves from 40.0% to 86.0% (+115% relative) and Sort Shape from 35.0% to 71.3% (+104% relative), directly demonstrating impact on the paper's stated focus area (Table 1).
- **Comprehensive ablation study covering all major design choices**: Table 2 provides 20 rows isolating S-PR, HSP, focal loss, uncertainty weighting, view count, and super-resolution factor. The ablation table is well-constructed and covers a useful range of configurations.
- **Non-monotonic view count finding is a useful practical insight**: Reducing to 3 views hurts (−5.9%), but adding a 5th view also hurts (−3.7%), providing actionable guidance that 4 views is an optimum (Table 2, Rows 6–7).
- **HSP addresses a concrete engineering bottleneck**: The coarse-to-fine 3D sampling directly resolves GPU memory overflow that would occur under uniform high-resolution sampling (Section 3.4), making the super-resolution approach computationally feasible.
- **Figure 3 clearly demonstrates the S-PR's occlusion advantage**: Side-by-side visualization shows the ring and azure spoke positions are unintelligible under standard PR but clearly visible under S-PR's filtered down view.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **The dominant contributor (S-PR) conflates adding a new camera view with rendering quality improvements, and the critical disentangling ablation is absent.** RVT-2 uses three views (top, front, right); S-RVT2 uses four (top, front, right, down). The "all-off" baseline (Row 10: no S-PR, no HSP, no focal, no uncertainty, 3 views, SR=1) recovers exactly RVT-2's 81.4%. But the paper never tests the simplest possible augmentation: **RVT-2 + 4th down view with no other changes**. Row 2 (no S-PR, 4 views, but with HSP + focal + uncertainty) gives 82.3% — only 0.9% above RVT-2 with the other components included, suggesting the 4th view without proper filtering is nearly valueless. However, this still conflates the effect of the extra components with the view. Conversely, Row 6 (S-PR, 3 views) = 81.9%, only 0.5% above RVT-2. The 5.5% gain from S-PR (Row 2 vs Row 1) is inseparable from the fact that S-PR's filtering is precisely what makes the new down view useful. Without a row of "RVT-2 + raw 4th view (no filtering, no other S-RVT components)", readers cannot determine whether the claimed architectural innovation (S-PR filtering) or the mere addition of a new camera perspective drives the results. This is the most important missing ablation.

- **HSP and super-resolution — the two most technically novel components — contribute near-zero improvement to the headline S-RVT2 result.** In Table 2 for S-RVT2: removing HSP is only −0.9% (Row 3), reducing SR from 4× to 1× is only −0.9% (Row 9). Yet the paper's framing places these components as co-equal with S-PR in addressing "epistemic uncertainty." By contrast, these components contribute 6.5% and 9.9% respectively for the simpler S-RVT base (Rows 13, 19). The paper acknowledges this discrepancy but explains it by noting that the zoom-in in RVT-2 "subsumes" the precision benefit (Section 3 states "S-RVT2 only adds a zoom-in process compared to S-RVT"), yet this crucial explanatory statement does not appear in the main-text discussion of ablations. As a result, the contribution narrative — that HSP and SR enable "high-precision manipulation" — is not supported by the S-RVT2 ablations for the claimed headline system.

- **The S-RVT "all-off" baseline (60.2%) is notably lower than the published RVT (62.9%), an unexplained discrepancy of −2.7%.** If S-RVT's 13.2% gain reported in Table 2 is measured against a weaker-than-published baseline, the true improvement over published RVT would be +10.5% (as in Table 1), but the ablation table slightly overstates the magnitude. The paper does not acknowledge or explain this discrepancy (Row 20 of Table 2), which affects the calibration of all ablation deltas.

### Minor

- **The epistemic/aleatoric uncertainty framing does not map cleanly onto the implemented components.** Occlusion is labeled "aleatoric" but resolving it by adding a new camera viewpoint is an information gain (reducing observational incompleteness) — more accurately described as reducing epistemic uncertainty about scene geometry. The uncertainty weighting in Eq. 3 is a standard multi-task loss balancer (Kendall et al. 2018) and has no mechanistic connection to the epistemic vs. aleatoric categories established in the introduction. The framework is internally consistent enough not to invalidate results, but readers will find the conceptual scaffolding does not deepen understanding of why the components work.

- **The real-world evaluation has no baseline comparisons.** Table 3 reports S-RVT at 50–80% success on 4 tasks with 10 test episodes each, but provides no comparison to RVT-2, a static-camera BC policy, or any other method on the same hardware setup. As such, the section provides qualitative plausibility but not comparative validation of S-RVT's claimed advantages on real hardware.

### Trivial

- The EUCB upsampling module's architectural details are deferred to the appendix (Section 3.3); a one-sentence summary of its structure in the main text would improve readability.

## Novel Insights

The most insightful empirical observation in this paper is the asymmetry between S-RVT and S-RVT2 in which components matter: HSP and super-resolution are highly impactful for S-RVT (−6.5% and −9.9% respectively when removed) but nearly inert for S-RVT2 (−0.9% each). This implies that the zoom-in procedure in RVT-2 already addresses the 3D precision bottleneck that HSP and SR were designed to solve, and the two systems are solving different bottlenecks. This has a general architectural implication: hierarchical sampling and super-resolution heatmaps are most valuable when the base model lacks a zoom-in mechanism, but become redundant once a spatial refinement procedure is built into the backbone. The remaining bottleneck for RVT-2-style systems is observational coverage — which S-PR addresses — rather than model resolution. This framing is more accurate than the paper's co-equal three-component narrative.

## Suggestions

1. **Add the missing ablation row: RVT-2 + 4th view only (no S-PR filtering, no HSP, no focal, SR=1)**. This single experiment would let the community understand how much of the 5.5% gain comes from rendering quality vs. camera geometry.
2. **Reframe the contribution honestly**: The main contribution of S-RVT2 is S-PR (filtered 4th down view). HSP and SR earn their keep primarily in the S-RVT (RVT-based) variant. The paper should make this asymmetry explicit rather than presenting all three as co-equal for the headline result.
3. **Explain the 60.2% vs. 62.9% baseline discrepancy** (Row 20 of Table 2 vs. published RVT) to clarify whether the S-RVT improvement is measured against a weaker-than-canonical baseline.
4. **Run at least one baseline on the real-world tasks** (e.g., vanilla RVT or RVT-2 on the same tasks) to provide comparative evidence.
