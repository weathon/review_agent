## Summary

This paper proposes the Super Robot View Transformer (S-RVT), an extension of RVT/RVT-2 for multi-task robotic manipulation that introduces three architectural components: a Super Point Renderer (SPR) with occlusion handling and an additional viewpoint, a Super-resolution Multi-View Transformer (S-MVT) that upsamples heatmaps for finer spatial precision, and a Hierarchical Sampling Policy (HSP) for memory-efficient 3D pose extraction from high-resolution heatmaps. On the RLBench benchmark, S-RVT2 achieves an 87.8% average success rate across 18 tasks, a 6.4 percentage-point improvement over RVT-2 (81.4%), with particularly large gains on high-precision tasks such as *Insert Peg* (86.0% vs. 40.0%) and *Sort Shape* (71.3% vs. 35.0%). The paper also includes real-world few-shot experiments on a physical xArm7.

## Strengths

- **Substantial empirical advance on a standard benchmark.** Table 1 shows S-RVT2 establishes a new state-of-the-art mean success rate on RLBench, with absolute gains of 6.4% over RVT-2 and 10.5% over RVT. The improvements on high-precision tasks are especially pronounced and well-documented.
- **Comprehensive ablation study.** Table 2 systematically isolates the impact of SPR, HSP, focal loss, uncertainty weighting, viewpoint count, and super-resolution factor across both S-RVT and S-RVT2 variants. This transparency allows readers to assess which design choices matter in which setting.
- **Qualitative evidence for the renderer.** Figure 3 provides intuitive visual comparisons showing that SPR mitigates occlusion artifacts, particularly in the novel *down* view, making the renderer’s contribution tangible.
- **Real-world proof of concept.** Section 4.4 demonstrates that a single S-RVT model trained on 15–20 human demonstrations per task can generalize to four diverse physical tasks, including the high-precision *plug charger* task.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **The epistemic/aleatoric theoretical framing is conceptually sloppy.** The paper maps SPR to *aleatoric* uncertainty (occlusion) and S-MVT/HSP to *epistemic* uncertainty (model limitations/discretization). However, occlusion from unmodeled scene geometry is a partial-observability problem, not irreducible aleatoric noise, and discretization is a fixed modeling bias rather than Bayesian epistemic uncertainty. Because the paper uses this taxonomy as its conceptual backbone, the theoretical scaffolding mischaracterizes the phenomena the method actually addresses. This weakens the conceptual contribution but does not invalidate the empirical results.
- **The differing module contributions across S-RVT and S-RVT2 are under-analyzed.** For the base S-RVT model, the ablation shows that HSP (–6.5%), focal loss (–5.3%), and super-resolution (–9.9%) are all critical. For S-RVT2, however, SPR dominates (–5.5%) while HSP (–0.9%), focal loss (–0.6%), and super-resolution (–0.9%) contribute marginally. The paper frames all three modules as “collaboratively” driving precision gains, yet does not explain why S-RVT2’s zoom-in mechanism apparently obviates the need for fine heatmap resolution and dense sampling. This discrepancy should be explicitly confronted rather than obscured.

### Minor
- **Real-world evaluation lacks baselines and statistical power.** Section 4.4 reports only 10 test episodes per task with no comparison to RVT-2 or even the ablated Row-20 equivalent. With such a small sample, the reported success rates cannot support claims of superiority outside simulation. The section is better framed as a preliminary feasibility study.
- **Minor mischaracterization of prior work.** The introduction describes Diffusion Policy and ACT as “task-specific” methods, but both are frequently trained multi-task. This is inaccurate and weakens the novelty framing.
- **Ablated RVT-equivalent underperforms published baseline.** Row 20 of Table 2 (60.2%) falls 2.7 points below the published RVT result (62.9%). The paper does not explain this gap, which raises questions about implementation parity. However, because the full S-RVT (73.4%) and S-RVT2 (87.8%) are evaluated under the same codebase, the relative improvements remain meaningful.

### Trivial
None.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Quantitative GPU memory and latency numbers comparing uniform dense sampling to HSP, to substantiate the claim that uniform sampling is infeasible at high resolution.
- A continuous-action regression baseline (e.g., direct SE(3) regression) to test whether heatmap discretization—however fine—is the right paradigm for high-precision manipulation.
- Side-by-side heatmap visualizations from RVT-2 and S-MVT for the same high-precision task to verify that super-resolution produces sharper peaks.

## Removed Points
These points are flagged to be removed, treat them with caution.

- **“Ablations falsify the collaborative three-module claim.”** This is overstated. While SPR dominates the S-RVT2 ablation, the S-RVT ablation shows that HSP (–6.5%), focal loss (–5.3%), and super-resolution (–9.9%) are all critical. S-RVT2 incorporates RVT-2’s zoom-in process, which likely reduces reliance on fine heatmap resolution; the paper should explain this shift, but the claim is not empirically falsified.
- **“The mechanism contradicts the stated goal of overcoming discretization.”** The paper explicitly proposes super-resolution for “finer-grained manipulation” (Abstract) and identifies “coarse-grained action space” as the limitation (Introduction). It does not claim to eliminate discretization entirely; upsampling produces finer discretization, which is consistent with the stated goal.
- **Concerns about baseline numbers being copied from prior papers.** The paper states that all baselines use 128×128 inputs. Reporting prior published numbers with variance bars where available is standard practice and not a methodological flaw.
- **Concerns about ±0.0 variance for RVT-2.** Deterministic policies evaluated on fixed test variations can yield zero variance across 5 rollouts; this is not inherently implausible.
- **DBSCAN robustness concerns and missing GPU memory evidence.** These are minor engineering details that do not threaten the core empirical claims.

## Novel Insights

The paper reveals an interesting empirical pattern: when combined with RVT-2’s zoom-in mechanism (S-RVT2), the renderer contribution dominates, while super-resolution and hierarchical sampling become less critical. This suggests that occlusion handling and viewpoint coverage may be the primary bottleneck for high-precision manipulation in the RVT family, and that zoom-in partially substitutes for fine heatmap resolution. Future work could explore whether SPR alone as a drop-in renderer for RVT-2 captures most of the gain, and whether the remaining modules are only necessary for the base RVT architecture without zoom-in.

## Suggestions

- Revise the epistemic/aleatoric framing to use standard terminology (e.g., partial observability and discretization bias) or remove the taxonomy entirely and motivate the modules directly by the engineering problems they solve.
- Add an explicit discussion of why S-RVT2 ablations differ from S-RVT ablations—specifically, why HSP and super-resolution matter less when zoom-in is available.
- Increase real-world test episodes to at least 20–30 per task and include a baseline comparison (e.g., RVT-2 or the ablated Row-20 equivalent) to support generalization claims.
