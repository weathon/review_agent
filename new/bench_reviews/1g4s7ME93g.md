Let me now write the review.</think>## Summary

The paper proposes Super Robot View Transformer (S-RVT), an enhancement to the Robot View Transformer (RVT) family of methods for multi-task robotic manipulation. S-RVT introduces three modules: (1) a Super Point Renderer using DBSCAN-based color-space clustering and orthographic projection to reduce occlusion artifacts, (2) a Super-resolution Multi-View Transformer that upsamples heatmap outputs for finer action resolution, and (3) a Hierarchical Sampling Policy that recovers 3D poses from multi-view 2D heatmaps via coarse-to-fine sampling. Evaluated on 18 RLBench tasks, S-RVT2 achieves 87.8% average success (vs. 81.4% for RVT-2), with particularly large gains on high-precision tasks (Insert Peg: 86% vs. 40%).

## Strengths

- **Large empirical gains on high-precision tasks**: The improvements on tasks like Insert Peg (40%→86%) and Sort Shape (35%→71%) are substantial and clearly demonstrate that better occlusion handling and higher-resolution action representations matter for precision manipulation. These are the hardest tasks in the benchmark and where prior methods fail most visibly.

- **Comprehensive ablation study**: Table 2 systematically varies SPR, HSP, focal loss, uncertainty weighting, number of views, and upsampling factor across both S-RVT and S-RVT2, providing useful design insights. The ablation shows each component contributes positively to the full system (e.g., removing SPR from S-RVT causes a 3.4% drop; removing HSP from S-RVT causes a 6.5% drop).

- **Practical solution to real engineering bottlenecks**: HSP directly addresses the GPU memory overflow problem of dense 3D sampling at high resolution. This is a legitimate systems-level contribution that enables the use of super-resolution heatmaps in practice.

- **Real-world feasibility demonstration**: The paper includes real-robot experiments across 4 tasks including a precision task (plug charger, 60%), which demonstrates the method can be deployed beyond simulation.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **The "uncertainty" framing is not technically realized and overclaims the paper's conceptual contribution**: The paper is centrally framed as addressing "epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty" (§1, §2.3, §5). However, the actual technical contributions are: (i) better point cloud rendering to handle occlusions, (ii) higher-resolution output heatmaps with focal loss, and (iii) a coarse-to-fine sampling heuristic. None of these model, quantify, or reduce uncertainty in the principled sense of Kendall & Gal (2017). "Aleatoric uncertainty" is equated to occlusions; "epistemic uncertainty" to discretization—these are metaphors, not uncertainty-reduction mechanisms. The only "uncertainty" term in the loss (Eq. 3) is Kendall et al.'s homoscedastic task weighting, which balances loss magnitudes across heads rather than modeling data noise or posterior uncertainty. No uncertainty estimates, calibration metrics, or OOD-aware behavior are reported. This mismatch between narrative and substance is significant because the uncertainty framing is the paper's primary conceptual hook—it appears in the abstract, introduction, related work, and conclusion.

- **Individual component contributions to S-RVT2's gains over RVT-2 are small and not statistically isolated**: While the ablations in Table 2 show each module helps, many effects on S-RVT2 are modest (removing HSP: −0.9%; removing focal loss: −0.6%; removing uncertainty weighting: −1.7%). Given RLBench's stochasticity (sampling-based motion planner, 5 evaluation rollouts per task per variation), these differences may not be statistically distinguishable from noise, and no confidence intervals on the aggregate average success rate are reported. The paper claims a "general boosting framework" but does not cleanly establish that all three modules are necessary or important for S-RVT2's headline result. Row 10 (81.4%) is called the "base without our components" but may not be an exact reimplementation of RVT-2—it differs in views (3 vs. 4) and SR factor, making attribution to the proposed modules ambiguous.

- **Real-world evaluation lacks baselines and is too limited to support generalization claims**: The conclusion claims S-RVT "demonstrates the generalization capability in imitation learning" from real-world results, but Table 3 provides only 4 tasks with 10 test episodes each (40 episodes total), no comparison to any baseline (RVT, RVT-2), and no standard deviations. The success rates (50–80%) are notably lower than simulation counterparts. Without a baseline, it is impossible to determine whether S-RVT improves over RVT/RVT-2 in the real world, or whether the real-world capability is simply inherited from the base method.

### Minor

- **Task-specific regressions are not discussed**: S-RVT2 underperforms RVT-2 on Slide Block (84% vs. 92%) and Place Wine (95.3% vs. 97.3% isn't really a regression, but S-RVT is clearly worse on Screw Bulb at 54% vs 88%). Since the paper positions S-RVT2 as a "general boosting framework," acknowledging and analyzing these task-specific trade-offs would strengthen the contribution.

- **The Super Point Renderer's DBSCAN-based filtering lacks robustness analysis**: S-PR filters point clouds using color-space clustering to remove "occluding elements like the tabletop." This assumes color separability between furniture and task objects—sensitivity to mis-segmentation, textured surfaces, or similarly-colored objects is never analyzed. The paper also does not explain whether/how S-PR operates in the real-world setup where there is a single external camera rather than four virtual cameras.

- **Key implementation details are missing for HSP and super-resolution**: The number of particles, search radius, and fine-grid resolution for HSP are not specified; the focal loss hyperparameters (α, γ) are not given. These directly affect the action-space resolution claims central to the paper.

- **Rotation loss notation is inconsistent**: Equation (2) writes the rotation and gripper losses as binary cross-entropy, but rotation is described as "quantized Euler angles into discrete bins," which would require multi-class cross-entropy. This should be clarified.

## Novel Insights

The ablation reveals an interesting pattern: HSP matters dramatically more for S-RVT (−6.5% without it) than for S-RVT2 (−0.9% without it). This suggests the "zoom-in" mechanism in RVT-2 already captures much of what HSP provides, making HSP redundant when combined with multi-resolution rendering. However, the paper does not discuss this interaction, which could have informed a more streamlined architecture design.

## Suggestions

- Tone down the "epistemic/aleatoric uncertainty" narrative significantly. The paper's real contributions are engineering improvements to virtual-view rendering, resolution, and 3D pose extraction—frame them as such rather than wrapping them in terminology from Bayesian modeling where no probabilistic uncertainty quantification is performed.
- Add RVT and RVT-2 baselines to the real-world experiments, even if only 2–3 tasks. This is essential for validating that simulated improvements transfer.
