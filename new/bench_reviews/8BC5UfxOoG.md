## Summary

This paper investigates whether example selection methods for in-context learning (ICL) amplify social biases in large language models. Through experiments across 8 LLMs and 4 example selection methods on a new dataset (EEC-paraphrase), the authors report three findings: (1) high-accuracy example selection does not guarantee low bias; (2) example selection amplifies LLM biases; (3) example selection contributes to spurious correlations. They propose ReBE (Remind with Bias-aware Embedding), which combines prompt tuning with a bias-contrastive loss to mitigate these biases while preserving ICL accuracy.

## Strengths

- **Important and under-explored problem**: The intersection of ICL example selection and social bias is genuinely novel and practically significant. Demonstrating that accuracy-optimized selection can worsen worst-case fairness failures is an important finding for responsible LLM deployment.

- **Extensive observational analysis**: Testing across 8 LLMs (LLaMA-2-7/13/70B, OPT-6.7/13/30B, GPT-J-6B, GPT-neo-2.7B) and 4 example selection methods with multiple random seeds provides a credible empirical foundation for the descriptive claims. The distinction between mean and maximum bias effects is a valuable nuance.

- **Well-designed method**: ReBE's bias-contrastive loss (pulling together same-label/different-demographic representations while pushing apart same-demographic/different-label representations) directly targets spurious correlations. Prompt tuning preserves ICL flexibility without modifying LLM parameters.

- **Thorough ablation (Table 4)**: The ablation cleanly demonstrates that L_bias is responsible for bias reduction while L_acc maintains accuracy, confirming the design rationale.

## Weaknesses

### Major:

- **The "amplification" claim is overstated relative to the evidence (Fatal framing issue)**: The title and abstract claim "example selection amplifies the biases of LLMs," but the paper's own Figure 2 shows that *mean bias often decreases* with ICL example selection, while only *maximum* bias values (over random seeds) increase. The paper acknowledges this in Section 3.3 ("although example selections reduce the mean bias value…"), but the framing remains misleading. The phenomenon is more accurately described as: example selection *increases the variance and worst-case bias* of LLMs, not that it uniformly amplifies bias. This distinction matters because "amplification" implies a systematic worsening, whereas the data shows a *trade-off*: some configurations are fairer, some are less fair. No statistical testing (confidence intervals, significance tests) is performed to assess whether the increased maxima are genuine distributional shifts or simply artifacts of sampling variability over random seeds.

- **The causal claim "example selection contributes to spurious correlations" is weakly supported**: Section 3.4 infers this claim from the observation that null (content-free) prompts show similar fear-label tendencies for male and female pronouns, while under ICL the male group has much higher sadness→fear misclassification. However, this only establishes that native model parameters don't fully explain the observed bias—it does *not* isolate "example selection" as the causal factor. Any ICL context (regardless of selection method) could produce this shift. What is needed but not provided is a controlled comparison across selection methods (or zero-shot prompts on the same test set demonstrating that the spurious correlation is absent without any in-context examples but present with them, or showing it varies systematically with selection method).

- **ReBE's evaluation is narrow and its claimed generality is overstated**: ReBE is only applied to 2 models per baseline (those with highest AvgGF), excluding the largest models (OPT-30B, Llama-2-70B). The paper's abstract and conclusion speak broadly about ReBE "effectively mitigating biases of LLMs," but this is only demonstrated on 4 small-to-medium models for gender bias, on a single task (sentiment classification). The Jigsaw toxicity results are relegated to the appendix without main-text discussion. Furthermore, Table 3 shows several cases where ReBE *increases* bias (red subscripts), particularly for Perplexity and Similarity baselines — the Perplexity + GPT-J-6B and OPT-13B rows show MaxTG and MaxFG *increasing* after "debiasing." These failures are acknowledged via color-coding but not analyzed or discussed.

### Minor:

- **EEC-paraphrase dataset concerns**: EEC-paraphrase is constructed by GPT-3.5-Turbo paraphrasing of EEC templates. The paper does not analyze whether the paraphrasing process introduces systematic semantic differences across demographic groups, which could conflate genuine content differences with measured bias. While Appendix A contains quality validation, the main text lacks discussion of this potential confound.

- **Race bias results are secondary**: Despite the paper framing its contribution as covering both gender *and* race, race bias results only appear in Appendix D.2, and no intersectional analysis is provided. Given only binary gender and two racial groups, the scope of bias evaluation is limited.

- **Missing comparison to broader debiasing methods**: Table 5 compares ReBE only against two simple prompt-level interventions (counterfactual and gender-balanced context). No comparison with established LLM debiasing methods (e.g., self-debiasing, counterfactual data augmentation, or other prompt-based mitigation) is provided, making it hard to assess ReBE's relative contribution.

### Trivial:

- The subscript notation in Tables 2–3 (e.g., "Acc_C(Min)" and parenthetical values) is not clearly defined in the table captions, making interpretation difficult without careful cross-referencing.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Systematic analysis of the α parameter (balancing L_acc and L_bias in the total loss), which directly controls the accuracy–fairness tradeoff and is crucial for practical deployment.
- Controlled experiments isolating the causal effect of example selection: e.g., comparing zero-shot prompts on the same test data, or varying selection method while controlling for prompt demographic content.
- t-SNE or similar visualization of learned bias-aware embeddings to verify that the representation space is genuinely restructured.
- Evaluation of ReBE on all 8 models used in the analysis section, not just the 2 worst-performing per baseline.

## Removed Points

- **Claim that EEC-paraphrase may not exist or be available**: The paper provides it via their anonymous repository; this is not a valid criticism.

- **Demand for larger models and more tasks as a core weakness**: The choice to exclude the two largest models is stated as a hardware limitation. While this limits generalizability claims, evaluating on 6 models with ReBE (covering 4 in main text) is reasonable for a methods paper. Downgraded to minor rather than major.

- **Overclaim about "first to discover" being unacceptable**: While the claim could be softened, this is a standard academic phrasing. The paper does cite Ma et al. (2023) and differentiates its focus. Not a substantive weakness.

- **Nitpick about missing hyperparameters in main text**: The paper references appendices for implementation details; this is standard practice and not a reproducibility issue.

- **Demand for theoretical proofs**: The paper is primarily empirical; demanding proofs for an empirical systems paper is unreasonable (per soft rules).

## Novel Insights

The separation of mean vs. maximum bias effects across random seeds is an important and underappreciated observation for ICL fairness. Most prior work reports only mean metrics; this paper shows that worst-case bias (the maximum over seed selections) can substantially worsen even as mean bias improves — a finding analogous to robustness concerns in distribution shift literature. This suggests the ICL community should adopt distributional reporting (variance, worst-case) rather than mean-only metrics when evaluating fairness, similar to how robust optimization considers tail risks.

## Suggestions

- Reframe the title and main claims to accurately reflect "worst-case bias amplification" rather than generalized "bias amplification," acknowledging the mixed mean-bias results upfront.
- Add confidence intervals or bootstrap-based bounds on the bias metrics across random seeds to strengthen claims about the distributional shift.
- Conduct a controlled experiment where zero-shot prompts (without any in-context examples) are evaluated on EEC-paraphrase to provide a proper baseline comparison for the spurious correlation claim.
- Test ReBE on at least 4–6 models (including one larger model like LLaMA-2-70B) to support the generalizability claim, and explicitly report and discuss cases where ReBE increases bias.
