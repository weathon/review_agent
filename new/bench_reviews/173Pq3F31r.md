## Summary

This paper proposes a unified transformer-based framework that jointly models expressive performance rendering (EPR) and automatic piano transcription (APT) by disentangling note-level score content and global performance style representations. The framework is trained on both paired and unpaired data using sequence-aligned (rather than note-aligned) supervision, and includes a diffusion-based performance style recommendation (PSR) module that infers style embeddings directly from score content, enabling automated rendering without manual style specification.

## Strengths

- **Alignment-free Seq2Seq formulation for EPR**: The model eliminates the need for note-level alignment—a practical bottleneck in prior EPR work—while achieving competitive results. Table 1 shows the APT model achieves Eavg=12.48, competitive with alignment-dependent baselines like Shibata et al. (13.95), and Table 3 shows Ours(PSR) achieves the highest alignment rate (92.27%) and lowest insertion rate (3.77%) among EPR models. This is a genuine and practical contribution.

- **Competitive APT performance**: Table 1 demonstrates state-of-the-art or near-state-of-the-art APT results, with the model achieving the lowest Eavg (12.48) and Espell (6.24), showing that the learned content representations capture key musical attributes effectively.

- **Novel problem framing**: The conceptual duality between EPR and APT, inspired by ASR/TTS joint modeling, is an interesting research direction that could stimulate further work on cross-task music representations.

- **PSR module concept**: The idea of automatically inferring performance style from score content (mimicking a pianist's interpretive process) is creative and addresses a real usability limitation of prior EPR systems that require manual style specification. Figure 4 provides visual evidence of era-appropriate style generation.

- **Disentanglement probe analysis**: Table 4 shows that style representations achieve much higher composer (77.46%) and performer (42.07%) identification accuracy than content representations (29.99% and 9.94%), providing useful analytical insight into what the learned representations encode, even if the disentanglement is imperfect.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Confounded comparison undermines the joint-modeling benefit claim**: The EPR-Only variant differs from Ours(Target) in two ways simultaneously: (1) joint training with APT and (2) training on additional unpaired data (75,913 scores + YouTube performances). The paper acknowledges both factors ("joint modeling, together with training on additional unpaired data") but then claims the improvement "validating the effectiveness of our joint framework" (Section 5.1, line 189). Without an ablation that trains EPR-Only with the same unpaired data but without APT supervision, one cannot attribute the improvement to joint modeling. There is also no APT-only ablation, so there is no evidence that joint modeling helps transcription. This confound undermines the paper's central motivation.

- **Overclaimed variance matching**: The paper states "our models exhibit duration and velocity variances that more closely match those of human performances compared with other baselines" (Section 5.1). For velocity variance, DExter (238.86) is far closer to Human (241.04) than Ours-Target (151.03) or Ours-PSR (161.51). For onset variance, DExter (0.20) is also closer to Human (0.12) than both Ours variants (0.02). The paper's claim is incorrect for 2 of 3 variance metrics. While the paper immediately qualifies DExter's closer variance as "unstable dynamics rather than meaningful expressiveness," this post-hoc explanation does not justify the factually incorrect statement that the authors' models more closely match human variance. This should be corrected.

- **Incomplete disentanglement evidence**: The content representation achieves 29.99% composer classification accuracy—far above random chance (~4% for 25 composers)—indicating substantial style-relevant information leaks into the content space. The paper's explanation (composers have characteristic pitch/rhythmic patterns) is reasonable but actually undermines the disentanglement claim: if content encodes composer-specific patterns, then style transfer that swaps only the style vector while retaining composer information in content will not fully transfer style. Additionally, the style transfer evaluation (Figure 5) has only 3 test cases with no reported number of participants or statistical significance, making it anecdotal. No standard disentanglement metrics (e.g., DCI, MIG) or adversarial regularization to enforce separation are provided.

### Minor

- **Limited EPR baselines**: The paper compares EPR against only VirtuosoNet (2019) and DExter (2024), despite discussing several directly relevant recent methods (ScorePerformer/Borovik & Viro 2023, Tang et al. 2023, Renault et al. 2023) in the related work section without including them as baselines. While two baselines provide some comparison, the "competitive performance" claim would be stronger with more contemporary methods.

- **PSR evaluation is predominantly visual**: Figure 4 shows era-level clustering of PSR-generated vs. real style vectors, but no quantitative metrics (e.g., Fréchet distance, nearest-neighbor retrieval accuracy, downstream EPR quality when using PSR vs. real style vectors) are provided. The gap between Ours-Target and Ours-PSR in Table 2 (e.g., KL(V): 1.76 vs. 2.67, MAE(V): 10.33 vs. 15.24) suggests PSR-generated styles are substantially worse, but this degradation is not analyzed.

- **No explicit disentanglement regularization**: The framework relies on architectural separation (note-level sequence vs. single global vector) and indirect supervision signals (APT loss encourages filtering style from content), with only a KL penalty on the style space (Eq. 5). There is no loss or constraint preventing content representations from encoding style information, which creates a structural incentive for content to encode style-relevant features (as confirmed by Table 4). An adversarial or mutual-information-based regularizer could strengthen both the method and its evidence.

## Nice-to-Haves

- An ablation isolating joint training from unpaired data would directly validate or refute the joint modeling claim. Training EPR-Only with the same unpaired data but without APT supervision, and an APT-only variant, would address the central confound.

- Quantitative PSR evaluation (e.g., Fréchet distance between PSR-generated and real style vector distributions, or downstream EPR metric comparison) would strengthen the PSR claims beyond visual inspection.

- An analysis of how the content representation's composer information leakage affects style transfer quality, or an adversarial content-style discriminator, would strengthen the disentanglement evidence.

This is a philosophical point about scope rather than a substantive flaw. The paper uses the analogy as motivation, not as a formal claim, and explicitly discusses differences. Removed as a scope-creep criticism.

- **APT decoder receives only z_y with no style input**: The critic argues this means APT must discard style "purely through the encoder bottleneck, with no explicit mechanism to do so." However, the APT loss itself (cross-entropy to reconstruct the score from z_y) provides an explicit training signal to discard style—the decoder doesn't need style because the target (score) doesn't contain it. ## Novel Insights

The confounded ablation—where joint training and additional unpaired data are introduced simultaneously—is a pattern common in joint modeling papers (visible in the calibration anchors for Joint Transformer, JEDI, and OmniProt, all scoring 3.5–5.0). This paper would have been significantly strengthened by an EPR-Only + Unpaired Data ablation, which would have been straightforward to implement and would have cleanly isolated the joint modeling contribution. The tension between the paper's claims and the data is instructive: the best evidence for the method's value (alignment-free Seq2Seq, competitive APT, PSR concept) does not actually depend on the joint modeling claim that the paper foregrounds.

## Suggestions

- Add an EPR-Only + Unpaired Data ablation to isolate the contribution of joint training versus data scaling. This single experiment would resolve the most critical confound.
- Correct the variance-matching claim to accurately reflect that DExter achieves closer velocity and onset variance to human, while your models achieve closer duration variance. The "unstable dynamics" explanation can remain but should not be used to justify an incorrect factual statement.
- Add quantitative PSR evaluation metrics rather than relying solely on visual clustering analysis.
- Consider adding an adversarial content-style regularizer (e.g., a gradient reversal layer that trains a classifier to predict composer from content while the content encoder optimizes against it) to strengthen the disentanglement and provide cleaner evidence.
