## Summary
SATURN introduces the first application of Mamba state-space models to goal-directed molecular generation with reinforcement learning, combining Augmented Memory with a “local sampling” mechanism. The paper elucidates how data augmentation and experience replay improve sample efficiency and shows that Mamba synergistically exploits this via strategic overfitting. On multi-parameter optimization tasks, SATURN outperforms many baselines and achieves competitive or superior results to the recent GEAM method under constrained oracle budgets, though the comparison with GEAM is flawed by an unfair treatment of pre‑training costs.

## Strengths
- **First application of Mamba to generative molecular RL** — The paper clearly positions and implements Mamba as a novel backbone for molecular design, distinct from prior RNN/transformer approaches (Sec. 3, lines 27, 80‑84).
- **Mechanistic insight into Augmented Memory** — Figure 2b‑c and accompanying text demonstrate that Augmented Memory “squeezes” the likelihood of augmented SMILES, making the agent more likely to generate any representation of replay‑buffer molecules. This explains *why* the technique works, going beyond the original empirical demonstration.
- **Synergistic “hop‑and‑locally‑explore” behavior** — Figure 2d‑e provides compelling visual evidence that Mumba, unlike RNN baselines, performs directional chemical‑space traversal and exhibits higher intra‑chunk Tanimoto similarity, directly linking architecture to sample efficiency.
- **Strong empirical gains against diverse baselines** — Tables 2‑4 show SATURN beating 22 models across multiple docking targets, with particularly large improvements in Strict Hit Ratio and lower Oracle Burden compared to most competitors.
- **Comprehensive experimental validation** — The paper reports >5,000 experiments across 10 seeds, uses appropriate statistical significance tests (95% confidence), and releases code, supporting reproducibility.
- **Clear presentation** — The workflow diagram (Fig. 1) and multi‑panel performance analysis (Fig. 2) effectively communicate both method and results.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None. The core methodology is sound and the empirical results are robust for the majority of baselines.

### Major
- **Unfair comparison with GEAM undermines the headline superiority claim** — The paper acknowledges GEAM’s large pre‑training oracle cost (“GEAM’s pre‑training requires the labeled ZINC 250k with all docking values already pre‑computed, so there is a large up‑front oracle cost,” line 188) but still frames SATURN as “outperforming 22 models” and achieving “superior MPO… with fewer oracle calls” without clearly restricting the claim to the *optimization* phase. When total oracle cost (pre‑training + optimization) is considered, GEAM’s ~1.25 M docking evaluations dwarf SATURN’s 3,000‑call optimization budget, reversing the sample‑efficiency narrative. This misleads readers about the practical advantage, especially regarding the prospect of direct high‑fidelity oracle optimization.
- **Speculative claim about direct high‑fidelity oracle optimization lacks quantitative backing** — The abstract and conclusion state that SATURN “may possess sufficient sample efficiency to consider the prospect of directly optimizing high‑fidelity oracles (e.g., MD simulations)” but provide no cost analysis linking the 3,000‑call budget to actual MD compute time. Given that MD can take GPU hours per molecule, the feasibility of 3,000 calls is not established, making the claim unsubstantiated.

### Minor
- **Missing ablation for the two‑phase (Saturn‑Tanimoto) novelty enhancement** — To recover Novel Hit Ratio, the paper introduces a two‑phase curriculum that first optimizes dissimilarity to training data. No ablation compares this against simpler alternatives (e.g., higher temperature, early stopping, a single‑phase reward with a dissimilarity term), so the necessity of the specific two‑phase approach is unclear (Sec. 4.3, line 244).
- **Statistical validation of “local sampling” is informal** — The intra‑chunk vs. inter‑chunk Tanimoto similarity heatmap (Fig. 2e) is presented without a formal statistical test (e.g., paired t‑test across seeds/chunks). While the visual difference is striking, a significance test would solidify the claim.
- **High variance with small batch size may affect reliability** — SATURN’s standard deviations are notably larger than GEAM’s (e.g., Hit Ratio std 18.5 vs 2.4 on `pur1`). The paper notes this but does not explore mitigation strategies (e.g., gradient accumulation, seeding) that could stabilize performance without sacrificing sample efficiency.
