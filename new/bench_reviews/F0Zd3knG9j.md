## Summary
This paper introduces a controlled hierarchical filtering procedure for tree-structured generative models, parameterized by a filtering level $k$ that truncates long-range correlations. Using this tunable benchmark, the authors demonstrate that vanilla encoder-only transformers trained on root classification and masked language modeling (MLM) tasks converge to the Bayes-optimal Belief Propagation (BP) predictor. The work provides systematic evidence of this convergence through calibrated probability matching, sequential learning dynamics that align with $\text{BP}_k$ oracles over training time, attention maps exhibiting hierarchical block structures, and layer-wise probing experiments. An existence proof is also constructed showing how BP can theoretically be embedded within a transformer matching the tree depth.

## Strengths
- **Controlled hierarchical benchmark:** The proposed filtering procedure is a well-designed experimental knob. By training on $k_{\text{train}}$ and testing on $k_{\text{test}}$, the paper cleanly isolates which correlation scales the transformer has learned. The bidirectional mismatch experiments (Figs. 3 & 4) showing transformers matching the accuracy of mismatched $\text{BP}_{k_{\text{train}}}$ oracles provide compelling causal-style evidence that the model's internal strategy is matched to its training distribution.
- **Calibrated probability matching, not just accuracy:** The paper goes beyond hard-label accuracy to show that transformers trained with one-hot labels spontaneously produce softmax outputs that closely match BP marginals (Fig. 1b, Fig. 11). This is particularly impressive in ambiguous ($k>0$) cases where the training labels do not reflect the underlying probabilistic marginals, strongly suggesting computational equivalence rather than mere memorization.
- **Sequential learning dynamics:** The "staircase" learning curves and $D_{\text{KL}}$ evolution (Figs. 1c-d, 5) clearly reveal that transformers discover correlations bottom-up, sequentially aligning with $\text{BP}_k$ for decreasing $k$ during training. This temporal resolution of hierarchical discovery is a clean and valuable insight into transformer training dynamics.
- **Mechanistic analysis via attention and probing:** The attention maps (Fig. 6) exhibit clear hierarchical block structures that systematically vary with $k$, and the probing experiments (Fig. 7) show that the $k$-th level ancestor is most accessible at transformer layer $k$. Together, these findings rule out trivial overfitting and support a spatially distributed implementation of message passing.
- **Existence proof for BP embedding:** Appendix E constructively demonstrates that BP can be implemented in $\ell$ layers with $\mathcal{O}(q^2)$ memory slots, trading depth for width. This provides a theoretical feasibility check that grounds the empirical observations.

## Weaknesses

### Major
- **Correlational mechanistic evidence does not strictly prove algorithmic implementation:** While probability matching, attention structures, and probing results are highly consistent with BP, they are inherently correlational. High probe accuracy and accessible ancestor information at layer $k$ indicate that representations *contain* hierarchical information, but not necessarily that the layer *computes* the BP update via message passing. Similarly, averaged attention maps demonstrate global statistical alignment with the tree but do not verify input-conditioned dynamic routing. Without causal interventions (e.g., mediation analysis, circuit ablations, or adversarial perturbations of proposed message vectors), the claim that the transformer "implements BP" remains a strong hypothesis rather than a proven fact. Alternative architectures or optimization pathways could yield identical external behavior and similar representational statistics.

### Minor
- **Limited scale and vocabulary restrict generalization claims:** All experiments use $q=4$, $\ell=4$ (sequence length 16), and a single transition tensor. While Appendix D.2 notes qualitative consistency across different grammars, the extreme overparameterization relative to the input space ($d=128$, hidden dim 2048 on $4^{16}$ possible sequences) means the model has ample capacity to approximate the mapping via brute-force pattern matching. Scaling to larger $\ell$ or $q$ would be necessary to confirm that the observed sequential and hierarchical mechanisms persist under greater complexity and tighter capacity constraints.
- **Existence proof relies on structural priors not verified in trained models:** The theoretical construction (Appendix E) assumes highly specific structural conditions (e.g., fully disentangled positional/semantic embeddings, dedicated memory slots). The paper explicitly notes these are "not imposed in our experiments" (Sec. 4), but does not analyze whether the trained transformer spontaneously learns these structural properties or uses a fundamentally different internal solution that happens to produce similar outputs. Bridging this gap would strengthen the mechanistic link.

### Trivial
- **Averaged attention maps obscure input-specific routing:** While the block-diagonal structures in averaged attention maps are indicative of global hierarchy learning, they smooth over the conditional dependencies that BP requires. Showing how attention dynamically routes for specific masked positions would provide richer mechanistic insight, though this is a presentation refinement rather than a core flaw.

## Nice-to-Haves
- **Scale experiments with constrained capacity:** Testing models with fewer attention heads, smaller embedding dimensions, or bottleneck layers would help determine if the hierarchical routing is a robust mechanistic outcome of the architecture or an artifact of massive overparameterization.
- **Dynamic visualization:** Illustrating attention routing on specific input sequences (rather than averages over $10^4$ inputs) could better demonstrate the input-conditioned message-passing behavior characteristic of BP.

## Removed Points
- **Missing hyperparameters / reproducibility concerns:** The harsh critic notes missing optimizer details, learning rate schedules, and attention head counts. However, the paper explicitly directs readers to Appendix B for a "full recap of this parametrization." These details are provided in the submission and are standard reproducibility requirements already met.
- **Claiming functional equivalence equals procedural equivalence / exact implementation:** The critic argues the paper mistakes output matching for exact algorithmic implementation. However, the paper carefully phrases its claims throughout as "approximate the exact inference algorithm," "plausible implementation," and "compatible with a natural implementation of BP" (Sec. 4). The authors explicitly state the existence proof uses priors "not imposed in our experiments" and does "not represent an exact explanation of the trained transformer computation." The critic misreads the authors' appropriately cautious framing.
- **Pre-training synergy is standard folklore:** The critic notes the MLM pre-training benefit (Fig. 1f) adds no novel insight. While consistent with known results, the paper uses this specific benchmark to mechanistically connect MLM's structural learning to the downstream supervised task's sample efficiency, providing a controlled demonstration of why the synergy occurs in this setting.

## Novel Insights
None beyond the paper's own contributions.

## Suggestions
- Clearly delineate between "evidence consistent with BP" and "proof of BP implementation" in the final claims to reflect the correlational nature of probing and attention analysis without overstating certainty.
- Consider adding a small-scale causal ablation or mediation analysis (e.g., masking specific attention heads or intervening on embedding dimensions during inference) to more directly test the proposed message-passing hypothesis.

## Score and Decision
Compared against ICLR calibration anchors for synthetic mechanistic/interpretability papers (e.g., works on transformers learning CFGs or hierarchical tasks, typically scoring 6.0-7.5 when clean and well-motivated), this paper stands out for its controlled filtering benchmark and calibrated probability matching, which provide stronger evidence than standard accuracy-only studies. However, the small scale ($q=4, \ell=4$) and reliance on correlational probing limit its impact compared to papers demonstrating scalable algorithmic learning or providing rigorous circuit-level verification. The paper is a solid, well-executed contribution to understanding transformers on structured data, placing it firmly above borderline but below top-tier mechanistic breakthroughs.
