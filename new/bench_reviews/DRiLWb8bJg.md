## Summary
This paper introduces SAPO, a maximum-entropy first-order model-based RL algorithm that integrates entropy-augmented returns and soft value targets into the short-horizon actor-critic framework (SHAC) for training with analytic gradients from differentiable simulation, alongside Rewarped, a parallel differentiable multiphysics simulation platform supporting rigid, articulated, and deformable bodies. The paper demonstrates that SAPO outperforms model-free (PPO, SAC) and FO-MBRL (APG, SHAC) baselines across six manipulation and locomotion tasks involving deformable materials.

## Strengths
- **Principled theoretical extension of max-entropy RL to FO-MBRL**: The derivation in Section 4.1 (Eq. 18–20) cleanly integrates entropy-augmented H-step returns and soft TD(λ) targets into the analytic-gradient actor-critic objective. The formulation is mathematically sound and directly compatible with differentiable simulation backpropagation.
- **Fills a documented gap in simulation infrastructure**: Table 1 provides a systematic feature comparison across nine platforms, showing Rewarped is uniquely parallel, fully differentiable, and multiphysics-capable (rigid + articulated + elastic + plasticine + fluid). The CUDA graph replay implementation for gradient checkpointing (Sec. 5.1) is a practical systems contribution enabling long-horizon differentiable MPM simulation.
- **Strong empirical results with rigorous methodology**: Table 2 shows SAPO outperforming all baselines across all six tasks, with substantial margins on deformable tasks (e.g., HandFlip: 90.0 vs. 38.2 APG; SoftJumper: 1820.5 vs. 956.6 APG). Results are reported over 10 seeds with 95% CIs, and baselines are standardized per Appendix C.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **The main-text ablation protocol is limited to a single task and bundles multiple engineering changes, weakening causal attribution of gains to the proposed entropy mechanism.** The core theoretical claim is that maximum entropy regularization stabilizes FO-MBRL over analytic gradients. However, SAPO simultaneously modifies SHAC along five axes: entropy formulation (I), entropy normalization (II), state-dependent variance (III), critic ensemble without target networks (IV), and architecture/optimizer changes (SiLU, AdamW, lower gradient clipping) (V, Sec. 4.2). The ablation in the main paper (Sec. 6.2, Table 3) evaluates (a) w/o V_soft, (b) w/o H_π, and (c) w/o H_π + V_soft on **HandFlip only**. In configuration (c), design choices III, IV, and V are collapsed into a single condition. While the paper states that individual ablations of III–V exist (Appendix F.4) and a cross-task variant exists for rigid-body tasks (Appendix F.3), these are unavailable in the stripped submission, leaving the main text without sufficient evidence to isolate the entropy contribution from the engineering tweaks. Without multi-task ablations that vary **only** the entropy term while holding optimizer, architecture, and critic design fixed, the reader cannot verify that SAPO's gains derive primarily from the proposed theoretical mechanism rather than standard RL engineering.

- **The evaluation measures sample efficiency (environment steps) but does not address the stated computational motivation.** The introduction frames the problem as compute-constrained: soft-body simulation is "several orders of magnitude slower," limiting RL due to "data/compute costs" (Sec. 1). Yet all comparisons use environment steps as the primary metric. First-order MBRL requires backpropagating through *H* steps of differentiable MPM physics per policy update, which involves substantially more FLOPs and wall-clock time per step than forward-only model-free RL or even standard SHAC (which has one critic vs. SAPO's double critic, plus entropy computation). Without compute-normalized reporting (wall-clock time or FLOPs to reach a given return), the headline claim that SAPO/Rewarped "enables scaling RL" on deformables is only partially supported. The paper delivers on sample efficiency claims, but the motivation-conclusion gap — claiming to solve a compute bottleneck while measuring only environment-step efficiency — is a meaningful inconsistency.

### Minor
- **Observation modality is not clearly specified in the results tables and figures.** Section 6 states that deformable tasks support "both state-based and high-dimensional (particle-based) visual observations," and Appendix B.1 mentions visual encoders. However, Figure 2 and Table 2 present all task results without specifying whether policies used state or image observations. The Limitations section (Sec. 7) clarifies that the current experiments use "(non-occluded) subsampled particle states," but this distinction is absent from the results themselves. If all main results use privileged state access, the contribution to real-world policy learning is narrower than the framing suggests, and the tables should state this explicitly.

### Trivial
- **Reward scaling conventions are inconsistently reported.** The paper normalizes entropy (Sec. 4.2, item II) but does not report absolute reward scales across tasks. Since entropy is reward-scale dependent, and tasks use heterogeneous reward functions (e.g., velocity maximization vs. manipulation objectives), the lack of reward-scale context makes it difficult to interpret the magnitude of improvements across tasks or to reproduce entropy temperature tuning.

## Nice-to-Haves
- **Gradient norm/variance statistics during training.** Computing and reporting the variance or norm of the analytic policy gradients (with and without entropy regularization) would provide direct empirical evidence for the claim that entropy smooths the optimization landscape. This is not essential but would strengthen the theoretical narrative.
- **Side-by-side trajectory visualizations.** Visualizing SAPO vs. SHAC trajectories on deformable tasks (as the paper partially does in Appendix Figure 11) in the main text would help demonstrate whether entropy leads to more diverse exploration or avoidance of local optima.
- **Wall-clock scaling experiments.** Reporting training time vs. number of parallel environments would validate the "scaling" framing beyond sample efficiency alone.

## Novel Insights
The paper's most interesting methodological insight is the recognition that maximum entropy regularization — well-studied in off-policy model-free RL (SAC) — has been underexplored in first-order model-based settings where the gradient signal comes directly from differentiable simulation rather than learned world models or Monte Carlo rollouts. The coupling of entropy-in-the-actor and soft-value-targets in TD(λ) for FO-MBRL is a clean synthesis that extends the short-horizon actor-critic paradigm. However, this insight is undercut in the presented main text by the bundled ablation design, which makes it difficult to determine how much of the performance gain stems from the theoretical contribution versus the concurrent adoption of proven RL engineering practices (layer normalization without target networks, AdamW, state-dependent variance). The observation that engineering changes alone yield nearly as much improvement as the entropy term on HandFlip (56 vs. 59 for w/o H_π + V_soft vs. w/o H_π) is genuinely interesting and suggests that FO-MBRL on deformable tasks may benefit more from modern optimization hygiene than from entropy in the actor objective — a finding worth highlighting rather than downplaying.

## Suggestions
1. **Restructure the ablation in the main text** to include at least one additional deformable task (e.g., SoftJumper) and explicitly show the ablation that varies **only** the entropy term while keeping optimizer, architecture, and critic design identical to SHAC. This is necessary to validate the central hypothesis.
2. **Add a wall-clock or FLOPs-per-step analysis** (at minimum a table reporting ms/step and total training time for SAPO vs. SHAC and a model-free baseline) to clarify the practical compute implications of the approach. If SAPO is slower per step but requires fewer total steps, quantify the crossover point.
3. **Explicitly label the observation modality** in Table 2 and Figure 2 captions (e.g., "state-based observations" or "subsampled particle states") and add a sentence in Section 6.1 stating whether any visual results are included in the main experiments.
4. **Report reward scales** for each task alongside the results to enable proper interpretation of entropy normalization and cross-task comparison.
