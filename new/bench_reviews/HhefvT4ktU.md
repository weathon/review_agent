## Summary

This paper presents a large-scale computational audit of racial and gender biases in Stable Diffusion XL (SDXL), measuring skew across six racial groups, two genders, 32 professions, and eight attributes. It introduces two debiasing approaches—SDXL-Inc, which combines 12 demographic-specific LoRA weight sets with random selection to achieve uniform race/gender output, and SDXL-Div, which fine-tunes on Flickr-Faces-HQ to increase within-race facial diversity. Finally, it reports four preregistered human-subject experiments examining whether exposure to inclusive versus non-inclusive AI-generated faces shifts participants’ stereotypical beliefs.

## Strengths

- **Large-scale, granular bias audit.** The paper evaluates 320,000 images across 32 professions (10,000 per profession) and 80,000 images across eight attributes, quantifying representational skews at a scale that substantially exceeds prior work (e.g., Bianchi et al.’s 10 professions/3 races/100 images each). Evidence: Sections 3.1 and 4.3, Figure 2.
- **First quantitative characterization and mitigation of racial homogenization.** Unlike prior audits, the paper measures within-race facial diversity via pairwise cosine similarity over roughly 50 million image pairs per race, showing that SDXL collapses variation (e.g., Middle Eastern mean similarity 0.61) and that SDXL-Div mitigates it (down to 0.41). Evidence: Sections 2 and 4.5, Figure 4.
- **Generalization testing of debiasing to unseen concepts.** SDXL-Inc is evaluated on 11 professions and eight attributes held out from fine-tuning, with standard deviations dropping sharply (e.g., gender σ from 40.3 to 2.7), indicating the LoRAs are not simply memorizing the 21 training professions. Evidence: Section 4.4, Figure 3.
- **Preregistered behavioral experiments linking model outputs to human stereotypes.** Four IRB-approved Prolific studies connect generative-model bias to shifts in human belief estimates, with an AI-generated label manipulation. Evidence: Section 4.6, Figure 5.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Superiority over baseline debiasing methods is asserted without evidence in the main text.** The abstract and introduction claim SDXL-Inc “outperform[s] alternatives across various benchmarks,” yet the main body contains no quantitative head-to-head comparison against Fair Diffusion, ITI-GEN, or even the authors’ own GPT-in-the-loop baseline (referenced only in an appendix figure/table). A core empirical claim of this magnitude must be substantiated in the main text, not deferred. Evidence: Abstract, Introduction (line 25), Section 4.4 (line 219).

### Minor
- **User-study non-inclusive stimuli are 100% homogeneous, and their selection procedure is unspecified.** In Studies 1 and 2, participants viewed six consecutive images that were all White (Study 1) or all male (Study 2). While the paper states these were generated by SDXL, it does not clarify whether they were randomly sampled or selected to achieve homogeneity. Because six consecutive homogeneous images constitute a stronger dosage than typical single-query SDXL exposure, the ecological validity and generalizability to real-world model use are unclear. Evidence: Section 4.6, lines 253–255.
- **Power analysis mismatches the experimental design.** The authors report a sample-size estimate for a paired-sample comparison (Cohen’s *d* = 0.5, *N* = 135), but the four experimental conditions appear to be between-subjects. The per-condition sample size for the image-viewing groups is not reported, making the power justification difficult to evaluate. Evidence: Section 4.6, line 249.
- **Training-data attribution relies on a non-representative subset.** The LAION-5B subset used to infer training-data bias was created via keyword matching, face cropping, and size thresholds. Because this filtering almost certainly alters demographic distributions relative to the full corpus, the conclusion that “SDXL contains biases that cannot be fully explained by the data it was trained on” is not fully supported. Evidence: Section 4.2, lines 101, 126.
- **Homogenization metric relies on an unvalidated proxy.** The paper quantifies racial homogenization using cosine similarity in a VGGFace ResNet-50 embedding space. Face-recognition embeddings are trained to suppress intra-class variation, so high similarity may partly reflect the embedding model’s inductive bias rather than purely visual homogeneity. Without validation against unsupervised perceptual metrics (e.g., LPIPS) or human judgments, the quantitative claim rests on an unvalidated proxy. Evidence: Sections 3.2.2 and 4.5, Figure 4.

### Trivial
None.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Per-LoRA image-quality and semantic-preservation metrics (e.g., FID, CLIP similarity to prompt) to verify that demographic parity does not trade off against profession accuracy or overall fidelity.
- Human diversity ratings across all six racial groups to validate (or calibrate) the embedding-space cosine-similarity measure used for SDXL-Div.

## Removed Points
These points are flagged to be removed, treat them with caution.

- The harsh critic claims the user-study stimuli are “artificially extreme” and “maximally skewed,” breaking the central causal claim. The paper does not state that images were cherry-picked to be 100 % homogeneous; for several professions (e.g., Dietitian at ~88 % White), an all-White sample of six is a plausible SDXL outcome. The critic’s assumption of artificial construction is unsupported by the text.
- The harsh critic claims SDXL-Inc’s uniform-distribution result is presented as an empirical finding without acknowledging the random-selection wrapper. The paper explicitly states in Section 4.2: “The basic idea of SDXL-Inc is to randomly select one of those 12 sets of weights based on the target distribution of interest (which is uniform in our experiments).” The circularity charge is therefore overstated.
- The harsh critic claims the user study is underpowered at ~34 participants per cell. The paper specifies that 135 participants were recruited for the *no-image baseline* per question; the per-cell N for the image-viewing conditions is never stated. The critic’s numerical claim is speculative.
- Any formatting or OCR-artifact complaints (e.g., garbled Table 2 values) are parser errors, not author errors.

## Novel Insights
None beyond the paper's own contributions.

## Suggestions
- Move quantitative baseline comparisons (Fair Diffusion, ITI-GEN, GPT-in-the-loop) from the appendix into the main text, or soften the abstract’s “outperform alternatives” claim to match the evidence presented.
- Clarify in the main text how user-study stimuli were generated (random sampling versus selection) and report per-condition sample sizes and a between-subjects power analysis.
