## Summary

The paper introduces Ambig-SWE, an interactive evaluation framework built on SWE-Bench Verified, where GitHub issues are synthetically underspecified by GPT-4o to study how LLM agents handle missing information. The evaluation decomposes the problem into three capabilities—detecting underspecificity (RQ2), asking targeted clarification questions (RQ3), and leveraging interaction to improve task completion (RQ1)—across six proprietary and open-weight models. Key findings show that models default to non-interactive behavior, struggle to distinguish well-specified from underspecified inputs (except Claude Sonnet 4), but can recover up to 74% of lost performance when interaction is enabled.

## Strengths

- **Well-structured decomposition of the problem.** The tripartite evaluation (detection, question quality, interactive problem solving) provides a clear diagnostic framework that enables targeted analysis of where models succeed or fail. This decomposition is a genuine conceptual contribution that goes beyond simply measuring end-to-end outcomes.

- **Insightful analysis of interaction strategies.** The distinction between informational and navigational queries (Table 1), the qualitative analysis of question-asking strategies (exploration-first vs. ask-immediately), and the finding that "question quality and integration matter more than extraction volume" yield nuanced, actionable insights. For instance, the observation that Qwen 3 Coder worsens when given navigational information due to rigid protocol-following is a concrete, non-obvious finding.

- **Controlled experimental design with causal interpretability.** Pairing each fully specified issue with an underspecified variant (rather than relying on naturally underspecified issues without paired ground truth) enables clean comparisons between Hidden, Interaction, and Full settings—an advantage the paper correctly argues for in §2.1.

- **Comprehensive cross-model comparison.** Evaluating six models across proprietary/open-weight families and scales (including models of similar task capability but different interaction patterns like Claude Sonnet 4 vs. Qwen 3 Coder) yields informative observations about diverse interaction styles and the non-correlation between model size/coding ability and interaction leverage ability.

- **Practical significance of findings.** The observation that models default to non-interactive behavior even under severe underspecificity, and that prompt engineering alone is insufficient for reliable detection, has direct implications for safe agent deployment.

## Weaknesses

### Major:

- **Ecological validity of synthetic underspecification limits generalizability of core claims.** The underspecified issues are generated by prompting GPT-4o to remove details from well-specified issues. The paper's own distributional difference analysis (§2.1) reveals that natural underspecified issues contain more concrete technical details (code snippets, error messages), reproducibility information, external links, and conversational fragments—features absent from the synthetic variants. While the paper provides a principled rationale for avoiding natural underspecified issues (lack of paired ground truth), this creates a construct validity gap: the benchmark measures performance under *LLM-generated summarization/erasure* of well-specified issues, not under organic underspecification where users never had a complete specification in mind. The claims about "critical gaps in how current state-of-the-art models handle missing information" and conclusions about "real-world agentic tasks" overstate what the evidence supports. The synthetic approach systematically ties underspecificity to a particular *style* of missingness (large, overt deletions from verbose issues) and to a known original that the simulated user can perfectly "replay." (The paper acknowledges this in §2.1 and §7 but understates its impact on the breadth of claims.)

- **Detection evaluation (RQ2) conflates instruction-following with genuine underspecificity detection.** The experiment presents models with prompts of increasing strength ("you can ask," "check carefully," "asking is critical") and measures whether they choose to interact. A model that consistently follows the prompt to ask questions—regardless of whether the issue is Full or Hidden—will have high FPR and low accuracy, even if it has perfect epistemic detection. Conversely, Qwen 3 Coder's 100% FNR is characterized as "fundamental limitations in certain training approaches" (§4.3), but could simply reflect a strong prior against asking questions. The current design cannot disentangle (a) instruction-following strength from (b) genuine underspecificity detection from (c) model priors about question-asking. This makes categorical claims about models "failing to detect" underspecificity insufficiently supported. A forced-classification task (e.g., "Is this specification complete?") or controlled cost-benefit setup would be needed to separate these factors.

- **Idealized user proxy inflates apparent value of interaction.** The GPT-4o proxy has access to the full original issue, responds only with information explicitly present in it, and provides ground-truth file locations when queried. This is an unusually cooperative and information-rich user: any syntactically reasonable question targeting a fact in the original issue receives a precise answer; the proxy never misunderstands, provides partial information, or drifts. Consequently, the measured performance gains from interaction (up to 74%) represent a near-upper-bound scenario. The paper acknowledges this briefly ("our simulated user proxy may be more cooperative than real users," §7) but then draws strong conclusions about what constitutes "good" question-asking strategies and "actionable clarifying questions" without qualification. The interaction insights may be directionally correct, but their magnitude is not representative of real-world scenarios.

- **Unequal turn limits confound cross-model comparisons.** Claude Sonnet 4 and Qwen 3 Coder receive up to 100 interaction turns while all other models are capped at 30 (§3.1). Since the paper finds that interaction does not improve efficiency (§3.2), models with more turns have a systematic advantage in the Interaction setting. While within-model comparisons remain valid, cross-model claims (e.g., about relative performance recovery) are confounded. This should have been controlled or its impact explicitly analyzed.

- **No naive question-asking baseline.** The paper draws conclusions about the quality of models' clarifying questions and interaction strategies, but there is no baseline where the agent asks a fixed set of generic questions (e.g., "What files need to be modified?", "What is the expected behavior?") without LLM-driven question selection. Without this, it is unclear how much of the performance gain comes from smart questioning versus simply receiving any additional information at all.

### Minor:

- **Information-gain metrics have limited discriminative power and do not establish causation.** Cosine distance between embeddings of the summarized task and cumulative knowledge after interaction is a rough proxy for information gain—sensitive to verbosity and lexical variation. The LLM-as-judge scores converge around 4/5 for all capable models (Figure 6), offering limited differentiation. The paper itself notes disconnects between information gain and task performance (e.g., Qwen 3 Coder achieving the highest cosine gain but not the best resolve rate; Haiku matching Sonnet 3.5 in gain but lagging dramatically in performance). Despite these internal contradictions, the paper argues that "question quality and integration matter more than extraction volume"—a plausible hypothesis that the current evidence does not causally establish.

- **Claims about the Interaction→Full gap deserve deeper analysis.** The paper reports that interaction recovers varying percentages of the Full performance (54-89%) but does not systematically analyze *why* the remaining gap exists. Is it because models fail to ask the right questions, fail to integrate answers, or because some information cannot be effectively communicated through the allowed interaction format? This would strengthen the diagnostic value of the framework.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Evaluation on naturally underspecified issues** (even without paired ground truth) as a supplementary analysis to assess ecological validity transfer.
- **Varying user proxy cooperativeness** (uncooperative, partially informed, occasionally incorrect) to test robustness of interaction findings.
- **Equalized turn limits** across all models or an explicit ablation on the effect of turn budget on performance.
- **Failure mode categorization** for the Interaction→Full gap (attribution to detection failure, question quality failure, or integration failure).
- **Cost analysis** (additional tokens/API calls per setting) to inform whether interaction gains justify the expense.

## Novel Insights

The most distinctive finding is the dissociation between information extraction and task performance: Qwen 3 Coder achieves the highest information gain (cosine distance 0.179) yet fails to convert that into proportionally higher resolve rates, while Claude Sonnet 4 achieves comparable gain with 50% fewer questions. This suggests that the field's current focus on whether models *can* ask clarifying questions understates the more fundamental challenge of how models *integrate* acquired information into their reasoning trajectories—a capacity that may not improve simply by scaling model size or question-asking ability. The observed rigidity of Qwen 3 Coder (continuing predetermined protocols despite corrective user input) is a particularly concrete illustration of this integration gap.

## Suggestions

- Add a simple non-LLM-driven question-asking baseline (e.g., always asking "What files need to be modified?" and "What is the expected behavior?") to isolate the marginal value of intelligent question selection versus mere information access.
- Run at least one model under both 30 and 100 turn limits to quantify the confound introduced by unequal turn budgets.
- Add a forced-classification condition (e.g., "Rate how complete this specification is on a scale of 1-5") to disentangle detection ability from interaction willingness in RQ2.
- Qualify claims about "real-world" applicability more explicitly in the abstract and introduction, acknowledging that the synthetic underspecification and idealized proxy represent a best-case controlled scenario rather than direct real-world simulation.
