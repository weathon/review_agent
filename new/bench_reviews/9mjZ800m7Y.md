## Summary

MODNAS proposes a hardware-aware multi-objective differentiable NAS algorithm that amortizes Pareto front profiling across multiple devices in a single search run. The core idea is a MetaHypernetwork conditioned on both a preference vector (encoding objective trade-offs) and a hardware device embedding, enabling zero-shot transfer to unseen devices without any additional search cost. The method is evaluated across 4 search spaces (NAS-Bench-201, MobileNetV3/OFA, HAT Transformer, GPT-2), 3 tasks (image classification, machine translation, language modelling), up to 19 hardware devices, and up to 3 objectives—reducing Pareto set build cost from O(CT) to O(1) compared to prior constrained NAS methods.

---

## Strengths

- **Genuine O(CT)→O(1) amortization contribution (Table 1, Sections 3, 4.4):** The reduction in complexity by conditioning a single MetaHypernetwork on both preference vectors and device embeddings is a well-motivated and practically important contribution. No prior gradient-based NAS method provides a global view of the Pareto front across multiple devices and constraints in a single run.

- **Comprehensive experimental evaluation (Figures 3, 8–11):** Testing on 4 qualitatively different search spaces (cell-based CNN, MobileNetV3, encoder-decoder Transformer for MT, decoder-only GPT), 3 tasks, up to 19 hardware devices, and 3 simultaneous objectives is unusually broad. The results consistently show MODNAS outperforming baselines across all settings.

- **Zero-shot transfer to unseen devices demonstrated (Figure 3, Section 4.1):** The use of 6 held-out test devices in NAS-Bench-201 (13 used for search, 6 at test time) provides concrete evidence that the MetaHypernetwork generalises beyond memorised device embeddings. Figure 5 reinforces this: MODNAS reaches high HV with only 24 evaluations on a test device while baselines require far more.

- **MGD ablation validates design choice (Figure 6):** The clean comparison of MGD against mean gradient, sequential, and MC-sampling update schemes provides direct evidence for the superiority of the MGD choice for updating the MetaHypernetwork across devices.

- **Scalability to three objectives (Figure 8):** The 3-objective experiment (accuracy, latency, energy) on FPGA/Eyeriss uses the same algorithmic framework without extra hyperparameter tuning and achieves HV near the global optimum, validating the claimed scalability.

- **Public code available:** The released repository (https://github.com/automl/modnas) facilitates reproducibility.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **MetaPredictor quality is unvalidated for zero-shot test devices.** The MetaPredictor is the exclusive gradient signal for hardware objectives (latency, energy) during search and is frozen after pretraining. No predictor quality metrics (Spearman rank correlation, RMSE, etc.) are reported in the main paper for held-out devices. The paper cites Lee et al. (2021b) for the predictor but does not reproduce its validation numbers in this context. Since the 6 test devices in NAS-Bench-201 and the test devices in HAT/OFA were never seen during predictor pretraining, the zero-shot hardware transfer claim relies on an unverified assumption of sufficient predictor generalisation. If the predictor's rank correlation on unseen devices is poor, the Pareto fronts for test devices in Figure 3 may not reflect genuinely good architectures. The paper's Limitations section acknowledges "the method relies on differentiable proxies for objectives, which may not always align with ground truth values," but does not quantify how well they do.

### Minor

- **"Beyond convex Pareto fronts" claim is imprecise (Section 4.3).** The paper states the HW-GPT-Bench space "features a non-convex Pareto front" and claims MODNAS demonstrates "effectiveness in optimizing beyond convex Pareto fronts." However, the optimization in Eq. (5) uses linear scalarization. For a finite discrete search space, linear scalarization can in principle access any Pareto-optimal point if the correct weight is used, but the method samples only 24 equidistant preference vectors, which may not provide adequate coverage of non-convex regions. The paper provides no analysis of Pareto front coverage relative to the true discrete Pareto front, nor any comparison against a Chebyshev or augmented scalarization that provably handles non-convex cases. The empirical result (Figure 11) shows competitive solutions, but the language about "non-convex fronts" overstates what the linear scalarization guarantees.

- **Budget comparison presentation is ambiguous for single-device use cases (Section 4.1).** The paper allocates to baselines "the same search time budget across all devices equivalent to the MODNAS search + evaluation" (22 GPU hours total for 19 devices = ~1.2 hours per device). This framing is correct for the amortization claim but makes it hard to assess MODNAS's quality in the single-device setting. Figure 5 provides the most relevant per-device comparison and shows MODNAS reaches ~0.94 HV with just 24 evaluations while baselines with many more evaluations lag behind—this partially addresses the concern—but only for one test device. Separating aggregate HV into training-device vs. test-device means would clarify how much of the improvement is amortization vs. pure search quality.

- **HAT baseline comparison is not fully apples-to-apples (Section 4.2).** The "HAT" data point in Figure 9 is described as "the architectures provided in their paper"—i.e., the original HAT architectures run under a different training setup, not an actively re-run baseline. This should be labeled "HAT (reference)" to distinguish from methods conducting fresh searches under equivalent conditions.

### Trivial

- **Limitations section is thin.** The acknowledged limitation ("gradients may favor one objective, leading to local optima") is the mildest possible framing. A candid limitations section would also acknowledge the scalarization coverage concern and the reliance on predictor quality for zero-shot generalization.

## Novel Insights

The key genuinely novel observation across all reviews is the framing of multi-device, multi-constraint NAS as a single amortized problem. Prior work has reduced the O(T) device cost via meta-learning (Lee et al. 2021b) or the O(C) constraint cost via supernet training (OFA), but MODNAS is the first method to simultaneously reduce both to O(1) in a gradient-based framework. The MetaHypernetwork acts as a joint "device × preference" function that is trained once and queried at test time for any device–constraint combination. The use of MGD (applied over devices rather than objectives—objectives are scalarized) is a subtle but effective design choice that prevents any single device from dominating the MetaHypernetwork gradient update. This inter-device gradient balancing, not just the amortization itself, appears to be a key reason for MODNAS's consistent performance across diverse devices including held-out ones.
