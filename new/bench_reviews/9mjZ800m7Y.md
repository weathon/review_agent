## Summary

MODNAS proposes a differentiable multi-objective NAS method that uses a MetaHypernetwork conditioned on both preference vectors and device embeddings, optimized via multiple gradient descent (MGD), to generate the full Pareto front of architectures across multiple hardware devices and objectives in a single search run. The key claim is reducing the search cost from O(CT) (running separate constrained searches per device and constraint) to O(1). The method is evaluated across 4 search spaces (NAS-Bench-201, MobileNetV3/OFA, HAT, HW-GPT-Bench), up to 19 devices, and up to 3 objectives (accuracy, latency, energy).

## Strengths

- **Novel and well-motivated formulation**: The combination of a device- and preference-conditioned MetaHypernetwork with MGD for multi-objective NAS is genuinely novel. Figure 2 cleanly separates preference conditioning (K hypernetworks) from device routing (learned attention), and the bilevel formulation in Eq. 5 is clearly derived. No prior gradient-based method returns the full Pareto front without repeating search.

- **Strong results on NAS-Bench-201 with ground truth**: Figure 3 shows MODNAS outperforming all baselines across all 19 devices—including 6 held-out test devices—with only a single search run. Figure 7 shows MODNAS matching or outperforming MetaD2A+HELP run 24 times. Figure 8 demonstrates scalability to 3 objectives, achieving HV close to the global optimum. These results use ground truth tabular data, making them fully verifiable.

- **MGD ablation clearly supports the design choice**: Figure 6 shows MGD achieves higher final HV and faster convergence than mean, sequential, and MC sampling gradient schemes—confirming the principled multi-gradient approach is essential rather than a simpler aggregation sufficing.

- **Exceptional experimental breadth**: 4 search spaces (CNN, encoder-decoder transformer, decoder-only transformer), 3 tasks (classification, translation, language modeling), up to 19 devices and 3 objectives. This is far broader than typical NAS papers.

- **Zero-shot device transfer is practically valuable**: The ability to generate architectures for unseen devices (Figure 3, red devices) and the result that only 2 search devices suffice for generalization to 17 test devices (Figure 25) makes the method practically useful.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Missing comparison against multi-scalarization differentiable NAS on large-scale benchmarks**: On the most practically impactful search spaces (OFA/MobileNetV3 and HW-GPT-Bench in Section 4.3), all baselines are blackbox methods (RS, LS, NSGA-II, etc.) that lack weight sharing. The paper itself acknowledges (Section 4.4) that OFA also has O(1) search cost via its pretrained supernetwork, and ProxylessNAS is a differentiable constrained NAS method—but neither is run as a multi-constraint baseline on these larger spaces. On NAS-Bench-201, the MetaD2A+HELP comparison (run 24 times) partially addresses this, but on the larger spaces, it remains unclear whether the hypernetwork + MGD machinery genuinely outperforms the simpler baseline of running a differentiable constrained NAS multiple times. The advantage over blackbox methods could be largely attributable to weight sharing rather than the core contributions (hypernetwork conditioning + MGD).

- **Large-scale results rely on proxy evaluation without standalone validation**: On OFA, architectures are evaluated using the pretrained OFA supernetwork as a proxy (Section 4.3: "To evaluate the 24 points generated by our MetaHypernetwork and baselines, we use the OFA pretrained supernetwork"). On HW-GPT-Bench, pretrained supernetwork weights are similarly used. While proxy evaluation is standard NAS practice, the hypervolume improvements on these most practically relevant spaces are only as meaningful as the proxy's ranking fidelity. Even 5-10 architectures validated through standalone training would significantly strengthen confidence. Notably, on NAS-Bench-201 and HAT—where ground truth or standalone evaluation (BLEU) is available—results are convincing, but these are the smaller/more constrained settings.

### Minor

- **No ablation isolating the hypernetwork's contribution**: The paper ablates the gradient aggregation scheme (MGD vs. mean/sequential/MC, Figure 6), but never ablates the hypernetwork conditioning itself. The RHPN (random hypernetwork weights) baseline is a straw man. A proper ablation would compare MODNAS against a version with separate scalarizations (e.g., K independent scalarized NAS runs with shared supernetwork but no hypernetwork). Without this, one cannot fully attribute Pareto front improvements to the hypernetwork's inductive bias rather than to MGD + weight sharing alone.

- **"Zero-shot transferability" terminology is slightly misleading**: The paper states (Section 3.2) that "Conditioning the MetaHypernetwork on the hardware embeddings allows us to generate architectures on new test devices without extra finetuning or meta-learning steps"—this requires computing a device embedding d_t by "evaluating a fixed set of reference architectures on device t." This is not truly zero-shot; it requires running inference on the target device. While the cost is minimal, the terminology overclaims.

- **Supernetwork training under multi-objective expectations is unanalyzed**: In Eq. 5's lower level, supernetwork weights are optimized w.r.t. the expectation over preference vectors r (line 14 of Algorithm 1 averages gradients across devices, implicitly over sampled r). This requires the supernetwork to be simultaneously optimal for all preference-weighted losses, a potentially harder requirement than standard one-shot NAS. The paper does not discuss whether this creates optimization conflicts or degrades supernetwork quality relative to single-scalarization supernetworks.

- **Search cost table excludes supernetwork pretraining**: Table 1 correctly states MODNAS has O(1) search cost, but this excludes the expensive supernetwork pretraining phase (weeks for OFA). While technically the supernetwork can be reused, this total cost context should be clearer for readers.

## Novel Insights

The paper reveals an interesting asymmetric cost structure in multi-objective NAS: the dominant cost is not searching for the Pareto front per se, but the O(CT) multiplicative cost from repeating search across devices and constraints. MODNAS's key insight is that by conditioning a single hypernetwork on both device embeddings and preference vectors, this multiplicative cost collapses to O(1). However, the evidence suggests this benefit is most cleanly demonstrated on tabular benchmarks where ground truth is available, while on larger spaces the advantage may conflate weight-sharing benefits with the hypernetwork+MGD mechanism.

## Suggestions

- Run a multi-constraint differentiable NAS baseline (e.g., OFA evolutionary search or a simplified DARTS with multiple scalarizations) on OFA/MobileNetV3 and HW-GPT-Bench to isolate the contribution of the hypernetwork+MGD approach over simpler multi-scalarization supernetwork-based alternatives.
- Validate 5-10 architectures from the OFA and HW-GPT-Bench Pareto fronts through standalone training to establish whether proxy improvements translate to real performance gains.
- Add an ablation without the hypernetwork (e.g., running MODNAS with independent scalarizations, no weight sharing across preferences) to quantify the hypernetwork's contribution.

## Evaluation

**Originality**: The formulation of multi-device, multi-objective NAS via a preference- and device-conditioned MetaHypernetwork with MGD is genuinely novel. The combination of these ideas in a single framework is creative and well-motivated.

**Importance**: Making multi-objective NAS efficient across devices is an important practical problem. The O(CT) → O(1) reduction is compelling if fully validated.

**Claim support**: Strong on NAS-Bench-201 (ground truth, multi-run baseline); weaker on larger spaces where proxy evaluation without standalone validation and lack of differentiable baselines leave the core claim less conclusively established.

**Soundness of experiments**: The experimental breadth is excellent, but the comparison gap on large-scale spaces is a real limitation. The MGD ablation is valuable but incomplete (missing hypernetwork ablation).

**Clarity**: The paper is clearly written with good figures and a modular algorithm presentation. The "zero-shot" terminology is slightly overclaimed.

**Value**: If the contributions can be more cleanly isolated on large-scale benchmarks, this would be a significant contribution to hardware-aware NAS.
