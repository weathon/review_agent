## Summary

MODNAS proposes a multi-objective differentiable NAS method that profiles the entire Pareto front across multiple hardware devices and objectives in a single search run, using a device- and preference-conditioned MetaHypernetwork optimized via multiple gradient descent (MGD). The key innovation is combining device embeddings with preference vectors in the hypernetwork to enable zero-shot transfer to unseen devices, reducing search cost from O(CT) to O(1) compared to methods requiring per-device or per-constraint searches.

## Strengths

- **Cross-device, cross-preference formulation is genuinely novel and practical.** The MetaHypernetwork H_Φ(r, d_t) conditioning on both preference vectors and device embeddings, enabling zero-shot transfer to unseen devices, is a clean design that addresses a real inefficiency in prior hardware-aware NAS. Figure 3 and Table 1 demonstrate that from a single 22-GPU-hour search, MODNAS outperforms baselines across 19 devices including 6 held-out test devices, whereas each baseline requires 19 separate search runs.

- **NAS-Bench-201 evaluation is thorough and convincing.** With ground truth from the tabular benchmark, 19 devices, multiple baselines, ablations on gradient schemes (Figure 6), and 3-objective experiments (Figure 8), this section validates the method in a controlled setting where Pareto optimality claims can be verified.

- **MGD ablation clearly demonstrates value.** Figure 6 shows MGD consistently outperforms mean gradient, sequential, and MC sampling over devices, confirming that handling inter-device gradient conflicts matters.

- **Broad empirical coverage across 4 search spaces and 3 tasks.** CNN cells (NB201), MobileNetV3 (ImageNet), encoder-decoder Transformers (WMT translation), and decoder-only GPT-2 (OpenWebText) cover qualitatively different settings, and the method scales to 3 objectives (accuracy, latency, energy) without additional tuning (Figure 8).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Linear scalarization cannot recover the full Pareto front for non-convex objective spaces, and this limitation is not discussed.** Eq. 5 uses linear scalarization r^⊤L_t, which provably cannot find Pareto-optimal points in concave regions of the objective space. The paper explicitly identifies HW-GPT-Bench as having "a non-convex Pareto front" (Section 4.3) and then claims MODNAS is "effective in optimizing beyond convex Pareto fronts," but does not acknowledge that any architectures in concave regions are unreachable by this method. This is not mentioned in the limitations section either. The practical impact may be limited (many fronts are near-convex), but the theoretical gap deserves honest discussion, especially since the paper itself identifies the non-convexity.

- **Pareto optimality claims for 3 of 4 search spaces rely solely on supernet proxy evaluation without standalone training verification.** For NB201, ground truth validates the results. For OFA and HW-GPT-Bench, evaluation is done via pretrained supernet inference with frozen weights (Section 4.3). For HAT, the evaluation method is unspecified (the paper says "we evaluate... using the BLEU score" without clarifying whether this involves full training or supernet inference). Supernet ranking correlations with standalone performance are known to be noisy in large spaces, so claiming "Pareto-optimal" architectures based solely on proxy scores somewhat overreaches. This is standard practice in the NAS field, but the paper's strong claims deserve stronger evidence in at least one large-scale space.

- **Evaluation asymmetry between MODNAS and baselines in large-scale experiments.** On HAT, baselines (RS, NSGA-II, MO-ASHA) presumably do full standalone evaluation per architecture, while MODNAS uses supernet inference. Even with 2.5× more wall-clock budget, baselines cannot amortize their evaluation cost the way a supernet can. For OFA, baselines "use the OFA pretrained accuracy predictor" (same proxy as MODNAS), which mitigates the asymmetry, but for HAT the asymmetry remains. The paper should either provide baselines the same proxy access or discuss how this affects fairness.

### Minor

- **The "first" claim (contribution 2) is somewhat overstated.** Prior hypernetwork methods (Navon et al., 2021; Lin et al., 2020) already learn Pareto-optimal solutions across preference vectors in a single training run. MODNAS's genuine novelty is extending this to NAS with cross-device transfer, not being the first to profile a Pareto front in a single run — the contribution should be stated more precisely.

- **MetaPredictor is frozen during search, and no analysis of its accuracy or error propagation is provided.** Since the MetaPredictor defines the hardware objective landscape during search, systematic errors for out-of-distribution architectures (likely early in search) could distort optimization. Reporting prediction error on held-out architectures and correlating it with search quality would strengthen confidence.

- **Lower-level optimization in Eq. 5 takes expectation over r~S, optimizing supernet weights for the average scalarization rather than any specific preference.** This could lead to weights that serve no preference particularly well, degrading proxy quality across the Pareto front. The paper does not discuss this tension, though the empirical results suggest it is not a severe issue in practice.

- **Table 1 claims O(1) search cost, excluding MetaPredictor pretraining (3–6 GPU hours) and ground truth hardware measurement collection.** While these costs are modest relative to total search time, they are non-zero and should be transparently reported alongside the O(1) simplification.

### Trivial
None.

## Removed Points

- **Harsh Critic's demand for error bars in a radar plot.** The paper states "mean of 3 independent search runs" in the figure caption; error bars in radar plots are impractical and the paper follows community conventions.
- **Harsh Critic's complaint that OFA experiments only update the MetaHypernetwork with frozen supernet weights, "reducing" the contribution.** This is actually a strength — it demonstrates the method works modularly with pretrained supernets for practical large-scale adoption, as noted in the Strength Finder.
- **Harsh Critic's claim that HAT evaluation method is "ambiguous."** The paper states "For HAT, we evaluate the architectures provided in their paper" — this refers to using the HAT (Wang et al.) paper's trained architectures as baselines. The MODNAS architectures' evaluation using "BLEU score" is indeed unspecified as standalone vs. proxy, but this is a minor ambiguity, not a fatal issue.
- **Strength Finder's claim about "OFA baselines using the OFA pretrained accuracy predictor" showing fairness.** This actually shows the baselines DO get the same proxy for OFA, partially mitigating the asymmetry concern rather than being a standalone strength.
- **Harsh Critic's complaint about the bi-level averaging over preferences being fundamentally problematic.** This is a theoretical concern that the empirical results do not validate — if this were a severe issue, the method would not converge well.

## Novel Insights

The device embedding clustering analysis (Figure 12) reveals that the MetaHypernetwork learns meaningful hardware similarity structure — devices with similar architectures cluster together. This provides interpretable evidence that the attention mechanism over hypernetworks is doing something sensible rather than memorizing. The method's ability to handle the OFA search space with frozen supernet weights (only training the MetaHypernetwork) while still outperforming baselines that search from scratch suggests that the search contribution is genuinely in the architecture selection policy, not in the weight optimization.

## Suggestions

- Add a brief discussion of linear scalarization's limitation for non-convex Pareto fronts, noting that Chebyshev or ε-constraint scalarizations could be explored as future work.
- For at least one large-scale space (HAT or OFA), train 5–10 discovered architectures to convergence and report both proxy and standalone metrics to validate proxy reliability.
- Tone down the "first" claim in contribution 2 to more precisely describe the novelty relative to PHN-style hypernetwork methods.
