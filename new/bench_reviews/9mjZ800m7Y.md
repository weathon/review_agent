## Summary

MODNAS proposes a hardware-aware, multi-objective differentiable NAS method that uses a MetaHypernetwork conditioned simultaneously on user preference vectors and hardware device embeddings to generate Pareto-optimal architectures across multiple devices in a single search run—reducing cost from O(CT) to O(1). The method applies Multiple Gradient Descent (MGD) across devices for the upper-level update and is validated on NAS-Bench-201 (19 devices), OFA/MobileNetV3 (12 devices, ImageNet-1k), HAT (3 devices, machine translation), and HW-GPT-Bench (8 devices, language modeling).

---

## Strengths

- **Single-run Pareto profiling across all devices/preferences (Table 1, Figure 3):** The architectural framing of replacing O(CT) repeated searches with O(1) MetaHypernetwork conditioning is principled. MODNAS simultaneously handles T=19 devices and sweeps over the preference simplex without re-running search. This is a genuine computational contribution.

- **Zero-shot transfer to unseen devices (Figure 3, radar plot test devices in red):** The MetaHypernetwork, by conditioning on device feature vectors rather than device IDs, handles 6 held-out test devices during inference with no extra search. The t-SNE clustering of device embeddings (Figure 12 in Appendix) corroborates that the network learns meaningful hardware similarity structure.

- **Breadth of evaluation:** Four qualitatively different search spaces (cell-based CNN, MobileNetV3, encoder-decoder Transformer, GPT-2-based decoder), three tasks (image classification, machine translation, language modeling), up to 19 devices, and 3 objectives. This is among the widest empirical coverage in recent NAS papers.

- **MGD ablation is the strongest internal ablation (Figure 6):** Comparing MGD against mean gradient, sequential, and MC-sampled updates provides clear, non-marginal evidence for the design choice. The convergence and final-HV gaps are substantial.

- **Scalability to 3 objectives without re-tuning (Figure 8):** Adding energy consumption as a third objective alongside accuracy and latency on FPGA/Eyeriss incurs no hyperparameter changes and achieves HV near the global optimum, demonstrating the method's flexibility.

- **Constraint-incorporated extension (Figure 7):** The soft latency-constraint variant, which removes objective gradients when the predictor satisfies the constraint, meaningfully extends practical applicability with no architectural changes and outperforms MetaD2A+HELP in Pareto front diversity.

- **Public code release:** https://github.com/automl/modnas facilitates reproduction and extension.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **The "outperforms" framing conflates efficiency with quality superiority.** The paper's primary comparison allocates baselines the *same total compute* as MODNAS across all devices—meaning in the NAS-Bench-201 experiment each baseline gets ~1.16 GPU hours per device (22 GPU hours ÷ 19 devices) versus MODNAS's 22 GPU hours total. The paper does acknowledge this design ("we allocate the same search time budget across all devices equivalent to the MODNAS search + evaluation") and Figure 20a shows MODNAS still wins at 2× baseline budget. However, the 2× test is far short of the 19× per-device gap in that experiment. The abstract's claim that the method "outperforms existing MOO NAS methods" without qualification overstates quality superiority relative to what the experiments actually establish. A more careful framing—*MODNAS achieves higher HV than baselines with equivalent total compute; on a per-device basis baselines use a fraction of MODNAS's budget*—would be accurate. Without at least one comparison in which a strong single-device baseline runs with full per-device compute, the claim of quality (not just efficiency) superiority is not cleanly supported. This matters because the efficiency claim itself is already compelling and does not require the additional quality claim.

### Minor

- **MetaPredictor quality is an unexamined dependency.** All hardware objectives (latency, energy) are optimized through the frozen pretrained MetaPredictor, which is trained on 2,000–5,000 architecture samples and never updated during search. The paper acknowledges this in the Limitations section ("the method relies on differentiable proxies for objectives, which may not always align with ground truth values") but provides no analysis of how predictor fidelity affects final Pareto front quality. In NAS-Bench-201, ground-truth latency values exist in the benchmark, so a comparison of predicted vs. true Pareto fronts is feasible. In HAT and HW-GPT-Bench, where the predictor is trained on a small sample of a much larger space, this gap could be substantial. Even a brief ablation varying predictor training set size would substantially strengthen the claims.

- **Gradient information asymmetry in the OFA experiment.** MODNAS updates the MetaHypernetwork using gradients from the frozen OFA supernetwork, while baselines use the OFA accuracy predictor as a black box. This gives MODNAS access to richer gradient signals beyond what the baselines see. The OFA result (Figure 10) is therefore partially attributable to gradient access rather than purely the MOO formulation—a point worth acknowledging explicitly.

- **HAT evaluation protocol is underspecified.** The paper states architectures are evaluated with BLEU score ("For HAT, we evaluate the architectures provided in their paper") but does not clearly state whether the 24 MODNAS-generated architectures use supernetwork weights for evaluation or are trained from scratch. This ambiguity makes it difficult to assess whether the Pareto fronts represent true quality or supernetwork proxy quality for HAT.

- **Thin statistical reporting.** All results report means over 3 independent runs with no confidence intervals or standard deviations in main figures. Given the known instability of gradient-based NAS (cited in the Limitations, Zela et al., 2020), 3 seeds without variance bars is insufficient to distinguish signal from noise on close comparisons in, e.g., Figure 3's radar chart.

### Trivial

- **Table 1 O(1) classification.** The O(1) search cost for MODNAS excludes MetaPredictor pretraining (3–6 GPU hours, stated in the text). The prose acknowledges this, but the table itself is slightly oversimplified. A footnote indicating the pretraining cost is amortized over many subsequent searches would be clearer.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A comparison where one strong single-device baseline (e.g., qEHVI or NSGA-II) is given the full per-device equivalent compute on at least one experiment, to characterize MODNAS's quality advantage independent of the efficiency advantage.
- An ablation varying MetaPredictor training set size (e.g., 200, 500, 2,000, 5,000 samples) to quantify the dependency of Pareto front quality on predictor fidelity.
- Visualization of architectural diversity across the preference simplex (operation frequency heatmap as preference weight sweeps from accuracy-heavy to latency-heavy) to confirm the MetaHypernetwork produces meaningfully diverse solutions rather than near-identical ones.
- Sensitivity test for zero-shot transfer on genuinely out-of-distribution devices (e.g., training on Samsung mobile CPUs, testing on FPGAs), since the 19 Samsung devices in NAS-Bench-201 are highly correlated.

- **Harsh Critic: NAS-Bench-201 is "structurally unsuitable" as ablation platform due to small size (15,625 architectures).** Partially valid as a observation, but NAS-Bench-201 is the standard benchmark for hardware-aware NAS ablations (same used by HELP, MetaD2A). - **Harsh Critic: 22 GPU hours on a single RTX2080Ti for a 15,625-architecture tabular space is "substantial overhead."** Accurate observation but the hypernetwork training cost is inherent to the method's design. - **Strength Finder: "Consistent superiority across four search spaces" as a generic strength.** Supported by specific figure evidence (Figures 3, 9, 10, 11) and is therefore specific enough to retain (kept in Strengths above).

## Novel Insights

The most genuinely novel insight in this work—not fully surfaced by individual reviewers—is the combination of two conditioning mechanisms in a single hypernetwork: preference-vector conditioning (from MOO literature) and device-embedding conditioning (from meta-learning NAS literature). Neither idea alone is new, but their integration within a differentiable one-shot framework with MGD as the aggregation rule creates a method that is strictly more general than prior hardware-aware NAS. The consequence is that the trained MetaHypernetwork implicitly learns a continuous manifold of architectures jointly indexed by user intent and hardware context, enabling both preference-sweeping and zero-shot device transfer at inference time with no additional search cost. The empirical finding that MGD substantially outperforms simpler aggregation schemes (mean, sequential, MC) is an actionable result for multi-device NAS practitioners beyond MODNAS specifically.

## Suggestions

1. **Rename or qualify the efficiency-quality claim.** Rewrite the abstract and Section 4.1 to clearly distinguish the efficiency contribution (same total compute, better use per device) from a quality contribution. Provide at least one experiment where a baseline gets the full per-device equivalent compute so the quality comparison is unambiguous.
2. **Add a MetaPredictor sensitivity ablation** (even just 3 training set sizes) in Section 4.1 where ground-truth NAS-Bench-201 latencies provide a reference. Even a correlation plot of predicted vs. true HV would substantially address this gap.
3. **Clarify HAT evaluation protocol:** state explicitly whether the 24 architectures from MODNAS are evaluated using shared supernetwork weights or standalone trained models, and account for that cost in the efficiency comparison.
4. **Report standard deviations** or at minimum standard errors across the 3 seeds in the main figures, given the acknowledged instability of gradient-based NAS.
