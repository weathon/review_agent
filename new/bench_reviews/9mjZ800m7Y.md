## Summary

MODNAS proposes a differentiable multi-objective NAS method that constructs a full Pareto front across multiple hardware devices in a single search run. It uses a MetaHypernetwork conditioned on preference vectors and device embeddings (the latter enabling zero-shot transfer to unseen devices), combined with Multiple Gradient Descent (MGD) to jointly optimize across all devices and objectives. The method is evaluated on 4 search spaces (NAS-Bench-201, MobileNetV3/OFA, HAT, HW-GPT-Bench) with up to 19 devices and 3 objectives.

## Strengths

- **Single-search Pareto front across devices is a genuinely valuable formulation.** The reduction from O(CT) to O(1) search cost (Table 1, Equation 5) directly addresses a real practical need. Figure 3 shows MODNAS outperforming all baselines across all 19 devices on NB201 despite baselines receiving 19× more search budget (one run per device), which is a compelling demonstration of the core claim.

- **MGD ablation provides clear evidence for the algorithmic contribution.** Figure 6 shows MGD achieving higher final hypervolume, better convergence, and superior anytime performance compared to mean, sequential, and MC-sampling gradient aggregation on NB201. This isolates the MGD contribution from other design choices.

- **Zero-shot device transfer is well-demonstrated.** The t-SNE visualization (referenced in Section 4.1, Figure 12 in appendix) confirms that learned embeddings capture device similarity. Transfer results on 6 held-out NB201 devices (Figure 3, red labels), 1 held-out HAT device (Figure 9), and 1 held-out OFA device show the mechanism works without finetuning.

- **Scalability to 3 objectives without modification is a practical advantage.** Figure 8 demonstrates accuracy/latency/energy optimization on NB201 with FPGA and Eyeriss energy data, approaching the global optimum front without additional search cost or hyperparameter tuning.

- **Generalization across diverse search spaces and tasks.** The method is evaluated on CNNs (NB201, MobileNetV3), encoder-decoder Transformers (HAT/WMT), and decoder-only Transformers (HW-GPT-Bench/OpenWebText), showing the approach is not specific to one architecture family.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Evaluation on large search spaces relies on supernet proxies without standalone trained validation.** On OFA, the paper explicitly states (Section 4.3): "To evaluate the 24 points generated by our MetaHypernetwork and baselines, we use the OFA pretrained supernetwork." On HW-GPT-Bench, the supernet weights are frozen and used for evaluation. Supernet accuracy is known to correlate imperfectly with standalone trained accuracy, particularly in DARTS-family methods (Zela et al., 2020, which the authors cite). While NB201 uses ground-truth metrics and provides strong evidence, the practically relevant spaces (OFA, HW-GPT-Bench) lack any validation that the supernet proxy is reliable for architectures found by MODNAS. This undermines the headline hypervolume improvements on these spaces. The paper acknowledges this in its limitations ("the method relies on differentiable proxies for objectives, which may not always align with ground truth values"), but acknowledging a limitation does not resolve it.

- **Baselines on large search spaces are exclusively blackbox, conflating weight-sharing efficiency with method contributions.** On HAT, OFA, and HW-GPT-Bench, every baseline (RS, LS, NSGA-II, RS-BO, qEHVI, etc.) evaluates architectures independently without weight sharing. MODNAS's dominant efficiency advantage on these spaces comes fundamentally from the supernet paradigm, not specifically from the MetaHypernetwork or MGD. The paper includes the RHPN baseline and MGD ablation on NB201 (Section 4.1, Figure 3, 6), which isolate contributions in that setting, but no weight-sharing baseline exists on any large search space. Adding a simple "DARTS with scalarized objectives" or "run the same supernet search independently per device" baseline on at least one large space would significantly strengthen claims that the MetaHypernetwork+MGD machinery specifically contributes beyond weight sharing.

### Minor

- **Ablations limited to NB201.** Key design choices—MetaHypernetwork architecture, K hypernetworks, Dirichlet sampling, number of search devices, MGD vs. alternatives—are all ablated only on the small tabular NB201 space (6,466 architectures). Whether these choices transfer to the much larger spaces (~10¹⁷–10²³ architectures) is not established. This is a meaningful gap given the different loss landscapes and supernet characteristics.

- **"First to provide a global view of the Pareto solutions in a single search run" (Section 1) is overclaimed.** Hypernetwork-based methods like PHN (Navon et al., 2021, cited by the authors) and Condor/Lin et al. (2020) also produce Pareto fronts from a single hypernetwork. The novelty is specifically in conditioning on device embeddings alongside preference vectors for multi-device transfer, which is a genuine contribution but should be stated more precisely rather than as an absolute first.

### Trivial
None.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Standalone validation of at least a few architectures from the Pareto front on OFA or HW-GPT-Bench (train from scratch, report true accuracy/perplexity) would directly address the supernet proxy concern and significantly strengthen the paper.

- A weight-sharing ablation baseline on at least one large space (e.g., running OFA supernet search independently per device with fixed scalarizations) would isolate the MetaHypernetwork+MGD contributions from the inherent advantage of weight sharing.

- Analysis of MetaPredictor accuracy on held-out architectures and its correlation with distance from the training distribution would increase confidence in the hardware objective guidance.

## Removed Points

These points are flagged to be removed, treat them with caution:

- **Harsh critic's claim that "zero-shot transfer" is misleading because reference architectures must be evaluated on new devices**: The paper clearly describes that device embeddings d_t are obtained "by evaluating a fixed set of reference architectures on device t" (Section 3.2). This is standard in hardware-aware NAS and is disclosed. "Zero-shot" here refers to no additional search or finetuning, not zero measurement cost. The criticism misreads the scope of the claim.

- **Harsh critic's claim about "O(1) notation could mislead readers into underestimating the absolute cost"**: The paper clearly discloses actual costs (6 days on 8 A6000 GPUs for HAT, 1 day on 8 GPUs for OFA). The O(1) notation is relative to T and C, which the paper specifies. This is a standard theoretical framing, not misleading when actual costs are given.

- **Harsh critic's concern about gradient conflicts in lower-level update**: The mean gradient for the supernet update (line 15 of Algorithm 1) is a standard and reasonable design choice in multi-task NAS. The concern about gradient conflicts is speculative without evidence that it causes problems in practice. The NB201 results with ground-truth metrics show the method works effectively.

- **Harsh critic's claim about MetaD2A+HELP comparison being unfair**: Running MetaD2A+HELP 24 times with different constraints is the appropriate comparison since MetaD2A+HELP is designed for single-constraint optimization. The paper's framing accurately presents the experiment as profiling quality comparison.

## Novel Insights

The key insight that the combination of device-conditioned MetaHypernetworks with MGD for upper-level updates constitutes a genuinely novel design in the differentiable NAS literature is well-supported: MGD ensures that the hypernetwork update direction simultaneously improves all devices, and the device-attention mechanism enables sharing statistical strength across devices while maintaining device-specific specialization. The ablation on NB201 (Figure 6) clearly shows MGD matters beyond simpler aggregation schemes.

## Suggestions

- Add a weight-sharing NAS baseline on at least one large space (e.g., OFA) that searches independently per device with fixed scalarizations. This single experiment would cleanly demonstrate whether the MetaHypernetwork+MGD framework adds value beyond weight sharing.

- Train and evaluate a few architectures (e.g., preference vector extremes and midpoint) from scratch on OFA to validate that supernet improvements correspond to genuine architecture improvements.

- Qualify the "first" claim in the introduction to specify first in the multi-device differentiable NAS setting rather than in all MOO settings.
