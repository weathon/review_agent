## Summary
MODNAS proposes a multi-objective differentiable neural architecture search algorithm that uses a conditional MetaHypernetwork and Multiple Gradient Descent to profile Pareto-optimal architectures across multiple hardware devices in a single search run, claiming zero-shot transferability to new devices via learned hardware embeddings.

## Strengths
- **Novel formulation**: The paper introduces a principled integration of hypernetworks, MGD, and device conditioning to solve multi-device MOO-NAS in a single search run.
- **Strong empirical validation**: Comprehensive evaluation across 4 search spaces (NAS-Bench-201, MobileNetV3, HAT Transformer, HW-GPT-Bench), up to 19 devices, and 3 objectives, demonstrating consistent superiority over baselines.
- **Wide baseline coverage**: Compares against 10+ methods including random search, evolutionary (NSGA-II, CMA-ES), Bayesian (qEHVI, RS-BO, LS-BO), and prior MOO-NAS methods (MO-ASHA, RMT, MetaD2A+HELP).
- **Rigorous ablations**: Figure 6 compares MGD against mean/sequential/MC-sampling gradient aggregation; Figure 7 evaluates constrained vs. unconstrained search.
- **Practical utility**: Zero-shot inference to new devices (conditioned on device features) eliminates need for per-device retraining.
- **Code release**: Repository provided for reproducibility.

## Weaknesses

### Major
- **MetaPredictor accuracy not reported** — The MetaPredictors for latency/energy are central to gradient-based search, as their predictions directly influence hypernetwork updates (Algorithm 1, line 6). The paper completely omits any evaluation of predictor quality (e.g., R², MAE, correlation on held-out architectures). Without this, we cannot assess whether search success is due to good predictors or luck. This is a fundamental methodological omission. *(Sections 3.2, 4.1–4.3)*

- **Fairness concerns in baseline comparisons** — In the MobileNetV3/ImageNet experiments, MODNAS starts from a pretrained OFA supernetwork and fine-tunes only the MetaHypernetwork, while baselines are "run for 192 hours using the OFA pretrained accuracy predictor" but are not explicitly described as starting from the same pretrained supernetwork weights. This creates an asymmetry: MODNAS leverages existing weight knowledge, while baselines may train from scratch. Additionally, Figure 5 plots baselines' hypervolume improving with evaluation count while MODNAS appears as a single point ("only 24 full evaluations"), an apples-to-oranges visualization. Although the text clarifies budgets, the figure is misleading without a fair per-evaluation comparison. *(Sections 4.3, Figure 5)*

- **Zero-shot transfer claim is overstated** — The paper states "13 for search and 6 at test time" but the test devices are additional Samsung Galaxy models, which are similar to search devices. True zero-shot transfer should demonstrate generalization to qualitatively new device families (e.g., from mobile CPUs to FPGAs or different architecture generations), not just more instances of the same category. The results show effective interpolation among similar devices, not extrapolation to novel hardware. *(Section 4.1, Figure 3)*

### Minor
- **O(1) complexity phrasing** — The paper states MODNAS has O(1) search cost and Pareto-set building cost, but Algorithm 1 loops over T devices per epoch, making per-epoch cost O(T). The intended meaning (no per-device retraining) is correct, but the phrasing could mislead readers about scaling. The distinction is clarified later but the initial claim is overly succinct. *(Section 4.4, Table 1)*

- **Limited analysis of gradient dynamics** — While MGD is validated via an ablation (Figure 6), there is no investigation of gradient conflicts or alignment across devices/objectives. Understanding when and why MGD succeeds would strengthen the methodological contribution.

### Trivial
- None that affect the core claims after parser artifact removal.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Report MetaPredictor accuracy metrics (R², MAE) for each hardware objective on held-out data.
- Clarify in the main text whether baselines in the MobileNetV3/GPT experiments start from the same pretrained supernetwork as MODNAS.
- Include diversity metrics (e.g., spacing, Δ-indicator) alongside hypervolume to assess Pareto front uniformity.
- Visualize concrete architectures selected for different devices/preferences to illustrate the conditional hypernetwork's behavior.
- Test zero-shot transfer on truly unseen device categories (e.g., train on mobile CPUs, test on edge TPUs).

## Removed Points
**These points are flagged to be removed, treat them with caution.** They were identified by the harsh critic but are either nitpicky, misunderstand the paper, or already addressed.

- *"O(1) claim is misleading because MODNAS iterates over T devices"* — The paper's O(1) refers to number of independent search runs required across devices, not per-iteration cost. Table 1 correctly contrasts with methods needing O(T) or O(CT) full searches. The claim stands as intended.
- *"MGD justification lacking theoretical or empirical depth"* — The method builds directly on Sener & Koltun (2018) for Frank-Wolfe solving; NAS papers rarely provide full convergence proofs for such components. The ablation shows clear benefit, which is sufficient empirical justification for an applied venue.
- *"MetaHypernetwork initialization not justified"* — Initializing to uniform distribution is standard practice; a non-uniform initialization would be a minor tweak, not a methodological gap.
- *"Line 15 averages device-specific gradients without weighting"* — This is a design choice (treating all devices equally) and is reasonable; requesting adaptive weighting is scope creep.
- *"Missing failure mode analysis"* — Useful for discussion but not a weakness; Figure 11's front is continuous.
- *"Missing appendix on proofs/hyperparameters"* — The parser strips appendices; the original submission contains them.

## Novel Insights
The paper's central insight is that architecture search across multiple devices and objectives can be amortized through a meta-learned hypernetwork that maps device features and preference vectors to architectural distributions. This turns what would be T independent searches into a single multi-task optimization, with MGD ensuring Pareto-stationary updates across device-specific objectives. The zero-shot capability emerges from the hypernetwork learning to associate new hardware with similar seen devices via the embedding space.

## Suggestions
- In rebuttal, provide MetaPredictor accuracy numbers (including evaluation on held-out architectures) and discuss how errors might affect search.
- Clarify baseline implementations in the main text: specifically state whether baselines in the OFA/GPT experiments begin from the same pretrained supernetwork weights or from scratch.
- For zero-shot claims, add a sentence in the limitations section clarifying that transfer works within the distribution of device types seen during training and that truly novel hardware families remain future work.
- Consider adding a brief analysis of gradient alignment across devices to better understand MGD's behavior.
