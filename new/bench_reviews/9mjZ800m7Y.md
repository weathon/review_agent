## Summary

MODNAS proposes a hardware-aware multi-objective differentiable NAS algorithm that profiles the Pareto front across multiple devices and objectives in a single search run. A MetaHypernetwork, conditioned on both preference vectors and hardware device embeddings, generates Pareto-optimal architectures without re-running search for each new device or constraint. The method combines linear scalarization with multiple gradient descent (MGD) across devices and is evaluated on four search spaces (NAS-Bench-201, MobileNetV3/OFA, HAT Transformer, GPT-2), three tasks, and up to 19 devices.

---

## Strengths

- **Single-run multi-device Pareto front (Table 1 + Figures 3, 9, 10, 11):** MODNAS is the first differentiable NAS method to reduce both search cost and Pareto set build cost to O(1) in the number of devices T and constraints C, versus O(CT) for constrained baselines like MetaD2A+HELP. This is a concrete, quantified structural contribution.

- **Broad and genuine empirical coverage:** Experiments span 4 search spaces (cell CNN, MobileNetV3, encoder-decoder Transformer, GPT-2), 3 tasks (image classification, machine translation, language modeling), and up to 19 devices with 3 objectives. This scope meaningfully exceeds most prior NAS work.

- **Quality superiority in asymmetric-budget experiments:** In the HAT experiment (Section 4.2), baselines receive 2.5× more total runtime budget than MODNAS; MODNAS still achieves higher hypervolume. In the OFA experiment (Section 4.3), baselines run for 192 hours versus MODNAS's 1 day on 8 GPUs; MODNAS still achieves higher average HV across 12 devices. These are the cleaner comparisons that support quality — not merely efficiency — superiority.

- **MGD ablation (Figure 6):** The ablation directly isolates MGD versus mean-gradient, sequential, and MC-sampling updates, showing MGD achieves higher final HV and faster convergence. This justifies a key algorithmic choice.

- **Sample efficiency on NAS-Bench-201 (Figure 5):** MODNAS achieves HV ≈ 0.94 with only 24 architecture evaluations on a held-out test device, while all baselines require substantially more evaluations to approach this level — a compelling efficiency result.

- **Scalability to 3 objectives (Figure 8):** MODNAS scales to optimizing accuracy, latency, and energy simultaneously without any methodological changes or hyperparameter retuning, demonstrating genuine extensibility.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **MetaPredictor accuracy on test devices is never validated.** Contribution 2 ("zero-shot transferability to new devices without re-search or fine-tuning") rests critically on the MetaPredictor accurately predicting latency/energy for the 6 held-out NAS-Bench-201 test devices. The MetaPredictor is pretrained on 13 training devices and then frozen. Its prediction accuracy on unseen test devices (Kendall-τ, Spearman-ρ, or RMSE) is never reported. Since the MetaPredictor's predictions directly drive upper-level gradient computation for hardware objectives, systematic bias on test devices would silently corrupt the zero-shot Pareto front quality. The t-SNE embedding clustering in Figure 12 is suggestive but does not substitute for measuring prediction accuracy. This is the most important gap for the paper's strongest claim.

- **NAS-Bench-201 headline budget comparison is ambiguous about quality vs. amortization superiority.** For the 19-device NAS-Bench-201 experiment, MODNAS uses 22 GPU hours total; each baseline receives 22/19 ≈ 1.16 GPU hours per device (one search run each). This is the legitimate "same total compute" framing for the amortization scenario, but it makes it impossible to isolate whether MODNAS finds *higher-quality* Pareto fronts or is simply a *more efficient amortization* strategy. Figure 20a runs baselines at double the total budget (still ≈ 2.3 hours/device); MODNAS remains superior, which is evidence but not a full equalization. The HAT and OFA experiments (where baselines get *more* total budget) provide better evidence of quality superiority, but the primary 19-device result remains ambiguous on this axis. The paper should clearly separate the amortization and quality claims; as written, both are asserted together based on the most budget-asymmetric comparison.

### Minor

- **K hypernetworks not ablated.** The MetaHypernetwork uses K independent hypernetworks combined via an attention-weighted sum (Section 3.2, Figure 2). K is a key design choice, but its sensitivity is never studied. No ablation across K ∈ {1, 5, 10, 20} is presented. Since K controls the capacity of the device-routing mechanism, this matters for reproducibility and for understanding where the gain comes from.

- **Linear scalarization's limitation for non-convex Pareto fronts is not empirically characterized.** The paper claims effectiveness "beyond convex Pareto fronts" (Section 4.3, HW-GPT-Bench), but does not report what fraction of the 24 sampled architectures fall on non-convex portions of the true Pareto front. On NAS-Bench-201, where ground-truth is computable, an overlay of MODNAS's 24 points against the true Pareto front would directly reveal coverage of non-convex regions. This would strengthen or correct the non-convexity claim.

None.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Pareto front overlay on NAS-Bench-201 ground truth:** Since NAS-Bench-201 provides exact ground-truth for all architectures, overlaying MODNAS's 24-point approximation and baseline fronts against the true Pareto front would directly quantify coverage quality in a way HV alone does not.

- **HV as a function of number of training devices:** Showing HV on the 6 test devices as training devices vary from 1 to 13 would quantify how much of MODNAS's advantage comes from multi-device meta-learning versus the hypernetwork architecture alone, and would guide practitioners on how many devices to include at search time.

## Novel Insights

The most genuinely novel insight in the paper is the framing of multi-device NAS as a multi-task learning problem solvable by a single hypernetwork conditioned on both hardware embeddings and preference vectors, with MGD aggregating across device gradients. This is cleaner than prior constrained-objective methods because it separates the Pareto front approximation problem from the constraint specification problem: a user can specify preferences at inference time without re-running any search. The empirical finding that only 2 training devices suffice for reasonable zero-shot generalization to 17 test devices (Figure 25 in appendix) — if validated by predictor accuracy metrics — would be a strong practical insight for deployment scenarios.

## Suggestions

1. **Report MetaPredictor prediction accuracy (Kendall-τ or Spearman-ρ) on held-out test devices** for each hardware metric separately, and add a scatter plot of predicted vs. ground-truth latency on test devices. This is essential to validate the zero-shot claim.

2. **Add equal per-device budget comparison for NAS-Bench-201**: run the best-performing baseline(s) with the full 22 GPU hours per device (on 1–3 representative devices) to cleanly separate amortization efficiency from Pareto front quality superiority.

3. **Ablate K (number of hypernetworks)** across at least K ∈ {1, 5, 10, 20} to characterize sensitivity and guide practitioners.

4. **Overlay MODNAS and baseline Pareto fronts against the NAS-Bench-201 ground-truth front** to directly measure coverage of the true Pareto set.
