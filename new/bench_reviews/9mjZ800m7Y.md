## Summary

MODNAS proposes a differentiable NAS framework that profiles the entire Pareto front across multiple hardware devices and objectives in a single search run. The core idea is a MetaHypernetwork conditioned on both preference vectors (scalarizations of the objective space) and device embeddings, optimized via Multiple Gradient Descent (MGD) to simultaneously handle multi-device and multi-objective conflicts. Experiments span 4 search spaces (NAS-Bench-201, MobileNetV3/OFA, HAT, HW-GPT-Bench), up to 19 devices and 3 objectives.

## Strengths

- **Principled and novel combination of MetaHypernetwork + MGD formulation**: Conditioning a hypernetwork on preference vectors and device embeddings, with MGD for cross-device gradient aggregation, is a clean and extensible design for multi-device multi-objective NAS. The modular architecture (MetaHypernetwork → Architect → Supernetwork/MetaPredictor) is well-decomposed and clearly specified (Algorithm 1, Fig. 1).

- **Strong ground-truth results on NAS-Bench-201** (Section 4.1, Fig. 3): With tabular ground-truth metrics available, MODNAS consistently outperforms 9 baselines across 19 devices. The MGD ablation (Fig. 6) provides clear evidence that MGD outperforms mean/sequential/MC gradient aggregation, and the 3-objective experiment (Fig. 8) demonstrates scalability.

- **Genuine efficiency advantage**: Table 1 and Figure 7 show that a single MetaHypernetwork producing architectures for arbitrary device-preference combinations achieves comparable/better HV than MetaD2A+HELP, which requires 24 separate constrained searches. The comparison of search complexity from O(CT) to O(1) is compelling.

- **Scope of evaluation**: 4 qualitatively different search spaces (cnn, encoder-decoder transformer, decoder-only transformer), 3 tasks (classification, translation, LM), up to 19 devices and 3 objectives—this breadth strengthens generalizability claims.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None

### Major

- **Large-scale experiments rely on supernet/predictor-based evaluation without from-scratch validation**: For OFA/ImageNet and HW-GPT-Bench, the paper evaluates architectures using the pretrained supernetwork (explicitly stated for OFA: "To evaluate the 24 points generated by our MetaHypernetwork and baselines, we use the OFA pretrained supernetwork") rather than from-scratch training. On NAS-Bench-201 (ground truth) and HAT (BLEU scores, presumably fine-tuned), results are verifiable—but on the two most novel and challenging search spaces, the absolute quality of discovered architectures remains uncertain. Supernet ranking quality is a known issue in NAS; architectures that rank well under shared weights may not hold their positions when trained independently. This matters because the claimed Pareto optimality of produced solutions can only be verified with ground-truth evaluation. The paper acknowledges this as a limitation but does not provide even partial verification.

- **No explicit verification that preference conditioning produces meaningfully distinct architectures**: The entire mechanism for profiling the Pareto front rests on conditioning the MetaHypernetwork on preference vector **r**. If the hypernetwork partially ignores **r**—producing similar architectures for different preferences—the "Pareto front profiling in a single run" claim collapses. The Pareto front plots (Figs. 7, 9, 11) show spread, but this could also result from sampling stochasticity. The paper provides no analysis of how architectural choices (kernel sizes, depths, expansion ratios) vary with **r**, or correlation/MI between **r** and these choices. Such analysis would be straightforward and would directly verify the mechanism.

### Minor

- **The O(1) search cost designation in Table 1 is somewhat misleading**: It conflates "no additional per-device/per-constraint cost" with "no cost." The total pipeline includes supernet training (substantial for OFA/transformer models), MetaPredictor pretraining, and MetaHypernetwork training. The narrative text contextualizes this, but the table presents O(1) as a standalone comparison without total GPU-hour accounting alongside methods that also need supernet pretraining (like OFA).

- **Theoretical justification for Equation 5 is thin**: The transition from per-device bi-level problems (Eq. 4) to a single objective with expectation over scalarizations and sum over devices (Eq. 5) lacks formal convergence guarantees. Convergence to a stationary point for Φ does not formally ensure that every conditional distribution α|(**r**, d_t) is Pareto-optimal for its corresponding preference. The paper relies on MGD for cross-device conflicts and scalarization for multi-objective conflicts, but the interaction between these two mechanisms lacks theoretical grounding. This is an acceptable empirical contribution, but the theoretical gap should be acknowledged.

## Novel Insights

The interaction between preference-conditioned hypernetworks and MGD is an underexplored design space. The key insight is that MGD naturally resolves device conflicts during MetaHypernetwork updates, while the preference vector handles objective tradeoffs—these are orthogonal mechanisms that complement each other. The empirical evidence from the MGD ablation (Fig. 6) showing clear superiority over mean/sequential/MC aggregation suggests that cross-device gradient conflicts are a real bottleneck that simpler aggregation schemes fail to address, making this more than just an incremental combination of existing techniques.

## Suggestions

- Validate the Pareto front quality on at least one large-scale search space (OFA or HW-GPT-Bench) by from-scratch training a small subset of architectures (5–10 spanning the preference spectrum) and reporting the gap between supernet estimates and true performance.
- Report the correlation between preference vectors **r** and specific architectural choices (e.g., kernel sizes, depths) for at least one experiment, to directly verify that the conditioning mechanism functions as intended.
- Add total GPU-hour costs (including all pretraining) to Table 1 or the text for fair comparison with methods that also require supernet training.
