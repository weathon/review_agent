## Summary
This paper formulates silent data corruption during gradient aggregation in distributed training as gradient inconsistency (workers receiving different noisy averaged gradients), proves it causes accumulated model divergence and failed convergence, and proposes PAFT—a system combining periodic parameter synchronization (PAFT-Sync) with dynamic frequency adjustment and overlapping communication (PAFT-Dyn). The paper provides convergence analysis showing PAFT restores standard SGD convergence rates, and implements the system on PyTorch Distributed, evaluating it on ResNet models (4-32 GPUs), GPT-2, and LLaMA-2 across four noise levels.

## Strengths
- **Complete theoretical arc from problem formulation to solution:** Theorem 3.2 (Eq. 7) proves that the $T_3$ term in the DSGD convergence bound only vanishes when $\eta_t=0$ (non-convergence under noise), and Theorem 4.2 (Eq. 10) shows PAFT-Sync replaces this with a controllable $\mathcal{O}(\sigma^2 H/L)$ term that vanishes at standard SGD rate. Corollary 4.3 establishes matching convergence rates. This is a genuine theoretical contribution linking the synchronization frequency $H$ to the convergence-error tradeoff.
- **Comprehensive empirical coverage across model scales:** Experiments evaluate ResNet-18, ResNet-50, GPT-2, and LLaMA-2 (via LoRA) across 4, 8, 16, and 32 GPUs with four noise levels ($\sigma^2 \in \{0.0001, 0.001, 0.01, 0.1\}$). Tables 1-2 demonstrate consistent improvement over Noised DSGD, and Figures 7-8 extend the results to LLM pretraining and fine-tuning, showing the method's architectural agnosticism.
- **Systems-aware design with overlapping communication:** Section 4.3 and Algorithm 2 (Lines 5-8) overlap parameter averaging with backward propagation. Table 3 shows PAFT reduces per-iteration overhead versus PAFT-Sync from 0.411s to 0.373s at 32 workers (~9.2% improvement), demonstrating the overlap mechanism works in practice on real GPU hardware.
- **Burst noise experiments validate recovery behavior:** Figure 9 demonstrates PAFT's ability to recover from intermittent large noise events (N(0, 0.1) and N(0, 1.0) every 500 iterations), modeling burst SDC scenarios where accuracy drops sharply and PAFT pulls parameters back from the valley.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **Missing comparison against standard robust aggregation baselines:** The evaluation (Section 5, Tables 1-2) compares PAFT only against "Noised DSGD" and "Oracle." It does not compare against established robust aggregation techniques (Krum, Multi-Krum, Trimmed Mean, Coordinate-wise Median) or standard checkpoint-based recovery protocols. While Section 7 and Section 6 frame this as a distinct problem from Byzantine fault tolerance, both settings involve gradient-level noise/corruption. Without showing whether parameter-level synchronization outperforms or complements these simpler gradient-level defenses, the claim that PAFT is an "effective fault-tolerant system" for SDC is not empirically established. A reader cannot determine whether PAFT's ~19% overhead (Table 3) is better than the cost of existing methods.

- **PAFT-Dyn's dynamic frequency adjustment is heuristic, not derived from the convergence proof:** Algorithm 2 (Line 11) sets $H_{\text{new}} = \|g_t^m\|/\sigma_{\text{est}}$ where $\sigma_{\text{est}} = \|\tilde{\theta}_t - \theta_t^m\|$ measures accumulated parameter drift. Section 4.2 (lines 254-285) states this balances the second and third terms in Eq. 10 by setting $H = \sigma_g/\sigma$, but the leap from Theorem 4.2's convergence bound to this specific heuristic is not formally derived. Moreover, parameter drift $\sigma_{\text{est}}$ is a non-linear accumulation of noise, data sampling variance, optimizer state, and learning rate decay; inverting it to estimate $\sigma$ is under-specified. This undermines the paper's headline claim of "dynamic synchronization frequency scheduling based on profiled error degrees," as the dynamic mechanism lacks the theoretical grounding that the static PAFT-Sync analysis provides.

- **The Gaussian noise abstraction disconnects from real SDC failure modes:** Definition 2.1 (Eq. 4) models SDC errors as i.i.d. Gaussian noise $\epsilon_t^m \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ added per-worker per-iteration. Real SDC from hardware bit flips and NVLink/PCIe communication noise are sparse, structured, and often correlated across tensor dimensions or memory addresses. While the paper frames this as a "generalization" (Section 2), the entire theoretical analysis (Lemma 3.1, Theorems 3.2 and 4.2) and the empirical results (all software-injected Gaussian noise) assume properties the cited failure modes do not exhibit. The paper's claim that PAFT targets "bit corruptions and communication noise" (Introduction, line 23; Conclusion, line 428) is not validated against hardware-realistic error patterns, limiting confidence in real-world applicability.

### Minor

- **No break-even analysis for practical deployment:** Table 3 shows PAFT adds a fixed ~18.9% overhead at 32 workers relative to vanilla DSGD. While Section 4.2 argues that dynamic adjustment reduces unnecessary synchronization, the paper never characterizes SDC occurrence rates in modern GPU clusters or provides an analysis of when PAFT's fault recovery benefit outweighs its constant communication penalty. Without a practical break-even calculation, it is unclear whether PAFT should be enabled during all training or only in high-fault-rate regimes.

- **Adam/momentum convergence claims are undersupported in the main text:** Section 4.4 (lines 300-305) hand-waves the extension to SGD momentum and Adam, stating that optimizer state divergence "may not be accumulated" due to moving averages, and defers the experimental evidence to Appendix D. The main text provides no theoretical analysis for how parameter resets interact with Adam's second-moment estimates, which are known to be sensitive to sudden parameter changes. Given that LLM experiments (GPT-2, LLaMA-2) use Adam (Section 5, line 337), the Adam convergence claim is central but insufficiently substantiated in the body.

### Trivial

- **Pseudo-code notation is syntactically ambiguous:** Algorithm 1 (Line 7, `t + 1 % H = 0`) and Algorithm 2 have unclear operator precedence. This should be written as `(t+1) % H == 0`. The intended logic is clear from context, but the notation is non-standard.

- **Figure numbering inconsistencies in the parser output:** The figure references in the text appear garbled (e.g., multiple figures labeled "Figure 4"). The original figures in the PDF are presumably numbered correctly, but the reference text is inconsistent.

## Nice-to-Haves
- A layer-wise divergence analysis showing which parts of models (early vs. late layers in ResNet/LLaMA-2) accumulate the most drift, to explore whether selective layer synchronization would be more efficient than global parameter averaging.
- Integration of PAFT with hybrid-parallel frameworks (DeepSpeed, Megatron-LM) to verify scalability in tensor/pipeline parallel training regimes.
- A brief ablation quantifying the individual contribution of overlap vs. dynamic scheduling to the overhead reduction (Table 3 combines both).

## Removed Points
These points are flagged to be removed, treat them with caution:
- **Harsh Critic Claim 1: "Self-defeating control plane invalidates efficiency claims"**: The reviewer argues that Line 11's `All-Reduce` negates overhead savings. However, this All-Reduce only transmits two scalars (gradient norm and noise estimate), which is orders of magnitude cheaper than full model parameter synchronization. The overhead is negligible compared to the data plane. This weakens to the minor concern about heuristic estimation above.
- **Harsh Critic Claim 2: "Algorithm 2 enforces sequential dependencies contradicting overlapping claims"**: The algorithm's `Wait for \tilde{\theta}_t` line is the synchronization point—this is inherent to any synchronization operation. The overlapping achieved is that the All-Reduce computation is initiated in one iteration while the next iteration's forward/backward computation proceeds. The reviewer conflates the existence of a barrier with the absence of overlap. The overlap mechanism itself (initiate sync → compute → wait for sync → update) is standard and correct.
- **Harsh Critic Claim 3: "The paper explicitly acknowledges it fails at high noise, contradicting the abstract"**: The paper does acknowledge a performance gap at high noise (Section 7, Section 5 text after Table 2), but this is honest limitation-setting, not abstract-level contradiction. The abstract states PAFT "mitigates" errors, not that it eliminates them at all noise levels.
- **Harsh Critic Claim 4: "Missing results for PAFT with Adam"**: The paper states it "provides results of synchronizing the momentum and precondition in Appendix D." Since the parser strips appendices, these experiments may exist in the original submission. This is a presentation issue in the main text (addressed as a Minor point) but not a missing-result issue.
- **Harsh Critic Claim 5: "Incremental theoretical analysis, standard result in distributed optimization"**: The specific derivation linking parameter synchronization frequency $H$ to bounded divergence and convergence recovery in DSGD with gradient inconsistency is not a standard result. While parameter averaging is well-known in local-SGD, the specific problem setting (gradient aggregation noise causing model divergence leading to non-convergence) and its theoretical characterization (the $T_3$ term) are novel contributions.
- **Strength Finder claim about the derivation of $H = \sigma_g/\sigma$ from Theorem 4.2 being "principled"**: This overstates the derivation. The paper notes this balance in passing but does not rigorously derive it from the theorem; this is an intuitive heuristic that the PAFT-Dyn component relies on.

## Novel Insights
The paper makes a structurally interesting observation that gradient aggregation errors cause model divergence to *accumulate* over training iterations (Lemma 3.1), meaning the damage from SDC is not just a per-step accuracy hit but a compounding divergence that eventually degrades gradients to uselessness ($\nabla f(\theta^i)$ having "no useful descent information" about $\theta^j$ when models diverge, Remark after Lemma 3.1). Periodic parameter synchronization acts as a reset that bounds this compounding, which is conceptually distinct from per-step gradient corrections (Byzantine robust aggregation) and from checkpoint-based recovery. The insight that parameter-level synchronization and gradient-level robust filtering address complementary aspects of gradient corruption—one preventing drift accumulation, the other filtering corrupted descent directions—suggests a future hybrid approach that combines both.

## Suggestions
- Replace the sole reliance on i.i.d. Gaussian noise with at least one experiment using structured noise patterns (e.g., bit-flip emulation, sparse corruption) to validate the Gaussian abstraction.
- Add comparisons against at least one robust aggregation baseline (e.g., Trimmed Mean or Coordinate-wise Median) on a representative task to contextualize PAFT's effectiveness.
- Provide a formal derivation or explicit assumptions justifying the $H = \|g\|/\sigma_{\text{est}}$ heuristic in PAFT-Dyn, or clearly scope it as a heuristic with empirical justification.
- Add a break-even analysis: given an SDC rate (e.g., one fault per N hours in a cluster), calculate when PAFT's recovery benefit justifies its ~19% constant overhead.
