## Summary

The paper proposes Contrastive Optimal Transport Flow (COT Flow), a generative model that combines optimal transport, contrastive learning, and consistency models for unpaired image-to-image (I2I) translation. COT Flow first trains a neural OT map between two unpaired data distributions via a saddle-point objective, then trains a consistency-style encoder along OT-derived interpolation trajectories ("COT Pairs") to enable one-step or few-step sampling. The authors also introduce COT Editor, which leverages the arbitrary-prior property of OT to perform zero-shot image editing including composition, shape-texture coupling, and medical image augmentation.

## Strengths

- **Unified conceptual framework**: The paper draws an insightful parallel between consistency models and contrastive learning (both use Siamese architectures, EMA, stopgrad), providing a principled motivation for the COT Pair formulation. This observation is non-obvious and useful for the community.

- **Flexible prior distribution**: Unlike standard diffusion models constrained to Gaussian noise, COT Flow can operate between arbitrary source and target distributions, enabling unpaired I2I translation and a dual-channel editing space (both trajectory endpoints editable). This is a genuine structural advantage.

- **Proposition 3.1 provides theoretical grounding**: The proof connecting the augmentation in Eq. 12 to the dynamic extension of the entropic OT plan links the proposed interpolation to a well-established theoretical framework, even if the practical gap from using T_φ instead of T* remains (discussed below).

- **Competitive FID with 1 NFE**: Table 1 shows COT Flow achieves the best FID on all three benchmarks compared to GAN baselines (also 1 NFE) and SDEdit (500 NFE). The one-step generation is practically meaningful.

- **Creative editing scenarios**: COT Editor's composition, shape-texture coupling, and cardiac augmentation showcase interesting zero-shot applications that highlight the flexibility of the formulation.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **Limited and outdated baselines**: The paper compares only against DiscoGAN, CycleGAN, MUNIT, and SDEdit. Missing are recent OT-based I2I methods (Neural Optimal Transport, OT-CFM, DSBM, Rectified Flow for I2I), consistency distillation/training on the same tasks, and modern flow-matching baselines. Without these, it is impossible to assess whether COT Flow offers advantages over the current state of the art—particularly given the paper's explicit claim that recent flow-matching and rectified-flow methods "implicitly approach OT" (Fig. 2b). Comparing to these omitted methods is essential.

- **The trilemma claim is unsubstantiated for mode coverage and speed**: The paper claims to solve the "generative learning trilemma" (quality, speed, mode coverage) and places COT Flow at the center of Fig. 2a's Venn diagram. However: (a) only FID is reported—no diversity or mode-coverage metrics (e.g., Precision/Recall, LPIPS diversity, IS), making the "mode coverage" pillar untested; (b) NFE is reported as the speed metric without wall-clock time, FLOPs, or GPU memory comparisons, and SDEdit with 500 NFE is a strawman for "fast diffusion" given that consistency distillation, progressive distillation, and DPM-Solver all achieve far fewer steps. The trilemma framing is central yet the evidence is thin.

- **OT component's necessity is not validated**: T_φ is the conceptual lynchpin—defining COT Pairs and underpinning Proposition 3.1—but no experiment isolates whether OT specifically is needed. Table 2 shows "Reverse OT" degrades results, but that confounds direction with OT quality. There is no ablation replacing T_φ with simpler alternatives (e.g., random matching, CycleGAN pseudo-pairs, or a fixed linear map), leaving open whether "any roughly aligned pairing" would yield similar results. Furthermore, there is no diagnostic of how well T_φ approximates true OT (e.g., transport cost convergence, visualizations of transport plans), and Proposition 3.1 assumes T* (the optimal map), not the learned T_φ.

- **Scalability unknown**: All experiments are at 64×64 or 128×128 on relatively simple domains. Whether the two-stage pipeline (minimax OT training + contrastive encoder training) and the SGAD optimization scale to 256×256+ remains unaddressed, which is precisely the setting where fast generation matters most.

### Minor

- **SGAD training stability**: The neural OT map T_φ is trained via a GAN-style adversarial objective (Eqs. 10–11), which is known to be unstable. No training curves, convergence diagnostics, or sensitivity analysis for this component are provided. The "Reverse OT" ablation (Table 2) shows FID nearly doubling, suggesting the method is highly sensitive to the OT map's direction/quality, yet this fragility is not analyzed.

- **COT Editor is purely qualitative**: Figures 1 and 4 show visually plausible results but there is no quantitative evaluation (e.g., FID for edited outputs, identity preservation metrics, user studies), no comparison to alternative editing methods, and no failure case analysis. The medical application (cardiac MRI augmentation) would particularly benefit from clinical or task-based evaluation.

- **The consistency–contrastive analogy is observational, not formal**: Section 3.1 notes structural similarities (EMA, stopgrad, Siamese architecture, distance loss) between CMs and contrastive learning but does not prove a formal equivalence or derive implications. The hypothesis that "the consistency function is a trajectory's origin encoder" is intuitively motivated but not rigorously tested.

- **Ablation gaps**: Table 2 does not ablate key hyperparameters (noise scale σ, EMA decay, time step distribution) or architectural choices (capacity of T_φ, ψ_ω). Given the sensitivity shown by the "Reverse OT" result, such analysis would be informative.

### Trivial

- Algorithm 1 uses the notation `θ ← stopgrad(θ + η∇_θ L)` which is slightly ambiguous (the stopgrad is presumably applied only to one branch of the loss computation, not the update itself), but the intent is clear from the surrounding text.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Add recent OT-based and consistency-based baselines (NOT, OT-CFM, Rectified Flow, Consistency Distillation) on the same I2I benchmarks.
- Report diversity metrics (Precision/Recall, LPIPS diversity) and wall-clock training/inference time.
- Visualize learned trajectories (e.g., in a 2D toy setting or via latent-space projections) to verify whether they are approximately straight and non-crossing.
- Test on at least one higher-resolution (256×256+) dataset.
- Provide diagnostics of T_φ quality (transport cost, convergence plots) and ablate with simpler pairing alternatives.

## Removed Points

- **Concern about SDEdit being an unfair comparison because it uses 500 NFE**: The reviewer suggested SDEdit with 500 NFE is a "strawman." However, this comparison actually *favors* COT Flow (1 NFE vs. 500 NFE), so by the hard rules, this is not a valid criticism on unfairness grounds. The real concern is that SDEdit is not a representative strong baseline for fast diffusion, which is already covered under "limited baselines."

- **Concern about missing implementation details (time step distribution, architecture details)**: While some details are in Appendix A, the paper does describe the core algorithm. Reproducibility concerns about unspecified hyperparameters fall under the hard rule against nitpicks about reproducibility.

- **Concern about Algorithm 1 stopgrad notation**: This is a minor clarity issue, not a substantive methodological flaw, and the intent is clear.

- **"Overclaim" that OT rhetoric is purely decorative without trajectory verification**: While partially valid, this is better characterized as a lack of empirical verification rather than the rhetoric being wrong in principle. The concern is captured under "OT necessity not validated."

- **Formatting/style issues**: Removed per hard rules.

## Novel Insights

The most interesting observation across the reviews is that the paper's core contribution may be more practical than theoretical: the COT Pair formulation—training a consistency-style encoder on OT-derived interpolation pairs—is a simple and effective recipe that happens to work, but the narrative burden placed on "OT gives the fastest sampling" is not well-supported. The one-step generation speed is a property of the consistency-style architecture (any such model could do one-step), while OT's role is primarily as a pairing/alignment mechanism between unpaired domains. This reframing would strengthen the paper: COT Flow is best understood as leveraging OT to construct meaningful positive pairs for contrastive-consistency training, not as proof that OT trajectories are straight and non-crossing.

## Suggestions

1. Compare against at least 2–3 recent OT-based I2I methods (e.g., Neural Optimal Transport, OT-CFM) and one consistency distillation baseline on the same datasets.
2. Add Precision/Recall or coverage metrics alongside FID to substantially test the mode-coverage claim.
3. Ablate T_φ by replacing it with simpler pairing (random matching, CycleGAN pseudo-pairs) to isolate OT's specific contribution.
4. Revisit the framing: position OT as enabling meaningful positive-pair construction for cross-domain consistency training, which is more precise than "OT gives the fastest sampling."

## Score and Decision

Calibration against similar papers:

- **DIOTM** (Accept poster, avg ~6.5): Neural OT for I2I, more complete evaluation but also limited resolution.
- **GENOT** (Reject, avg ~6): Neural OT with theory-practice gap, limited experiments — similar pattern to this paper.
- **RDMD** (Reject, avg ~4): Rectified diffusion with limited baselines and overclaims — very similar weaknesses.
- **One-step Flow Matching** (Reject, avg ~5): Limited evaluation, strong claims — similar pattern.

This paper has a genuinely interesting idea (linking consistency models and contrastive learning via OT pairs) and promising initial results, but the evaluation is too narrow (only FID, only 3 small benchmarks, outdated baselines), the central claims are substantially overextended (trilemma "solved," "fastest" sampling), and the OT component's specific necessity is unvalidated. This places it below GENOT (which had at least complete baselines for its setting) and roughly at the level of RDMD/One-step Flow Matching, which received scores of 3–5.
