I'll now write the review.</think>## Summary

This paper studies localized machine unlearning, where only a small subset of model parameters are modified to remove the influence of a forget set. Drawing on memorization literature, the authors investigate localization strategies derived from memorization hypotheses (Deepest, CritMem), compare them with SalUn-style saliency localization (SalLoc), and through ablations identify channel-level granularity with weighted-gradient criticality as the best design. They propose DEL (Deletion by Example Localization), which combines this localization strategy with reset-and-finetune, and demonstrate state-of-the-art results on CIFAR-10/ResNet-18 and SVHN/ViT across unlearning and utility metrics.

## Strengths

- **Systematic investigation connecting memorization and unlearning.** The paper carefully evaluates multiple localization strategies derived from memorization hypotheses (Deepest, Shallowest, CritMem, SalLoc) and identifies that data-agnostic strategies fail to balance unlearning and utility, while data-dependent strategies with appropriate granularity succeed. The progression from analyzing existing approaches to designing an improved strategy is methodical and well-motivated, with honest reporting that direct translations of memorization hypotheses perform poorly (Fig. 2).

- **Thorough ablations and control experiments.** Table 1 cleanly isolates the effects of granularity (parameter vs. channel) and criticality criterion (gradient vs. weighted gradient). Table 3's random-masking control convincingly demonstrates that success depends on *which* parameters are selected, not merely *how many*. Table 4's investigation of forget-set-dependent vs. forget-set-agnostic criteria provides nuanced understanding of when tailoring matters (non-IID more than IID).

- **Strong empirical performance.** DEL achieves the best results among localized methods on all metrics across datasets, forget set types, and parameter budgets. Notably, it is robust to reduced parameter budgets (Figure 3) and can be paired with multiple unlearning algorithms effectively.

- **Practical efficiency improvement over CritMem.** By adopting a non-iterative, batched approach while retaining CritMem's beneficial design choices (channel granularity, weighted gradients), DEL achieves better results at lower computational cost.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **The connection between memorization and the proposed method is weaker than framing claims.** The paper's title and framing promise "improved localized unlearning through the lens of memorization," but the two localization strategies directly derived from memorization hypotheses (Deepest and CritMem) perform poorly as unlearning localizers (Fig. 2). DEL's localization strategy takes two components from CritMem (channel granularity, weighted gradients) and embeds them in a fundamentally different algorithm (batched, non-iterative). The paper does not validate that the channels selected by DEL are those that "memorize" the forget-set examples (e.g., via Feldman memorization scores or prediction depth), so the "memorization lens" functions more as narrative framing than a substantiated causal story. The honest discussion in Section 7 partially addresses this, but the framing throughout the paper still implies a stronger connection than is established. This does not invalidate the empirical contribution, but it tempers the claimed conceptual advance.

- **Limited scale and diversity of empirical evaluation.** Experiments are confined to CIFAR-10/ResNet-18 and SVHN/ViT. These are small-scale settings, and the paper does not test on harder benchmarks (CIFAR-100, ImageNet-scale), larger architectures, or non-vision domains where practical unlearning needs are most pressing. The "sets a new state-of-the-art for unlearning metrics" claim in the abstract is stronger than what two small benchmarks support. Additionally, SVHN results are only presented for the non-IID forget set in the main text, with IID results relegated to the appendix. Several conclusions (e.g., tailoring to S being "more important" for non-IID) are drawn from limited experimental conditions.

- **Missing SCRUB baseline and SSD as a localized comparison.** SCRUB (Kurmanji et al., 2024) is described in Section 3 as a prominent unlearning method building on NegGrad+, but is not included in experimental comparisons. Similarly, SSD (Foster et al., 2024) is described as a localized unlearning method but only appears as a full-parameter baseline in Table 2, not directly compared as a localized method against DEL. These are among the most directly competing baselines, and their absence weakens the "state-of-the-art" claim.

### Minor

- **Hyperparameter h is introduced but never ablated.** The channel criticality score uses the average of the top-h parameter scores, but the value of h is not reported in the main text and no sensitivity analysis is provided. Given that channel sizes vary across layers and architectures (especially for ViT), understanding h's impact is important for reproducibility and practical guidance.

- **Channel/neuron definition for ViT is underspecified.** Section 5.2 mentions "neuron more broadly, encompassing non-convolutional architectures" without specifying how channels/neurons are defined in the transformer architecture used for SVHN experiments. This is critical for reproducibility and understanding the method's applicability.

- **Full-parameter baselines are not budget-normalized.** The paper emphasizes that DEL modifies only 30% of parameters while achieving better unlearning metrics. However, full-parameter baselines update all weights without analogous constraints (e.g., early stopping tuned specifically to hit oracle forget accuracy). This structural asymmetry means that localized methods' advantage in Δ metrics may partly reflect their inherent tuning flexibility rather than superiority of the localization strategy itself. The paper partially acknowledges this in its discussion of why localized methods avoid "overshooting" the oracle forget accuracy, but the broader comparison framing could be more precise.

- **Marginal improvements on some settings.** For the IID forget set in Table 2, DEL's improvements over SalLoc-RL on Δ_forget (0.97 vs. -3.81) and Δ_MIA (-0.97 vs. 3.80) are clear, but its Δ_test (1.87) is considerably worse than several baselines (NegGrad+: 4.21, Random Label: 4.93). The paper does not discuss scenarios where one might prefer a method that over-forgets but preserves test accuracy more effectively.

## Novel Insights

The most interesting finding is that direct translations of memorization localization hypotheses into unlearning strategies perform poorly — "later layers memorize" (Deepest) yields bad unlearning despite being a well-established memorization claim, and CritMem's iterative approach, while more promising, is both expensive and insufficient. The paper's actual contribution is more pragmatic: channel-level grouping with weighted gradients, implemented in a batched one-shot fashion, works better than parameter-level gradient saliency. This is a solid engineering insight but, somewhat ironically, it demonstrates that the path from memorization theory to practical unlearning improvement is less direct than one might hope.

## Suggestions

- Temper the "memorization lens" framing to accurately reflect that the contribution is primarily an improved localization heuristic informed by design choices from the memorization literature, rather than a validated causal connection between memorization and unlearning.
- Add the missing baselines (SCRUB, SSD as a localized method) to strengthen the empirical comparison.
- Include a sensitivity analysis for h and explicitly describe how channels/neurons are defined for ViT architectures.
- Restrict the "state-of-the-art" claim to the specific setting tested (small-scale vision classification) rather than making a field-wide claim.
