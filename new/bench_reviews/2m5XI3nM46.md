This paper studies localized machine unlearning—modifying only a small subset of parameters to remove the influence of a forget set—and proposes an improved localization strategy inspired by the memorization literature. The authors first investigate localization strategies derived from memorization hypotheses (Deepest layers, CritMem) and find them inadequate, then design a new strategy combining channel-level granularity with weighted-gradient criticality scores, implemented non-iteratively and in batched form. Paired with a simple Reset+Finetune unlearning algorithm, this yields DEL (Deletion by Example Localization), which outperforms prior localized and full-parameter methods on CIFAR-10/ResNet-18 and SVHN/ViT across forget accuracy, MIA efficacy, and test accuracy.

## Strengths

- **Thoughtful investigation connecting memorization and unlearning**: The paper takes a principled approach to investigating whether hypotheses about where memorization occurs (deepest layers vs. scattered channels) translate into effective localization for unlearning. While direct translations largely fail, this systematic investigation itself is a valuable negative finding that frames the ultimate contribution honestly (Section 7: "directly translating memorization hypotheses into localization strategies does not help unlearning").

- **Well-designed ablation studies**: The random-vs-structured masking experiment (Table 3) convincingly demonstrates that localization identifies genuinely critical parameters rather than benefitting from merely modifying a fixed fraction. The granularity/criticality ablations (Tables 1 and 4) systematically isolate the contributions of channel-level granularity and weighted gradients, providing clear takeaway messages for future work.

- **Consistent empirical improvements**: DEL achieves the best results among localized methods across datasets, forget set types, and metrics. On CIFAR-10 Non-IID, Δ_forget = 0.43±1.06 and Δ_MIA = 0.64±1.23 for DEL vs. -2.80±1.45 and 3.30±1.54 for SalLoc-RL (the prior SOTA localized method), which are meaningful improvements with non-overlapping confidence intervals on Δ_forget.

- **Practical advantages over CritMem**: The proposed localization strategy retains CritMem's beneficial properties (channel-level granularity, weighted gradient criterion) while being non-iterative and batched, offering substantial efficiency gains over CritMem. The robustness to parameter budget (Figure 2) is a genuine practical strength—unlike SalLoc, DEL does not catastrophically degrade as the budget shrinks.

- **Honest discussion of limitations**: The paper openly acknowledges that direct memorization-to-localization translation fails and provides nuanced analysis of when forget-set-specific tailoring matters (Table 4: more important for non-IID than IID forget sets).

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **Gap between the memorization narrative and the actual contribution undermines the framing**: The title and introduction promise "improved localized machine unlearning through the lens of memorization," but the best-performing method (DEL) uses channel-level granularity and weighted gradients—design choices that are engineering-heuristic improvements over SalLoc, not principled instantiations of memorization theory. The direct applications of memorization hypotheses (Deepest, CritMem) fail, and as the authors note, CritMem's weighted-gradient criterion is essentially the Fisher-information-like product |θ·g|, which is a standard importance measure in the broader literature rather than a memorization-specific insight. The paper's main conceptual claim—that "insights from (Maini et al., 2023) regarding the granularity and criticality criterion" guided the design—holds, but this is a thin thread for the "through the lens of memorization" framing, and the contribution is better described as an improved saliency-based localization method.

- **Limited experimental scope for broad SOTA claims**: Experiments are conducted on only CIFAR-10/ResNet-18 and SVHN/ViT. While these are standard benchmarks in the localized unlearning literature, claiming DEL "sets a new state-of-the-art for unlearning metrics" (Abstract) across "different datasets, forget sets and metrics" based on two small-scale vision benchmarks is an overclaim. The paper does not address whether findings generalize to larger models, different architectures (e.g., deeper CNNs, different normalization), or more complex tasks.

- **Missing important baseline**: SCRUB (Kurmanji et al., 2024), described in the paper's own related work as building on NegGrad+ via distillation, is a widely-cited strong baseline in unlearning but is not included in the experimental comparison. Given that the paper claims to "outperforms the state-of-the-art localized and full-parameter methods," this omission weakens the claim, especially since SCRUB is a full-parameter method that the localized methods should be compared against.

- **Evaluation limited to one MIA variant and forget accuracy**: Unlearning quality is measured primarily through forget accuracy and a single MIA efficacy metric from Fan et al. (2023). As the field has noted, different MIAs and privacy metrics can yield qualitatively different conclusions about unlearning effectiveness. A stronger evaluation would include additional attack types or distributional metrics (e.g., KL divergence from the oracle, loss distributions) to verify that the improvements are robust to stronger adversarial evaluation.

### Minor

- **Per-method per-budget hyperparameter tuning without transparency**: The paper states that "throughout the paper, we tune hyperparameters separately for each budget and localization strategy" (Section 4). While this is disclosed, it raises questions about practical deployability—each configuration requires a separate search—and about fairness of comparison, since it is unclear whether comparable search effort was applied to all methods.

- **No quantitative efficiency comparison**: The paper motivates localized unlearning partly by efficiency gains (modifying fewer parameters), but provides no wall-clock time, FLOPs, or memory comparisons between DEL and baselines. While the localized nature of DEL suggests lower computational cost, computing forget-set gradients to construct the mask adds overhead that is not quantified.

- **Limited forget set diversity**: All experiments use 10% forget sets. It is unknown whether DEL's advantages hold for smaller (e.g., 1%) or larger (e.g., 20%, 50%) forget sets, or for sequential unlearning requests, which are practically important scenarios.

- **Hyperparameter h (number of top scores per neuron) is underexplored**: The choice to average the top h parameter criticality scores per channel is introduced in Section 5.2 but the effect of h is not studied in the main ablations, leaving its sensitivity unanalyzed.

### Trivial

- The SVHN results are presented primarily through a radar chart (Figure 4) without full tabular results in the main text, making detailed comparison harder.

- Include SCRUB as a baseline to complete the comparison with full-parameter SOTA methods.
- Report wall-clock time for all methods (including localization overhead) to substantiate efficiency claims.

## Novel Insights

The most interesting finding is the disconnect between memorization localization hypotheses and effective unlearning localization: the hypothesis that "later layers memorize" (Baldock et al., 2021) yields poor unlearning, and the direct application of CritMem's algorithm (Maini et al., 2023) is both expensive and ineffective for unlearning budgets. Yet the *design choices* from CritMem—channel-level granularity and weighted-gradient criticality scores—when combined into an efficient non-iterative scheme, do yield state-of-the-art results. This suggests that the value of the memorization literature for unlearning lies not in precise localization of where data is stored, but in providing useful inductive biases about what granularity and importance measures to use when selecting parameters for modification. The finding that Shallowest (modifying the earliest layers) is actually a stronger baseline than Deepest for unlearning (contrary to the "later layers memorize" hypothesis) is also noteworthy and underexplored in prior work.
