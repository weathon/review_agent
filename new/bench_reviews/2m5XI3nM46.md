## Summary

This paper investigates whether hypotheses about where memorization occurs in neural networks can inform improved localized machine unlearning. Through systematic comparison of localization strategies (Deepest, Shallowest, CritMem, SalLoc), the authors identify key design choices—channel-level granularity and weighted-gradient criticality—and combine them into a practical, non-iterative localization strategy. Pairing this with the Reset+Finetune algorithm yields DEL (Deletion by Example Localization), which outperforms SalUn (the prior SOTA localized method) and several full-parameter baselines on CIFAR-10/ResNet-18 and SVHN/ViT across IID and non-IID forget sets.

## Strengths

- **Systematic, well-motivated ablations.** Table 1 carefully decomposes localization into granularity × criticality-criterion axes in a non-iterative setting, providing clean, actionable evidence for why channel-level weighted gradients outperform SalLoc's parameter-level plain gradients. This is methodologically rigorous.
- **Shallowest as a strong overlooked baseline.** Showing that shallow-layer localization handily outperforms the commonly-used deepest-layer baselines (EU-k) is a genuinely useful finding for the community, backed by Figure 2.
- **Budget robustness.** DEL maintains strong performance when the parameter budget is reduced from 30% to 20%, while SalLoc degrades significantly (Figure 3). This is practically important and well-evidenced.
- **Random-mask control (Table 3).** The layer-distribution-matched random mask experiment cleanly demonstrates that method success depends on *which* parameters are selected, not just *how many*—a meaningful sanity check.
- **Honest framing in Section 7.** The paper explicitly acknowledges that "directly translating memorization hypotheses into localization strategies does not help unlearning," providing intellectual honesty and a useful data point in an ongoing cross-community discussion.
- **Dual forget-set evaluation.** Testing both IID and non-IID forget sets on CIFAR-10 reveals the nuanced role of forget-set distribution on localization quality (Table 4), which deepens the contribution beyond a single-setting comparison.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None. The paper makes real contributions and is not fundamentally unsound.

### Major

- **Missing SCRUB baseline.** SCRUB (Kurmanji et al., 2024) is explicitly described in Section 3 as a competitive method that builds on NegGrad+ with distillation, yet it is absent from Table 2 and all benchmark comparisons. Given that the paper claims "new state-of-the-art against both localized and full-parameter methods," omitting a named, described SOTA baseline undermines this central claim. This is the most significant experimental gap.

- **No efficiency measurements despite efficiency being a core motivation.** The paper's entire rationale for localized unlearning centers on efficiency: "we desire…substantially more computationally-efficient" methods (Section 2.1) and parameter budget is framed as a proxy for efficiency. Yet no wall-clock time, FLOPs, or memory usage numbers are reported anywhere—not even a comparison to retraining from scratch. Without this, the central claim that localized unlearning is *efficient* is unverifiable.

- **Limited experimental scope prevents strong generalization claims.** All experiments are on small vision classifiers: CIFAR-10/ResNet-18 and SVHN/ViT. SVHN results appear only in Figure 4 as a radar chart with limited quantitative detail. The paper claims SOTA broadly, but these are settings where even retraining from scratch is inexpensive, which undermines the efficiency motivation. Testing on at least one larger-scale setting (e.g., CIFAR-100 or an ImageNet subset) would meaningfully strengthen the claims.

- **Title/framing tension with conclusion.** The title "through the lens of memorization" implies that memorization insights drive improvements. However, Section 7 explicitly states that "directly translating memorization hypotheses into localization strategies does not help." The actual contribution is a better combination of channel granularity and weighted-gradient criticality—which is inspired by but not equivalent to memorization localization. This framing misaligns with the paper's own findings and may mislead readers.

### Minor

- **SSD categorization ambiguity.** SSD (Foster et al., 2024) is described in Section 3 as identifying parameters "disproportionately important to the forget set"—making it structurally a localized method—yet Table 2 lists it under "Full-parameter Unlearning" without explanation. This categorization skews the narrative about localized vs. full-parameter methods and should be clarified or justified.

- **Top-h hyperparameter not ablated.** The neuron criticality score averages the top-h parameter scores within each channel (Section 5.2), but h's value and sensitivity are never reported or ablated in either the main text or appendix. This is a central design choice in the proposed method.

- **IID forget-set results weaken the memorization-informed localization story.** Table 4 shows that for IID forget sets, train-set-agnostic criteria (weights-only, weighted gradients on full train set) match the forget-set-specific criterion. The paper acknowledges this but frames it as a distribution effect, leaving open the question of whether the method's advantage on non-IID sets is primarily about granularity engineering rather than forget-set-informed localization.

- **Best-budget/best-algorithm selection for each method.** Table 2 reports each localized method at its best identified budget and best-paired algorithm, while full-parameter baselines appear at single instantiations. The paper notes this explicitly but does not provide a clearly matched hyperparameter selection protocol for baselines, creating a mild asymmetry.

### Trivial

- Forget set size is fixed at 10% throughout; there is no variation to test boundary conditions (e.g., 1% or 25% forget set), which is a limitation but not critical given the paper's scope.

## Novel Insights

The most genuinely novel insight in this paper—supported by the ablation in Table 1—is that operating at *channel granularity with weighted-gradient criticality* in a single non-iterative pass strikes a better localization-quality/efficiency tradeoff than either the fine-grained SalLoc (individual parameters, plain gradients) or the expensive CritMem (iterative channel selection). This insight is practically actionable and provides a clean design principle for future localized unlearning work. The secondary insight—that shallowest-layer localization outperforms deepest-layer localization as a data-agnostic baseline—contradicts intuition and prior practices (EU-k), and is a useful corrective for the field.
