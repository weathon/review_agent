## Summary

This paper investigates whether hypotheses from the memorization literature about *where* in a network training data is stored can inform localized machine unlearning. Through a systematic study comparing data-agnostic strategies (Deepest/Shallowest layers) and data-dependent strategies (CritMem, SalLoc), the authors find that direct translations of memorization hypotheses perform poorly for unlearning, but that two design insights from CritMem—channel-level granularity and weighted-gradient criticality scores—can be incorporated into an efficient one-shot localization algorithm. The resulting method, DEL (Deletion by Example Localization), resets identified critical channels and finetunes them on the retain set, achieving strong unlearning performance across datasets, forget-set distributions, and parameter budgets.

## Strengths

- **Systematic investigation of localization strategies (Section 4, Figure 2):** The paper provides a thorough head-to-head comparison of four localization strategies across varying parameter budgets, yielding valuable insights—e.g., Shallowest is a strong baseline outperforming the Deepest/EU-k approach of Goel et al. (2022), and CritMem directly adapted from the memorization literature is insufficient. This investigation is itself a genuine contribution to the field.

- **Clean ablation demonstrating parameter selection matters (Table 3):** The random-vs-standard masking experiment is well-designed—both conditions share the same per-layer parameter distribution, isolating the effect of *which* parameters are selected. The consistent degradation with random masking (e.g., DEL's Δ_MIA worsens from 3.36 to 9.07) convincingly validates that the localization strategy drives performance.

- **DEL meaningfully improves over SalUn with simple design changes (Table 2):** On CIFAR-10 Non-IID, DEL (α=30%) achieves Δ_forget=0.43 and Δ_MIA=0.64 versus SalLoc-RL's Δ_forget=−2.8 and Δ_MIA=3.30, dramatically closer to oracle. These improvements come from two interpretable modifications: channel granularity and weighted gradients (|θ·g| instead of |g|).

- **Robustness across parameter budgets (Figure 2):** DEL maintains near-oracle performance across budgets from ~5% to 100%, while other strategies degrade sharply at small budgets. This is practically important since selecting the optimal budget may be difficult in real settings.

- **Honest and nuanced discussion of the memorization–unlearning connection (Section 7):** The paper explicitly acknowledges that "directly translating memorization hypotheses into localization strategies does not help unlearning" while arguing that specific design insights from the memorization literature did yield improvements. This positions the contribution honestly rather than oversimplifying.

- **Cross-dataset and architecture validation (Table 2, Figure 4):** Results on CIFAR-10/ResNet-18 and SVHN/ViT demonstrate that DEL generalizes beyond a single experimental setting.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Missing SCRUB baseline undermines the breadth of the "state-of-the-art" claim.** SCRUB (Kurmanji et al., 2024) is discussed in Section 3 as a prominent full-parameter method that builds on NegGrad+ via distillation, yet it is absent from all experimental comparisons (Tables 2, 8; Figures 3, 4). The paper's abstract claims DEL "sets a new state-of-the-art for unlearning metrics, against both localized and full-parameter methods." While the paper includes 6 full-parameter baselines (Finetuning, NegGrad+, NegGrad, Random Label, L1-sparse, IU, SSD), SCRUB is widely regarded as a strong baseline that often outperforms its NegGrad+ foundation. Its absence leaves the full-parameter comparison incomplete. This does not invalidate the localized-vs-localized comparison, but it does weaken the broad SOTA claim against full-parameter methods.

- **The always-finetuned classifier layer is an uncontrolled confound for interpreting localization contributions.** Section 4 states the unlearning algorithm "finetunes only the identified critical parameters and the classifier layer using only the retain set." The classifier head is always updated regardless of the localization mask. While this is consistent across all localized methods (making relative comparisons fair), it raises the question of how much absolute performance is driven by classifier retraining versus targeted localization—especially at small budgets (e.g., α=16%) where classifier parameters may constitute a significant fraction of updated parameters. The random-vs-standard masking experiment (Table 3) partially addresses this (both conditions finetune the classifier, yet standard masking outperforms random), confirming that localization matters. However, an ablation explicitly isolating the classifier's contribution would strengthen the interpretation of why DEL works. Without it, the claim that "localized unlearning succeeds due to selecting critical parameters" (Section 6) conflates two factors.

### Minor

- **The memorization lens framing is slightly stronger than the actual contribution warrants.** The title promises "Improved Localized Machine Unlearning Through the Lens of Memorization," and the paper's narrative suggests that memorization insights directly led to the improvements. In reality, direct translations of memorization hypotheses (Deepest, CritMem) failed; the actual contribution is two design choices (channel granularity + weighted gradients) discovered through ablation that were *inspired* by CritMem. The paper acknowledges this in Section 7, but the abstract and title do not reflect this nuance. The real contribution is an improved localization strategy with memorization-informed design choices, not a demonstration that the memorization lens directly enables better unlearning.

- **Dataset/architecture confound in cross-dataset evaluation.** CIFAR-10 uses ResNet-18 and SVHN uses ViT, making it impossible to independently attribute generalization to dataset or architecture factors. While both combinations show DEL performing well, cross-architecture validation on the same dataset (e.g., ViT on CIFAR-10) would strengthen generality claims.

- **Section 4 investigation limited to a single dataset/architecture/forget-set configuration.** The foundational comparison of localization strategies (Figure 2) uses only CIFAR-10/ResNet-18 with a non-IID forget set from two classes. The conclusions about which strategies work best are presented as general findings but validated in only one configuration before the method is proposed. The later experiments (Section 6) provide broader validation of DEL specifically, but not of the initial strategic comparison.

## Novel Insights

The paper reveals an interesting asymmetry in the memorization–unlearning connection: while the *hypotheses* about where memorization occurs (Deepest layers, CritMem's per-example iterative channel reset) do not directly translate into effective unlearning strategies, the *design primitives* drawn from the same literature (channel-level granularity as a "smoothing" mechanism, weighted gradients as a conservative criticality criterion) do yield meaningful improvements. This distinction between "translating hypotheses" and "transferring design insights" is a subtle but important finding that the community should note when drawing connections between interpretability/memorization research and practical unlearning methods.

## Suggestions

- Add SCRUB as a baseline in the full-parameter comparison to fully substantiate the SOTA claim; even if SCRUB outperforms NegGrad+, demonstrating where DEL stands relative to it would be valuable.
- Run a simple ablation: apply DEL's localization + RFT but *without* forcing the classifier layer into the update set. This directly quantifies the classifier's contribution and would resolve the confound concern.
- Add a sentence to the abstract or introduction qualifying the "through the lens of memorization" framing to reflect that the contribution is design insights inspired by memorization research rather than direct hypothesis translation, consistent with the honest discussion in Section 7.
