## Summary
This paper studies localized machine unlearning through a memorization-inspired lens. It compares several localization strategies, proposes a new channel-level weighted-gradient localization rule, and combines it with reset-plus-finetune to form DEL, which empirically outperforms prior localized and full-parameter baselines on the paper’s benchmark protocol across CIFAR-10/ResNet-18 and SVHN/ViT.

## Strengths
- **Interesting and well-motivated problem framing.** The paper does more than introduce another unlearning heuristic: it explicitly asks whether hypotheses from the memorization literature can inform *where* to intervene for localized unlearning. That bridge between memorization and unlearning is conceptually valuable.
- **Systematic empirical investigation of localization choices.** Section 4 compares Deepest, Shallowest, CritMem, and SalLoc rather than only presenting the final method. This is a genuine strength: the paper learns from negative results and shows, for example, that shallow-layer localization is a surprisingly strong baseline while Deepest is poor at small budgets.
- **Good ablations on the core design axes.** Table 1/Section 5 isolate two important choices—channel vs. parameter granularity and gradients vs. weighted gradients—and the paper does provide evidence that the proposed combination is strongest in the studied setup.
- **Strong control experiment showing that parameter identity matters.** Table 3 is one of the paper’s best pieces of evidence: random masks matched to the same layerwise structure perform substantially worse, supporting the claim that localized unlearning succeeds because it selects the *right* parameters, not just because it updates fewer of them.
- **Consistent empirical gains on the adopted benchmark metrics.** On the reported CIFAR-10 and SVHN settings, DEL is competitive to clearly better than SalLoc/SalUn and the listed full-parameter baselines in forget accuracy / MIA efficacy tradeoffs, while remaining reasonably strong on test accuracy.
- **Generally clear writing and candid discussion.** The paper is fairly honest that direct translations of memorization localization do not work well (e.g., Deepest, CritMem), and that the final method is inspired by but not identical to prior memorization localization approaches.

## Weaknesses

###: Fatal
None.

### Major:
- **The paper’s headline claims are stronger than what its evaluation directly establishes.** The paper defines unlearning in Definition 2.1 as matching the retrained model’s output distribution on the forget set, but the actual evaluation uses forget accuracy, one MIA-based score, and utility metrics. The paper itself notes that “rigorous evaluation methods … are very expensive,” yet still claims “state-of-the-art” unlearning in the abstract and conclusion. The experiments do support that DEL is strong on *standard proxy metrics*, but they do not directly validate the stronger notion introduced in Section 2.1. This is a real overclaim, not a fatal flaw, because proxy-based evaluation is common in the area—but the claim should be narrowed accordingly.
- **Efficiency is asserted more than demonstrated.** Efficiency is a central motivation for localized unlearning, and the paper repeatedly argues that DEL is more practical and efficient than alternatives such as CritMem. However, the main text reports no wall-clock time, FLOPs, or end-to-end cost accounting for localization plus unlearning versus full-parameter baselines. Since DEL still requires gradient computation over the forget set and subsequent finetuning, the practical efficiency case is incomplete.
- **Empirical breadth is limited relative to the generality of the conclusions.** The main design analysis is largely built on one setup (CIFAR-10/ResNet-18 with a specific non-IID forget-set construction), with broader support coming from a second dataset/architecture pair. That is enough to make the method interesting and credible, but not enough to fully justify broad conclusions about memorization-informed localized unlearning in general. Claims about the superiority of data-dependent channel-level weighted gradients should be framed as strong evidence in the tested regimes, not as a broadly established principle.

### Minor
- **The localization contribution is somewhat confounded by aggressive per-method tuning.** The paper states: “throughout the paper, we tune hyperparameters separately for each budget and localization strategy,” and Table 2 reports methods at their “best identified parameter budget and best-paired unlearning algorithm.” This is acceptable for benchmarking best achievable performance, but it weakens causal attribution to the localization principle itself. The paper does partly address this with controlled comparisons in Figures 2–3 and Table 1, so this is a limitation rather than a decisive flaw.
- **Some design choices remain under-analyzed, especially the top-\(h\) aggregation in channel scoring.** Section 5.2 defines neuron criticality as the average of the top \(h\) parameter scores, but the main text provides no sensitivity analysis for \(h\). Since this is part of the final method, robustness to that choice would matter.
- **The memorization framing is somewhat stronger than the final evidence warrants.** The paper’s most successful method is inspired by elements of memorization localization, but the paper’s own findings also show that direct memorization-based strategies do not work particularly well. The conclusion is nuanced on this point; the title/abstract are less so.
- **Sequential or repeated unlearning is not studied.** All experiments appear to evaluate one-shot unlearning. Since practical deployment often involves repeated requests, it would be useful to know whether repeated localized resetting/finetuning remains stable.

### Trivial
- **Absolute metrics are sometimes less visible than oracle-relative deltas.** Reporting deltas to oracle is reasonable, but more direct presentation of absolute forget/test/MIA values in the main text would make it easier to assess whether small deltas correspond to meaningful behavioral differences.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Add end-to-end runtime / FLOPs / memory comparisons against SalLoc, full-parameter baselines, and retraining.
- Include a sensitivity study for the hyperparameter \(h\) and perhaps budget \(\alpha\).
- Evaluate additional forget-set sizes and repeated/sequential unlearning requests.
- Broaden experiments to at least one more challenging dataset or larger-scale model.
- Soften wording from “state-of-the-art unlearning” to “state-of-the-art on standard unlearning proxy metrics” unless stronger evaluation is added.

## Novel Insights
The most interesting synthesis is that this paper’s value is less “memorization localization directly solves unlearning” and more “memorization studies provide useful *design primitives* for localization.” The evidence suggests a nuanced middle ground: coarse units (channels/neurons) and weighted-gradient scoring appear to regularize localization better than fine-grained parameter saliency, while naive translations of memorization hypotheses such as “just edit the deepest layers” fail badly. That is a meaningful insight for future localized unlearning work even if the stronger memorization narrative should be toned down.

## Suggestions
- Reframe the main claim more precisely: DEL achieves state-of-the-art performance on the paper’s adopted proxy-based unlearning benchmarks, rather than establishing stronger faithful-unlearning guarantees.
- Add explicit efficiency measurements for localization + unlearning, not just parameter-budget arguments.
- Add a controlled experiment fixing the unlearning algorithm, budget, and tuning protocol across methods to isolate the localization contribution more cleanly.
- Provide an ablation on the top-\(h\) channel aggregation parameter and briefly explain how channels/neurons are grouped in the ViT case.
- If space permits, include one broader stress test: different forget-set sizes or sequential unlearning.
