## Summary
This paper proposes **AugKD**, a logits-based knowledge distillation method for image super-resolution that augments KD with two ideas: auxiliary distillation samples generated by zoom-in/zoom-out transformations, and invertible-augmentation-based label consistency regularization. The empirical results are strong overall: across EDSR, RCAN, and SwinIR, and across multiple SR scales and benchmarks, AugKD consistently improves over scratch, vanilla KD, and several SR/distillation baselines.

## Strengths
- **Addresses a real and practically important problem.** Distillation for SR is genuinely harder than for classification, and the paper clearly identifies a useful target setting: response-based KD that works even when intermediate teacher features are unavailable. This practical motivation is stated explicitly in the introduction: feature-based methods can be impractical because “the architecture of teacher models is often inaccessible,” whereas AugKD is “logits-based and independent of network architectures.”
- **Simple, architecture-agnostic method with broad empirical applicability.** Unlike feature-matching approaches, AugKD only relies on teacher outputs and is evaluated on both CNN and Transformer backbones (EDSR, RCAN, SwinIR), including heterogeneous distillation in Table 4.
- **Strong empirical results relative to the included baselines.** The method improves over scratch and vanilla KD consistently, with especially noticeable gains on Urban100. For example, in Table 2 (EDSR, ×4), AugKD reaches 26.45 dB on Urban100 versus 26.21 for KD and 26.34 for CSD; similar trends hold across scales and on RCAN in Table 3.
- **Good ablation coverage on the main components.** Tables 6 and 7 do show that both auxiliary samples and consistency regularization contribute, and that both zoom-in and zoom-out are useful.
- **Useful extensions beyond the core benchmark.** The paper includes heterogeneous teachers, a real-world SR setting with NIQE, and integration with quantization / FAKD, which strengthens the practical story.

## Weaknesses

###: Fatal

None.

### Major:
- **The paper does not cleanly isolate how much of the gain is truly due to distillation, versus due to a stronger augmentation/regularization recipe for student training.**  
  This is the most important weakness because the paper’s headline claim is specifically about *empowering KD*. In Section 3.3–3.4, AugKD changes training in two coupled ways: it introduces extra transformed samples and adds a consistency-style objective, while also using teacher supervision on those samples. However, the ablations in Tables 6 and 7 only toggle AugKD components within the KD pipeline; they do **not** include a student-only control using the same zoom-in/zoom-out samples and/or invertible augmentations without teacher supervision.  
  As a result, the experiments establish that **AugKD as a training recipe works**, but they do not fully establish that the gains are specifically from better *teacher-to-student transfer* rather than mainly from better SR training augmentations. This weakens the central causal claim, though not the empirical usefulness of the method.
- **Some of the mechanistic claims are stronger than the evidence supports.**  
  The paper repeatedly argues that in SR, teacher knowledge is “shaded by GT” and that auxiliary samples “unshade” teacher knowledge. This is a plausible and interesting hypothesis, but Figure 2 does not directly prove that mechanism; it mainly shows that vanilla KD changes student-teacher similarity only modestly and that AugKD increases it more. That supports the claim that AugKD helps the student mimic the teacher, but not the full causal explanation offered in Section 3.2.  
  This matters because the paper’s conceptual contribution is not only a method but also an explanation of why KD behaves differently in SR. The current evidence is suggestive rather than definitive, so the mechanism should be framed more carefully as a hypothesis supported by experiments, not as an established fact.
- **The “significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art KD methods across a range of SR tasks” claim is somewhat broader than what the experiments strictly justify.**  
  The paper compares against a respectable set of baselines, but not every comparison is uniformly matched across architectures/settings. Some methods are unavailable or omitted in certain settings, and several results are reproduced by the authors. This does not invalidate the comparisons, and the paper is justified in emphasizing AugKD’s universality. However, the broad SOTA claim should be stated a bit more carefully: the experiments support superiority over the **included** baselines and settings, but not necessarily a blanket statement over all KD methods in SR.

### Minor
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity is not analyzed.**  
  Equation (4) introduces at least two key weights, `λ_kd` and `λ_augkd`, but the paper provides no sensitivity study in the main text. Since some gains on standard benchmarks are modest in absolute dB terms, understanding whether the method is robust to these choices would improve confidence.
- **Training overhead is not reported.**  
  AugKD introduces auxiliary-sample supervision and consistency regularization, which implies extra teacher/student forward passes during training. Inference efficiency is unaffected, but training-time cost is relevant for practicality and should be quantified.
- **The label consistency design could be better justified empirically.**  
  Section 3.4’s invertibility argument is sensible for pixel-level tasks, but the paper does not ablate which invertible augmentations matter most. In particular, the motivation for color inversion (“prompts the student models to be more sensitive to essential structural features”) is plausible but somewhat speculative without a per-augmentation analysis.
- **The real-world SR evidence is useful but limited.**  
  Table 5 only reports NIQE, which is a weak single-axis proxy for perceptual quality. This is enough for a supporting experiment, but not enough to make a strong standalone claim about real-world perceptual SR quality.

### Trivial
- **Some formulations could be clarified more carefully.**  
  In Section 3.3, the zoom-out case uses reconstruction against the original LR image, which is mathematically consistent but could be explained more intuitively to avoid confusion about what target is being reconstructed at that auxiliary scale.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Add a **student-only augmentation baseline** using the same zoom-in/zoom-out samples and invertible augmentations but without teacher supervision; this is the single most important missing experiment.
- Add a **hyperparameter sensitivity study** for `λ_kd` and `λ_augkd`.
- Report **training-time overhead** relative to vanilla KD and scratch.
- Provide a **per-augmentation ablation** for flip/rotation/color inversion.
- Expand heterogeneous distillation beyond the single RCAN-student setting shown in Table 4.

## Novel Insights
The most important synthesis is that this paper appears stronger as a **practical SR training/distillation recipe** than as a clean mechanistic paper about *why* KD works in SR. Its empirical case is convincing enough that the method is useful and likely adoptable, but its scientific claim about “unshading” teacher knowledge is under-isolated because the method bundles KD with a more favorable transformed-data training regime. Put differently: the paper likely has a real contribution, but the contribution is best described as a strong architecture-agnostic recipe for SR distillation, not yet as a decisive explanation of KD’s mechanism in pixel-level restoration.

## Suggestions
- Add the missing **augmentation-only, no-teacher** baseline to isolate the KD-specific contribution.
- Reframe the “teacher knowledge is shaded by GT / unshaded by auxiliary samples” narrative as a **hypothesis supported by evidence**, not a fully established mechanism.
- Soften the broadest SOTA claims to “outperforms the included baselines across diverse architectures and settings.”
- Report **training-time cost** and **loss-weight sensitivity**.
- Add a small ablation on individual invertible augmentations, especially color inversion.
