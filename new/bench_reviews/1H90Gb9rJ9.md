## Summary

This paper addresses the problem of lossless compression of neural network (NN) representations of Boolean networks (BNs), arising from NN-based technology mapping. The core contribution is a framework that reformulates the σ-equivalence class of a multilinear polynomial (MP) as a system of linear inequalities (Theorem 3.1), enabling an integer linear program to find compressed NN representations while preserving functional equivalence. The authors also introduce an NPN-classification-based acceleration algorithm that exploits shared structure across subproblems, and a depth-constrained optimization algorithm (optMaintainDepth) using the "leeway" concept. Experimentally, the methods achieve up to 70%/60% reduction in connections/neurons and up to 34.3× speedup in optimization time on realistic digital circuit benchmarks.

---

## Strengths

- **Theorem 3.1 (Eq. 7)**: The result that σ-equivalence classes of MPs are exactly characterized by linear constraints (Aq ≤ b) is a non-trivial theoretical contribution. It transforms an intuitively combinatorial constraint into one amenable to ILP, forming the foundation of the entire approach.

- **NPN acceleration framework (§3.2, Theorems 3.2–3.4)**: The interplay between PN-invariant criteria, inverse NPN transformations, and preservation of MMP optimality is handled carefully and rigorously. The framework yields up to 34.3× speedup (sha3, K=10) and 5.9× over caching (aes, K=11), demonstrated in Figure 6 on industrially relevant circuits.

- **optMaintainDepth algorithm (§3.3, Definition 3.8, Lemma 3.3)**: The leeway-based characterization provides a principled and efficient O(V(V+E)) algorithm for depth-constrained optimization, achieving up to 50% connection and 20% neuron reduction on layer-merged NNs without depth increase—something no prior method achieves.

- **Lemma 3.1**: Cleanly formalizes the relationship between MP sparsity (via weighted ℓ₀ norms) and NN size, providing clear design targets: ‖p‖₀,c^uni for neuron count and ‖p‖₀,c^deg for connection count. Figure 5 confirms the degree criterion consistently outperforms uniform in both metrics.

- **Scalability**: Evaluation on circuits spanning 1,928 to 126,883 logic gates (uart, sha3, ecg, aes) demonstrates applicability to industrially relevant scales.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **ℓ₁ relaxation gap uncharacterized**: Lemma 3.1 establishes that neuron/connection counts are ℓ₀ quantities, but the actual optimization (Corollary 3.1) minimizes the ℓ₁ norm. The paper honestly acknowledges this ("Although the solution to the relaxed problem may differ from the actual minimum, both aim for sparsity," p. 6), yet provides no empirical characterization of how large the gap is in practice. For small circuits (k ≤ 4), the true ℓ₀-minimum can be enumerated exhaustively; comparing ℓ₁-ILP solutions to true optima would validate whether the relaxation is tight in practice, or whether the reported 70%/60% reductions are close to achievable upper bounds. Without this, the quality of compression is not fully characterized.

- **ILP solver unnamed**: The entire quantitative case—compression ratios in Figure 5 and optimization times in Figure 6—depends on an ILP solver that is never identified in the paper. Section 4 mentions only the NPN canonical form algorithm of Zhou et al. (2020). Without knowing the solver name, version, and settings (e.g., optimality gap tolerance), the results are not reproducible and cannot be compared by future work. Since the paper asserts determinism for its results but ILP solver behavior can vary across implementations, this is a genuine reproducibility gap.

### Minor

- **No validation of simulation throughput**: The abstract and introduction motivate the work with the claim that smaller NNs enable "fast and efficient" circuit simulation. However, all experiments measure either compression ratios (neuron/connection counts) or time to *build* the compressed NN representation—not time to *run* it. Actual simulation throughput on hardware is not measured. The connection between fewer parameters and faster simulation depends on hardware architecture, sparsity exploitation, and memory layout, none of which are evaluated. This makes the application framing partially unsupported. The core algorithmic contributions are valid, but the "high-throughput circuit simulation" claim in the abstract is overstated.

- **NPN sharing condition coverage unreported**: Theorem 3.4 requires that the permuted phase assignments π^(1)(φ^(1)) = π^(2)(φ^(2)) match across class members (condition (ii) in Figure 4). The fraction of subproblems satisfying this condition—and thus actually benefitting from shared solutions—is never reported. This analysis would help explain why the speedup varies considerably across circuits and K values.

### Trivial

- The benchmark circuits are presented without clear justification for selection; reporting mean reduction across all circuits alongside peak figures would give a more complete picture than citing only best-case values.

## Novel Insights

The key insight bridging the theoretical and algorithmic contributions is that σ-threshold equivalence—an inherently discrete, combinatorial relation defined over the Boolean hypercube—can be fully characterized by a set of linear inequalities on the real-valued MP coefficients. This collapses what appears to be an intractable search over functional equivalents into a convex feasible set, amenable to ILP with ℓ₁ sparsity promotion. The NPN acceleration then adds a second layer of structural reuse: not just caching solutions per unique function (as in the cached baseline), but propagating solutions *across* structurally related functions via algebraic transformations. The combination of these two ideas—linear characterization of equivalence classes and transformation-based solution transfer—is specific to the NN-of-Boolean-networks setting and does not have direct analogues in standard deep learning compression.
