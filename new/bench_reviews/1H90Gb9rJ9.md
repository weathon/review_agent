## Summary

This paper proposes a deterministic, lossless optimization framework for neural network representations of Boolean networks. It formulates sub-network optimization as an integer linear program that minimizes multilinear polynomial sparsity (correlated with NN size) under functional equivalence constraints, and accelerates the process via NPN classification to exploit shared representations among Boolean function equivalence classes. Experiments on standard circuits demonstrate up to 70% connection reduction and consistent speedups over naive and caching baselines.

## Strengths

- **Theoretically grounded sparsity-size correspondence:** Lemma 3.1 establishes a clean, analyzable mapping between weighted l0-norm of multilinear polynomial vectors and concrete NN architectural costs (neurons ↔ monomials, connections ↔ weighted degrees). This provides a mathematically principled objective for threshold network compression.

- **Rigorous invariance analysis for NPN acceleration:** Theorems 3.2–3.4 and the introduction of the permuted phase assignment τ_{π(φ)} correctly handle the failure of input-negation invariance, extending classical NPN classification to the optimization objective in a way that is theoretically sound. This is a non-trivial contribution that makes the acceleration strategy valid.

- **Depth-constrained optimization with the leeway metric:** Definition 3.8 and Lemma 3.3 correctly characterize non-critical vertices on longest paths, enabling architecture-aware compression decisions that preserve inference latency — a practical necessity for hardware deployment that prior NN-based mapping methods lacked.

- **Clear experimental validation across multiple settings:** Figure 5 reports compression results across four circuits and a range of K values, showing consistent improvements under both unconstrained (`optAll`) and depth-preserving (`optMaintainDepth`) regimes.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Terminology "minimal" conflates l1-relaxed solutions with true l0 minimizers.** Definition 3.3 defines an MMP as an arg min over the l0-norm, but Corollary 3.1 solves the l1-relaxed ILP. The paper acknowledges on page 5 that "the solution to the relaxed problem may differ from the actual minimum," yet consistently refers to the outputs as "minimal" and frames them as MMP representations. The l1 relaxation is a well-known sparsity heuristic that does not guarantee true l0 minimality, especially in structured combinatorial spaces. This means the compression results measure sparsity relative to the naive construction, not optimality relative to the theoretical minimum. The paper should clarify that it finds an approximate or relaxed optimum, or provide empirical bounds on the l1–l0 gap on tractable instances.

### Minor

- **Baselines are limited to the uncompressed MP-to-NN construction and simple caching.** The baselines in Section 4 (Naive, Cached, and the Gavier et al. 2023 construction) are reasonable for establishing that the proposed method improves on the prior NN-based mapping pipeline. However, the "34.3× speedup" is relative to a Naive baseline that does not employ any optimization beyond independent subproblem solving. For a method claiming "practical relevance to applications such as high-throughput circuit simulation" (Abstract), the speedup magnitude appears more impressive precisely because the baselines are algorithmically simple. A comparison against a more competitive baseline (e.g., a well-tuned memoization strategy that captures shared substructures beyond exact function identity) would better contextualize the acceleration claim.

- **No wall-clock runtime decomposition between canonicalization, ILP solving, and transformation application.** Section 4.2 reports total optimization time but does not break down how much of the computation is spent on NPN canonical form computation (which involves evaluating up to 2^{k+1}k! symmetry operations), ILP solving via `ttToMP`/`mpToMMP`, and inverse transformation. Without this breakdown, it is difficult to assess how much of the observed speedup comes from the NPN classification logic versus the ILP solver itself, or whether canonicalization overhead diminishes the net gain at higher K values.

### Trivial

- **The paper's experimental focus extends to K = 10–11**, while the main results in Figure 5 do show the full range from K = 2 to 10. The higher K values are useful for demonstrating scaling behavior, and the method's applicability to these regimes is valid. This is a minor observation rather than a flaw.

## Novel Insights

The paper's most distinctive contribution is the integration of classical Boolean function classification (NPN equivalence) with integer linear programming for neural network compression. The realization that PN-invariance criteria allow the optimization objective to commute with certain symmetries — and the precise identification of why input-negation invariance fails and how the permuted phase assignment τ_{π(φ)} restores it — provides a principled bridge between symbolic Boolean analysis and convex optimization that could generalize to other structured network compression problems.
