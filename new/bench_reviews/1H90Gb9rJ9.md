## Summary

This paper presents a deterministic algorithm for losslessly optimizing neural network (NN) representations of Boolean networks (BNs) under Heaviside threshold activations. The core idea is to reformulate each local two-layer sub-NN optimization as an integer linear program (ILP) by identifying minimal multilinear polynomial (MMP) representations within σ-equivalence classes—polynomials that, while numerically different, produce identical threshold outputs. To scale this to large circuits, the authors propose an NPN-classification-based acceleration algorithm that reuses solutions across sub-problems sharing the same canonical form. An architecture-aware selection algorithm (`optMaintainDepth`) extends this to depth-constrained layer-merged NNs. Experiments on four digital circuit benchmarks report up to 70%/60% reduction in connections/neurons relative to the prior SOTA mapping method, and up to 34.3×/5.9× speedup in optimization time.

---

## Strengths

- **Clean theoretical framework with provable guarantees.** The formalization of σ-equivalence classes as a polyhedron (Theorem 3.1) enabling an exact ILP characterization (Corollary 3.1) is novel and principled. The connection between MMP sparsity and sub-NN size (Lemma 3.1) is clearly established.

- **Lossless (functional-equivalence-preserving) compression.** Unlike the dominant NN compression literature (pruning, quantization), the method provably preserves the function computed by the network. This is important for safety-critical applications—a genuine differentiating property acknowledged and exploited in the paper.

- **Theoretically justified NPN-based acceleration.** Theorems 3.2–3.4 rigorously justify transferring MMP solutions across NPN-equivalent sub-problems under PN-invariant criteria. The resulting 34.3×/5.9× speedups over Naive/Cached baselines are substantial and consistent across benchmarks.

- **Architecture-aware depth-constrained optimization.** The leeway-based vertex selection algorithm (Definition 3.8, Lemma 3.3) cleanly handles the practical constraint of maintaining network depth in layer-merged architectures, and the `optMaintainDepth` algorithm is well-motivated and analyzed.

- **Experiments cover multiple benchmarks and settings.** Results span four circuits of varying scale (uart 1,928 to aes 126,883 gates), two cost criteria, two architecture types, and a range of K values—providing reasonably comprehensive coverage for the paper's scope.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
*None identified.*

### Major

- **The ℓ₁ relaxation gap is unanalyzed.** The paper's core technical claim is to "optimize" NN representations, yet the ILP in Corollary 3.1 minimizes an ℓ₁-norm as a surrogate for the ℓ₀ objective. The paper acknowledges that "the solution to the relaxed problem may differ from the actual minimum" (§3.1) but never quantifies this gap empirically or theoretically—even for small k where exhaustive search is feasible (e.g., k ≤ 4 or 5). The headline reductions of "up to 70%/60%" are measured relative to the unoptimized SOTA baseline, not relative to any lower bound or true optimum. Without this analysis, it is unclear how much compression is left on the table due to the relaxation, and the "optimization" framing is only partially justified.

- **No ILP solver details or scaling analysis.** The paper relies on an ILP solver as its central computational engine but provides no information: which solver? what parameters? how does solve time scale with k? The ILP has up to 2^K variables (K up to 11 in experiments → 2048 binary variables), and no timing breakdown distinguishing ILP solve time from NPN canonical form computation is reported. For a paper that motivates practical use in "industrial settings," this omission makes it impossible to assess computational viability at scale.

### Minor

- **Local sub-NN optimization is not proven to be globally optimal for the layer-merged architecture.** In the layer-merged NN, inter-depth composition yields W' = W₁^(d+1)W₂^(d) (Eq. 5), so the actual connection matrix of the merged network depends on a product of weight matrices across consecutive depths. The paper measures connections/neurons in the final merged architecture (Figure 5b), but the optimization is performed locally per sub-NN. No analysis is provided showing that locally minimizing each sub-NN's MMP leads to globally optimal or near-optimal merged connection counts. For the unmerged NN, the link between local and global is direct; for the layer-merged NN, it is not established. This is a real but moderate concern—the empirically observed reductions in Figure 5b are genuine, but the theoretical justification for why local MMP minimization is a good proxy for the merged network is missing.

- **Depth increase from `optAll` is not quantified or discussed.** From Remark 2.1 and §3.3, applying `optAll` to all non-PO vertices adds a threshold to their output, increasing the depth of the resulting unmerged NN. Figure 5a shows depth in gold bars, and the paper acknowledges the trade-off implicitly by offering `optMaintainDepth` as an alternative. However, the depth increase from `optAll` is never quantified or discussed in the main text, which matters for latency-sensitive applications.

- **Limited benchmark diversity in main results.** Only four circuits are reported in the main body (uart, sha3, ecg, aes). The paper mentions additional results in Appendix G.2, but the main evaluation may under-represent the diversity of real-world circuits (varying structure, logic depth, function types). Broader evaluation would strengthen the claims.

### Trivial

- Timing reported over 3 trials with no formal statistical test; only mean/std shown. Acceptable for a deterministic mapping (as the paper notes), but the efficiency evidence is relatively thin.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **End-to-end simulation latency measurement.** The paper's motivation is faster NN simulation on modern matrix-multiplication hardware. A direct measurement of inference throughput or wall-clock simulation time for the optimized vs. unoptimized NNs on a GPU/CPU would substantiate the practical claim rather than relying on proxy counts.

- **Comparison against logic synthesis preprocessing (e.g., ABC resyn2).** Standard Boolean network optimization tools could potentially achieve some of the same structural reductions before NN mapping. Positioning the proposed method against or alongside such tools would clarify when NN-specific optimization adds value over classical logic minimization.

- **Empirical analysis of NPN class distribution.** Reporting the ratio of unique NPN classes to unique Boolean functions across the benchmarks would help readers understand when the NPN acceleration is most valuable (many functions per class) vs. marginal (all functions in distinct classes).

- **Ablation on ℓ₁ vs. ℓ₀ quality for small k.** Even for k ≤ 4, an exhaustive comparison of ℓ₁ solutions to true ℓ₀-optimal MMPs would bound the relaxation gap concretely.

## Novel Insights

The paper's most genuinely novel observation is that σ-equivalence classes (functions producing identical threshold outputs over the Boolean hypercube) can be characterized exactly as polyhedral sets via Sierpiński matrix structure, enabling MMP computation as a standard ILP rather than an exhaustive combinatorial search. The secondary insight—that NPN invariance of the optimization criterion under permutation and output negation (PN-invariance), combined with pre-applying the phase assignment to the canonical form before solving, enables correct transfer of MMP solutions across NPN classes—is a clean and technically substantive result. Together, these two ideas form a principled, modular framework for lossless NN compression in a setting (exact Boolean function representation with threshold activations) where prior compression methods simply do not apply.
