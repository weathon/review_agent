## Summary

This paper addresses the problem of optimizing neural network (NN) representations of Boolean networks (BNs) while preserving functional equivalence (lossless optimization). The authors formalize the problem of compressing two-layer sub-NNs by finding Minimal Multilinear Polynomials (MMPs) under Heaviside threshold σ-equivalence, cast it as a constrained integer linear program (ILP), and propose an NPN-classification-based acceleration strategy that exploits shared representations among subproblems. They further develop architecture-aware optimization algorithms for both depth-unconstrained and depth-constrained settings, demonstrating up to 70% connection reduction, 60% neuron reduction, and 34.3× speedup over naive baselines on four benchmark circuits.

## Strengths

- **Principled theoretical framework**: The paper provides rigorous definitions and proofs (Theorems 3.1–3.4, Lemmas 3.1–3.3) establishing that lossless NN compression can be formulated as constrained optimization over σ-equivalence classes, and that NPN transformations preserve optimality under PN-invariant criteria. The connection between multilinear polynomial sparsity and NN subnetwork size (Lemma 3.1) is clean and insightful.

- **Practical algorithmic contributions with strong empirical results**: The NPN-based objective-aware algorithm yields consistent and substantial speedups (up to 34.3× over naive, 5.9× over caching). The reported structural reductions (up to 70% connections, 60% neurons) relative to the SOTA NN-based technology mapping of Gavier et al. (2023) are significant and demonstrated across four circuits of varying sizes.

- **Well-motivated architecture-aware design**: The distinction between `optAll` and `optMaintainDepth` settings is motivated by real-world latency constraints, and the leeway-based selection criterion (Definition 3.8, Lemma 3.3) is a clean graph-theoretic contribution for respecting depth constraints while still enabling optimization.

- **Clear problem decomposition**: The paper carefully decomposes the overall problem into (a) sub-NN optimization via MMP (§3.1), (b) cross-subproblem acceleration via NPN classification (§3.2), and (c) architecture-aware integration (§3.3), making the approach modular and understandable.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **Gap between ℓ₁ objective and ℓ₀-true cost is uncharacterized.** The actual neuron/connection counts correspond to weighted ℓ₀ norms (‖p‖_{0,c^{uni}} and ‖p‖_{0,c^{deg}}, Lemma 3.1), but the optimization minimizes the ℓ₁ surrogate ‖c⊙q‖₁ (Corollary 3.1). While the paper acknowledges this relaxation ("both aim for sparsity"), no analysis or empirical characterization is provided for how often ℓ₁-optimal solutions match ℓ₀-optimal ones, or how large the gap can be. Since the headline results ("up to 70% and 60% reduction") are in terms of ℓ₀ metrics while the optimization targets ℓ₁, the connection between what is optimized and what is reported is insufficiently justified. An empirical evaluation of the ℓ₀/ℓ₁ gap—even on a subset of subproblems—would significantly strengthen the claims.

- **Compositional equivalence argument is implicit, not formalized.** The paper's central claim is "lossless optimization" of the composed NN representation. The σ-equivalence (Definition 3.2) guarantees local correctness per gate: (σ∘p')(x) = (σ∘p)(x) for all x ∈ 𝔹^k. The compositional argument—that replacing each local MP by an MMP in the same σ-class preserves the global Boolean function—relies on the observation that each sub-NN receives only Boolean inputs and produces the correct Boolean output, and the BN computation is compositional over these Boolean values. This argument appears sound, but it is never formalized as a theorem despite being central to the paper's main claim. Moreover, the architectural change from ζ(x) = W₂σ(W₁x + b₁) to ζ'(x) = σ(W₂σ(W₁x + b₁)) for MMP vertices introduces additional non-linearities in the merged architecture, and the correctness of the composition after this change warrants explicit statement and proof. The absence of this formalization, or at least a clear proposition, is a notable gap for a paper whose central claim is "lossless."

### Minor

- **Missing functional equivalence validation.** Given that the method claims lossless optimization, an empirical verification of functional equivalence (e.g., exhaustive checking on small circuits, or SAT-based combinational equivalence checking on larger ones) would provide essential experimental confirmation that the optimization preserves correctness. While the ILP constraints are designed to ensure σ-equivalence per gate, implementation bugs or solver edge cases could violate equivalence—direct experimental verification would close this gap.

- **ILP solver and per-instance details are sparse.** The paper does not identify which ILP solver was used, whether solutions are provably optimal, nor report per-gate solve times or failure/time-out rates as a function of k. Some experiments are omitted due to "compute constraints" (e.g., Naive on uart K=11 and aes K=11). These details matter for assessing whether the approach is practical at scale and whether the "lossless" guarantee is robustly achieved across all subproblems.

- **Only four benchmark circuits are evaluated.** While they range from ~2K to ~127K logic gates, a broader evaluation on standard logic synthesis benchmarks (e.g., EPFL, IWLS) would strengthen confidence in the generality of the results.

- **Complete NPN acceleration algorithm deferred to appendix.** The core algorithmic details of the NPN-based acceleration (including the handling of the permuted phase assignment and the formal correctness argument) are only sketched in §3.2, with the full algorithm relegated to Appendix D.4. For a methods paper, having the key algorithmic pseudocode and its correctness argument in the main body would improve accessibility.

### Trivial

- The paper's conclusion states the results "demonstrate" lossless optimization as established fact, but given the implicit compositional argument (see Major weakness above), this could be more carefully phrased.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Measurement of actual NN inference/simulation time before and after optimization, to confirm that the structural (neuron/connection) reductions translate to wall-clock speedups on relevant hardware.

- Comparison with standard Boolean logic optimization tools (e.g., ABC resyn) applied before technology mapping, to contextualize the overall pipeline's effectiveness relative to conventional logic synthesis.

- A characterization of the ℓ₀/ℓ₁ gap: how often does the ℓ₁ relaxation recover the ℓ₀-optimal solution, and what is the worst-case approximation ratio?
