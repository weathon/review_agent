## Summary
This paper proposes methods for losslessly optimizing neural network (NN) representations of Boolean networks (BNs). The key ideas are: (1) reformulating the optimization of two-layer Heaviside-threshold sub-NNs as finding minimal multilinear polynomials (MMPs) under σ-equivalence constraints (Corollary 3.1), solved via integer linear programming with ℓ₁ relaxation; (2) exploiting NPN (Negation-Permutation-Negation) classification of Boolean functions to accelerate optimization by sharing solutions across equivalence classes; and (3) architecture-aware algorithms (optAll, optMaintainDepth) that select which sub-NNs to optimize while controlling network depth. Experiments on digital circuit benchmarks show up to 70% connection reduction, 60% neuron reduction, and up to 34.3× speedup over naive baselines.

## Strengths
- **Elegant formalization linking MP sparsity to NN size.** Lemma 3.1 establishes a clean, direct relationship between the number/degree of monomials in a multilinear polynomial and the neurons/connections in the corresponding threshold NN. This is a useful observation that connects algebraic structure to architectural cost, enabling the ILP-based optimization.

- **Novel characterization of σ-equivalence classes via linear constraints.** Theorem 3.1 provides a polyhedral (linear inequality) description of the σ-equivalence class of multilinear polynomials, which makes the MMP search problem tractable as an ILP. This is the paper's strongest theoretical contribution—it transforms what would be a combinatorial search into a structured optimization problem.

- **Principled exploitation of NPN symmetry with PN-invariant criteria.** Theorems 3.2–3.4 establish that MMP solutions can be transferred across NPN-equivalent Boolean functions when the cost criterion is PN-invariant, which the uniform and degree criteria are (Lemma 3.2). This yields measurable speedups (5.9× over caching) and is a clean integration of classical logic-synthesis ideas with the MMP optimization framework.

- **Meaningful empirical compression on realistic benchmarks.** The 50–70% connection and 20–60% neuron reductions on circuits like sha3, ecg, and aes are substantial and demonstrate that the prior NN mapping (Gavier et al., 2023) leaves significant redundancy. The consistent improvement across both the uniform and degree criteria adds credibility.

## Weaknesses

### Major:

- **The global "lossless" claim lacks a compositional proof.** The paper's central promise is lossless optimization—preserving functional equivalence of the overall BN→NN mapping. The σ-equivalence machinery (Theorem 3.1, Corollary 3.1) is established *locally* at the level of individual multilinear polynomials: it guarantees that σ∘p' = σ∘p = f for each sub-NN on Boolean inputs. However, the paper does not prove that composing these locally-optimized sub-NNs preserves the global BN function. The gap matters because: (a) In the original mapping, intermediate signals in the composed NN carry *integer* values (the MP polynomial outputs), not just Booleans. Replacing an MP p with an MMP p' and adding a σ threshold at its output changes an integer-valued signal to a Boolean-valued one. Whether downstream computation depends on the specific integer values (not just their thresholded Boolean) is never formally analyzed. (b) For `optAll` on the unmerged NN, an informal argument that all intermediate values become Boolean (since every sub-NN now has σ at its output) and each sub-NN correctly computes its BF from Boolean inputs could work—but this argument is not made in the paper. (c) For `optMaintainDepth` on the layer-merged NN, some intermediate values remain integer while others become Boolean, and the paper addresses only the *depth* constraint (via the leeway definition) without showing functional equivalence is preserved under such mixed representations. This is a significant gap in the proof of the paper's central claim.

- **Conflation of ℓ₀-minimality ("MMP") with the ℓ₁-relaxed ILP solution.** Definition 3.3 defines an MMP as an ℓ₀-minimizer over the σ-equivalence class—a combinatorial optimization problem. Corollary 3.1 then formulates an ℓ₁-regularized ILP and the paper acknowledges its solutions "may differ from the actual minimum." However, the term "MMP" and "minimal" are used throughout (including the abstract's "deterministic algorithm to optimize") to refer to the ℓ₁ solutions. This is not merely a naming issue: it means the paper presents heuristic solutions without characterizing their suboptimality. No empirical comparison to true ℓ₀ minima (feasible via enumeration for small k) or theoretical approximation bounds are provided. The experiments report compression ratios against an overparameterized baseline, not against the true optimum, leaving open how much of the improvement comes from the ℓ₁ heuristic vs. the inherent redundancy of the baseline mapping.

- **Limited evaluation baselines.** Compression results are compared only against Gavier et al. (2023), which is known to produce structurally redundant NN representations. It is unclear how much of the 70% connection reduction is due to the proposed MMP optimization specifically versus the fact that the baseline is already highly suboptimal. A natural comparison is: apply standard logic synthesis optimization (e.g., ABC's `resyn`) to the BN *before* mapping to NN, then compare the resulting NN size against the MMP-optimized version. Such a comparison would reveal whether similar compression could be achieved by optimizing the BN at the Boolean level first. For runtime, the comparison against naive (per-vertex) and cached (per-unique-function) baselines is informative, but without an ablation isolating the contribution of the PN-invariance trick beyond generic NPN canonicalization, it is unclear how much of the speedup comes from the objective-aware component specifically.

### Minor:

- **The leeway-based algorithm for `optMaintainDepth` is described only briefly in §3.3 and the appendix, without pseudocode in the main text.** The key idea—adding predecessor hidden vertices to the DAG—is mentioned but the algorithm for iteratively selecting vertices and augmenting the graph is deferred entirely to Appendix E. Given that this is one of the three main contributions, more detail in the main text would improve clarity.

- **ILP solver and scalability details are sparse.** The paper does not specify which ILP solver is used, its configuration, or per-instance solve times as a function of K. The footnote in Figure 6 mentions compute constraints for some K=11 instances, but without per-instance timing data, it is hard to assess the practical limits of the approach. Standard technology mapping uses K∈{4,6}, where the ILP has 16–64 variables, which is very manageable, but the paper tests up to K=10–11 (1024–2048 variables) where the ILP appears to become expensive.

- **No measurement of actual NN inference/simulation time.** The stated motivation is that suboptimal NNs lead to "slower simulation performance," but no experiment measures wall-clock inference time before and after optimization. Given that reducing connections/neurons does not always linearly translate to wall-clock speedups (due to hardware-specific factors like parallelism and memory access patterns), this would strengthen the practical relevance claim.

### Trivial:
- The terminology "multilinear polynomial" is standard, but the paper uses "MP vector" and "MMP" somewhat interchangeably between the polynomial and its vector representation, which can be confusing on first reading.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Empirical comparison of ℓ₁-relaxed solutions vs. true ℓ₀-minima for small k (by brute-force enumeration over the σ-equivalence class) to quantify the relaxation gap.
- Comparison against BN optimization via standard EDA tools (e.g., ABC) as a preprocessing step prior to NN mapping.
- Wall-clock inference time measurements for the optimized vs. unoptimized NNs on a standard hardware platform.
- Ablation of NPN-class-based speedup into: (a) generic NPN canonicalization + caching, vs. (b) PN-invariance-aware objective reuse.

## Novel Insights
The paper's observation that σ-equivalence classes of multilinear polynomials have polyhedral descriptions (via Sierpiński matrices) is genuinely novel and potentially useful beyond this specific application. It connects the theory of threshold logic to integer linear programming in a way that, to my knowledge, has not been exploited before. The PN-invariance property of certain cost criteria enabling transfer of MMP solutions across NPN classes is also a clean insight—effectively showing that the optimization landscape is symmetric under permutations and output negations, which reduces the search space by up to 2^(k+1)k! per equivalence class.

## Suggestions
1. **Add a formal proof or argument for global functional equivalence.** For the `optAll` unmerged case, state clearly that all intermediate values become Boolean and that each sub-NN correctly computes its BF from Boolean inputs (arguing by induction on depth). For `optMaintainDepth`, identify the precise conditions under which inserting σ at vertices with positive leeway preserves the global function, or acknowledge the limitation rigorously.
2. **Distinguish "MMP" from "ℓ₁-approximate MMP" in terminology.** Either rename the ℓ₁ solutions (e.g., "approximate MMPs" or "ℓ₁-MMPs") or add a clear statement early on that the ILP solves a relaxation and may not produce true minima.
3. **Report per-instance ILP solve times as a function of k and the ILP solver details**, so readers can assess feasibility for their own use cases.
4. **Compare against a BN-optimized-then-mapped baseline** (e.g., run ABC resyn before mapping) to contextualize the compression gains.
