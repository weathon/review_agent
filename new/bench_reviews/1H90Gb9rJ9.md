## Summary

The paper addresses lossless optimization of Heaviside threshold neural network (NN) representations of Boolean networks (BNs), a setting where existing lossless compression methods (for ReLU or tanh) are inapplicable. The key technical contribution is Theorem 3.1, which characterizes σ-equivalence classes of multilinear polynomials via linear constraints using the Sierpiński matrix, enabling formulation of the optimization as an integer linear program (ILP). The paper then introduces NPN-classification-based acceleration for solving the resulting ILPs across subproblems, and architecture-aware algorithms (optAll, optMaintainDepth) for applying optimization in depth-constrained settings. Experiments on four digital circuit benchmarks show up to 70% connection and 60% neuron reduction, and up to 34.3× speedup over naive approaches.

## Strengths

- **Theorem 3.1 is a genuine and elegant theoretical contribution.** Characterizing σ-equivalence classes of multilinear polynomials as polyhedra (linear constraints via the Sierpiński matrix) is non-trivial and enables the entire optimization framework. The derivation is clean and correct.

- **The NPN acceleration is both principled and practical.** Theorems 3.2–3.4 establish that MMP solutions transfer correctly across NPN equivalence classes when the criterion is PN-invariant (Lemma 3.2), and Figure 6 demonstrates consistent speedups (up to 34.3× over Naive, 5.9× over Cached) with error bars. This is not heuristic — it is guaranteed to preserve optimality.

- **The architecture-aware framework addresses real deployment concerns.** The leeway-based optMaintainDepth algorithm (Definition 3.8, Lemma 3.3) enables optimization under depth constraints, which is crucial for latency-sensitive applications. - **Strong empirical compression results.** On the unconstrained optAll setting (Figure 5a), the method achieves up to 70% connection and 60% neuron reduction relative to prior SOTA (Gavier et al., 2023), providing convincing evidence that the approach works in practice.
