## Summary
This paper studies lossless optimization of neural-network representations synthesized from Boolean networks. The core contributions are: (i) a formulation of subnetwork compression via optimization over σ-equivalent multilinear polynomial representations, (ii) an NPN-class-based acceleration strategy that reuses work across equivalent local subproblems, and (iii) architecture-aware policies for applying these local optimizations either aggressively or while preserving final network depth.

## Strengths
- **Technically interesting formulation of lossless local compression.** The paper cleanly connects subnetwork size to multilinear polynomial support via Lemma 3.1, then characterizes σ-equivalence classes through linear constraints in Theorem 3.1. This gives a principled way to search for functionally equivalent but structurally smaller two-layer threshold subnetworks.
- **The NPN-based acceleration idea is both nontrivial and practically useful.** Section 3.2 is one of the strongest parts of the paper: theorems 3.2–3.4 justify when optimization results can be transferred within NPN classes, and the experiments in Figure 6 show substantial and consistent speedups over naive and cached baselines.
- **Good architectural awareness beyond a purely local method.** The distinction between unmerged and layer-merged NNs is meaningful, and the leeway-based `optMaintainDepth` strategy is a sensible way to preserve depth while still compressing a subset of subnetworks.
- **Empirical reductions are substantial within the stated pipeline.** Figure 5 shows large reductions in neurons/connections relative to the prior mapping pipeline, including up to roughly 70% connection reduction and 60% neuron reduction for unmerged networks, with nontrivial gains even under depth constraints.
- **The paper is generally clear about the construction pipeline and what is being optimized.** The mapping sequence from BN to LUT/MP/MMP/NN is easy to follow, and the figures help explain the subproblem decomposition.

## Weaknesses
###: Fatal
- **The paper overstates “minimal” optimization relative to the actual solved objective.** This is the most serious issue. Definition 3.3 defines an MMP as a minimizer of a cost over the σ-equivalence class, and Lemma 3.1 ties the relevant costs for neuron and connection counts to weighted \(\ell_0\)-type objectives. But in §3.1 the paper explicitly says these are NP-hard and then replaces them with a weighted \(\ell_1\) objective: “*Although the solution to the relaxed problem may differ from the actual minimum, both aim for sparsity*.” Despite this, Corollary 3.1 states that “*the set of MMP representations … equals the solution set*” of the resulting ILP with \(\ell_1\) objective. As written, this is too strong: the optimization actually performed is a deterministic, lossless **surrogate sparsification** method, not exact minimization of neuron/connection count. Because this claim is central to the framing, it materially weakens the paper’s main theoretical claim.

### Major:
- **The practical motivation about faster NN simulation is not directly validated.** The abstract and introduction motivate the work in terms of improved simulation efficiency and practical relevance for “high-throughput circuit simulation,” but the experiments only measure structural compression and optimization-time speedup. There is no measurement of inference/runtime, throughput, or memory behavior of the resulting NNs on any target backend. Thus the paper supports “smaller synthesized NN representations” and “faster optimization/compilation,” but not the stronger practical claim that the resulting NN simulations are actually faster in practice.
- **Scalability of the core optimization remains insufficiently characterized.** The local optimization has \(2^k\)-scale representations, and the paper acknowledges NP-hardness of the integer program. Yet it does not provide a direct breakdown of the ILP/solver burden as \(K\) grows, nor a more explicit ceiling on practical applicability. The missing Naive runs at larger settings (“not reported due to compute constraints”) and the absence of solver-runtime decomposition make it hard to judge how far the method can scale beyond the tested regime.
- **The gap between the true sparsity objective and the \(\ell_1\) surrogate is not analyzed empirically.** Since the paper’s compression quality depends on the surrogate matching the desired \(\ell_0\)-style objectives well enough, some small-\(k\) study of the optimality gap would have been very valuable. Without it, the reader cannot tell whether the reported reductions are close to the best attainable lossless compression or merely one reasonable sparse solution.
- **The compression evaluation is somewhat narrow for the strength of the “state-of-the-art” wording.** The main structural reduction claims are demonstrated relative to the prior BN-to-NN mapping pipeline from Gavier et al. (2023), which is the correct baseline for this specific pipeline contribution. But the wording occasionally reads broader than the evidence supports. The paper convincingly shows improvement over the previous BN-to-NN construction, but not a broader claim about being best among all possible exact logic/representation optimization pipelines.

### Minor
- **Runtime analysis of the NPN acceleration could be more diagnostic.** Figure 6 shows convincing speedups, but the paper would be stronger with a breakdown into canonicalization cost, `ttToMP`, `mpToMMP`, and inverse-transform overhead. That would clarify when NPN classification wins over caching and why gains vary with \(K\) and benchmark.
- **The architecture-level optimization is not globally optimal, though the wording sometimes comes close.** `optMaintainDepth` is a reasonable depth-preserving selection strategy, but it is still a heuristic architecture-level policy rather than a proof of globally minimal final layer-merged size under a depth constraint.
- **Absolute before/after counts would help interpret practical significance.** The percentage reductions are impressive, but absolute neuron/connection counts would make the impact easier to assess.

### Trivial
- None.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Add an empirical study for small \(k\) comparing the \(\ell_1\) solution against the true \(\ell_0\) optimum.
- Report actual inference/runtime measurements of the synthesized NNs on at least one execution backend.
- Provide a runtime decomposition for the NPN acceleration pipeline.
- Include absolute network sizes before/after optimization in addition to percentages.
- Clarify solver details and stopping criteria for the integer programs.

- **“No experimental verification of functional equivalence.”** Removed because the paper’s construction is analytic rather than empirical, and its main guarantees are mathematical. The paper defines functional equivalence formally and builds the method around σ-equivalence constraints; this is not a setting where random input testing is required to substantiate the core claim.
- **Criticism that the paper should compare against unspecified missing related works.** Removed per instruction not to speculate about absent literature.
- **Claim that the leeway idea lacks novelty because critical-path analysis is standard.** Weakened/removed as a novelty objection. While the intuition is familiar, the use here is appropriate and concretely integrated into the BN-to-NN depth-preserving selection problem.
- **Pure reproducibility complaints about code release / omitted implementation minutiae.** Removed per instruction.
- **Broad criticism that the baseline is “unfair” because it favors the authors.** Not applicable here; the main baseline is indeed the prior BN-to-NN pipeline the paper extends, which is a fair comparison for the claimed contribution.

## Novel Insights
The paper is strongest when read not as a general “NN compression” paper, but as a **compiler optimization paper for a very specific symbolic-to-neural representation pipeline**. Under that interpretation, the most valuable contribution is the combination of (i) exact preservation of Boolean functionality through σ-equivalence constraints and (ii) cross-subproblem amortization through NPN classes. The main weakness is that the paper mixes this narrower, solid contribution with broader language about “minimal” representations and practical simulation gains that are not actually established. Narrowing the claims would make the paper substantially more convincing.

## Suggestions
- **Fix the central claim language.** Replace “minimal” / “optimizing neuron and connection count” with wording that accurately reflects what is solved: deterministic lossless optimization using a weighted \(\ell_1\) surrogate over the σ-equivalence class.
- **Repair Corollary 3.1 or its interpretation.** As written, it overclaims equivalence to MMPs under the original size objectives. Either redefine the optimized object around the \(\ell_1\) criterion or clearly separate exact MMPs from the surrogate objective actually solved.
- **Add a small-\(k\) exact study.** For small \(k\), enumerate or exactly solve the true \(\ell_0\) objective and compare with the proposed surrogate. This would greatly strengthen the credibility of the compression claims.
- **Add backend runtime measurements.** Even one CPU/GPU experiment showing whether the smaller networks are actually faster would materially improve the practical case.
- **Report solver/runtime details more clearly.** Include ILP solver settings, typical solve times by \(k\), and a breakdown of where the NPN speedups arise.
- **Tone down the broad SOTA wording.** The paper clearly improves the previous BN-to-NN mapping pipeline; claim that precisely.
