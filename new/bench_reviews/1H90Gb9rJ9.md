## Summary

This paper presents a method for lossless optimization of neural network (NN) representations of Boolean networks (BNs). The core idea is to find minimal multilinear polynomial (MMP) representations of the Boolean functions in a BN by solving an integer linear program (ILP) constrained to the σ-equivalence class of the original multilinear polynomial. The paper proves that σ-equivalence classes can be exactly characterized by linear constraints (Theorem 3.1), enabling the ILP formulation. A second contribution is an NPN-classification-based acceleration scheme that exploits shared structure among subproblems, with formal guarantees that optimality is preserved under PN-invariant criteria. A third contribution is a depth-preserving variant (optMaintainDepth) that selects which sub-NNs to optimize without increasing NN depth.

## Strengths

- **The σ-equivalence characterization via linear constraints (Theorem 3.1) is a clean, non-trivial theoretical result.** The proof that the Sierpiński matrix combined with sign-dependent diagonal constraints exactly captures threshold equivalence for integer vectors enables the entire optimization framework and is rigorously derived.

- **The NPN-based acceleration with formal optimality guarantees (Theorems 3.2–3.4) is a genuine algorithmic contribution.** The insight that the permuted phase assignment must be applied *before* MMP computation while permutation and output negation can be applied *after* (preserving optimality under PN-invariant criteria) is subtle and well-exploited. Theorem 3.4 guarantees that MMP solutions for canonical forms yield MMP solutions for all class members without sacrificing optimality—a non-trivial guarantee.

- **Significant empirical compression over SOTA.** Figure 5a shows optAll reduces connections by up to ~70% and neurons by up to ~60% on the aes benchmark relative to the unmerged NN of Gavier et al. (2023). Even under depth constraints (Figure 5b, optMaintainDepth), connections are reduced by up to ~50% and neurons by up to ~20%. These are large, consistent improvements across all four benchmarks.

- **Principled connection between criteria and NN structure.** Lemma 3.1 precisely maps the uniform criterion to neuron count and the degree criterion to connection count in the two-layer NN, and experiments (Figure 5) consistently confirm that c^deg leads to fewer connections than c^uni, validating the criteria-target alignment.

- **Clean problem decomposition.** The approach—decomposing BN→NN optimization into per-vertex MMP subproblems, accelerating via NPN classes, then reassembling with architecture-aware selection—is well-structured and modular.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **The ℓ₁-to-ℓ₀ relaxation gap is unquantified, limiting interpretability of the compression claims.** The true objective is minimizing neurons/connections, which corresponds to a weighted ℓ₀-norm (Lemma 3.1, Eq. 6). The paper solves an ℓ₁ relaxation instead, acknowledging that "the solution to the relaxed problem may differ from the actual minimum" (§3.1). However, no quantification of this gap is provided—neither on small exhaustive instances (where brute-force ℓ₀ minimization is feasible for k ≤ 6) nor via empirical checks of how often ℓ₁ solutions achieve ℓ₀ sparsity. This matters because the headline compression percentages (70%/60%) are measured against the unoptimized SOTA, not against the true optimum. Without knowing the gap, we cannot tell whether the method is near-optimal or whether substantially more compression is achievable. The abstract's claim of "a deterministic algorithm to optimize" (line 17) further overstates the contribution by not mentioning the relaxation.

- **The speedup baselines are limited, reducing the informativeness of the 34.3× and 5.9× numbers.** The paper acknowledges "Due to lack of prior work, we define two baselines" (§4.2). The Naive baseline re-solves every vertex independently—so weak that the 34.3× speedup is largely uninformative. The Cached baseline (per unique BF) is more meaningful, and the 5.9× speedup over it is a solid result. However, no baseline exploits input/output symmetries (which NPN does) without the PN-invariant criterion integration—such a baseline would more directly test whether the paper's objective-awareness (not just NPN classification) contributes to the speedup. The NPN speedup analysis in Appendix D.4 is also deferred from the main text, making it hard to assess.

### Minor

- **No experimental verification of functional equivalence.** Theorem 3.1 guarantees that any feasible ILP solution is σ-equivalent to the original MP, providing a strong theoretical guarantee. However, for a paper whose central selling point is *lossless* compression, independent experimental verification (e.g., checking that the optimized NN matches the original BN on all 2^n inputs for small circuits, or on large random samples for larger ones) would strengthen confidence in the implementation's correctness. The theoretical guarantee is sound, but ILP solver integer handling and numerical issues could introduce implementation errors.

- **The compression percentages are measured relative to the unoptimized SOTA (Gavier et al., 2023), which produces unminimized NNs by construction.** While this is a fair comparison against the only existing mapping pipeline, the lack of comparison against any alternative compression approach (e.g., running standard logic minimization tools like ABC on the BN before NN mapping) leaves open the question of how much of the improvement comes from the proposed MMP optimization versus the general idea of minimizing the BN before mapping.

### Trivial
None.

## Nice-to-Haves

- End-to-end simulation speed benchmarks comparing wall-clock time for evaluating the optimized NN vs. the original BN, which would directly validate the paper's stated motivation about faster simulation.
- Quantification of the ℓ₁-to-ℓ₀ gap on small exhaustive instances (k ≤ 6) to determine how close the ℓ₁ solutions are to true ℓ₀ optima.
- A before/after NN visualization for a small circuit to make the compression tangible and reveal whether coefficient magnitudes grow substantially.
- A bound or empirical analysis of MMP coefficient sizes, since the ILP minimizes weighted ℓ₁ norm without explicit magnitude constraints.

## Novel Insights

The paper's key insight—that σ-equivalence for threshold NNs can be characterized as a polyhedron via the Sierpiński matrix—is elegant and enables a principled ILP formulation where previously only heuristic compression existed. The observation that NPN classification can be made objective-aware (not just symmetry-exploiting) by requiring PN-invariance of the criterion is a useful design principle that could generalize to other optimization settings where symmetries intersect with objective structure.

## Suggestions

- On small exhaustive instances (k ≤ 5 or 6), enumerate all BFs and compare the ℓ₁-optimal MMP cost to the true ℓ₀-optimal cost (obtainable by brute force). Even a small table showing the distribution of gaps would substantially address the Major weakness.
- In the abstract, replace "a deterministic algorithm to optimize" with more precise language such as "a deterministic algorithm to find minimal multilinear polynomial representations via ℓ₁-relaxed optimization," to accurately reflect the contribution.
- Add a simple functional equivalence check: for one small benchmark, verify that the optimized NN output matches the original BN truth table on all 2^n inputs. This is easy to implement and would add a concrete verification layer.

## Evaluation Axes

- **Originality:** High. The σ-equivalence linear constraint characterization and objective-aware NPN classification are novel contributions with no direct prior work in this specific setting.
- **Importance of research question:** Moderate-to-high. NN-based technology mapping for BNs is a specialized but practically motivated area (circuit simulation, neurosymbolic hardware). The problem is clearly stated and the gap in prior work is real.
- **Claims well supported:** Partially. The theoretical framework is rigorous and the compression results are significant, but the ℓ₁ relaxation gap is unquantified and the speedup baselines are limited. The "lossless" claim is theoretically sound but not empirically verified.
- **Soundness of experiments:** Moderate. The experiments demonstrate clear compression improvements and consistent speedups, but lack key ablations (ℓ₁ vs. ℓ₀ gap, functional equivalence verification, alternative compression baselines).
- **Clarity of writing:** Good. The paper is well-structured with clean definitions, theorem statements, and a logical flow from problem formulation to solution. Some algorithmic details are deferred to appendices.
- **Value to research community:** Moderate-to-high. The theoretical framework opens a new approach to lossless NN compression of BNs, and the NPN acceleration is a reusable algorithmic idea.
