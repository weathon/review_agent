## Summary

CERTAINLYUNCERTAIN introduces a 178K-sample VQA benchmark for evaluating multimodal uncertainty awareness in vision-language models (VLMs), organized around a five-category taxonomy distinguishing epistemic (Knowledge, Complexity, Extraneous) and aleatoric (Temporal, Ambiguity) uncertainty. The benchmark is constructed via two complementary pipelines—image inpainting for the Extraneous category and caption-prompted GPT-4 generation for the other four categories—forming contrastive answerable/unanswerable pairs. The paper also proposes a confidence-weighted accuracy metric, presents evaluations of 15+ VLMs, and demonstrates through SFT/R-tuning/DPO experiments that fine-tuning on this data improves uncertainty awareness while largely maintaining standard VQA performance.

---

## Strengths

- **Large-scale contrastive benchmark with fine-grained taxonomy** (§2.1, Table 1): The 178K-sample dataset is substantially larger than comparable benchmarks (TUBench: ~2K, UNK-VQA: 10K, Table 2), covers five distinct uncertainty sub-categories, and is designed as contrastive pairs—a methodological choice that supports studying the boundary between answerable and unanswerable questions.

- **Creative inpainting pipeline for the Extraneous category** (§2.2, Figure 2): The three-step saliency identification → Grounded-SAM masking → LaMa inpainting pipeline produces visually natural contrastive image pairs without crude artifacts, advancing over the image-masking or object-copying approach in UNK-VQA.

- **Comprehensive fine-tuning experiments with generalization evidence** (§3.1–3.3, Tables 5–6): Three training paradigms (SFT, R-tuning, DPO) are evaluated, and fine-tuning shows positive transfer to external refusal (UNK-VQA: 41.32 → 59.70), standard VQA (VQAv2 maintained at 77.32 with data mixing), and hallucination benchmarks (POPE: 81.30 → 86.31), not merely in-distribution improvements.

- **Confidence-weighted accuracy metric addresses a real gap** (§2.3, Eq. 2, Table 3): Existing metrics measure either correctness (accuracy, F1) or calibration (ECE) but not both simultaneously. The proposed metric rewards confident correct predictions and penalizes confident incorrect ones, and Figure 4 empirically shows it is positively correlated with LAVE_idk accuracy and *more* negatively correlated with ECE than plain accuracy—a useful property for calibration-aware evaluation.

- **Generative AI paradox as a novel empirical finding** (§2.2, Figure 3): The observation that GPT-4V fails to answer questions it itself generated demonstrates an interesting failure mode of current VLMs and independently motivates the benchmark, regardless of the taxonomy choices.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None — the paper's core finding that VLMs are poorly calibrated and poorly handle uncertain questions is robustly supported across 15 models, multiple metrics, and external benchmarks.

### Major

- **Caption-sourced ground truth validity for 62% of the data**: 110K of 178K samples (Knowledge, Complexity, Temporal, Ambiguity categories) are generated by prompting GPT-4 with DOCCI text captions—an IDK label is assigned for questions GPT-4 determines to be unanswerable *from the caption*. However, models under evaluation see the actual image, which contains information not captured in any caption. A question labeled IDK from caption text may be visually answerable. The paper reports a ">93% valid sample rate" for other splits (Appendix Table 7), but the methodology for this estimate—how many items were sampled, who reviewed them, and whether reviewers saw images or captions—is not described in the main text. The extraneous split (38% of data) does have human validation, but the remaining 62% is unverified at the image level. This matters because the paper's central claim that "VLMs perform poorly on CERTAINLYUNCERTAIN" partly rests on these labels being correct. That said, the external transfer results (UNK-VQA, TDIUC, POPE, VizWiz), which use entirely independent labels, do support the core finding robustly.

- **Complexity awareness category creates internal inconsistency in the taxonomy**: The paper defines Complexity awareness as "recognizing when a question is difficult because it involves many parts," yet the accompanying example in Figure 1 shows an *answerable* question ("What objects create a juxtaposition?" → "Airplane and barbed wires"). The paper notes "for simplicity all answers in this figure are normalized to 'I don't know'"—but if the correct response for complexity-type questions is sometimes a real answer, not IDK, then this category is not an *uncertainty type* in the same sense as the others. It is unclear how the benchmark separates complex-but-answerable instances from complex-but-IDK ones, and how models are evaluated when the correct response is a legitimate answer.

### Minor

- **The epistemic/aleatoric labels are applied in a non-standard way**: The paper maps "Temporal" (future events) and "Ambiguity" (multiple interpretations) to aleatoric uncertainty, while in standard Bayesian uncertainty theory aleatoric uncertainty means irreducible inherent randomness (like quantum measurements or coin flips). Temporal uncertainty about future events ("Will the airplane encounter turbulence?") is principally *epistemic* in Bayesian terms—it would resolve with sufficient future data. The paper's definitions (§2.1) are internally consistent and workable, but using the canonical epistemic/aleatoric labels introduces confusion and invites scrutiny that the taxonomy doesn't fully survive. The authors could clarify that their usage is domain-adapted.

- **Confidence-weighted metric validated only on in-pipeline model variants** (Figure 4 caption explicitly states: "data-points in this plot are from evaluation results on extraneous split of different model variants in our experiments"): Correlation with ECE and LAVE accuracy is shown on a small, homogeneous population (fine-tuned Qwen-VL-Chat variants). The metric's generalizability across the full set of architecturally diverse VLMs in Table 4 is not demonstrated.

- **Significant VQAv2 accuracy drops with pure fine-tuning** (Table 6): Training on CERTAINLYUNCERTAIN-only causes VQAv2 to drop from 77.26 to 69.77 (LoRA-SFT) and from 76.94 to 49.95 (Instruct-tuning). The paper emphasizes that data-mixing addresses this, which it does (77.32 with Ours+LLaVA), but readers should be aware that the proposed dataset requires mixing with general instruction-following data to avoid standard VQA degradation. AMBER also drops uniformly across all Qwen-VL-Chat fine-tuning conditions (87.7 → 81.3 or lower), a limitation acknowledged but not analytically diagnosed.

- **No random or trivial baseline in Table 4**: The benchmark is roughly balanced (76.6K IDK / 76.6K non-IDK in train). Without a "always answer IDK" or "always answer" baseline, it is difficult to interpret absolute accuracy figures (models range from 44% to 72%). A trivial baseline would anchor whether models at 44–66% LAVE accuracy are doing anything substantively beyond exhibiting bias.

- **DPO pair construction not documented**: §3.1 describes DPO as using contrastive pairs from CERTAINLYUNCERTAIN, but does not specify how preferred vs. rejected responses are assigned. For answerable questions, the IDK response should be rejected; for unanswerable questions, it should be preferred. Whether this distinction is applied or pairs are constructed uniformly is consequential and unstated.

### Trivial

- The GQA-sourced 53K training samples augment the training split by simply changing answers to IDK, but no verification that the corresponding image perturbations actually render questions unanswerable is reported. Since these are training-only samples, the impact on evaluation is indirect, but the practice is worth noting.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A human study sampling ~200 caption-sourced IDK questions and showing the actual images to annotators (not the captions) would directly validate the key assumption for the 62% of data without human image-level review.
- An ablation comparing models trained solely on caption-sourced vs. image-sourced data and evaluated separately would help identify which data source drives the fine-tuning gains.
- A confusion matrix (answer vs. IDK × predicted answer vs. IDK) per sub-category would reveal whether models fail primarily at refusing unanswerable questions or at correctly answering answerable ones—important for understanding what the fine-tuning actually teaches.
- Broader validation of the confidence-weighted accuracy metric across all 14 open-source VLMs in Table 4 (not just fine-tuned variants) would strengthen the metric's claim to generality.

## Removed Points

- **Harsh Critic §3.1: DPO preferred/rejected framing is intentionally asymmetric**: The paper uses "preferred" = IDK for unanswerable and "preferred" = correct answer for answerable within the contrastive pairs. While documentation is sparse (see Minor weakness above), the DPO setup's logic follows directly from the contrastive pair construction, so the critique overstates the ambiguity. Partially retained as a Minor weakness about documentation only.

- **Harsh Critic: Confidence metric's P(pred) breaks down if model is overconfident in IDK**: The critique argues P(pred) = P("yes") that the model's own answer is correct cannot measure miscalibration because it relies on accurate self-assessment. However, the paper follows prior work (Whitehead et al., 2022) precisely in using this approach, and Figure 4 shows empirically it correlates with ECE as expected. This is a valid theoretical concern for future work but does not invalidate the metric's empirical utility.

- **Harsh Critic: LAVE_idk normalization introduces noise from Mistral-7B evaluator**: The two-stage normalization is inherently imperfect, but this is a general challenge for LLM-as-judge evaluation that is not specific to this paper. The paper follows the LAVE methodology (Mañas et al., 2024) which has demonstrated strong human correlation; calling this out as a standalone weakness is a generic concern that applies to any paper using LLM evaluation.

- **Harsh Critic: Inpainting artifacts may make GQA questions remain answerable**: A legitimate low-priority concern, but GQA samples are used only for training (not testing), and the paper has validated the evaluation split separately. The concern about training data quality applies broadly and is not unique to this construction.

- **Harsh Critic: "Generative AI paradox" framing is interesting but trivially explained by the fact GPT-4 cannot see the image when creating questions for image-based categories**: Partially true but the paper carefully notes this observation for the caption-based GPT-4/GPT-4V pipeline (Figure 3 shows GPT-4V failing to answer its own GPT-4V-generated questions), which is a genuine paradox.

## Novel Insights

The most genuinely novel observation is the "generative AI paradox": GPT-4V, when prompted to both generate uncertain questions *and* answer them, fails at the answering task even though it designed the questions. This points to an asymmetry between the model's generative knowledge (what kinds of things are uncertain) and its deployed inference behavior (confidently hallucinating answers to those same questions). This asymmetry has implications beyond benchmarking—it suggests that knowledge of uncertainty categories is insufficient for calibrated refusal behavior, and that explicit refusal training (as demonstrated in the fine-tuning experiments) is necessary even for frontier models. The dataset also reveals that recent strong open-source VLMs (Qwen2-VL-72B, LLaVA-OV-72B, InternVL2-76B) perform no better than older, smaller LLaVA-1.6 variants on uncertainty awareness despite large leaps on standard benchmarks, suggesting that standard benchmark scaling does not transfer to uncertainty awareness.

## Suggestions

1. Conduct image-level human validation on a random sample of ~500 caption-sourced IDK questions to provide concrete evidence for the validity of the 62% of samples currently relying only on caption-derived labels.
2. Report a trivial baseline (always-IDK, always-answer) in Table 4 to calibrate the absolute performance numbers.
3. Add a brief ablation in Table 5/6 splitting the training data into caption-sourced vs. image-sourced contributions to identify which source drives improvement.
4. Clarify the taxonomy note about epistemic/aleatoric terminology (especially for Temporal and Ambiguity) with a brief remark acknowledging the broader terminological debate, and justify the adopted definitions for the vision-language domain.
5. Document DPO pair construction (preferred/rejected assignment logic) in §3.1.
6. Extend Figure 4 correlation validation to the full heterogeneous VLM population in Table 4 to strengthen the confidence-weighted accuracy metric's generalizability claim.
