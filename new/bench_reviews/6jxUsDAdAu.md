## Summary
This paper investigates benign overfitting in over-parameterized linear models under general covariate shift. It establishes a sharp, non-asymptotic excess risk bound for ridge regression (Theorem 2) that cleanly decomposes the impact of shift in major and minor directions, recovering known in-distribution and under-parameterized OOD bounds as special cases. Additionally, the paper analyzes the regime of large minor-direction shifts, providing a lower bound showing a slow $\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{n})$ rate for ridge regression and contrasting this with a fast $\mathcal{O}(1/n)$ rate for Principal Component Regression (PCR) when the signal is concentrated in major directions.

## Strengths
- **Sharp and General Non-Asymptotic Bound**: Theorem 2 provides a tight excess risk bound for ridge regression under general covariate shift, requiring only finite second moments for the target covariance. The bound is sharp, as it recovers the Tsigler & Bartlett (2023) in-distribution result and the Ge et al. (2024) under-parameterized OOD bound by specializing parameters, demonstrating internal consistency and correct scaling.
- **Clean Multiplicative Decomposition**: The risk bound is expressed in a multiplicative form relative to the in-distribution bias-variance terms, isolating the degradation effects into interpretable matrix norms (e.g., $\frac{\text{tr}[\mathcal{T}]}{k}$ for major direction shift). This framework offers a transparent way to audit OOD performance based on source-target geometry.
- **Insight into Minor Direction Sensitivity**: The analysis reveals that for benign overfitting under shift, only the overall magnitude of the target covariance in minor directions matters, rather than the fine-grained spectral alignment (assuming relatively isotropic source minors). This relaxes structural assumptions compared to prior restrictive settings.

## Weaknesses
### Major
- **Structural Mismatch in Algorithm Comparison**: The comparison between ridge regression and PCR in Section 4 is methodologically asymmetric. The lower bound for ridge regression (Theorem 4) scales with $\|\beta^*\|^2$, indicating a worst-case analysis where the signal may reside in minor directions. In contrast, the upper bound for PCR (Theorem 5) explicitly assumes $\beta_{-k}^* = 0$, meaning the signal is strictly confined to major directions. This "apples-to-oranges" comparison conflates algorithmic truncation advantages with OOD robustness. To substantiate the claim that PCR is superior for handling covariate shift, the analysis should characterize the excess risk of ridge regression under the same signal support restriction ($\beta_{-k}^* = 0$). If ridge regression also achieves a fast rate for sparse signals, the claimed separation is overstated.
### Minor
- **Regime Ambiguity in Lower Bound Instance**: The slow rate of ridge regression is demonstrated using a specific instance where the effective rank $r_k \approx \sqrt{n}$, which is borderline regarding the benign overfitting condition $n < r_k$. While the paper correctly shows that in-distribution risk remains controlled in this instance (via regularized ridge), the regime is fragile. The attribution of the slow rate primarily to covariate shift rather than a breakdown of the high-rank regime could be clearer, as the separation between "OOD degradation" and "regime transition" is tight in this construction.

## Nice-to-Haves
- A phase diagram or visualization showing how the excess risk bound degrades as a function of effective rank $r_k$ and target alignment ratio to clarify the regime boundaries.
- Discussion of ridge regression's bias-variance trade-off under sample-splitting protocols to match the statistical budget used for PCR.

## Removed Points
- **Criticism regarding $n < r_k$ violation**: The harsh critic argues that the lower bound instance violates $n < r_k$, invalidating the OOD narrative. However, Corollary 3 in the paper explicitly demonstrates that in-distribution risk is $\mathcal{O}(1/n)$ for this instance with appropriate regularization, proving that benign overfitting holds. The degradation in OOD is thus genuinely attributable to the shift, not a regime failure.
- **Criticism regarding basis alignment dependence**: The critic claims the bound depends on basis alignment, contradicting the paper's claim about spectral structure independence. This weakness is removed/weakened because the bound depends on $\text{tr}(\Sigma_{S,-k}\Sigma_{T,-k})$, which, under the standard assumption of roughly isotropic minor eigenvalues for the source, reduces to dependence on the overall magnitude (trace) of the target minor covariance, consistent with the paper's claim.

## Novel Insights
The paper's multiplicative decomposition of OOD risk highlights a duality: while source effective rank facilitates implicit regularization in-distribution, OOD robustness in minor directions relies on magnitude matching rather than eigenstructure alignment. This suggests that over-parameterization can "absorb" certain types of shifts (magnitude changes) but remains vulnerable to others (activation of suppressed subspaces), a distinction with implications for understanding robustness in high-dimensional models.

## Suggestions
- **Address the Comparison Asymmetry**: Explicitly analyze or bound the excess risk of ridge regression when $\beta_{-k}^* = 0$ under the large shift instance. If the risk remains high due to variance inflation from the shift, this would close the gap and strengthen the claim of PCR's superiority.
- **Clarify Regime Boundaries**: Add a remark discussing the role of effective rank in the lower bound construction. Clarify whether the slow rate persists for instances with higher $r_k$ (e.g., $r_k \gg n$) to ensure the result is robust to the benign overfitting regime parameters.
