## Summary

DeLLMa proposes a structured decision-making framework for LLMs that decomposes a decision problem into four steps grounded in classical utility theory: state enumeration, state forecasting, utility function elicitation (via pairwise comparisons and Bradley-Terry fitting), and expected utility maximization. Experiments on agriculture planning (USDA crop data) and financial investing (stock returns) show consistent accuracy improvements over zero-shot, self-consistency, and chain-of-thought baselines across GPT-4, Claude 3, and Gemini 1.5.

---

## Strengths

- **Principled formalization.** The framework is cleanly rooted in expected utility theory (Eq. 1–2), making every step auditable and interpretable. The four-step decomposition (state enumeration → forecasting → utility elicitation → maximization) provides a transparent normative justification for the approach.

- **Cross-model consistency.** DeLLMa improves over zero-shot prompting across three distinct LLM families (GPT-4, Claude 3, Gemini 1.5; Figure 2 right), demonstrating that gains are not an artifact of fine-tuning to a specific model.

- **Scaling behavior.** Figure 3 (left) shows clear and linear accuracy gains as a function of both sample size and overlap percentage, confirming that the method scales predictably with inference-time compute—a desirable and non-obvious property.

- **Variance reduction design.** Sharing state samples across all actions (§3.3) is a non-obvious engineering choice that reduces variance in utility estimates and is empirically validated in Figure 3.

- **Human audibility.** The decision-tree visualization (Figure 3 right) concretely demonstrates that DeLLMa's intermediate outputs—forecasted states, assigned utilities, probability weights—can be inspected and corrected, which is a genuine and practical advantage over black-box prompting in high-stakes settings.

- **Identifies a real failure mode of LLM baselines.** The observation that CoT and self-consistency can *underperform random guessing* on larger action sets, because they echo contextual sentiment rather than reason under uncertainty, is well-documented and illuminating.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Evaluation limited to two domains, each with a single time period.** Both Agriculture and Stocks evaluate over exactly one historical outcome window (2022 crop year; December 2023 returns). All 120 instances per domain are generated by enumerating subsets of 7 items drawn from the *same* underlying realization. While the instances are not trivially identical (different subsets yield different optimal items), they are strongly correlated: a method that correctly identifies the one or two top-performing items will accumulate wins across many overlapping subsets. The headline claim that DeLLMa "consistently enhances decision-making accuracy" is therefore difficult to assess with confidence—what is demonstrated is that a structured prompting approach outperforms naive prompting on two specific historical data points. Repeating the Agriculture evaluation over multiple USDA report years (e.g., 2018–2022) and the Stocks evaluation over multiple month-windows would be needed to establish the robustness of the gains.

- **State forecasting ablation weakens the centrality of the forecasting contribution.** Table 2 shows that for GPT-4 and Gemini 1.5, replacing DeLLMa's calibrated state forecasting with a uniform, underspecified, or overspecified distribution causes only a 2–7 percentage point drop. The paper acknowledges this ("DeLLMa appears to be robust against misspecified forecast states") but does not investigate what this implies about *which component* is driving performance. This suggests the utility elicitation step is doing most of the work, yet no ablation isolates its contribution independently from the Bradley-Terry and sampling machinery.

### Minor

- **Independence assumption in state forecasting is unquantified.** Algorithm 1 factorizes the state distribution as a product of independent marginals. In agriculture, climate, yield, and price are strongly correlated (drought suppresses yield and raises price simultaneously); in stocks, sector effects create co-movement. The paper acknowledges this simplification ("which we posit for computational simplicity") but offers no empirical assessment of how much this assumption costs. Even a qualitative discussion of the directions and magnitudes of cross-factor correlations in the evaluated domains would be informative.

- **Comparison with o1 is asymmetric in DeLLMa's favor.** Table 3 compares DeLLMa (full multi-step pipeline, 64 samples per action, Bradley-Terry fitting) against o1-preview with only a zero-shot prompt. The paper acknowledges this is a zero-shot prompt (Figure 22) and frames the result as showing the benefit of "specialized" vs. "general-purpose" inference-time reasoning. This framing is defensible, but the conclusion would be significantly strengthened if o1 were given a decision-theory-informed structured prompt, or at minimum if the paper discussed what gains may remain on the table from better o1 prompting.

- **Human evaluation includes paper authors as annotators.** The preference agreement study (Table 4) uses the paper's own authors alongside 5 external volunteers. While the shuffling procedure reduces positional bias, author familiarity with the system's behavior is a subtle confound. Furthermore, the inter-annotator agreement of 67.0% (±6.3%) is very close to the human-LLM agreement scores (65–68%), suggesting the preference task is highly subjective and the headroom above agreement is not large.

- **DeLLMa-Naive comparison conflates multiple factors.** The ablation between DeLLMa-Naive (fixed total 50 samples, no overlap, no variance reduction) and DeLLMa-Pairs (64 per-action samples, overlap, variance reduction, full Rank2Pairs BT) changes sample size, overlap, and the BT fitting strategy simultaneously. No ablation cleanly isolates the Bradley-Terry contribution from direct per-sample-size scaling.

### Trivial

- The abstract states "up to a 40% increase in accuracy over competing methods"—a best-case bin figure rather than the typical 5–15 pp average improvement visible in the figures. While technically accurate, this framing overstates typical gains and would benefit from also quoting the average improvement.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Multi-period evaluation**: Re-running Agriculture over multiple USDA report years and Stocks over multiple investment windows would be the single highest-ROI addition to the paper, substantially strengthening the generality of the claims.
- **Portfolio/multi-action extension**: The paper defers to future work the setting where actions can be combined into a weighted portfolio. Preliminary results on even a simplified portfolio task would better motivate the practical value of the framework.
- **Oracle upper bound experiment**: An oracle run using the true state distribution and utility function would quantify the gap between DeLLMa's current components and the ideal; this would help prioritize future improvements (forecasting vs. utility elicitation).
- **Deeper analysis of CoT failure mode**: CoT can underperform zero-shot and even random guessing in some settings. A mechanistic analysis of why this happens (echoing contextual sentiment, anchoring on one factor, etc.) would be of independent interest to the research community.

## Removed Points

*These points are flagged to be removed; treat them with caution.*

- **Harsh critic's "structural flaw" label on the 120-instance correlated evaluation.** Kept as a **Major** concern about evaluation generalizability but downgraded from "fatal/structural." The instances are correlated, but they are not trivially reducible to one prediction: the optimal item varies per subset, and the task genuinely changes with action-set size. The concern about limited temporal coverage is real and important, but does not invalidate the results—it limits their scope.

- **Data leakage from parametric knowledge in Stocks.** The paper explicitly states that providing historical prices aims to "prevent possible data leakage" but also to "promote LLMs to use their common-sense knowledge." Since the paper's own design invites use of background knowledge, the critic's framing of this as an uncontrolled confound mischaracterizes the intent. The concern is acknowledged and partially addressed; the remaining ambiguity is inherent to the domain and not unique to DeLLMa.

- **"40% improvement is misleading" as a standalone criticism.** "Up to X%" is standard scientific phrasing. Moved to Trivial.

## Novel Insights

The paper's most insightful empirical finding—that CoT and self-consistency can systematically *underperform random baselines* on larger action sets due to context-sentiment anchoring—is a genuine and underappreciated failure mode. This observation suggests that inference-time reasoning methods designed for deterministic tasks may be actively harmful in probabilistic decision problems, because they amplify the model's tendency to commit to a single narrative rather than marginalize over uncertainty. DeLLMa's decision-theoretic scaffolding sidesteps this failure by forcing explicit enumeration of counterfactual states before committing to a utility ranking.

## Suggestions

1. **Run Agriculture evaluation over multiple USDA report years (e.g., 2018–2022).** This is the most important concrete fix and would substantially substantiate the generality claim.
2. **Ablate Bradley-Terry vs. direct LLM utility scoring** to isolate what the preference-learning step adds beyond the sampling and variance-reduction machinery.
3. **Frame the o1 comparison more carefully** or add a structured-prompt o1 variant; the current framing ("specialized vs. general-purpose") is defensible but invites skepticism.
4. **Provide a qualitative analysis of the independence assumption** using known cross-factor correlations in each domain, with a discussion of the direction of any expected bias.
5. **Replace author annotators with fully external annotators** in the human evaluation, or at minimum report separate agreement statistics for authors vs. volunteers.
