
## Weaknesses

### Fatal
- **The main benchmark does not directly evaluate the full FHAD task as defined.** FHAD is explicitly defined in Section 3 as outputting both abnormality type and bounding box location: “the method needs to output… (1) the semantic flag of abnormality… (2) the location of the abnormality… in the form of a bounding box.” But for the main benchmark, AIGC Human-Aware 1K, the paper states: “Since the location of the abnormalities is ambiguous, we do not annotate the bounding box. Instead, we evaluate the accuracy of the bounding box location by assessing the repair quality.” This is a serious task/evaluation mismatch. Repair quality is not a clean substitute for localization accuracy because it conflates detection correctness, box quality, prompting, and inpainting quality. Since the paper’s headline contribution is *fine-grained* detection with localization, this weakens the central empirical support.

### Major:
- **The repair evaluation does not convincingly validate the claim that repaired humans are more anatomically realistic.** Table 2 relies mainly on CLIP-based similarities, a “Human Concept Score” defined as similarity to the text “an image contains human,” and preservation metrics against the original input. These are weak proxies for the core claim of improved real-world human plausibility. The reported gains are also very small (e.g., CLIP 41.87→41.97, Human CLIP 26.36→26.42). The preservation metrics are useful for showing locality of edits, but they do not by themselves show anatomical correctness.
- **The redundant-body detection component is under-specified relative to its importance.** Section 4.2 gives the intuition, but the actual operational decision rule remains unclear. “Significant semantic difference” is not concretely defined, Eq. (8) is not sufficiently precise, and the role of threshold \(\tau\) is not analyzed. This matters because redundant-part detection is half of the proposed problem and is used first in the pipeline.
- **The paper lacks component-level ablations needed to justify the full pipeline.** HumanCalibrator combines fine-tuned AHD, grounding, inpainting-based redundant detection, filtering, and cyclic repair, but the experiments do not isolate the contribution of these pieces. Missing analyses include at least: effect of the grounding-based filter in Appendix A.2, effect of cyclic repair, sensitivity to \(\tau\), and whether gains come primarily from the absent detector versus the rest of the system.
- **The empirical claim about “current VLMs” is somewhat overstated relative to the evaluation protocol.** The paper does show poor performance for the tested baselines, but this conclusion is based on prompt-based pipelines, and on AIGC Human-Aware 1K the outputs are post-processed by another LLM. This does not invalidate the reported result, but it makes the broader claim about VLM capability less clean than the paper sometimes suggests.
- **Evaluation of the redundant setting is statistically thin.** Appendix Table S3 shows only 158 redundant cases total, which is modest given the category breakdown in Table 1. Some category results are likely based on very small counts, yet the paper provides no uncertainty estimates and no discussion of result stability.

### Minor
- **False discovery rates for some absent categories are nontrivial.** Table 1 shows FDRs around 10–13% for several absent categories (e.g., absent hand 12.19%, absent arm 12.92%). For a repair pipeline, these false alarms matter because they can trigger unnecessary inpainting on otherwise acceptable images.
- **The paper does not clearly analyze performance on normal images despite having such data.** AIGC Human-Aware 1K includes 343 “No Abnormality” examples (Table S3), but the paper does not foreground an image-level false positive analysis on these normal cases, even though this is critical for deployment.
- **Training/evaluation domain gap is present and underexplored.** AHD is trained on COCO images with synthetic masked-out body parts, then evaluated on real AIGC abnormalities. The idea is reasonable and may indeed transfer, but the paper does not analyze how much the synthetic-to-AIGC gap matters.
- **The comparison to HumanRefiner is limited.** The paper compares against a reproduced pose-conditioned repair step for preservation metrics, but not against a full prior method on the full detection task. That weakens claims of superiority over the closest existing approach.
