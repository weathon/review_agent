# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces VideoJudge, a multimodal large language model (MLLM) designed to evaluate video understanding tasks. The authors propose a bootstrapping framework to generate training data and create meta-evaluation benchmarks without human annotations. The framework includes a generator-evaluator pipeline that synthesizes labeled data, enabling the training of MLLM judges capable of generating instance-specific rubrics and assigning ratings. Experiments demonstrate that the 3B and 7B VideoJudge models perform comparably or better than larger models, achieving strong alignment with human judgments across multiple benchmarks.

## Strengths
1. The paper presents a novel bootstrapping framework for training MLLM judges in video understanding, eliminating the need for costly human annotations and providing a scalable approach to evaluation.
2. The authors provide extensive experiments across multiple benchmarks, demonstrating that the VideoJudge models match or exceed the performance of much larger models, with strong alignment with human judgments.
3. The framework’s ability to generate instance-specific rubrics enhances interpretability, and the rubric-driven supervision shows potential for improving evaluation performance.
4. The paper is well-organized, with clear explanations of the methodology, experiments, and results, making it accessible to readers.

## Weaknesses
1. The reliance on a generator-evaluator pipeline with self-generated labels raises concerns about potential biases and the quality of synthesized data, which could impact the generalizability of the model.
2. The framework's performance is limited by the quality of the generated data, which may not always reflect true human preferences or capture nuanced video understanding tasks accurately.
3. The framework’s effectiveness is constrained by the quality of the video descriptions generated by VLMs, as the descriptions serve as semantic context for the training process.

## Questions
1. How does the VideoJudge model handle videos with complex or ambiguous content that may not be easily describable in text?
2. Could the bootstrapping approach be extended to other multimodal tasks beyond video understanding, and if so, what modifications would be necessary?
3. How does the framework account for potential biases introduced by the generator-evaluator pipeline, and are there measures in place to ensure data quality and model robustness?
