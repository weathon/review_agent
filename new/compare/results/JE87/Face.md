# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces a novel method to address identity leakage in face recognition systems, particularly those that employ frequency-domain transformations for privacy preservation. The authors propose an identity-centric attack that performs identity linkage and face regeneration directly from protected templates, without recovering the original pixels. This approach highlights a critical distinction between pixel-level and identity-level privacy, showing that preventing pixel-level reconstruction does not necessarily prevent identity leakage.

## Strengths
1. The paper presents a novel approach to privacy-preserving face recognition by focusing on identity-level privacy rather than pixel-level reconstruction, which is a significant contribution to the field.
2. The authors provide empirical evidence through their experiments, demonstrating the effectiveness of their method in extracting identity information from protected templates.
3. The paper is well-structured and clearly written, with detailed explanations of the methodology and results.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper could benefit from a more detailed comparison with existing methods, particularly those that also claim to address identity leakage in face recognition systems.
2. The experiments are conducted on a limited number of datasets, which may not fully represent the diversity of real-world scenarios.
