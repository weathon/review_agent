# Review

## Summary
This paper studies the problem of characterizing the distributional equivalence class for linear non-Gaussian latent-variable models. The authors introduce a new tool, edge rank constraints, which fills a missing piece in the toolbox for latent-variable causal discovery. They further provide a procedure to traverse the whole equivalence class and develop an algorithm to recover models from data up to such equivalence.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow.

2. The authors propose a new tool, edge rank constraints, which fills a missing piece in the toolbox for latent-variable causal discovery.

3. The authors establish the graphical criterion for when two graphs with arbitrary latent structure and cycles are distributionally equivalent and further provide a procedure to traverse the whole equivalence class.

4. The authors develop an efficient algorithm to recover the equivalence class from data, which is, to our knowledge, the first structural-assumption-free method for latent-variable causal discovery.

## Weaknesses
1. In Proposition 1, the authors claim that when G is acyclic, it suffices to check each single L∈L. However, this result is not obvious to me. Could the authors provide a proof for this claim?

2. The authors introduce a new tool, edge rank constraints, which fills a missing piece in the toolbox for latent-variable causal discovery. Could the authors provide more examples to demonstrate the usefulness of edge rank constraints in latent-variable causal discovery?

3. The authors develop an efficient algorithm to recover the equivalence class from data. Could the authors provide a more detailed description of the algorithm and discuss its computational complexity?
