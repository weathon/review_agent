# Review

## Summary
This paper presents a new image quality assessment (IQA) task paradigm, called grounding-IQA, which combines multimodal referring and grounding with IQA for more fine-grained quality perception. The grounding-IQA is divided into two subtasks: grounding-IQA-description (GIQA-DES) and visual question answering (GIQA-VQA). To achieve grounding-IQA, the authors have developed a dataset called GIQA-160K and a benchmark called GIQA-Bench. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method in facilitating more fine-grained IQA applications.

## Strengths
1. The paper introduces a new IQA task paradigm that integrates multimodal referring and grounding with IQA, which is a novel approach to fine-grained quality assessment.

2. The authors have developed a dataset and a benchmark, which are valuable resources for researchers working on similar tasks.

3. The paper provides a detailed description of the proposed method, including the automated annotation pipeline and the evaluation criteria, which makes it easy to understand and replicate.

4. The experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method in facilitating more fine-grained IQA applications.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion of the limitations of the proposed method and potential directions for future research.

2. The authors could provide more examples of how the grounding-IQA paradigm can be applied in real-world scenarios to give readers a better sense of its practical utility.

3. The paper could include more detailed comparisons with existing IQA methods to highlight the advantages of the proposed method.

## Questions
1. How can the grounding-IQA paradigm be extended to other image quality assessment tasks, such as no-reference IQA?

2. What are the potential challenges and limitations of the proposed method, and how can they be addressed in future research?

3. How can the findings of this study contribute to the development of more advanced image quality assessment algorithms and systems?
