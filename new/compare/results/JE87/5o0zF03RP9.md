# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes IncentRL, a method that augments the standard RL objective with a KL divergence term between the predicted outcome distribution and a preferred outcome distribution. The authors conduct experiments on a 2-state MDP, MountainCar, and MiniGrid Doorkey 8x8. The authors also conduct ablation studies on the effect of the weight of the KL term, and the update method for the weight.

## Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
- The idea of using a KL divergence term to guide the RL agent is interesting.

## Weaknesses
- The experiment results are not convincing. In Experiment 1, the final mean reward is still increasing for $\beta = 0.1, 0.3$. In Experiment 2, the results for $\beta = 0.3, 1.0$ are missing. In Experiment 3, the results of the baseline are missing.
- The experiment environments are too simple. Experiment 1 is a 2-state MDP, and Experiment 2 is MountainCar. The authors should conduct experiments on more complex environments, such as MiniGrid or atari games.
- The ablation study is not convincing. The authors claim that the Bayesian adaptation of $\beta$ is better than fixed $\beta$ by showing that the posterior mean of $\beta$ concentrates near the effective region. However, the authors should show that the performance of the Bayesian adaptation of $\beta$ is better than the fixed $\beta$.

## Questions
- In Experiment 3, why does $\beta = 0.01$ perform better than $\beta = 0.0$?
- In Experiment 3, why does the final success rate of IncentRL not reach 100%?
