# Review

## Summary
This paper studies the theoretical limits of image watermarking capacity, demonstrating that current methods are far from reaching these bounds. The authors propose a new model, "Chunky Seal," which significantly increases the message capacity of watermarking, showing that higher capacities are achievable with novel architectures and training approaches. The paper also discusses the importance of understanding these limits to improve watermarking techniques for content provenance.

## Strengths
1. This paper presents a novel exploration of the theoretical capacity limits for image watermarking, addressing a significant gap in the field. By proposing the Chunky Seal model and demonstrating its effectiveness, the authors contribute a valuable perspective on pushing the boundaries of watermarking capacity, which has important implications for content provenance and digital security.

2. The paper is well-structured, with a clear progression from theoretical foundations to practical applications. The authors effectively communicate complex concepts, such as capacity bounds and robustness constraints, making the content accessible to readers with varying levels of expertise.

3. The significance of this work is underscored by its potential impact on future watermarking research. By identifying the limitations of current models and proposing a scaled-up solution, the paper encourages the community to consider innovative architectural and training approaches, which could lead to advancements in watermarking techniques.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper's focus on image watermarking may limit the generalizability of the proposed methods to other domains, such as video or multi-modal content. Future work could explore how these techniques apply to broader contexts.

2. The theoretical bounds presented are based on specific assumptions and may not fully capture the complexities of real-world applications. Further research could refine these bounds to better align with practical scenarios.

## Questions
1. How does the proposed Chunky Seal model perform under different types of attacks or distortions not covered in the paper? Are there specific vulnerabilities that need to be addressed?

2. What are the computational requirements for implementing Chunky Seal, particularly in terms of training time and resource usage? How feasible is it for deployment in resource-constrained environments?
