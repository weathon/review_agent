# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a joint model for expressive piano performance rendering (EPR) and automatic piano transcription (APT), with a performance style recommendation (PSR) module. The joint model encodes both scores and performances to disentangled content and style representations, and decodes them to recover the original sequence. The PSR module is trained separately to generate style embeddings conditioned on the score. The model is evaluated on both EPR and APT tasks and the disentanglement of content and style representations.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
2. The proposed method is reasonable and the experimental results are promising.
3. The model successfully disentangles content and style representations, as shown by the performer/composer identification experiment and visualization.

## Weaknesses
1. The proposed method is mainly based on Beyer & Dai (2024) and the main contribution is the PSR module, which is not well evaluated. The EPR results are mostly ablation studies and the APT results are not better than Beyer & Dai (2024).
2. The EPR-Only model is not well described and it is not clear what the difference is between EPR-Only and Ours (Target).
3. The style transfer experiment is not well designed. The Target style is not generated by the PSR module, so it is not clear what the PSR module actually learns.

## Questions
1. What is the EPR-Only model and what is the difference between EPR-Only and Ours (Target)?
2. How is the Target style in the style transfer experiment obtained? Does the PSR module actually learn to generate the Target style?
