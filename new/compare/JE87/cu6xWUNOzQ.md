# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces a nonlinear multimodal model for predicting brain responses to speech, combining audio and linguistic features from pre-trained models (e.g., Llama, Whisper). The authors claim that this approach improves prediction performance over traditional unimodal linear models and provides insights into multimodal processing in the brain.

## Strengths
1. The paper addresses an important topic in neuroscience by focusing on the integration of multimodal information in the brain.
2. The proposed method shows some improvements in prediction performance over traditional unimodal models.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper lacks substantial innovation. The core idea of using language models to predict brain activity has been explored in previous works, such as [1,2]. The main contribution appears to be the combination of audio and linguistic features, which is a relatively minor advancement.
2. The methodology section is unclear and lacks details about the model architecture and training procedures.
3. The paper claims to reveal distributed multimodal processing patterns in the cortex but does not provide sufficient evidence or analysis to support this claim.
4. The improvements in prediction performance are marginal and may not be statistically significant.
5. The paper fails to provide a comprehensive evaluation of the model's performance across different brain regions and audio stimuli.
6. The authors do not discuss the interpretability of the model's predictions or how they can be used to gain insights into brain function.

References:
[1] Aw, K. L., & Toneva, M. (2022). Training language models to summarize narratives improves brain alignment. arXiv preprint arXiv:2212.10898.
[2] Bonnasse-Gahot, L., & Pallier, C. (2024). fmri predictors based on language models of increasing complexity recover brain left lateralization. arXiv preprint arXiv:2405.17992.

## Questions
1. How does your approach differ from previous work in this area, and what novel insights does it provide?
2. Can you provide more details about the model architecture, training procedures, and evaluation metrics used in your study?
3. What evidence supports the claim of distributed multimodal processing patterns in the cortex, and how does your model contribute to understanding brain function?
