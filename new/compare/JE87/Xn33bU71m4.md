# Review

## Summary
The paper benchmarks the performance of several LLMs in reverse engineering tasks such as name recovery and type inference. The paper shows that without fine-tuning, the performance of these LLMs is low. Even with fine-tuning, the performance is not significantly better.

## Strengths
The paper benchmarks the performance of several LLMs in reverse engineering tasks such as name recovery and type inference.

## Weaknesses
The paper is essentially benchmarking the performance of several LLMs in reverse engineering tasks such as name recovery and type inference. The paper does not provide much insight into why the performance is low and what can be done to improve the performance. The paper does not propose any novel techniques to improve the performance of LLMs in reverse engineering tasks.

The paper says that "existing studies primarily emphasize fine-tuning LLMs for particular applications, often without providing a clear rationale for selecting a given model". However, the paper does not provide a clear rationale for selecting the particular LLMs that it benchmarks.

The paper says that "to address this gap, we evaluate nine prominent LLMs, including Llama2, CodeLlama, and Deepseek-R1, on reverse engineering tasks". However, the paper does not explain why these nine LLMs are representative of all LLMs. There are hundreds of LLMs available, and it is not clear why these nine are particularly interesting.

The paper uses decompiled code as input. However, it is not clear how decompiled code is obtained. The paper does not explain the process of decompilation and how different decompilation techniques affect the performance of LLMs.

## Questions
Why is the performance of LLMs low in reverse engineering tasks such as name recovery and type inference? What can be done to improve their performance?

What is the rationale for selecting the particular LLMs that are benchmarked in this paper?

How does the process of decompilation affect the performance of LLMs?
