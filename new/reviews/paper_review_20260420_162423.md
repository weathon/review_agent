## Summary

This paper presents a tracklet-based jersey number recognition system that combines Real-ESRGAN super-resolution, OWLv2 open-vocabulary detection, collage-based temporal aggregation, and Qwen3-VL vision-language model inference with LoRA fine-tuning. The core contribution is a collage-based formulation where multiple frames are aggregated into a grid (up to 25 crops) and processed by a single VLM call, replacing traditional per-frame OCR with confidence-weighted aggregation. The paper reports accuracy improvements from 49.7% (zero-shot) to 76.41% after LoRA fine-tuning on an undefined 1,840 sample set, and demonstrates reduced recognition-stage inference time through fewer total model calls.

## Strengths

- **Novel collage-based aggregation strategy**: The paper proposes an innovative approach to temporal aggregation that offloads frame-level confidence thresholding to the VLM through visual collage construction (Section IV-A-3, Figures 8, 11). This is substantiated by Table I showing reduction from 635 individual crop recognitions to 5 collage recognitions.

- **Comprehensive legibility backbone evaluation**: Despite computational constraints, the paper systematically evaluates five architectures (ResNet34, EfficientNetV2, MobileNet, ConvNeXt, Swin-T) with quantitative metrics including accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 across 6,827 images (Section III-A, Figures 1-7).

- **Insightful error analysis**: The paper rigorously categorizes failure modes including single/double-digit confusion, OWLv2 detection failures, Real-ESRGAN artifacts, and ground truth label errors, with concrete examples like Figure 14 showing dataset mislabeling (Section IV-D).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

- **Core contribution is evidentially unsupported**: The paper claims "LoRA fine-tuning improves accuracy from 49.7% in the zero-shot setting to 76.41%" and that the approach is "faster" (Abstract), but these claims are made without comparing the proposed method's accuracy against the baseline method [1] on the **same test set**. Without knowing whether the baseline achieves 30%, 60%, or 90% on the 1,840-sample evaluation set, the 76.41% figure is uninterpretable. Table I compares runtime (9.05s vs 1.19s) but not accuracy. This means the central claim—that the proposed approach "improves" jersey number recognition—lacks empirical foundation.

### Major

- **Undefined and inconsistent evaluation protocol**: The "1,840 sample set" used for the headline 76.41% result is never defined: its origin within the SoccerNet train/validation/test splits is unspecified, the sampling methodology is unstated, and the class distribution is unknown (Section V-C-1, line 351). The paper evaluates on different arbitrary subsets across sections: 5 tracklets (Section III.A), 20 tracklets (Swin-T comparison), 2 tracklets (full pipeline test), and 1 tracklet (runtime timing in Section V.D). The confusion matrix in Figure 13 shows 40 classes without explanation—SoccerNet jersey numbers are typically 0-99, so this restriction must be justified. These omissions make the results unverifiable and irreproducible.

- **Statistically invalid experimental conclusions**: The decision to reject Swin-T in favor of ResNet34 for the legibility stage was based on a full-pipeline evaluation of only **two tracklets** (Section III.A, line 215-218: "Due to computational constraints, the full pipeline comparison was executed on only two tracklets...the Swin-T version performed 0.34% worse than the baseline"). Conclusions drawn from n=2 are statistically meaningless. Similarly, the runtime comparison (Table I) uses a single tracklet ("SoccerNet test tracklet 263"), rendering the timing claims unreliable.

- **Absence of component ablation**: The pipeline introduces four major changes simultaneously (Real-ESRGAN, OWLv2, collage aggregation, VLM recognition), yet the paper provides no ablations to isolate the contribution of the collage mechanism specifically. It is impossible to determine whether the 76.41% result stems from the novel collage formulation or simply from replacing PARSeq with a large pre-trained VLM (Qwen3-VL). The paper acknowledges this limitation implicitly in Section V.D where it admits the timing excludes OWLv2 and Real-ESRGAN preprocessing, but there is no ablation to quantify their impact on accuracy.

### Minor

- **Incomplete computational analysis**: Section V.D compares recognition-stage timing but explicitly excludes OWLv2 detection and Real-ESRGAN upscaling from the proposed method's tally. The paper claims "the proposed recognition architecture is faster in practice" but this conclusion is based on an incomplete measurement (line 392-394: "The measured runtime comparison only considers the OCR inference stage rather than the entire localization-and-recognition pipeline because OWLv2 itself was not timed").

- **Ambiguous hardware environments**: The paper uses inconsistent hardware (A6000 GPU for preprocessing, Google Colab G4 for training and testing) without controlling for or reporting variance across environments (Section IV-C).

### Trivial

- Qualitative failure case visualizations showing specific misclassified collages

## Removed Points

These points are flagged to be removed, treat them with caution:
- **"Not yet released" or availability concerns about models**: The paper cites Real-ESRGAN, OWLv2, Qwen3-VL, and PARSeq—all are established, released models as of the current date. These exist.
- **"Unfair comparison" with baseline**: The asymmetry in timing (excluding OWLv2/Real-ESRGAN) favors the baseline, not the proposed method, so this is not a critique of overclaiming.
- **Missing related works**: Per guidelines, I do not add missing citations without external verification.

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions. The collage-based temporal aggregation for tracklet-level VLM reasoning is a genuinely novel formulation that shifts jersey recognition from frame-wise OCR toward sequence-level visual reasoning. However, the experimental validation fails to substantiate whether this architectural choice delivers real benefits over simpler alternatives.

## Suggestions

1. **Priority 1**: Run the baseline Koshkina & Elder [1] pipeline on the exact same 1,840-sample test set and report per-tracklet accuracy for direct comparison. Without this, the paper's central claim cannot be assessed.
2. **Priority 2**: Conduct ablations evaluating Qwen performance on (a) single best crops vs. collages, and (b) with/without Real-ESRGAN upscaling, to isolate the collage mechanism's specific contribution.
3. **Priority 3**: Re-run the Swin-T vs. ResNet34 comparison on at least 100+ tracklets, or remove the claim that Swin-T is inferior from the paper.
