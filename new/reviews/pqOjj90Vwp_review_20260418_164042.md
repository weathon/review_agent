## Summary
The paper develops a unified framework for characterizing the logical expressivity of arbitrary GNN models that decompose into aggregation-plus-combination operations (GACNNs). The central contribution is a recursive construction (Theorem 3 / Proposition 5) mapping any GACNN computation graph to an equivalent set of first-order logic formulas with counting quantifiers (FOC), which is then applied to (a) unify logical characterizations of 8+ existing architectures including MPNN, Subgraph GNNs, NBFNet, Local 2-GNN, 2-FGNN, SEAL (Proposition 7), (b) derive homomorphism expressivity for GACNNs (Theorem 8), and (c) read off k-WL upper bounds from variable counts (Proposition 9, Corollary 10).

## Strengths
- **General, constructive recipe.** Theorem 3 and Proposition 5 give a local, parent-children-based rewriting rule that turns any GACNN computation graph into an equivalent logic grammar. This avoids the bespoke, architecture-specific proofs used in prior work and enables analysis of novel architectures.
- **Unification across subareas.** Proposition 7 puts node-level (MPNN, Subgraph GNNs), link-level (NBFNet, SEAL), and higher-order (Local 2-GNN, 2-FGNN) models in a single logical language, making source-structure distinctions (single-source, two-source, multi-source) explicit and interpretable. The observation that 2-GNNs cannot express GrandParent while NBFNet can (Section 6.1) is a clean concrete payoff.
- **Extends homomorphism expressivity.** Theorem 8 generalizes the constructions of Dell et al. (2018) and Zhang et al. (2024) to arbitrary GACNNs (under the label-free / edge-composition-neighbor assumption) and explicitly addresses a conjecture from Zhang et al. (2024).
- **Simple WL-bound procedure.** Proposition 9 turns k-WL upper bounds into a variable-counting exercise on the grammar in Proposition 5 — a genuinely simpler tool than prior approaches.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None. The core framework is internally coherent and the main theorem (Theorem 3) is a constructive local rewriting that does not hinge on any questionable step.

### Major
- **Definition 2's asymmetry vs. the paper's own equality claims.** The second bullet ("for all φ ∈ Φ, there exists χ that captures φ on arbitrary graphs") and the fourth bullet ("for any χ, for each N there exists φ that agrees on graphs with ≤ N nodes") are genuinely asymmetric. The paper does acknowledge this explicitly ("there does exist GNN models which is not captured by any logic formulas, and thus we relax the statement to be the forth statement"), and the operative equivalence for the downstream results is actually bullet 5 (separation power, i.e. the Cai–Fürer–Immerman style correspondence). However, the paper then writes Corollary 10 as a chain of set-theoretic equalities (MPNNs = 1-WL, FGNN = 3-WL) without foregrounding that these are separation-power equalities modulo the finite-graph relaxation. Readers who take Definition 2 literally will be confused about whether the "=" in Corollary 10 is exact function equivalence or separation equivalence. The paper should state upfront that "=" throughout refers to equality of induced equivalence relations on graphs (separation power), not pointwise function equality.
- **Corollary 10 asserts strict inequalities but the main text only derives one direction.** Proposition 9 and the grammar-inspection procedure give upper bounds on WL levels. The strict "<" separations in Corollary 10 (e.g., Subgraph GNN (strong) < Local 2-FGNN < FGNN) require showing that the containment of equivalent logic sets is proper, i.e. exhibiting either a distinguishing formula in the larger grammar not captured by the smaller, or a separating pair of graphs. The main text gives no such witnesses and does not cite specific appendix propositions; the reader must take on faith that these strict inequalities hold. Even if the arguments live in the appendix (which the reviewer cannot see), the main text should at least gesture at how each separation is established.

### Minor
- **Scope of "complete" and "arbitrary".** The title's "Towards a Complete…" and the abstract's "arbitrary GNN architectures" are a somewhat aggressive framing given that Section 7 flags Graphormer-GD-style models (distance injection, global positional encodings, spectral filters, graph transformers with learned attention biases) as out of scope. The paper could be more precise that "arbitrary" means "arbitrary within the GACNN paradigm."
- **Theorem 8's label-free, edge-composition neighbor restriction is stated only inline.** Section 6.2 says "To simplify the discussion, we first assume no node / edge labels" and requires neighbor relations definable as edge compositions. These conditions should appear in the theorem statement itself; currently Theorem 8 reads as fully general.
- **Variable counting subtlety in Proposition 9.** The paper writes the grammar in a form that naively counts variables, but footnote 1 also notes that variable reuse is a legitimate device. Whether every grammar can be rewritten to its minimum-variable form without losing expressivity is not explicitly addressed in the main text, so the "trivial" variable count claim needs at least a sentence of justification.
- **Proposition 6 notation inconsistency.** The RHS uses `ω_G'` and `ω_G''` while the LHS and surrounding text use `φ_G`. Presumably a typo.

### Trivial
- In Proposition 7, the formula for 2-GNN, `φ(x,y) := ∃^{≥N} z (φ'(x,z) | ∃^{≥N} z (φ'(z,y)))`, has confusing parenthesization: the `|` appears to be an alternative between grammar productions but is enclosed inside a single ∃z. If so, move the parenthesis; if not, clarify scoping.
- The formula on line 222 (text body) reads `Connect(x, y) := ∃z (Connect(x, y ∧ E(y, z)) | E(x, y))` — the placement of `∧` inside `Connect(...)` is a typo.
- Section 6.2 alternates between "homomorphism expressivity" and "homogeneous expressivity"; these should be consistent.
- Very minor typos throughout (e.g., "$v \in N(x)$" where it should be "$y$" on line 125; "the forth statement" → "the fourth statement").

## Nice-to-Haves
- A worked separating example: two graphs and a formula explicitly showing that, e.g., Local 2-FGNN distinguishes them but Subgraph GNN (strong) cannot, derived from the grammar in Proposition 7. This would make the strict inequalities in Corollary 10 concrete.
- A decision procedure (or at least a sufficient condition / checklist) for whether a proposed new architecture falls within GACNN. Users encountering a new model should be able to verify membership mechanically.
- Extending Theorem 8 to the labeled case.

## Novel Insights
None beyond the paper's own contributions. The genuinely novel observations — that AGG/COM corresponds to ∃^{≥N}, that source-structure of message passing (single/two/multi-source) maps to quantifier structure in the induced formulas, and that homomorphism expressivity arises from stripping negation and high-count quantifiers — are all the paper's own.

## Suggestions
- In the abstract and title, replace "complete" / "arbitrary" with language that makes the GACNN scope explicit (e.g., "a general logical framework for GNNs decomposable into aggregation and combination operations").
- State Theorem 8 with all assumptions (no labels, neighbor = edge composition) explicitly in the theorem statement.
- In or immediately after Corollary 10, point the reader to the specific appendix results establishing each strict separation, or provide a one-paragraph sketch for at least one non-trivial separation.
- Clarify in the main text that "=" in Corollary 10 denotes equality of separation power / induced equivalence on finite graphs, not pointwise function equality.
- Add a short paragraph giving a checklist for determining GACNN membership of a novel architecture.
