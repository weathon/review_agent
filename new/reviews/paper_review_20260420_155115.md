## Summary

This paper presents a tracklet-level jersey number recognition pipeline for soccer broadcast footage. The system chains Real-ESRGAN super-resolution, OWLv2 open-vocabulary number detection, collage-based temporal aggregation, and a LoRA-fine-tuned Qwen vision-language model to predict jersey numbers from player tracklets. LoRA fine-tuning improves accuracy from 49.7% (zero-shot) to 76.41%, and the collage formulation reduces OCR-stage inference calls from 635 to 5 per tracklet. The paper also compares several legibility classifier backbones as supporting analysis.

---

## Strengths

- **Accuracy improvement from LoRA fine-tuning is the paper's central quantitative finding** (Section V.C.1): zero-shot Qwen achieves 49.7% while two epochs of LoRA fine-tuning raise accuracy to 76.41% on the 1,840-sample set, a 26.7 percentage-point gain. This delta is large enough to be meaningful.

- **Collage-based aggregation as a pipeline simplification**: the multi-frame collage input is a clean engineering insight that eliminates per-frame confidence aggregation heuristics, offloading temporal reasoning to the VLM in a single forward pass. The motivation is clearly articulated (Section IV.A.3).

- **Comprehensive error taxonomy in Section IV.D**: five concrete failure categories are identified—single/double-digit confusion, OWLv2 missed detections, Real-ESRGAN texture hallucination, ground-truth label errors, and aggressive legibility filtering—each supported by specific examples. The confusion matrix (Figure 13) correctly identifies single/double-digit confusion as the dominant error mode, and the text engages with this honestly.

- **Real-ESRGAN preprocessing with visual evidence**: Figures 9 and 10 provide before/after comparisons at both individual-crop and collage levels, showing substantively sharper digit boundaries after 4× upscaling.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **No comparison to the Koshkina & Elder [1] baseline on the same test set.** This is the most serious problem. The paper repeatedly positions Koshkina & Elder [1] as the direct point of comparison (introduction, related work, methodology), yet never runs their system on the 1,840-sample SoccerNet test set. A 76.41% accuracy figure in isolation — compared only to the authors' own zero-shot configuration — does not establish that the proposed system represents an improvement over existing work. The paper cannot claim to "outperform" the baseline without this direct comparison. This is not a missing ablation; it is the core missing experiment.

- **Unexplained training/validation loss inversion in Figure 12.** The loss table (Figure 12 caption) shows validation loss (~0.22) consistently and substantially below training loss (~0.325–0.285) from the first measured step throughout all 250 training steps. In standard supervised learning this pattern is reversed; it is consistent with validation data overlapping with training data, a systematically easier validation distribution, or a logging artifact. The paper provides no explanation. Because 76.41% is the paper's headline result and it derives from this fine-tuning run, this anomaly must be explained before the result can be fully trusted.

### Minor

- **Runtime comparison scopes out the dominant costs.** Section V.D presents Table I as evidence that "the proposed recognition architecture is faster in practice," but the table covers only the OCR stage. The paper's own text explicitly notes that OWLv2 was not timed and that Real-ESRGAN preprocessing took 20 hours for the full dataset. The conclusion drawn ("the proposed recognition architecture is faster in practice") is not supported by the timed data, which captures only the cheapest stage of the proposed system. The claim should be scoped to "recognition-stage inference only" — a narrower and accurate version of the claim the paper actually makes.

- **CNN legibility classifier evaluation lacks ground truth to support its conclusion.** Section III.A concludes that ResNet34, EfficientNetV2, and MobileNet are inadequate because all produce average scores below 0.5 on five tracklets. But the paper never establishes what fraction of frames in those tracklets are actually legible. If most frames are genuinely illegible, sub-0.5 scores reflect correct behavior. The inference that these models are deficient — and that this motivates architectural replacement — is not supported without frame-level labels. Additionally, EfficientNetV2 and MobileNet are evaluated with weights not fine-tuned for this domain, while ResNet34 has domain-specific weights from [1], making the comparison inherently confounded.

- **Swin-T decision based on two tracklets.** The decision to reject Swin-T (Section III.A) is based on a 0.34% performance difference on a two-tracklet evaluation. The paper acknowledges computational constraints, and this is a reasonable practical limitation, but the conclusion that Swin-T "did not warrant replacing the baseline" cannot be drawn from n=2 tracklets — a single correct/incorrect prediction can swing the metric by ~50 percentage points. The image-level evidence (Swin-T F1 ~0.9 vs. ResNet34 F1 ~0.55 on 6,827 crops) arguably warranted a larger pipeline test.

- **Collage selection policy for long tracklets is unspecified.** Section IV.A.3 states the collage uses "up to 25 crops" but never specifies how the 25 are sampled when a tracklet has more frames. Section V.B.4 acknowledges information loss but provides no solution. This directly affects recognition on long tracklets and is a reproducibility gap.

- **Test set identity is ambiguous.** Section V.C.1 reports accuracy on a "1,840 sample set" without clarifying whether this is the standard SoccerNet held-out test split and what fraction was used in training. This makes the number harder to interpret.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Ablation comparing collage-based Qwen against per-frame Qwen with majority voting would isolate the benefit of the grid representation from the benefit of using Qwen at all.

- Representative collages before and after fine-tuning, for failure-to-success cases, would clarify whether LoRA improves genuine OCR ability or mainly output-format compliance.

## Novel Insights

The collage-as-single-VLM-input formulation is a simple but underexplored trick: instead of aggregating frame-level predictions with hand-tuned confidence weights, placing a grid of crops into a VLM's context window allows the model to do implicit temporal voting. Whether this beats naive per-frame majority voting is not tested, but the idea that spatial tiling can substitute for temporal aggregation is worth isolating in future work. The error analysis also surfaces a practical dataset-quality issue: SoccerNet's tracklet-level weak supervision propagates incorrect labels at the frame level, which may inflate error rates across all methods benchmarked on this dataset.

---

## Suggestions

1. **Run the Koshkina & Elder [1] baseline on the same 1,840-sample test split** and report results side by side. This is essential to establish whether the proposed system is competitive.
2. **Explain the validation-below-training loss pattern** in Figure 12: audit the data split for overlap and report train/validation sizes.
3. **Scope the runtime claim correctly**: retitle Table I to "OCR-stage inference comparison" and note that full-pipeline comparison including Real-ESRGAN and OWLv2 is future work.
4. **Provide frame-level legibility labels for the five tracklets** used in Section III.A, so the CNN comparison is grounded in ground truth.
5. **Specify the collage sampling policy** (e.g., uniform stride, top-confidence crop selection) for long tracklets.
