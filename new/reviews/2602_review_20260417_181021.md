This paper identifies two regularities in autoregressive video diffusion models—(1) middle-layer features are highly redundant across denoising steps, and (2) cross-frame attention becomes sparse in deeper layers—and leverages these findings to propose Separable Causal Diffusion (SCD), an encoder-decoder architecture that runs causal reasoning once per frame while iterating a lightweight frame-wise diffusion decoder across denoising steps. Experiments on small-scale pretraining benchmarks (TECO-Minecraft, UCF-101) and a large-scale fine-tuning setup from Wan 2.1 show SCD achieves 2–3× lower latency while maintaining competitive generation quality.

## Strengths

- **Insightful probing analysis with intervention validation.** The cosine similarity analysis (Fig. 2, consistently >0.95) and cross-frame attention mass visualization (Fig. 4) provide concrete empirical evidence for redundancy and sparsity. Crucially, these observational findings are validated through intervention: skip-layer finetuning (Fig. 3) and frame-diagonal mask finetuning (Fig. 5) both recover baseline quality, strengthening the claims beyond mere correlation.

- **Principled and effective architectural design.** SCD directly operationalizes the two findings: the causal encoder runs once per frame (exploiting step-wise redundancy), and the frame-wise decoder avoids cross-frame attention (exploiting depth-wise sparsity). The design is clean and well-motivated, achieving 4× speedup on TECO-Minecraft and ~1.3× on VBenCh with comparable quality.

- **Honest and transparent limitations discussion.** The paper explicitly acknowledges that step-wise invariance weakens near the end of the trajectory (similarity drops to ~0.8), that deep layers retain non-zero cross-frame attention, and that these residual couplings may explain the slight quality gap on VBenCh. This transparency adds credibility.

- **Comprehensive ablation study.** Multiple design choices are ablated: encoder vs. decoder depth scaling (Tables 1–2), concatenation strategy (Appendix Table 4), context corruption magnitude (Appendix Table 6), skip-layer configurations, and leave-one-out layer analysis for fine-tuning (Fig. 7). These provide useful practical guidance.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **The central conceptual claim ("causality is separable from denoising") overstates the evidence.** The title and framing suggest a general architectural principle, but what the paper demonstrates is that causal reasoning is *approximately* and *mostly* separable from the denoising process in specific model families and settings. The own Limitations section concedes a measurable quality gap attributable to residual temporal couplings, and the similarity drop from >0.95 to ~0.8 in late denoising steps (Fig. 2) indicates information is lost by fully amortizing temporal reasoning. The phrase "causality is separable" implies a cleaner decomposition than the evidence supports. This matters because the gap between the paper's framing and its actual evidence could mislead future work into treating SCD's architecture as the final answer rather than an important step requiring refinement.

- **Evaluation is limited to relatively short-horizon, low-complexity settings, leaving long-horizon and error accumulation properties untested.** TECO-Minecraft (128²), UCF-101 (64²), and even VBenCh evaluate relatively short clips. The core motivation for causal/AR video generation is streaming and long-horizon generation, yet no experiment tests quality under extended autoregressive rollouts (e.g., 100+ frames) where error accumulation and context drift would most challenge SCD's once-per-frame encoder. The probing analyses (Figs. 2, 4) are conducted under teacher-forcing (clean context) conditions, not under open-loop rollout where past frames are model-generated and imperfect. If separability patterns degrade under compounding errors, the practical value for streaming is undermined. Given the paper's emphasis on real-time applications, this is a significant gap.

- **Parameter-count mismatch in the primary high-resolution comparison.** In Table 3, SCD (1.6B) is compared against Self Forcing (1.3B). The paper reports SCD achieves "strong performance while being ~1.3× faster" with a Total Score of 84.03 vs. 84.26, but the 30% parameter advantage makes it difficult to isolate whether the efficiency gains stem from architectural separation or simply from having more compute capacity. A matched-parameter baseline (SCD-1.3B or Self Forcing-1.6B) would substantially strengthen the architectural claims.

### Minor

- **The fine-tuning recipe for adapting pretrained models involves ad hoc choices with limited justification.** The 25-layer encoder / 5+5-layer decoder split is motivated by leave-one-out analysis (Fig. 7), but this analysis only shows which *individual* layers are important in the original model—it does not directly justify combining the first and last 5 layers into a decoder. The input reparameterization (feeding high-noise current frames during training) is also introduced without systematic exploration of alternatives.

- **No direct comparison with recent hybrid AR-diffusion models (MarDini, VideoMAR).** While these are architecturally related, the authors discuss them in related work but provide no experimental comparison. This makes it hard to assess the marginal contribution of SCD's specific design (once-per-frame encoder + multi-step frame-wise decoder) versus the general encoder-decoder paradigm.

### Trivial
- The caption of Figure 8 appears to have a description that could be confusing: the text states "SCD achieves superior training efficiency" but the bar chart description mentions "Remove One Block" bars being higher in seconds, which would mean slower training. This may be a parser artifact or labeling inconsistency.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Long-horizon rollout evaluation** (e.g., 100+ frames) comparing SCD vs. fully causal baselines under autoregressive generation, measuring temporal consistency and error accumulation—directly addressing the paper's own stated motivation for real-time streaming.

- **Feature caching baseline within the original causal architecture.** If middle-layer features are nearly identical across steps, a simple caching mechanism within the existing architecture could achieve some of SCD's speedups without architectural changes. Comparing SCD against this simpler alternative would clarify when full architectural separation is necessary.

- **Hybrid variant** where $c_i$ is updated at a few key denoising steps rather than strictly once, to close the quality gap observed on VBenCh and test whether the remaining residual couplings are important.

## Removed Points

These points are flagged to be removed; treat them with caution.

- **Claim that probing is "not causally identified" in a strict sense.** The harsh reviewer argues that skip-layer and frame-diagonal experiments "show that the base model's weights can be adapted to a modified architecture" rather than establishing causal structure. However, the successful adaptation after only brief finetuning (5K steps) *does* provide meaningful evidence about the functional role of different layers—it shows the information in those layers is not irreversibly entangled. The probing conclusions are observational but the intervention experiments add causal support. This concern is valid but partially addressed.

- **Demand for comparison with acceleration methods like ToCa, Δ-DiT, or BlockDance.** These are training-free caching/acceleration methods for *non-causal* video diffusion, operating in a fundamentally different setting. SCD targets a different problem (architectural redesign of *causal* diffusion), and comparing against orthogonal acceleration methods would mix categories. The relevant comparison domain is causal/AR video models, where the paper provides appropriate baselines.

- **Memory/KV-cache overhead analysis.** While relevant for streaming, the paper measures wall-clock latency and throughput (the more practical metrics) and the encoder-decoder design explicitly eliminates the extra KV-cache pass that prior AR models require (§5.3). This is addressed in principle.

- **Demand for FVD on from-scratch models.** The paper reports comprehensive pixel-domain metrics (LPIPS, SSIM, PSNR) plus FVD for TECO-Minecraft and UCF-101, which are standard for these benchmarks. Adding more FVD numbers is a nice-to-have, not a core flaw.

- **Request for training compute comparison.** The paper does include training efficiency results (Fig. 8). This is not a missing experiment.

## Novel Insights

The most insightful finding is that cross-frame causal attention in video diffusers naturally collapses into a sparse, depth-wise pattern even when trained with dense causal masks—early layers concentrate temporal reasoning while deeper layers shift to intra-frame rendering (Fig. 4). This is an emergent property of the trained model, not an explicit architectural bias, and it directly motivates the practical question of whether dense causal attention throughout the network is unnecessary. The skip-layer experiment (Fig. 3) is the most convincing validation: removing 15 of 30 middle layers across denoising steps and recovering quality with brief finetuning demonstrates that this redundancy is not just statistically observed but functionally expendable.
