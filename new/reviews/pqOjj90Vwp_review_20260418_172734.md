## Summary

This paper proposes a unified framework (GACNNs—General Aggregate-Combine Neural Networks) for determining the logical expressivity of arbitrary GNN architectures decomposable into aggregation (AGG) and combination (COM) operations. The central result (Theorem 3) constructively maps AGG operations to counting quantifiers (∃⁽ᴺ⁾) and COM operations to logical connectives (¬, ∧), enabling the derivation of equivalent logic sets for eight prominent GNN families. The paper further connects these logic sets to homomorphism expressivity (Theorem 8) and WL upper bounds (Proposition 9, Corollary 10), claiming to resolve a conjecture from Zhang et al. (2024).

## Strengths

- **Unified, constructive framework:** The GACNN abstraction (Section 4) cleanly decomposes diverse GNN architectures—including MPNNs, Subgraph GNNs, FGNNs, NBFNet, and SEAL—into a shared AGG/COM structure. This unification is non-trivial and enables systematic analysis where prior work treated each architecture ad hoc.

- **Interpretable logical characterizations:** Proposition 7 provides explicit logic grammars for eight GNN families side-by-side. The structural differences (e.g., NBFNet's single-source φ(x,z)∧E(z,y) vs. 2-FGNN's multi-source φ'(x,z)∧φ''(z,y) vs. Local 2-GNN's two-source split) directly explain *why* these models differ in power, going beyond mere WL hierarchy placement.

- **Novel methodological contribution for homomorphism expressivity:** The recursive construction of homomorphism expressivity from logic grammars (Section 6.2, Figure 2e–f) is genuinely new. The 2-FGNN worked example demonstrates that the construction yields concrete, verifiable graph families, extending the approach of Dell et al. (2018) and Zhang et al. (2024) to arbitrary GACNNs.

- **Clean graph-readout/logic connection:** Proposition 6's correspondence between graph-level readout (AGG over node tuples) and existential quantification over tuples is an elegant and underappreciated observation.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Central theorems (3 and 8) stated without proof sketches in the main text.** Theorem 3 is the paper's lynchpin: it asserts that the constructed Φ_K is the equivalent logic set of χ_K (satisfying all five conditions of Definition 2, including discriminating-power equivalence). The theorem is stated with a constructive recipe but no argument appears in the main text for *why* the construction works—particularly why counting quantifiers capture arbitrary permutation-invariant aggregation, and why the resulting logic set satisfies condition 5 of Definition 2. This gap is precisely the technically difficult step that Barceló et al. (2020) resolved carefully for the MPNN case via separation arguments connecting continuous representations to binary discriminating power. The paper cites Barceló et al. but does not reproduce or adapt that argument. Similarly, Theorem 8 (equating homomorphism count equality with GACNN non-distinguishability) is stated without any proof sketch. For a theory paper, the absence of proof sketches for the two most important results makes the claims unverifiable in the main text.

- **Corollary 10 overclaims by conflating upper bounds with exact characterizations and strict inequalities.** Proposition 9 establishes an *iff* for upper bounds: a GNN is at most k-WL iff its logic set uses ≤k variables. This yields upper bounds on WL power. However, Corollary 10 uses equality signs (e.g., "FGNN = 3-WL") and strict inequalities ("Subgraph GNNs (weak) < Subgraph GNNs (strong)"). The equalities require matching lower bounds (showing each GNN *achieves* the claimed WL power), and the strict inequalities require exhibiting a formula in the larger set not expressible in the smaller. Neither is derived from this paper's framework alone. For instance, "MPNNs = 1-WL" comes from Barceló et al. (2020) and Morris et al. (2019); "FGNN = 3-WL" requires a separate lower-bound argument. The strict inequalities (e.g., Subgraph GNN weak < strong) require showing the logic grammar of the stronger model strictly contains the weaker one, but the paper does not provide containment proofs. The paper should clearly distinguish what its framework formally derives (upper bounds via variable counting) from what it imports from prior work (exact equalities, strict separations).

### Minor

- **Definition 2's fourth condition weakens the "complete" framing in the title.** The ideal correspondence (for every χ ∈ 𝒳, there exists φ ∈ Φ such that φ(u) = χ(u) for all G, u) is relaxed to: for every χ and every N, there exists φ agreeing on all graphs of size ≤N. The paper acknowledges this (line 81), but the title's claim of a "complete" framework and the abstract's "comprehensive and systematic relationship" do not reflect this limitation. Since on graphs of size ≤N, there are finitely many non-isomorphic structures, this condition is relatively easy to satisfy for any sufficiently expressive logic set, and does not by itself establish a tight correspondence. The fifth condition (discriminating power equivalence) does the real work but is not highlighted as the substantive part.

- **Insufficient justification for removing negation and ∃⁽ᴺ⁾ (N≥2) in the homomorphism construction.** The construction in Section 6.2 (step 1, line 236) strips negation and ∃⁽ᴺ⁾ for N≥2 from the logic set before constructing F, but does not explain in the main text why this removal yields the correct homomorphism expressivity. Since negation is an essential GNN operation (via COM), and the resulting loss of information needs justification (e.g., why positive formulas suffice for homomorphism expressivity), this step merits at least a brief argument or reference.

- **The 2-GNN formula in Proposition 7 reuses variable z in nested quantifiers.** The entry "2-GNN: φ(x,y) := ∃⁽ᴺ⁾ z (φ'(x,z) | ∃⁽ᴺ⁾ z(φ'(z,y)))" uses z inside nested quantifiers with the same name. The paper itself warns against variable reuse in the context of homomorphism expressivity (footnote 1). While this may be intentional as syntactic sugar, it creates ambiguity about whether the inner and outer z are the same variable or shadowed, and whether this affects the logic set's expressiveness.

### Trivial
- The abstract claims the paper "addresses several open problems" but only one conjecture (from Zhang et al., 2024) is explicitly identified as resolved.

## Novel Insights

The paper's most insightful observation is that the *structure* of a GNN's update rule (single-source vs. multi-source aggregation, which variables are connected by edges vs. logic formulas) directly translates into the *grammar* of its equivalent logic set, and from there to both homomorphism expressivity and WL upper bounds via purely syntactic operations (variable counting, negation removal). This creates a single pipeline: GNN architecture → AGG/COM decomposition → logic grammar → homomorphism expressivity → WL bound, which is genuinely more systematic than prior ad hoc analyses. However, this pipeline's reliability depends on the unverified Theorems 3 and 8.
