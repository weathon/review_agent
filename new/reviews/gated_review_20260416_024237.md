# Review of gated.md

## Summary

This paper systematically investigates gating mechanisms in transformer attention layers, evaluating 30+ variants across 15B MoE and 1.7B dense models trained on 3.5T tokens. The key finding is that applying a head-specific sigmoid gate after SDPA (Scaled Dot-Product Attention) consistently improves performance (~0.2 PPL reduction, +2 MMLU), enhances training stability (enabling larger learning rates, eliminating loss spikes), and reduces first-token attention mass. The authors attribute these benefits to introducing non-linearity between the value and output projections and enabling query-dependent sparse gating, which they connect to mitigation of "attention sinks."

## Strengths

- **Unusually comprehensive systematic ablation.** The paper evaluates over 30 gating variants across five axes (position, granularity, head-sharing, multiplicative/additive, activation function) with fair parameter-matched baselines (Table 1). This scope is rare in architecture papers and provides actionable guidance for practitioners.

- **Large-scale, resource-intensive experiments.** Training 15B MoE and 1.7B dense models on 3.5T tokens is non-trivial. The stability experiments (Table 2, Fig. 1 right) showing gated models tolerate 8e-3 learning rates where baselines diverge demonstrates genuine practical value.

- **Novel empirical findings on attention sinks.** The observation that SDPA output gating dramatically reduces first-token attention mass (Fig. 2, 46.7% → 4.8%) while value gating does not, and the analysis linking this to head-specific query-dependent sparsity, advances understanding beyond prior work. The decomposition showing massive activations are not necessary for attention sinks (Sec. 4.3) is insightful.

- **Insightful architectural analysis.** The observation that gating at G₁ and G₂ introduces non-linearity between Wᵥ and Wₒ (which form a low-rank linear map when composed, per Eq. 6) provides a plausible explanation for why these positions outperform G₅.

## Weaknesses

### Major
- **"Attention-sink-free" claim substantially overstates evidence.** The paper repeatedly claims gated models "eliminate" attention sinks and are "attention-sink-free." However: (a) attention sink is evaluated only as average mass on the first token—a proxy, not the full phenomenon; (b) the metric drops to 0.048, not zero; (c) other potential sink locations (special tokens, EOS, mid-sequence) are not evaluated. The strong claims in the abstract and conclusion are not commensurate with this narrow evidence. **Recommendation:** Soften to "strongly attenuates first-token attention sinks" or similar.

- **Causal attribution to "low-rank expressivity" is under-substantiated.** The core mechanistic claim is that non-linearity between Wᵥ and Wₒ explains gating's effectiveness. Yet Table 3 shows RMSNorm alone (row 5, no added parameters) achieves competitive PPL reduction, and other non-linear variants (SiLU-only, row 6) underperform significantly. The NS-sigmoid experiment conflates sparsity reduction with distribution shifts. No rank or representational capacity measurements are provided. The narrative treats a plausible hypothesis as established fact.

- **Sparsity-sink-performance causal chain is correlational.** While sparser gating correlates with less sink and better performance (Table 4), the paper doesn't establish that sink elimination *causes* the gains. Both could be effects of sparsity, or sparsity could improve conditioning independently. The long-context results (Table 5) are suggestive but based on a single benchmark (RULER) and one model pair, without controls using alternative stabilizing mechanisms (e.g., RMSNorm at G₁).

### Minor
- **Missing direct comparisons with closely related work.** The Forgetting Transformer (Lin et al., 2025) applies a forget gate to softmax attention outputs and demonstrates strong length extrapolation. No head-to-head benchmark comparison is provided, weakening claims of novelty. Quantizable Transformers (Bondarenko et al., 2023) similarly uses SDPA output gating for quantization—acknowledged but not empirically compared.

- **Limited scale diversity.** All experiments at 1.7B dense / 15B MoE. Whether the very low mean gating scores (~0.116, implying ~88% suppression) scale healthily to 7B+ models is untested. The human review of Forgetting Transformer raised similar concerns about "robustness across model sizes."

- **Training stability claims are narrowly demonstrated.** While Table 2 shows impressive stability on 1.7B dense models, the MoE experiments (the paper's primary platform) do not verify stability at 3.5T tokens, where instability matters most.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Theoretical analysis of how much representational capacity is actually gained by the low-rank non-linearity insight; direct comparison with Forgetting Transformer on common benchmarks; evaluation on additional long-context benchmarks (LongBench, InfiniteBench); per-seed variance statistics; assessment of whether extreme gating score sparsity affects in-context learning or retrieval capabilities.

## Removed Points

**These points are flagged to be removed, treat them with caution:**

- Various harsh critic complaints about "unfair comparison" with baselines that are actually holding parameters constant (the paper includes parameter-matched baselines in Table 1 rows 2-4, and explicitly reduces FFN width when adding gates in dense models).

- The claim that "NS-sigmoid does not cleanly isolate sparsity"—while technically valid, designing a truly neutral "non-sparse nonlinearity" is difficult and this is appropriately flagged as a limitation/weakness to be addressed rather than a fatal flaw.

- Complaints about FlashAttention kernel compatibility—the paper notes "<2% wall-time latency" which is a reasonable practical estimate, and detailed kernel fusion analysis is outside scope.

## Novel Insights

The paper's most valuable contribution is demonstrating that **attention sink mitigation can be achieved without modifying the softmax attention structure itself**—only by gating its output. This decouples sink elimination from prior approaches (modified softmax, register tokens, streaming windows) and opens practical pathways. The insight that head-specific query-dependent sparsity matters more than mere non-linearity (value gating reduces activations but not sinks; head-shared gating reduces neither) is genuinely novel.

## Suggestions

1. **Temper mechanistic claims:** Explicitly distinguish empirical findings from hypotheses in the abstract/introduction. Replace "eliminates attention sinks" with "strongly attenuates first-token attention sinks."

2. **Add head-to-head comparison:** Include Forgetting Transformer as a baseline on key experiments (RULER, MMLU, stability).

3. **Strengthen causality:** Add an ablation with RMSNorm at G₁ (Table 3 row 5 is close but not in main MoE table) and compare extrapolation performance to isolate whether sparsity specifically matters or just non-linearity/conditioning.

4. **Clarify scope:** State explicitly that "attention-sink-free" applies to first-token sinks only, pending broader evaluation.

## Score and Decision

**Calibration comparison:**

- *Above:* **"Small-scale proxies for large-scale Transformer training instabilities"** (8,8,8,8) — More careful causal claims, cleaner ablations, but the current paper has larger-scale practical impact and broader architectural search.

- *Comparable:* **"Forgetting Transformer"** (6,8,5,8) — Similar mechanism, but this paper has more systematic variant search, larger scale, and additional stability findings. The Forgetting Transformer was accepted (poster) despite noting "insufficient evaluation" and "missing baselines"—similar but less severe issues here.

- *Below:* **"Methods of Improving LLM Training Stability"** (3,5,3,3) — Less systematic, weaker claims, withdrawn.

- *Related:* **"When Attention Sink Emerges"** (8,8,6) — Stronger causal analysis, but purely empirical-observational without architectural intervention.

This paper has genuine strengths in systematic experimentation, scale, and practical impact. The empirical core—SDPA-output gating improves performance and stability—is robust and valuable. However, the overreaching on "attention-sink-free" claims and unverified mechanistic attribution to low-rank expressivity are significant issues that would require revision for a top venue. As presented, the contribution lands in **borderline Accept/Reject** territory: the experimental effort and practical findings warrant acceptance, but the overclaims and missing direct comparisons must be addressed.
