## Summary

This paper investigates whether causal temporal reasoning in autoregressive (AR) video diffusion models is separable from the iterative denoising process. Through systematic probing experiments on WAN-2.1 T2V-1.3B, the authors identify two complementary regularities: (1) early/middle-layer features are near-redundant across denoising steps (cosine similarity >0.95), and (2) cross-frame attention in deeper layers becomes increasingly sparse. Motivated by these findings, they introduce **Separable Causal Diffusion (SCD)**, an encoder–decoder architecture where a causal transformer encoder runs once per frame to produce context tokens, while a lightweight frame-wise diffusion decoder handles per-frame iterative denoising. SCD is evaluated both in pretraining (TECO-Minecraft, UCF-101) and fine-tuning (WAN 2.1 → VBench) settings, demonstrating 2–4× speedup on small benchmarks and ~1.3× throughput improvement at scale with competitive quality.

## Strengths

- **Multi-faceted probing methodology is systematic and convergent.** Five complementary lines of evidence (cosine similarity, PCA visualization, skip-layer finetuning, cross-frame attention mass, frame-diagonal finetuning/mask removal) reinforce the core separability claim. No single probe is conclusive, but their convergence is compelling. The finetuning experiments go beyond observation to show the redundancy/sparsity can actually be exploited without quality loss.

- **Architecture is directly motivated by empirical findings.** SCD is not an ad-hoc design—the encoder/decoder split directly operationalizes the two observed regularities (step-invariant early layers → encode once; frame-local deep layers → decode frame-wise). This makes the contribution feel principled rather than engineered. The analogy to next-token prediction in LLMs is apt and instructive.

- **Efficiency gains are substantial and validated across multiple settings.** SCD-M achieves 4.6× speedup on TECO-Minecraft and 3.5× on UCF-101 while *improving* FVD. In the large-scale fine-tuning setting, SCD (1.6B) achieves 11.1 FPS vs. Self Forcing (1.3B) at 8.9 FPS—a credible gain since the larger model is faster. The 20% training-efficiency improvement in rollout distribution matching (Figure 8) is an additional practical benefit.

- **Leave-one-out layer importance analysis (Figure 7) is a principled contribution.** Rather than guessing which layers to assign to encoder vs. decoder, the authors conduct a data-driven validation loss experiment on WAN-2.1, revealing that earliest and latest layers are most important. This drives the fine-tuning architecture design.

- **Honest and explicit limitations section.** The paper acknowledges that step-wise invariance weakens in the last 10 denoising steps (~0.8 similarity vs. >0.95 earlier) and that deep layers retain non-zero cross-frame attention mass. This maturity is welcome and lends credibility to the overall analysis.

- **Broad validation across model types.** The separability patterns are verified on WAN-2.1, a Self-Forcing model (Appendix Figs. 11-12), and a Diffusion Forcing 3D-UNet (Appendix Fig. 13), demonstrating generality across architectures and training objectives.

### Minor

- **Parameter counts absent from Tables 1–2.** The paper says SCD "follows the DiT parameterization scheme (B/M/L)" and decomposes total depth into encoder + decoder, which implies a fixed total depth budget. However, since the decoder is described as "lightweight" and operates without cross-frame attention, SCD variants may have fewer effective parameters than Causal-DiT-M/B. Without explicit parameter counts, it is difficult to fully disentangle whether the 4× latency reduction arises from the decoupling principle alone or is partly from a smaller effective model. This is especially important for Tables 1–2 (the primary pretraining evidence), since in Table 3 the larger SCD (1.6B) is faster than Self Forcing (1.3B), making the gain there more clearly attributable to architecture.

- **Fine-tuning architecture (Section 6.2) is underspecified regarding weight sharing.** The paper states: "we designate the first 25 layers of the pretrained 30-layer model as the causal encoder, and combine its first 5 and last 5 layers to form the diffusion decoder, resulting in a total of 35 layers." This implies layers 1–5 of the original model appear in *both* the encoder and decoder. The paper never clarifies whether these overlapping layers are weight-shared, independently initialized from the pretrained weights, or copied and then fine-tuned separately. The answer materially affects the total parameter count and how the encoder and decoder interact. A diagram or explicit clarification would substantially improve reproducibility.

- **Abstract overclaims VBench results.** The abstract states SCD "matches or surpasses the generation quality of strong causal diffusion baselines." In the pretraining setting (Tables 1–2), SCD genuinely surpasses all baselines. However, in Table 3 (VBench), SCD scores 84.03 vs. Self Forcing at 84.26 and is strictly lower on all three subscores (Total: 84.03 vs. 84.26; Quality: 85.14 vs. 85.25; Semantic: 79.60 vs. 80.30). The paper itself correctly calls this "competitive" in the Results section; the abstract's "matches or surpasses" is a mild overstatement that should be corrected to "competitive with" or "nearly matches."

### Trivial

- **Figure 8 labeling is confusing.** The figure description as parsed labels the bars "Full WAN" and "Remove One Block," with "Remove One Block" bars being *taller* (more time) than "Full WAN." The caption reads "SCD achieves superior training efficiency than causal diffusion baselines." If "Full WAN" represents SCD and "Remove One Block" represents the causal baseline, the labeling is counterintuitive and should be clarified (e.g., rename to "SCD" and "Full Causal Diffusion").

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Iso-parameter ablation for Tables 1–2.** Adding a row "Causal-DiT-M matched to SCD-M parameter count" (or reporting total FLOPs/parameter counts for all models) would make the efficiency attribution unambiguous.

- **Per-head cross-frame attention analysis.** Figure 4 pools attention mass across all heads per layer, which may obscure heads that carry disproportionate temporal information in deep layers. A head-level breakdown or targeted ablation (remove only the high-mass heads vs. all cross-frame attention) would strengthen the temporal independence claim.

- **Bring key ablation results into the main text.** The context noise corruption ablation (Appendix Table 6) and the concatenation comparison (Appendix Table 4) are important design decisions. At least a one-sentence summary of findings should appear in the main text.

- **Results on longer-horizon generation (100+ frames).** To fully demonstrate whether the KV cache efficiency of the causal encoder scales with context length, an experiment on very long rollouts would be valuable. The paper focuses on 5s clips, but the motivation explicitly invokes real-time streaming.

- **Theoretical grounding for step-invariance.** A brief discussion connecting the observed step-invariance to the structure of diffusion processes (coarse structure determined early, fine detail later) would strengthen the conceptual contribution beyond an empirical observation.

## Removed Points

*These points are flagged to be removed; treat them with caution.*

- **Probing generalizability from large to small models (Harsh Critic #3).** The paper explicitly states in Section 4 that it repeats its observations "on autoregressive video models trained from scratch and on other conditioning: consistent behaviors are summarized in Appendix A.2," and further validates on a 3D-UNet trained with Diffusion Forcing (Appendix Fig. 13). The concern that separability is an emergent property of only large pretrained models is directly addressed by the paper.

- **Cosine similarity ignores magnitude (Harsh Critic, Section 4.1 note).** This is a generic methodological quibble. The paper uses cosine similarity for directional redundancy, which is appropriate for measuring whether features carry the same structural information. The skip-layer finetuning (Figure 3) provides independent functional evidence that the redundancy is real and exploitable.

- **Comparison against VideoMAR/MarDini as baselines (Neutral Reviewer weakness #3).** Per the rules, missing related work comparisons are not evaluated; we cannot confirm these papers exist or whether settings are comparable.

- **Encoder exposure bias is "structurally identical" to Teacher Forcing mismatch (Harsh Critic, Section 5.3).** The paper explicitly addresses this via the Gaussian context corruption (Eq. 7) and through fine-tuning with high-noise encoder inputs (Section 6.2). The approach is standard in the field (citing prior work [11, 67]). The concern that context-level noise injection differs from input-level noising is legitimate but pedantic given the paper's explicit ablation in Appendix B.3.

- **FAR conditioning mismatch in Table 1.** The harsh critic notes FAR-M-Long conditions on 144→156 while SCD uses 36→264. Both are evaluated in the same table columns, and the paper specifically labels the two evaluation columns separately. This is not a fairness issue—the conditions are clearly delineated.

- **Attention mass aggregation hides individual head behavior (Harsh Critic, Section 4.2 note).** While technically true, per-head analysis is a "nice-to-have" enhancement rather than a critical flaw. The aggregate pattern is still strong evidence, and the finetuning validation (Fig. 5) confirms functional separability at the layer level regardless of individual head behavior.

## Novel Insights

The paper's most genuinely novel observation is that causal temporal reasoning in AR video diffusion models is *functionally separable*—not just structurally sparse—from iterative denoising, and that this separation can be exploited without performance regression. While encoder-decoder hybrid architectures for video generation have appeared before (VideoMAR, MarDini), this paper is the first to ground such a design in a systematic mechanistic analysis of *where* causal reasoning actually occurs in a learned model. The leave-one-out importance analysis as a method for deciding encoder/decoder layer assignment is a transferable technique for architecture adaptation from pretrained bidirectional models. The specific finding that middle-layer features stabilize with >0.95 cosine similarity as early as the first denoising step, and that this stability persists across diverse architectures (DiT, UNet, Self-Forcing), suggests a structural regularity in how causal video diffusion models organize their computation that warrants further theoretical investigation.
