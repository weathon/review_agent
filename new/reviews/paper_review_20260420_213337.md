# Review of ./papers/paper.md

## Summary

This paper proposes a tracklet-level jersey number recognition pipeline for broadcast soccer footage. It replaces the traditional frame-wise OCR approach with a collage-based aggregation method: images from a player tracklet are upscaled with Real-ESRGAN, cropped via open-vocabulary detection (OWLv2), assembled into a grid collage, and fed to a LoRA-fine-tuned Qwen vision-language model for tracklet-level prediction. The authors report moving from 49.7% zero-shot accuracy to 76.41% after two epochs of parameter-efficient fine-tuning, and show reduced recognition-stage latency by collapsing many per-crop OCR calls into a small number of collage forwards.

## Strengths

- **Novel tracklet-level aggregation with a VLM.** Using a collage of multiple noisy crops as input to a vision-language model, rather than traditional confidence-based voting over per-frame OCR outputs, is a conceptually clean departure from prior work (Sections IV-A, VII).
- **Demonstrated fine-tuning efficacy.** The paper shows a large accuracy gain from zero-shot to LoRA-adapted Qwen on a 1,840-sample evaluation set, suggesting the VLM can learn to reason over the collage format (Section V-C).
- **Well-motivated architectural choices.** The move from pose-estimation-based torso cropping to direct number localization with OWLv2 is clearly motivated by the baseline's imprecise crops and multi-stage communication errors (Sections IV-A-2, V-A-4).
- **Candid error analysis.** Section V-D identifies concrete failure modes (single/double-digit confusion, detection failures, super-resolution artifacts, ground-truth noise) that could usefully guide future work.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **No end-to-end baseline accuracy comparison on the same split.** The paper identifies Koshkina & Elder’s 2024 framework as its baseline and describes it in detail, but never reports that baseline’s end-to-end tracklet-level recognition accuracy on the same data used for the proposed method. The central quantitative result (76.41%) is presented in isolation, so the reader cannot determine whether the new pipeline improves upon, matches, or falls short of existing methods. This omission substantially weakens the claim that the approach is a useful alternative (Sections I, V-C).
- **Ambiguous evaluation protocol for the main accuracy claim.** The paper reports 76.41% accuracy after LoRA fine-tuning on a “1,840 sample set,” but never clarifies whether these are tracklets or individual images/collages, nor whether the figure represents validation or held-out test accuracy. The train/validation/test split for Qwen fine-tuning is also not described. Because the entire paper is framed around *tracklet*-level aggregation, the metric must be explicitly tracklet-level test accuracy from a clearly defined split to be interpretable (Abstract, Section V-C).

### Minor
- **Incomplete inference-time comparison limits the efficiency claim.** Table I compares the recognition stage only (PARSeq on 635 crops versus Qwen on 5 collages), explicitly excluding OWLv2 timing and not reflecting the baseline’s legibility filter, which would reduce the number of crops PARSeq processes in practice. While the paper acknowledges this comparison is restricted to the recognition stage, the broader claims in the abstract and introduction about remaining “computation efficient” are consequently less well supported (Section V-D, Table I).
- **Missing quantitative ablations for core preprocessing and detection components.** The paper argues that Real-ESRGAN super-resolution and OWLv2 open-vocabulary detection improve the pipeline, but provides only qualitative visuals (Figures 9–10) and no ablation showing their effect on final end-to-end accuracy. Without this, the positive contribution of these components is assumed rather than measured (Sections IV-A, V-A).
- **Under-specified collage construction and prompting details.** The paper states the model receives “a prompt asking for the most frequent number in the collage” and uses “up to 25 images per tracklet,” but omits the exact prompt template, the strategy for selecting which crops populate the collage (e.g., first 25, random sample, confidence-ranked), and the output parsing logic that maps generated text to a numeric class. These omissions impair reproducibility (Section IV-A).

### Trivial
- **Uncommented training dynamics in Figure 12.** Validation loss begins substantially below training loss and remains lower throughout fine-tuning, which is unusual and may indicate a very small validation set or a methodological irregularity. The paper does not discuss this. (Section V-C, Figure 12)

## Nice-to-Have
- A direct end-to-end runtime comparison that includes super-resolution, detection, and recognition stages for both the proposed pipeline and the baseline.
- Quantitative OWLv2 detection audit (precision/recall of jersey number localization) on the dataset to validate its suitability as a drop-in replacement for the baseline localization stage.
- A “single-best-crop” baseline fed to the same fine-tuned Qwen to empirically demonstrate that multi-frame collage input provides gains beyond selecting one high-quality crop.

## Removed Points
- Criticism that the legibility backbone comparisons “read like exploratory coursework” is partially stylistic; the paper explicitly states these experiments were supporting studies not incorporated into the final system and acknowledges their computational and sample-size limitations.
- Concerns about the 2-tracklet full-pipeline comparison being too small are already transparently disclosed by the authors as a computational constraint; this does not affect the final system.
- The characterization that the related work discussion of legibility backbones is “largely irrelevant” is overstated, as it provides design motivation for moving away from CNN-based legibility filtering.

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions.

## Suggestions
- Run the full baseline pipeline on the exact same Qwen evaluation data split and report end-to-end tracklet-level accuracy. This single addition would ground the entire contribution.
- Explicitly state whether the 76.41% result is tracklet-level test accuracy, describe the split construction, and report the number of tracklets (not just “samples”).
- Add ablations: run the pipeline without Real-ESRGAN and with an alternative localization method (e.g., the baseline pose + cropper) to isolate the impact of each new component on final accuracy.
