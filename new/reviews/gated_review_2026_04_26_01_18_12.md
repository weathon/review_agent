## Summary

This paper systematically investigates gating mechanisms in softmax attention across 30+ variants of 15B MoE and 1.7B dense models. The central finding is that a simple head-specific sigmoid gate after the Scaled Dot-Product Attention (SDPA) output consistently improves performance, training stability, and long-context extrapolation. The paper attributes this effectiveness to two factors—introducing non-linearity into the low-rank $W_V W_O$ mapping and imposing query-dependent sparse gating on SDPA outputs—and shows that this mechanism eliminates the attention sink phenomenon (reducing first-token attention from 46.7% to 4.8%).

## Strengths

- **Comprehensive and well-structured ablation across 30+ gating variants**: Table 1 systematically varies gating position, granularity, head-specificity, multiplicative vs. additive form, and activation function, establishing that SDPA output gating ($G_1$) with head-specific sigmoid is the most effective configuration (0.265 PPL reduction, +2.03 MMLU). The scope of ablation goes beyond prior work like Switch Heads or NSA, which confound gating with routing.

- **Novel finding that query-dependent, head-specific gating eliminates attention sinks**: Table 4 and Figure 6 provide clear evidence that SDPA gating ($G_1$) reduces both massive activations (M-Act: 1053→94) and attention sinks (F-Attn: 0.467→0.048), while value gating ($G_2$) reduces activations but *not* sinks (F-Attn stays at 0.297), and head-shared gating fails to eliminate sinks. This disentanglement is genuinely new and impactful.

- **Honest reporting of negative results**: The paper clearly reports that value gating reduces massive activations without eliminating attention sinks (Table 4, row 5), that $G_3$, $G_4$, $G_5$ provide minimal benefit (Table 1), and that NS-sigmoid underperforms (Table 4, row 7). This transparency strengthens the work.

- **Parameter-controlled dense model experiments**: Table 2 reduces FFN width to match parameter counts, providing clean comparisons. The headwise gating result (Table 1, row 10: 1.6M added params, PPL 5.792 vs. 5.953 for a 201M-parameter baseline) further demonstrates that the gains are not merely from added parameters.

- **Practical significance**: The most effective variant adds <2M parameters (<2% wall-time latency, Section 3.1), making it immediately adoptable, and enables training at higher learning rates (Table 2, rows 10/14 vs. 5/11) and better long-context extrapolation (Table 5: 58.82 vs. 31.65 RULER at 128k).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **The non-linearity and sparsity factors are presented as co-equal "two key factors" (Abstract, Section 1), but the evidence shows sparsity is the dominant driver**: SiLU alone at $G_1$ with no extra parameters gives only a negligible PPL improvement of 0.051 (Table 3, row 6: 5.975 vs. baseline 6.026), despite introducing non-linearity at the same position. RMSNorm at $G_1$ gives a moderate 0.179 improvement (row 5: 5.847). By contrast, the full sigmoid gating with its learned sparse scores achieves 0.265 improvement (row 2: 5.761). The data clearly shows non-linearity is a necessary but minor factor, while sparsity drives the bulk of the gains. The co-equal framing is misleading—the abstract and introduction should reflect that sparsity is the primary mechanism with non-linearity providing a smaller supplementary contribution. This matters because it affects how the community understands and builds upon this work.

- **The NS-sigmoid ablation (Table 4, row 7) confounds sparsity reduction with dynamic range change, weakening the causal sparsity claim**: The NS-sigmoid $\sigma(x) = 0.5 + 0.5 \cdot \text{sigmoid}(x)$ constrains scores to $[0.5, 1.0]$, which simultaneously reduces sparsity AND changes the multiplicative scale the gate can apply to outputs. The paper treats this as evidence that "less sparse gating is worse" (Section 4.2), but the degraded performance (PPL 5.900) could also result from the gate being unable to strongly suppress signals (minimum value 0.5 rather than near 0). The other lines of evidence for sparsity—head-shared gating (same sigmoid, less sparse, PPL 5.801) and input-independent gating (same sigmoid, PPL 5.917)—are cleaner but still correlational: they vary where and how sparsity arises rather than isolating sparsity itself while holding capacity constant. A sparsity-regularized ablation would strengthen this claim.

### Minor

- **The MoE main results (Table 1) lack a direct parameter-matched control where the FFN/expert capacity is reduced to offset the 201M gating parameters**: While the headwise result (row 10, 1.6M params, PPL 5.792) largely mitigates this concern, and the dense experiments (Table 2) do match parameters, the primary result table still compares a 201M-parameter addition against a zero-parameter baseline. The dense protocol (reducing FFN width) should ideally be replicated for MoE experiments that anchor the paper's main claims.

- **The "query-dependency" explanation for why $G_1$ eliminates attention sinks while $G_2$ does not is imprecise**: Section 4.2 attributes the difference to SDPA gating being "query-dependent" (depending on $X_i$, the current token) versus value gating being "key-dependent" (depending on $X_j$, each source token). However, both gates use the same input $X$ after pre-norm (footnote 1); the real distinction is architectural—$G_1$ operates on the *aggregated* attention output while $G_2$ operates on individual value vectors *before* the weighted sum. The paper should explain why this architectural distinction matters for attention sinks rather than appealing to "query-dependency."

- **The MoE experiments train for only 400B out of 3.5T tokens, and it is unclear whether early-checkpoint gains persist at full training**: Table 2 shows that benefits increase with more training data (400B→3.5T), but the Table 1 MoE results are all at 400B tokens. The gains might be larger at full training, but this is unverified for the main experimental setting.

### Trivial
None.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A sparsity-regularized ablation (e.g., a sigmoid gate with a uniformity regularizer on gate values) to cleanly isolate the effect of sparsity from dynamic range and capacity.
- Mechanistic investigation into why $G_1$ (post-aggregation) eliminates sinks while $G_2$ (pre-aggregation) does not, beyond the query/key-dependency framing—potentially probing whether the distinction is about aggregation order.
- Experiments at larger scale (7B+) where training stability issues become more pronounced, to verify that the stability benefits generalize.

## Removed Points

These points are flagged to be removed; treat them with caution.

- **"The paper's claim that $G_5$ doesn't address the low-rank problem is incorrect"**: The Harsh Critic argues that gating after $W_O$ introduces non-linearity before the *downstream* layer norm, so it does "address" low-rank in some sense. However, the paper is specifically arguing about breaking the $W_V W_O$ composition *internally*, and $G_5$ operates after this composition is complete. The paper's statement is narrowly correct. Removed as a misunderstanding of the paper's scope.

- **"Training dynamics analysis missing: what causes the baseline spikes?"** The paper does provide analysis in Section 4.3 and Appendix A.3 showing massive activations from FFN at early layers propagated through pre-norm, and Appendix A.5 discusses clipping experiments. While a deeper gradient analysis would be nice, this is beyond the paper's stated scope. Removed as scope creep.

- **"Missing comparison with Forgetting Transformer"**: The paper references Forgetting Transformer in related work. Demanding a direct experimental comparison is outside the paper's scope—it investigates gating in standard softmax attention, not a comparison with all related attention variants. Removed as scope creep/missing related work concern.

- **"Larger scale experiments needed"**: All experiments are at 1.7B dense or 2.5B active MoE. While larger scale evidence would strengthen the claims, this is a nice-to-have rather than a major weakness, given the consistent results across multiple settings. Moved to Nice-to-Haves.

- **"The abstract overclaims breadth by citing 30 variants, many of which are minor ablations"**: The paper is transparent about what the 30 variants constitute—it's a systematic ablation across well-defined axes. This is normal practice for ablation studies. Removed as a formatting/presentation nitpick.

## Novel Insights

The paper's most notable novel finding is the disentanglement of attention sink elimination from massive activation reduction: value gating ($G_2$) eliminates massive activations (M-Act: 1053→125) *without* eliminating attention sinks (F-Attn: 0.297), while SDPA gating ($G_1$) eliminates both. This demonstrates that massive activations and attention sinks are *partially independent* phenomena, contrary to the common conflation in the literature. The implication that attention sinks require specifically *query-dependent, post-aggregation* intervention (not just activation reduction) is a concrete and actionable insight for the community.

## Suggestions

- Reweight the non-linearity and sparsity claims to reflect the empirical evidence: emphasize that non-linearity is a necessary enabling condition but sparsity is the primary performance driver. A sentence revision in the abstract like "attributing this primarily to query-dependent sparsity, with non-linearity as a contributing factor" would be more accurate.
- Add a brief clarifying note that the NS-sigmoid ablation changes both sparsity and dynamic range, and note that the stronger evidence for sparsity comes from the head-shared and input-independent ablations which preserve the sigmoid's full range.
