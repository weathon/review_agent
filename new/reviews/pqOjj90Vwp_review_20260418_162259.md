## Summary

This paper proposes a framework for characterizing the logical expressivity of arbitrary GNN architectures that can be decomposed into aggregation (AGG) and combination (COM) operations, named General Aggregate-Combine Neural Networks (GACNNs). The central result (Theorem 3) is a grammar construction mapping each AGG step to a counting existential quantifier (∃≥N) and each COM step to Boolean combinations (¬, ∧), producing an "equivalent logic set" for any GACNN. Building on this, Proposition 7 derives explicit logic grammars for eight GNN families (MPNNs, two variants of Subgraph GNNs, NBFNet, Local 2-GNN, 2-FGNN, SEAL, 2-GNN), Theorem 8 gives a general method for homomorphism expressivity, and Corollary 10 establishes an expressivity hierarchy.

---

## Strengths

- **Unified characterization of eight GNN families (Proposition 7):** Unlike prior work (Barceló et al., 2020; Huang et al., 2024) that analyzed individual architectures separately, Proposition 7 derives syntax-level logic grammars for MPNNs, both weak and strong Subgraph GNNs, NBFNet, Local 2-GNN, 2-FGNN, SEAL, and 2-GNN within a single recursive framework. This enables direct, grammar-level comparison of what each model can and cannot express.

- **General homomorphism expressivity construction (Theorem 8):** The paper provides a constructive two-step procedure (remove negation and ∃≥N for N≥2, then map variables to nodes/edges) that yields the homomorphism expressivity of any GACNN. Section 6.2 claims this resolves an open conjecture from Zhang et al. (2024). The worked 2-FGNN example (Section 6.2, Figure 2f) makes the procedure concrete.

- **Readable WL-bound derivation (Proposition 9 / Corollary 10):** The variable-counting argument in Proposition 9 is simple and checkable: reading off the number of variables appearing in the logic grammar directly yields the WL upper bound. The resulting hierarchy (MPNNs = 1-WL < Subgraph GNNs (weak) = NBFNet < ... < FGNN = 3-WL) is a practical analytical tool.

- **Concrete failure mode revealed (Section 6.1):** The analysis showing that 2-GNNs cannot express GrandParent(x,y) := ∃z(Parent(x,z) ∧ Parent(z,y)) provides a syntax-level, interpretable explanation for why 2-GNNs are ill-suited for link prediction—a result that is not easily seen from WL-based analysis.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None that definitively invalidate the framework in principle.

### Major

- **Theorem 3 carries the entire paper but has no proof or proof sketch in the main text, and the appendix is absent from the submission.** The paper ends with: *"Rest of paper (reference and Appendix) is removed."* Theorem 3 is the engine of everything downstream—Proposition 5, Proposition 7, Theorem 8, Corollary 10 all depend on it. The most nontrivial direction (condition 5 of Definition 2: *all GNNs indistinguish u,v iff all φ ∈ Φ classify them the same*) is essentially a completeness theorem for the constructed logic relative to the GNN class. No argument, even a sketch, appears in the main text. For a theory paper, the proof of the central theorem IS the contribution; without it, reviewers cannot assess correctness.

- **The "equivalent logic set" (Definition 2) does not establish true bidirectional equivalence—condition 4 is explicitly a finite approximation—yet the framing in the title, abstract, and section headings presents the result as full equivalence.** The paper itself acknowledges: *"there do exist GNN models which is not captured by any logic formulas, and thus we relax the statement."* Condition 4 only requires, for any N, the existence of φ ∈ Φ that agrees with a given χ on graphs of size ≤ N. This is weaker than pointwise equivalence. The word "equivalent" in "Equivalent Logic Sets" and "complete logical framework" in the title are both misleading relative to what Definition 2 actually guarantees. This is not a trivial framing choice: it affects what Theorem 3 and Proposition 7 actually prove about the relationship between GNNs and logic.

- **Theorem 8's construction discards negation and ∃≥N for N≥2 from Φ but then claims full graph-separation equivalence.** The logic with counting quantifiers and negation is strictly more expressive than its positive existential fragment. The paper states the construction but provides no explanation of why reducing to this fragment is lossless for graph separation. This is a non-trivial claim—it is not obvious that two graphs separated by a negated or high-count formula cannot be separated by a positive-existential formula from Φ—and it is the crux of Theorem 8's correctness.

### Minor

- **Corollary 10 asserts strict inequalities (< rather than ≤) without separating examples in the main text.** Strict inequalities require exhibiting a graph pair where the stronger model succeeds and the weaker fails. The proofs are presumably in the missing appendix, but even a brief pointer to the separating construction would strengthen the main text.

- **The 2-GNN formula in Proposition 7 exhibits variable reuse.** The stated grammar is φ(x,y) := ∃≥N z (φ'(x,z) | ∃≥N z (φ'(z,y))). The variable z is bound twice in nested scope, which is a non-standard syntactic practice for logic formulas and whose interaction with condition 5 of Definition 2 (indistinguishability) is not discussed. The paper explicitly notes in a footnote (Section 6.2) that variable reuse is avoided in the homomorphism construction; the same care is warranted here.

- **The union argument for Proposition 5 is asserted without justification.** Proposition 5 extends Theorem 3 (finite layers) to Φ = ∪_{l=0}^∞ Φ^(l) (arbitrary layers). Specifically, condition 5 of Definition 2 needs to hold for the union, not just for each finite Φ^(l) separately. Verifying this requires an argument that the infinite union does not introduce spurious distinctions. No such argument appears.

- **No clear delineation of which Proposition 7 results are new vs. recoveries of known results.** The MPNN case follows Barceló et al. (2020) by the authors' own admission. Some other entries may also recover known characterizations. Stating explicitly which results are new contributions of this framework vs. reproductions would clarify the paper's added value.

### Trivial

- The limitation section (Section 7) mentions only Graphormer-GD as a model outside the GACNN scope. Models with real-valued attention where weights are computed by a joint function of sender and receiver features, spectral GNNs, or models with positional encodings injected at intermediate layers are also outside the scope and worth a brief mention.

## Novel Insights

The most genuinely novel synthetic observation across all reviews is the gap between what the paper *claims* and what Definition 2 *proves*. The framework is not a "complete logical framework" in the conventional sense: it constructs an equivalent logic set whose discriminating power matches the GNN class (condition 5), but the correspondence between individual GNN models and individual formulas is only local/finite (condition 4). This two-level structure—set-level equivalence plus only local pointwise approximation—is actually a principled and defensible design choice; it mirrors how completeness theorems work in descriptive complexity. The paper would be stronger, not weaker, if it foregrounded this structure rather than using "equivalence" and "complete" in the headline claims. Additionally, Theorem 8's connection between the positive-existential fragment of Φ and homomorphism expressivity is a concrete bridge between two research traditions (logical characterization and subgraph counting) that are typically studied in isolation—a genuinely valuable contribution if the losslessness of the fragment reduction is properly argued.

## Suggestions

1. **Add a proof sketch for the completeness direction of Theorem 3 to the main text.** Even two or three sentences sketching why the constructed Φ satisfies condition 5 of Definition 2 would allow reviewers to assess the central claim.
2. **Revise the title and abstract** to use hedged language consistent with Definition 2. "Towards" in the title is already a hedge; the abstract should similarly note that individual GNN→formula correspondence is finite-approximation rather than exact.
3. **Justify why discarding negation and ∃≥N (N≥2) does not lose graph-separating power** for Theorem 8. This is the key gap in Section 6.2 and likely not hard to argue, but it is currently absent.
4. **Resolve the variable reuse in the 2-GNN formula** in Proposition 7, either by renaming the bound variable or by explicitly noting this is syntactic sugar and defining its semantics.
