## Summary

This paper proposes a tracklet-based jersey number recognition pipeline for soccer broadcast footage that replaces a multi-stage baseline (legibility classifier → pose estimator → PARSeq OCR) with a streamlined architecture using Real-ESRGAN super-resolution, OWLv2 open-vocabulary detection, and collage-based temporal aggregation fed to a LoRA-fine-tuned Qwen3-VL vision-language model. LoRA fine-tuning improves accuracy from 49.7% (zero-shot) to 76.41% on 1,840 samples, and the collage formulation reduces the number of recognition calls compared to per-crop OCR.

## Strengths

- **Novel and well-motivated problem formulation**: Treating jersey number recognition as reasoning over multiple noisy observations (collage of tracklet crops) rather than single-frame OCR is a genuinely interesting conceptual shift (Sections I, IV). This leverages VLM capabilities for aggregation under uncertainty in a way that per-frame pipelines cannot.

- **Substantial accuracy improvement from LoRA fine-tuning**: The 26.7 percentage point gain from 49.7% to 76.41% (Section V-C) on the 1,840-sample test set directly demonstrates that adapting the VLM to the task is effective.

- **Honest and informative error analysis**: Section IV-D catalogs specific failure modes (single/double-digit confusion, OWLv2 detection failures, Real-ESRGAN hallucinated textures, ground-truth label errors). The confusion matrix (Figure 13) confirms the dominant error pattern (e.g., 4↔44). This transparent reporting lends credibility.

- **Effective visual evidence for super-resolution preprocessing**: Figures 9–10 provide side-by-side comparisons showing that Real-ESRGAN 4× upscaling meaningfully improves digit legibility in both individual crops and collage grids, supporting this design choice.

- **Thorough and transparent evaluation of abandoned legibility backbones**: The comparison of five architectures (ResNet34, EfficientNetV2, MobileNet, Swin-T, ConvNeXt) with detailed metrics (Figures 1–7) provides honest design insight, including the finding that better isolated legibility (Swin-T) did not translate to better end-to-end recognition (Section III-A-2).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **No end-to-end accuracy comparison with the baseline [1] on the same test set.** The paper's central claim is that the VLM-based collage approach is a useful alternative to the baseline pipeline (Koshkina & Elder, CVPRW 2024), yet it never reports the baseline's accuracy on the same evaluation set. The abstract's "49.7% to 76.41%" compares zero-shot to fine-tuned within the proposed system — it is not a comparison against any competing method. Without knowing what the baseline achieves end-to-end, the reader cannot determine whether the proposed system represents an improvement, a regression, or a lateral move. This is the single most critical gap, as the paper's stated contribution is offering an alternative to the baseline pipeline.

- **No ablation isolating the collage mechanism's contribution.** The collage-based temporal aggregation is the paper's most distinctive design choice, but no experiment tests whether simpler alternatives would achieve comparable results. For instance, would per-frame Qwen predictions with majority voting work as well? Would feeding only the single highest-confidence crop work nearly as well? Without at least one ablation against a simpler aggregation strategy, the paper cannot establish that the collage formulation itself is what drives performance, as opposed to the VLM fine-tuning or better crop quality from OWLv2 doing most of the work. This affects claims throughout Sections I, IV, and VII.

- **Misleading runtime "faster in practice" claim.** Table I compares only the recognition stage (Qwen on 5 collages vs. PARSeq on 635 crops), and the paper acknowledges it excludes OWLv2 timing. Yet the conclusion still states "the proposed recognition architecture is faster in practice" (Section V-D). This is misleading: OWLv2 processes 635 frames at 0.1–0.5s each (per Section V-A), adding 63–317 seconds — far exceeding the 7.86s recognition-stage savings. The claim should either be withdrawn or properly scoped (e.g., "the recognition stage is faster") rather than presented as a pipeline-level advantage.

### Minor

- **Evaluation protocol is underspecified.** The 1,840-sample test set is not clearly defined: are these tracklets, individual frames, or collages? Train/validation/test split construction is not described. LoRA hyperparameters (rank, alpha, target modules) are not reported. No variance or confidence intervals are provided for the accuracy figure. These gaps make it harder to contextualize the reported 76.41%.

- **Disproportionate space on abandoned components.** Approximately a third of the paper (Section III) is devoted to comparing legibility classifier backbones (ResNet34, EfficientNetV2, MobileNet, Swin-T, ConvNeXt), none of which are used in the final system. Meanwhile, the components actually in the pipeline (OWLv2, collage construction, Qwen fine-tuning) receive comparatively thin evaluation. This structural imbalance weakens the paper's focus.

- **Ground truth noise acknowledged but not quantified.** Section VI-A and Figure 14 identify incorrect labels in the SoccerNet dataset, which the authors recognize inflate the error rate. Even a manual check of a small random sample (e.g., 100 tracklets) to estimate the prevalence of mislabels would help bound the impact on reported accuracy.

- **Inconsistent model naming.** The VLM is referred to as "Qwen OCR model" (Section IV-A-4), "Qwen3-VL" (Section IV intro, Figure 8), and "Qwen-3.5" (Section IV-A-3). These may be the same model, but the inconsistency creates confusion about which specific model and version is used.

### Trivial
- The Results section (V) interleaves "Strengths" and "Limitations" subsections that would be more naturally placed in a Discussion section.

## Novel Insights

The paper's most interesting conceptual contribution — treating jersey number recognition as reasoning under uncertainty over multiple noisy observations rather than single-frame OCR — is genuinely novel for this domain. However, the paper's own evidence subtly cuts against this framing: the dominant failure mode (single/double-digit confusion from partial crops in the collage) is arguably *compounded* by the collage approach (Section IV-D-1: "If most of the crops in the grid show a partial number, the model is biased towards the partial reading"). This suggests the aggregation mechanism may sometimes amplify rather than mitigate the noise it was designed to handle, which is a nuance the paper does not fully engage with despite honestly describing the symptom.

## Suggestions

- Report the baseline [1] accuracy on the same SoccerNet test split, or at minimum cite the baseline's published accuracy on its own test set and discuss comparability.
- Add at least one ablation comparing collage-based aggregation against a simpler alternative (e.g., per-frame Qwen prediction + majority voting) to establish the collage's specific contribution.
- Withdraw or properly scope the "faster in practice" claim in Section V-D to "faster recognition-stage inference," since the full-pipeline runtime including OWLv2 was not measured.
- Specify the 1,840-sample test set composition (tracklets vs. collages), the train/val/test splits, and the LoRA hyperparameters.
