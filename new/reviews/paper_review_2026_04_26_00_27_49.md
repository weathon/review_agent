## Summary

The paper proposes a jersey number recognition pipeline for broadcast soccer footage that replaces a multi-stage baseline (legibility classification → pose estimation → PARSeq OCR) with a streamlined approach using Real-ESRGAN super-resolution, OWLv2 open-vocabulary detection, collage-based aggregation of up to 25 crops per tracklet, and LoRA-fine-tuned Qwen3-VL for recognition. The primary reported result is that LoRA fine-tuning improves accuracy from 49.7% (zero-shot) to 76.41%, alongside a runtime comparison showing fewer recognition calls with the collage approach.

## Strengths

- **Novel problem formulation**: Treating jersey number recognition as reasoning over multiple imperfect observations via a collage input to a VLM, rather than single-image OCR with confidence aggregation, is a conceptually interesting reformulation that could leverage temporal redundancy in tracklets (Abstract, Sections I, IV-A-3).

- **OWLv2 consolidation**: Replacing both the legibility classifier and pose-based torso localization with a single open-vocabulary detection stage is a reasonable simplification that produces tighter, number-focused crops and implicitly filters illegible frames (Sections IV-A-2, V-A-2, V-A-4).

- **Candid failure analysis**: The paper transparently documents key failure modes including single/double-digit confusion, OWLv2 detection failures, ESRGAN hallucination artifacts, and ground truth labeling errors in SoccerNet (Section IV-D).

- **LoRA fine-tuning demonstrates feasibility**: The 49.7% → 76.41% improvement from zero-shot to LoRA-fine-tuned shows that adaptation of a VLM to this task is viable (Section V-C-1).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **No comparison with the baseline on the same evaluation setup**: The paper frames its contribution as an improvement over the Koshkina & Elder (2024) baseline, yet never reports that baseline's accuracy on the same test split. The 76.41% figure is meaningless in isolation — the reader cannot determine whether this represents an improvement, regression, or parity. This is the single most important comparison needed to validate the paper's central claim (Abstract, Section V-C).

- **No ablation study isolating component contributions**: The proposed pipeline simultaneously replaces the legibility classifier, the localization module, the aggregation strategy, the recognizer, and adds super-resolution preprocessing. Without even a single ablation (e.g., collage aggregation vs. per-frame recognition with the same VLM, or the effect of super-resolution on final accuracy), none of the design choices are empirically validated. The 76.41% result cannot be attributed to any specific component (Sections IV-A, V-C).

- **Runtime comparison is misleading**: Table I compares PARSeq inference on all 635 individual crops against Qwen inference on 5 pre-built collages, explicitly excluding OWLv2's runtime. However, the baseline pipeline includes a legibility filter that would discard many crops before they reach PARSeq, so counting all 635 PARSeq calls inflates the baseline cost. Meanwhile, the baseline's legibility+pose stages are replaced by OWLv2, whose cost is excluded. The paper concludes "the proposed recognition architecture is faster in practice" — a claim that goes beyond what this partial comparison supports (Table I, Section V-D).

### Minor

- **Legibility model comparisons are conducted on tiny, unrepresentative samples**: The CNN backbone comparison evaluates on only 5 tracklets; the Swin-T end-to-end comparison uses only 2 tracklets (24.28% of test tracklets). The 0.34% Swin-T deficit on 2 tracklets is statistically meaningless and does not constitute a valid design justification. The ConvNeXt model is evaluated at the image level but never tested end-to-end in the pipeline (Sections III-A-1, III-A-2).

- **The "Qwen-3.5" nomenclature inconsistency**: Section IV-A-3 refers to "Qwen-3.5" while the rest of the paper consistently uses "Qwen3-VL". This causes confusion about which model version was actually used (Section IV-A-3 vs. elsewhere).

- **Ground truth errors undermine evaluation reliability**: The paper identifies incorrect labels in SoccerNet but provides no quantification of their prevalence. If ground truth is unreliable, this affects both training and evaluation, and the claim that true performance exceeds 76.41% is asserted without evidence (Section IV-D-4).

### Trivial

- **25-crop cap is arbitrary and unexplored**: The collage uses up to 25 crops without justification or analysis of how many tracklets exceed this threshold or how accuracy varies with crop count (Sections IV-A-3, V-B-4).

- **LoRA hyperparameters not specified**: The paper mentions using LoRA but does not report rank, alpha, or target modules (Section IV-A-4).

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Per-class accuracy breakdown**: A per-class analysis, especially for single- vs. double-digit jerseys, would clarify whether collage aggregation actually mitigates the partial-visibility problem it claims to address.

- **Ablation of Real-ESRGAN**: The visual improvement from super-resolution is shown (Figures 9-10) but never quantified in terms of final accuracy.

- **Collage vs. majority-vote baseline**: Running the fine-tuned Qwen-VL on individual crops and aggregating via majority vote would directly test whether the collage formulation adds value beyond the VLM itself.

## Novel Insights

The paper's most interesting insight is using open-vocabulary detection (OWLv2) to simultaneously solve localization and legibility filtering — a failed detection implicitly signals illegibility. However, the paper itself acknowledges this introduces new failure modes (OWLv2 is not trained for this domain), and without end-to-end comparison with the baseline, whether this consolidation actually helps remains an open question.

## Suggestions

- **Most critical**: Report the Koshkina & Elder baseline's tracklet-level accuracy on the same 1,840-sample test set, with the same evaluation protocol. This single number would transform the paper from unsupported claims to a testable contribution.
- Run at least one ablation — ideally collage vs. per-frame Qwen recognition — to validate the collage design choice.
- Either make the runtime comparison end-to-end (including OWLv2, Real-ESRGAN) or revise the claim from "faster in practice" to "fewer recognition-stage calls."
- Provide per-class accuracy and a quantitative analysis of how many tracklets exceed the 25-crop cap.
