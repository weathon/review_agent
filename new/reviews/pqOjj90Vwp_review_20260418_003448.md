## Summary

This paper proposes a framework for characterizing the logical expressiveness of GNN architectures via first-order logic with counting quantifiers (FOC). The central contribution is the General Aggregate-Combine Neural Network (GACNN) formalism, which decomposes GNN layers into aggregation (AGG) and combination (COM) primitives. Building on this, the paper provides a recursive construction (Theorem 3, Proposition 5) for the "equivalent logic set" of arbitrary GACNNs, applies this to derive unified logical characterizations for eight prominent GNN families (Proposition 7), establishes a general procedure for determining homomorphism expressivity (Theorem 8), and claims a complete expressivity ordering (Corollary 10). An open conjecture from Zhang et al. (2024) is reportedly resolved.

---

## Strengths

- **Unified framework with broad coverage.** The GACNN decomposition into AGG/COM operations cleanly subsumes MPNNs, Subgraph GNNs (weak and strong), NBFNet, Local 2-GNN, 2-FGNN, SEAL, and 2-GNN within a single formalism. Proposition 7's side-by-side logical characterizations of these eight architectures is a concrete deliverable with immediate value to the community.

- **Resolution of an open problem.** Theorem 8 provides a general construction procedure for homomorphism expressivity given a GACNN's logic set, reportedly resolving the Zhang et al. (2024) conjecture that the homomorphism expressivity of any GACNN-expressible GNN exists and can be constructed systematically.

- **Practical interpretability.** Logical formulas are more interpretable than WL-level comparisons. The paper's analysis of why 2-GNNs fail to express the GrandParent rule while NBFNet can is a well-executed concrete illustration of the framework's explanatory value.

- **Bellman-Ford / Floyd-Warshall analogy.** The identification of single-source (NBFNet, Bellman-Ford-like) versus multi-source (2-FGNN, Floyd-Warshall-like) quantifier structure as the key expressivity axis is a genuine conceptual insight.

- **Extendability.** The recursive grammar in Proposition 5 means new GNN architectures can have their expressivity characterized without ad hoc case analysis — a meaningful advantage over prior work (Zhang et al., 2024; Xu & Zou, 2024) that lacked this generality.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None identified that fully invalidate the paper's claims, though the major issues below substantially weaken confidence in the correctness of the headline result.

### Major

- **Variable reuse in the 2-GNN formula in Proposition 7 (real notational/correctness issue).** The 2-GNN formula is stated as: `φ(x, y) := ∃^≥N z (φ'(x, z) | ∃^≥N z (φ'(z, y)))`. The bound variable `z` is reused across a nested quantifier within the same formula. The paper explicitly avoids variable reuse in Section 6.2/footnote 1 for good reason (it conflates distinct logical roles and affects variable counting). If this is a typo and the inner quantified variable should be `w`, the variable count for 2-GNN changes, potentially affecting the WL upper bound claimed in Corollary 10. This needs resolution because the entire paper's value rests on precise logical notation.

- **Corollary 10's strict inequalities and equality claims are not supported in the main text.** Proposition 9 establishes that a GNN's expressiveness is *bounded by* k-WL iff its logic set uses at most k variables — this is an upper bound. Strict inequalities (e.g., "Subgraph GNN (weak) < Subgraph GNN (strong)") require separation witnesses: an explicit formula provably in the stronger model's logic set but not the weaker's. Similarly, equalities like "FGNN = 3-WL" require both upper and lower bounds (the latter showing 3-WL is fully captured by FGNN), yet only the former is established in the main text. If these proofs reside entirely in an appendix, the main text should at minimum present the separation arguments or cite the specific lemmas. As written, Corollary 10 is asserted without derivation in the main body of a paper whose headline contribution is this expressivity ordering.

### Minor

- **Restrictive scope of homomorphism expressivity construction (Theorem 8).** The construction in Section 6.2 requires removing all formulas containing negation `¬` or counting quantifiers `∃^≥N` for `N ≥ 2` from `Φ`. This reduces the homomorphism expressivity characterization to a monotone existential fragment, excluding the majority of what makes the equivalent logic set rich. The practical implications — which GNN classes lose significant information from this restriction, and what structural patterns depend on negation or N≥2 counting — are not discussed. This limits the utility of Theorem 8 for models whose expressiveness primarily manifests in non-monotone or high-counting patterns.

- **Binary output assumption limits generality beyond what is acknowledged.** The paper restricts GNN outputs to {true, false} and mentions this in passing (Section 2), but does not discuss where the framework's results apply or fail to apply for real-valued GNNs. A brief discussion of what is lost and whether the Boolean properties captured by the framework fully determine the discrimination power of real-valued GNNs would sharpen the paper's claims.

- **Definition 2's finite-graph relaxation is underexplained.** Bullet 4 of Definition 2 relaxes "equivalent" to a finite-graph approximation: for any fixed N and any `χ ∈ X`, there exists `φ ∈ Φ` that agrees with `χ` only on graphs with at most N nodes. The paper introduces this relaxation with minimal discussion ("there does exist GNN models which is not captured by any logic formulas"). It is not stated which of the eight GNN families in Proposition 7 have exact all-graph logical equivalences vs. only finite-approximation guarantees — a question material to interpreting all subsequent results.

### Trivial

- **Abstract overstates scope.** The abstract states the framework handles "arbitrary GNN architectures," but Section 7 explicitly limits this to GNNs built solely from aggregation and combination operations (excluding, e.g., Graphormer-GD). The abstract should match the stated limitation.

## Novel Insights

The paper's clearest original conceptual contribution is identifying that the structural distinction between GNN architectures (NBFNet vs. Local 2-GNN vs. 2-FGNN vs. SEAL) maps directly onto structural properties of their FOC grammar — specifically, whether the dominant quantifier pattern is single-source (one fixed variable propagating), dual-source (two fixed variables), or auxiliary-intermediate-variable (three-variable formulas introducing a "witness" node). The Bellman-Ford / Floyd-Warshall analogy for describing this progression from single-source to multi-source expressiveness provides a genuinely interpretable conceptual lens. The logical derivation of homomorphism expressivity from the grammar via variable-to-node mapping (Theorem 8) is also a noteworthy synthesis connecting the two expressivity metrics.

- **Claims supported:** Partially. Theorem 3 and Proposition 7 are clearly motivated. Corollary 10's strict inequalities and equalities are asserted without visible derivation in the main text; the notational error in the 2-GNN formula introduces correctness uncertainty.
