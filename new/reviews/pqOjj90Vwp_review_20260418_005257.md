## Summary

This paper proposes General Aggregate-Combine Neural Networks (GACNNs), a unified computation paradigm that decomposes arbitrary GNN layers into principled aggregation and combination operations. The central contribution is a systematic method (Theorem 3, Proposition 5) for constructing the *equivalent logic set* — the set of FOC (first-order logic with counting quantifiers) formulas that a GACNN captures — for any model fitting this paradigm. The framework is applied to characterize the logical expressivity of MPNNs, Subgraph GNNs, NBFNet, SEAL, Local 2-GNN, and 2-FGNN; a general procedure for deriving homomorphism expressivity from the logic set is provided (Theorem 8), resolving an open conjecture from Zhang et al. (2024); and a variable-counting shortcut for obtaining WL upper bounds is derived (Proposition 9).

---

## Strengths

- **Genuine unification.** The GACNN framework cleanly subsumes a heterogeneous collection of architectures (node-level, subgraph-based, pair-level for link prediction) under the same AGG/COM primitives, enabling direct cross-model comparison for the first time.
- **First logical characterization of link-prediction GNNs.** Proposition 7 provides unified logic descriptions of NBFNet, SEAL, Local 2-GNN, and 2-FGNN — distinguishing them by their relational structure in a way prior work (Barceló et al. 2020; Huang et al. 2024) did not.
- **Conjecture resolved.** Theorem 8 and the accompanying construction resolve an open conjecture from Zhang et al. (2024): whenever a GNN can be described as a GACNN, its homomorphism expressivity exists and can be derived from the equivalent logic set via a constructive procedure.
- **Elegant WL upper-bound method.** Proposition 9's variable-counting argument makes upper bounds mechanically readable from the logic grammar, which is simpler than constructing WL-simulation arguments or counterexample graphs directly.
- **Interpretability.** The logic-based view makes expressivity gaps between models human-readable (e.g., NBFNet is single-source because its formulas are anchored to one endpoint; SEAL uses a triple-variable $\psi(x,z,y)$ for simultaneous two-endpoint awareness), adding qualitative insight beyond raw WL levels.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Strict inequalities in Corollary 10 are unsubstantiated.** Corollary 10 asserts a strict ordering: `MPNNs = 1-WL < Subgraph GNNs (weak) = NBFNet < Subgraph GNNs (strong) < Local 2-FGNN < FGNN = 3-WL` and `1-WL < SEAL < 4-WL`. The upper bounds (≤) follow from Proposition 9's variable counting. However, each strict inequality (`<`) also requires a *lower bound*: exhibiting a pair of graphs/nodes separated by the stronger class but not the weaker. No such separating examples or citations for them are provided anywhere in the paper. Some relations (MPNN = 1-WL, known Subgraph GNN > 1-WL results) are inherited from prior work, but newly claimed strict separations — such as `Subgraph GNN (strong) < Local 2-FGNN` and `Local 2-FGNN < FGNN` — are asserted without any supporting argument. Without lower bounds, Corollary 10's strict ordering claims are conjectures, not corollaries. This is a non-trivial gap since the paper's stated contribution includes "expressivity comparisons."

### Minor

- **The finite-approximation relaxation in Definition 2 is unresolved.** The 4th condition of Definition 2 only requires that for each GNN χ and each graph size bound N, there exists a formula matching χ on graphs up to N nodes — not necessarily a single formula matching χ on *all* graphs. The paper explicitly acknowledges this relaxation because "there do exist GNN models which are not captured by any logic formulas." However, it never identifies *which* of the specific models analyzed in Proposition 7 require this relaxation versus admitting an exact (uniform) equivalence. Without this, the interpretation of "equivalent logic set" for the models in Proposition 7 is ambiguous. If certain models only have finite-graph approximations, the expressivity comparison ($\Phi_1 \subset \Phi_2$) and the strict orderings may hold only in a weakened sense.

- **Neighborhood indicator expressibility is an implicit assumption in Theorem 3.** Theorem 3 produces formulas of the form $\exists^{\geq N} \mathbf{v} (\varphi_j(\mathbf{v}) \wedge \mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{v} \in \mathcal{N}_i(\mathbf{u})})$. For these to be FOC formulas, the neighborhood indicator $\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{v} \in \mathcal{N}_i(\mathbf{u})}$ must itself be FOC-expressible. The paper makes this assumption explicit only in Section 6.2 ("We assume that the concept of neighbors in GACNNs is described by composition of edges"), but does not state it as a precondition of Theorem 3. For models where neighborhood is defined by non-trivial structural properties (e.g., shared ancestors, distance bounds), this assumption may fail, and the theorem's output would fall outside FOC. The condition should be stated as an explicit precondition of Theorem 3 and verified for each model in Proposition 7.

- **Convergence of the infinite union in Proposition 5 is asserted without argument.** Proposition 5 defines $\Phi = \bigcup_{l=0}^{\infty} \Phi^{(l)}$ as the equivalent logic set for unbounded-depth GACNNs. The claim that this union forms a well-defined fixed point equivalent logic set requires a monotonicity (or at least consistency) argument: specifically, that the discriminating power of $\Phi^{(l)}$ is monotonically non-decreasing in $l$ and that the infinite union inherits the equivalence properties of Definition 2. The paper asserts the result follows "directly" without proof or sketch. While likely true, this is a non-trivial claim for a theory paper.

- **Scope limitation is minimized in the abstract and introduction.** The abstract promises "a general method for determining the homomorphism expressivity of *arbitrary* GNN models" and the paper is titled "Towards a Complete Logical Framework." However, the framework applies only to GACNNs. Graph Transformers, spectral GNNs, and architectures with positional encodings or global structure injection fall outside scope. The limitations section names only Graphormer-GD. The scope should be stated more precisely in the abstract/introduction to avoid overclaiming.

### Trivial

- **"FGNN = 3-WL" vs. "2-FGNN" notation inconsistency.** Proposition 7 consistently uses "2-FGNN" as the model name, but Corollary 10 writes "FGNN = 3-WL" without the "2-" prefix. This is likely a typo but is confusing since "FGNN" without a prefix appears nowhere else in the main body.

- **Variable scoping issue in 2-GNN formula.** The 2-GNN formula in Proposition 7 reads: $\varphi(x,y) := \exists^{\geq N} z (\varphi'(x,z) \mid \exists^{\geq N} z (\varphi'(z,y)))$. Both existential quantifiers bind the same variable $z$, which is a scoping ambiguity. The inner $z$ should be renamed (e.g., $z'$) for formal correctness.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Empirical case study.** While this is a theory paper, even a small synthetic experiment verifying that a learned MPNN aligns with/against the predicted logical pattern would substantiate the paper's interpretability claims.
- **Characterize tightness of variable-counting bounds.** Proposition 9 gives upper bounds on WL-level via variable counting. Providing examples where the bound is tight versus loose (and why) would help practitioners calibrate expectations when using this tool.
- **Discuss extension to attention-based architectures.** Even a negative result showing soft attention cannot be captured in FOC would sharpen the paper's scope statement and be valuable to the community.
- **Clarify what is lost in the homomorphism construction.** Section 6.2 removes all formulas with negation or $\exists^{\geq 2}$ before constructing homomorphism subgraphs. A brief analysis of what distinguishing power is sacrificed by this restriction would help readers understand the limitations of the resulting characterization.

The claim is that the *discriminating power* of these logic sets is the same — a valid comparison. This is not a flaw.

## Novel Insights

The most genuinely novel synthesis across reviewers: the paper's framework reveals that the expressivity gap between link-prediction GNN architectures (NBFNet, SEAL, Local 2-GNN, 2-FGNN) can be precisely understood as a *structural difference in how logic variables are quantified* — from single-source anchoring (NBFNet), to two-source separation (Local 2-GNN), to fully joint multi-source (2-FGNN), to three-variable simultaneous source-path-target encoding (SEAL). This logical lens provides a more interpretable and principled account of these architectures' relative power than WL-hierarchy comparisons alone, and the uniform treatment within a single vocabulary is a conceptual step forward. The resolution of Zhang et al. (2024)'s conjecture about homomorphism expressivity existence is an additional concrete deliverable that anchors the theoretical framework in a known open problem.
