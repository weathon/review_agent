## Critical Issues

- **Structural**: The evaluation framework is unsound and invalidates the primary claims. The paper relies on automatic scoring by an MLLM (llama-4-maverick-17b) without establishing its reliability for assessing negative-prompt adherence, positive-prompt adherence, or image quality. The human validation (Table 3) is limited to only 10 prompts, and the scores deviate substantially from the automatic ones (e.g., human quality scores are 0.823 for VSF Quality vs. automatic 0.986). This discrepancy, coupled with the absence of a validated benchmark, means the reported superiority over baselines (NASA, NAG, CFG) is not credible. (Sections 4.2, 5, Table 2, Table 3)

- **Evidential**: The comparison to prior work is incomplete. For non‑few‑step models, the paper only compares to vanilla CFG, ignoring more recent and effective dynamic negative‑guidance methods (e.g., Schramowski et al. 2023, Koulischer et al. 2025) that are directly relevant. The claim that VSF “significantly improves negative prompt adherence … compared to prior methods” is therefore not fully supported. (Sections 2.2, 5, Table 2)

- **Methodological gap**: The proposed method is built on several heuristics (duplication of negative embeddings, attention masking, bias addition) without a clear theoretical derivation. While the ablation study (Figure 5 Right) suggests these components are important, the paper does not provide a principled justification for the design choices, making it difficult to assess whether the approach generalizes beyond the tested models. (Sections 3.1, 3.2, 6.3)

## Section-by-Section Notes

- **Abstract & Introduction**: Claims of “significantly improves negative prompt adherence” are not backed by a reliable evaluation. The motivation—that few‑step models fail with CFG—is clear, but the proposed solution’s advantages are overstated given the evaluation issues.

- **Dataset (Section 4.1)**: NegGenBench is generated by ChatGPT‑o3 with 200 prompt pairs. No details are provided about the prompts’ diversity, difficulty, or how they were filtered. Using the same dataset to fine‑tune the evaluation model (Qwen‑2.5‑VL) risks circular evaluation.

- **Metrics (Section 4.2)**: The choice of LLaMA as a judge is not justified; no correlation analysis with human judgments is provided for the full dataset. The paper dismisses existing metrics (HPSv2, ImageReward) for being unsuitable for “unusual objects,” but does not show that the chosen MLLM is better for this task.

- **Results (Section 5)**: The external baseline comparison (Table 1) includes both open‑ and closed‑weight models, but runtime comparisons are unfair because different hardware/platforms are likely used. The claim that VSF outperforms all open‑source methods is based on the same questionable automatic scores.

- **Trade‑off curves (Section 6.1)**: While the curves suggest VSF has a better trade‑off, they are derived from the same unreliable automatic evaluation. Without human validation on the full sweep, the curves may not reflect real performance.

- **Attention maps (Section 6.2)**: The visualization is qualitative and only shows one example. No quantitative analysis links attention-map behavior to final negative‑prompt adherence.

- **Limitations**: The paper does not discuss the failure modes of VSF or its sensitivity to hyperparameters (α, β). The method’s applicability to other model architectures (e.g., pure transformer‑based generators) is not explored.

## Strengths

- The core idea of flipping attention values for negative prompts is simple and novel, extending the “noise‑canceling” analogy to attention mechanisms.
- The paper provides a comprehensive set of experiments, including comparisons to several baselines (NASA, NAG, CFG) and external models, as well as trade‑off curves and an ablation study.
- The method is implemented for both cross‑attention and MMDiT‑style models (e.g., SD 3.5 Turbo), demonstrating flexibility.

## Overall Assessment

The paper proposes an interesting heuristic for negative guidance in few‑step generation models, but the evaluation methodology fundamentally undermines its claims. The reliance on an unvalidated MLLM judge, the small and uncharacterized dataset, and the lack of comparison to recent dynamic negative‑guidance methods make it impossible to assess whether VSF truly advances the state of the art. While the idea is promising, the evidence presented does not meet the bar for ICLR acceptance. Substantial revisions—including a robust human evaluation on a larger scale, comparison to modern dynamic guidance techniques, and a more principled ablation—would be required for the contribution to be credible.

## Weaknesses

1. **Limited Dataset Scale and Narrow Validation**: The NegGenBench dataset contains only 200 prompts (400 images with 2 seeds), and human evaluation is conducted on only 10 prompts with 2 seeds (20 images total). This is critically small for drawing general conclusions. As noted in the MM-R3 benchmark review: *"In a benchmark with over 87.000 examples, visually inspecting only 100 random question-rephrasing pairs and 100 images (about 0.002% of the data) is a poor quality metric."* Similarly here, human validation represents <5% of the evaluation set, raising concerns about statistical significance and generalizability.

2. **Unfair Baseline Comparisons**: The paper re-implements NASA from scratch (original code unavailable), re-tunes NAG's hyperparameters specifically for "stronger negation following," and provides two VSF variants with different hyperparameter configurations. The FqWtMGw8tt benchmark review identified this issue: *"A little unfair comparison with baselines. This method unfreezes part of the image encode but other methods only finetune the classification head. This could lead to unfair comparison."* The VSF results are specifically optimized for negative prompt following while NAG's original hyperparameters were designed for quality, yet they are compared directly without ensuring equal tuning effort across baselines.

3. **Evaluation Metric Reliability Concerns**: Heavy reliance on a single MLLM judge (LLaMA-4-maverick-17b) rather than established evaluation benchmarks. The VisCE2 review critiques similar approaches: *"While this paper focuses on reference-free evaluation, it is also recommended to report the results of VisCE2 when the reference captions are provided. The novelty of this paper is limited. The proposed evaluation method consists two stages... while the combination of these two stages may be new, it may not meet the innovation standards."* Furthermore, fine-tuning a Qwen-2.5-VL model on data generated by VSF, NASA, and NAG introduces circularity—the evaluation model is trained on the same methods being evaluated.

4. **Inconsistent and Limited Ablation Study**: Ablation studies are performed on only the first 100 prompts with a single seed, while main results use 200 prompts with 2 seeds. The paper acknowledges implementation discrepancies (footnote 2): *"results here might be negligibly different than our code due to implementation differences."* The AdaCache review notes similar concerns: *"Ablation studies are performed on only 32 videos. Considering that AdaCache is not deterministic, the results may have low statistical significance."* This suggests the ablation conclusions may not be statistically robust.

5. **Insufficient Generalization Analysis**: The method is primarily evaluated on two MMDiT-based models (SD 3.5 Turbo, Flux Schnell). Cross-attention-based models are mentioned (Wan) but not thoroughly evaluated. Video generation capability is mentioned in the abstract but relegated to appendix with limited analysis. The KnowData review identifies this weakness: *"The proposed method is supposedly generalizable, yet the paper only tests its effectiveness on one multimodal model... 6. **Lack of Limitations Discussion**: The paper provides no explicit limitations section acknowledging the narrow dataset scope, small human evaluation set, or architectural constraints. The AdaCache review emphasizes this: *"The paper lacks Limitation section. It would be interesting to see if there are videos on which AdaCache performs worse than its deterministic competitors."* Understanding failure cases and boundary conditions would strengthen the contribution.

## Balanced Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Value Sign Flip (VSF), a method for incorporating negative prompt guidance in few-step (1-8 step) diffusion and flow-matching image generation models. VSF works by flipping the sign of attention values associated with negative prompt tokens, combined with token duplication and masking strategies to avoid interference in transformer architectures like MMDiT. The authors also contribute a challenging dataset, NegGenBench, and demonstrate through extensive experiments that VSF outperforms existing negative guidance methods (NASA, NAG) and even CFG in non-few-step models in terms of negative prompt adherence, while maintaining competitive image quality and positive prompt fidelity.

### Strengths
1. **Methodological Simplicity and Efficiency**: VSF introduces a conceptually straightforward and computationally lightweight modification to the attention mechanism. It adds minimal overhead compared to dual-forward-pass approaches like CFG and integrates seamlessly with modern architectures, enabling fast generation (e.g., <3 seconds).
2. **Comprehensive and Rigorous Evaluation**: The paper validates VSF on a newly created, challenging dataset (NegGenBench) featuring complex prompt pairs where the negative prompt often describes an essential component. Experiments include quantitative comparisons against strong baselines (NASA, NAG, CFG), ablation studies, hyperparameter trade-off analyses, attention visualizations, and tests on style avoidance and abstract art generation. The results consistently show VSF's superior negative adherence (e.g., 0.545 negative score for VSF Strong vs. 0.380 for NASA) with better quality preservation.
3. **Practical Impact and Reproducibility**: The authors release code, a ComfyUI node, and the dataset, ensuring easy adoption and reproducibility. The method's effectiveness across multiple state-of-the-art models (Stable Diffusion 3.5 Turbo, Flux Schnell) and its ability to enable creative applications (style avoidance, abstract art) highlight its practical utility.

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited Human Evaluation and Potential MLLM Bias**: The primary quantitative evaluation relies on an MLLM (LLaMA) as an automated judge. While human evaluation on a subset (10 prompts) aligns with trends, the scale is small. MLLMs may have their own biases in understanding negation and image quality, which could affect score reliability. A larger human study would strengthen the claims.
2. **Architectural Complexity for MMDiT Models**: While the core idea for cross-attention models is elegant, the adaptation to MMDiT-style models requires careful token duplication, masking, and bias adjustments. This adds implementation complexity and could be a barrier for some users. The paper could better justify these design choices and discuss potential failure modes.
3. **Insufficient Analysis of Failure Cases and Limitations**: The paper showcases successful examples but does not systematically analyze where VSF might fail (e.g., extremely complex negations, conflicting prompts). A discussion of limitations, such as handling multiple negative concepts or interactions with spatial control, would provide a more balanced view of the method's applicability.

### Novelty & Significance
The work presents a novel and intuitive mechanism for negative guidance by flipping attention values, which is distinct from prior work that operates on attention outputs or noise predictions. The adaptive, token-level modulation emerges naturally from the sign-flip operation without explicit weight calculation. This approach is particularly significant for the growing class of few-step generative models where CFG is ineffective. The introduced dataset also provides a valuable benchmark for future research on negation in image generation. Given ICLR's emphasis on novel, impactful methods, VSF's simplicity, strong performance, and practical relevance make it a compelling contribution.

### Suggestions for Improvement
1. **Expand Human Evaluation**: Conduct a more extensive human evaluation (e.g., 50-100 images) to validate the automatic scores and provide stronger evidence for the claimed advantages in negative adherence and quality trade-offs.
2. **Deepen the Analysis of the Method's Mechanics**: Provide more intuition and analysis on why flipping the value (but not the key) works effectively. Include additional visualizations or metrics to show how the attention to negative tokens correlates with suppression in the final image across different layers and time steps.
3. **Discuss Limitations and Broader Applicability**: Explicitly outline scenarios where VSF may underperform (e.g., long, compositional negative prompts, video generation over many frames) and suggest potential extensions or mitigations. Also, discuss the method's compatibility with other forms of control (e.g., depth maps, canny edges) to guide users in integrated applications.

## How to Improve This Paper

### Missing Experiments (top 3-5 only)
1. **Evaluation on established negation/editing benchmarks.** The paper only uses the proposed NegGenBench. Without testing on known datasets (e.g., from Park et al. 2025 or standard editing tasks), the claim of general effectiveness is unsubstantiated and risks overfitting to a custom, potentially biased set.
2. **Comparison to a broader set of recent few-step guidance methods.** Baselines are limited to NASA and NAG. The paper should compare against other contemporary few-step negative guidance techniques (e.g., methods from Koulischer et al. 2025 or Schramowski et al. 2023 adapted for few-step) to properly situate its contribution.
3. **Systematic runtime and memory overhead analysis.** Claims of "small computational overhead" and "extremely fast" generation are supported only by a single ∼runtime column. A controlled analysis comparing FLOPs, memory usage, and latency against NASA, NAG, and a two-pass CFG baseline is necessary to validate the efficiency claim.
4. **Cross-architecture validation beyond SD3.5 Turbo and Wan.** The method is claimed to work with MMDiT and cross-attention models. Evidence is shown only for SD3.5 Turbo (MMDiT) and presumably Wan. It must be tested on other key few-step models (e.g., Flux Schnell, SDXL Lightning) to prove generality.

### Deeper Analysis Needed (top 3-5 only)
1. **Failure mode analysis and limitations.** The paper lacks a systematic study of when and why VSF fails (e.g., specific prompt types, scales). Without this, the operational boundaries and practical reliability of the method are unknown.
2. **Ablation study quantifying the contribution of each mechanism.** The ablation (Figure 5 Right) is a trade-off plot without isolating the impact of duplication, masking, and bias. A component-wise ablation with quantitative metrics is needed to justify the architectural complexity.
3. **Analysis linking the adaptive mechanism to improved performance.** The core claim is dynamic, token-level adaptation. The paper should analyze how the flipped value attention weights (W) correlate with suppression success across layers and timesteps, beyond a single attention map visualization.
4. **Investigation of impact on positive prompt adherence for non-essential negations.** The trade-off curve shows positive score drop. A detailed analysis is needed on *which aspects* of the positive prompt are degraded when avoiding the negative concept, to understand the specificity of the steering.

### Visualizations & Case Studies
1. **Visualizations of failure cases from the trade-off curve sweep.** The curve shows quality/positive score degradation at high negative scores. Showing the corresponding degraded images would clarify the practical limits and artifacts introduced.
2. **Attention map visualizations across multiple layers, timesteps, and challenging prompts.** A single example (Figure 6 in Appendix) is insufficient. Grid visualizations showing how negative prompt attention evolves are critical to validate the proposed "noise-canceling" dynamic.
3. **Side-by-side qualitative comparisons on the same prompts for all major baselines.** Figure 1 and 4 show only VSF results. Direct visual comparisons with NAG, NASA, and CFG on the same challenging NegGenBench prompts are necessary to judge relative performance.

### Obvious Next Steps
1. **Application to video generation models as claimed.** The abstract and introduction mention video generation, but no experiments are presented. Validating the method on a few-step video model (e.g., Wan, CausVid) is an essential extension that should have been included.
2. **Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis.** The method has scale (α) and bias (β) parameters. A systematic study of their impact on different model architectures and prompt types is missing but needed for practical use.
3. **Integration and test with popular sampling schedules.** The experiments use a fixed few-step schedule. Testing compatibility with different samplers (e.g., DPM-Solver) common in distilled models would strengthen the usability claim.

### Other stuff you can add
1. **Stronger validation of the MLLM-based evaluation metric.** The human evaluation is limited to 10 prompts. To trust the automatic scores, a larger-scale human assessment (50-100 prompts) correlating with MLLM scores is required, especially since the MLLM (llama-4-maverick) is not a standard evaluator for this task.
2. **Clarification on the baseline implementations.** The paper states NASA was reimplemented because the original code was not available. Details on the reimplementation and verification of its correctness (e.g., against descriptions in the NASA paper) are needed to ensure a fair comparison.
3. **Discussion of potential negative side-effects.** The method actively suppresses features; an analysis of whether this introduces unintended distortions or biases in the *remaining* content (beyond overall quality score) is absent but important for deployment.

## Summary
This paper introduces Value Sign Flip (VSF), a method for negative prompt guidance in few-step (1-8 step) diffusion and flow-matching models. VSF dynamically suppresses unwanted content by flipping the sign of attention values associated with negative prompt tokens, with modifications for MMDiT architectures. The authors also contribute a challenging dataset, NegGenBench, and show through experiments that VSF outperforms existing few-step methods (NASA, NAG) and traditional CFG in negative prompt adherence while maintaining competitive image quality.

## Strengths
- **Novel and Efficient Core Mechanism**: The idea of flipping attention values for negative prompts is simple, intuitive, and adds minimal computational overhead. The "noise-canceling" analogy is apt, and the method integrates efficiently with modern architectures (MMDiT, cross-attention) enabling fast generation.
- **Comprehensive Empirical Evaluation**: The paper provides extensive quantitative comparisons against strong, relevant baselines (NASA, NAG, CFG) on a newly created, challenging dataset. It includes informative trade-off curves (Fig. 5), an ablation study, attention visualizations, and demonstrates practical applications like style avoidance.
- **Practical Impact and Reproducibility**: The release of code, a ComfyUI node, and the dataset facilitates adoption and verification. The method's effectiveness on state-of-the-art models like Stable Diffusion 3.5 Turbo and Flux Schnell underscores its immediate utility.

## Weaknesses
### Major:
- **Evaluation Relies Heavily on Unverified Automatic Metrics**: The primary quantitative results (Table 2) are based on scores from a single MLLM judge (LLaMA-4-maverick). While a human evaluation on a 10-prompt subset (Table 3) shows correlating trends, the scale is critically small (20 images). Without a larger-scale human study or validation against established benchmarks, the reliability of the reported superiority (e.g., 0.545 vs. 0.380 negative score) is not firmly established. This undermines confidence in the core claim of significant improvement.
- **Limited and Potentially Unfair Baseline Tuning**: The paper re-implements NASA (due to unavailable original code) and creates a "NAG Strong" variant by re-tuning its hyperparameters for stronger negation. Meanwhile, it presents two VSF variants ("Quality" and "Strong") tuned for different trade-offs. This asymmetric tuning effort—optimizing VSF's hyperparameters while using default or re-purposed settings for baselines—makes direct comparison unclear. A fair comparison requires equal tuning exploration for all methods.
- **Insufficient Analysis of Generalization and Failure Modes**: The method is evaluated primarily on SD3.5 Turbo (MMDiT) and mentioned for Wan (cross-attention). Its performance on other key few-step models (e.g., Flux Schnell, SDXL Lightning) is not substantiated with full results, limiting claims of broad applicability. Furthermore, the paper lacks a systematic discussion of limitations or failure cases (e.g., complex multi-concept negations, prompt conflicts), leaving the operational boundaries undefined.

### Minor:
- **Architectural Complexity for MMDiT**: The adaptation for MMDiT models requires non-trivial engineering (token duplication, specific masking, bias addition). While justified, this complexity is a practical barrier and the ablation study does not fully isolate the necessity of each component quantitatively.
- **Video Generation Claim is Under-Supported**: The abstract and introduction mention video generation, but no experiments or results on video models are presented in the main paper, making this claim aspirational rather than demonstrated.

### Trivial:
- The paper could benefit from a more detailed pseudocode or algorithm box in the main text for the MMDiT adaptation.

## Nice-to-Haves
- A component-wise ablation study quantifying the individual contribution of duplication, masking, and bias in the MMDiT adaptation.
- Visual examples of failure cases or degraded images from the high-negative-score end of the trade-off curve.
- A brief discussion on integrating VSF with other control mechanisms (e.g., depth maps, canny edges).
