## Summary
This paper introduces Social-R1, a reinforcement learning framework aimed at enhancing social reasoning in LLMs. The core contributions are a new challenging benchmark, ToMBench-Hard, and a social thinking reward model (TRM) designed to provide trajectory-level supervision based on social information processing theory. The framework combines outcome and thinking rewards. Experiments show that Social-R1, applied to 4B-8B parameter models, achieves significant improvements on multiple social reasoning benchmarks, with the 4B model reportedly surpassing LLaMA3-70B across all evaluations.

## Strengths
- **Addresses a Clear and Important Gap:** The paper convincingly argues that social intelligence is under-explored in RLVF and that existing benchmarks are simplistic. The creation of ToMBench-Hard, a rigorously constructed, multi-dimensional benchmark designed to thwart superficial shortcuts, is a solid and potentially valuable contribution that meets a genuine need in the field.
- **Strong and Comprehensive Empirical Results:** The paper demonstrates consistent and significant performance gains across six diverse social reasoning benchmarks (both in-domain and out-of-domain). The result that a 4B-parameter model can outperform a 70B model is striking and well-documented, highlighting the potential efficiency of targeted RL training.
- **Effective Ablation Studies:** The ablation study in Section 5 effectively isolates the contributions of the key components (hard dataset vs. thinking reward), providing clear evidence that both the ToMBench-Hard dataset and the trained thinking reward model are necessary for optimal and robust performance across tasks.

## Weaknesses
### Major:
- **The core contribution of the thinking reward model (TRM) is not convincingly validated.** The paper's central claim is that integrating "process-level thinking rewards" is key to fostering human-like social intelligence. However, the experimental evidence does not robustly support this. The ablation study (Table 3) shows that the variant **Social-R1 w/o TRM** (using only outcome rewards on ToMBench-Hard) achieves dramatic gains, often matching or exceeding the full Social-R1 model on key benchmarks (e.g., SimpleToM 97.18% vs 93.65% for 4B). The marginal, inconsistent gains from adding the TRM undermine the claim that it is a critical, novel component. The primary driver of success appears to be RL on a high-quality, hard dataset.
- **Lack of analysis and validation for the Social Thinking Reward Model (TRM).** The TRM is trained on trajectories scored by GPT-5. There is no validation that these scores correlate with human judgments of social reasoning quality. Furthermore, the paper provides no qualitative analysis or examples showing *what* the TRM learns to reward or penalize (e.g., high vs. low-scoring trajectories). Without this, its claimed role as a proxy for "human-like mental processes" or its grounding in Social Information Processing theory remains an unsubstantiated assertion. The poor performance of the GPT-4o-as-TRM variant further complicates interpretation without deeper analysis.
- **Insufficient investigation of generalization and overfitting concerns.** The most dramatic gains are on the in-domain ToMBench-Hard test set (+67%). While out-of-domain gains are positive, they are more modest (e.g., +1.4% on SocialIQA). The paper does not adequately address whether the method is optimizing for the specific adversarial style of ToMBench-Hard. A stronger test would involve evaluation on social reasoning tasks with fundamentally different formats (e.g., open-ended dialogue, explanation generation) or on adversarial sets designed to test for shortcut exploitation on *other* benchmarks.

### Minor:
- **The comparison to LLaMA3-70B, while striking, may be misleading as a primary benchmark.** LLaMA3-70B's performance on some tasks (e.g., SocialIQA 46.21%, MotiveBench 56.33%) is anomalously low compared to other models of similar scale, raising questions about its suitability as a strong baseline. Comparisons against a broader suite of state-of-the-art open-source models (e.g., Command R+, Llama 3.1 405B) would provide a more rigorous and convincing performance assessment.
- **Missing critical baseline: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on ToMBench-Hard.** The paper lacks a comparison to a model trained via standard SFT on the same ToMBench-Hard training data. This baseline is essential to disentangle the contribution of the RL framework from the simple effect of being trained on higher-quality, harder data.
- **Lack of statistical significance reporting.** Performance metrics are presented as single-point averages. Given the relatively small size of some test sets (e.g., 200 for ToMBench-Hard), improvements of a few percentage points could be due to variance. Reporting confidence intervals or statistical tests would strengthen the empirical claims.

### Trivial:
- **The training curves in Figure 2 are poorly explained.** The y-axis is unlabeled, and the values lack context (e.g., scale, normalization), making them difficult to interpret in support of the training dynamics.

## Nice-to-Haves
- A qualitative analysis of what the TRM rewards, with concrete examples of high and low-scoring reasoning trajectories.
- An experiment evaluating the model on open-ended, generative social tasks (e.g., explaining mental states in dialogue) to test if improvements extend beyond multiple-choice format.
- A direct comparison with the ToM-RL method (Lu et al., 2025b) trained and evaluated under comparable settings to better position the advancement.

## Removed Points
*These points are flagged to be removed, treat them with caution.*

- **Weakness: "The benchmark comparison to ToM-RL in Table 1 is an unfair apples-to-oranges comparison."** *Removed. The paper explicitly states ToM-RL only covers the Belief dimension, and the comparison is presented to illustrate the relative difficulty and different focus of the benchmarks, not as a direct, like-for-like evaluation. The authors are transparent about this asymmetry.*
- **Weakness: "The human performance baseline lacks detail on annotator count and inter-annotator agreement."** *Removed. While more detail is nice to have, the paper states samples were "cross-checked independently by three annotators to ensure quality and consistency" (Section 3.1). Demanding specific inter-annotator metrics is a methodological detail not required for the core claims.*
- **Weakness: "The claim that CoT 'fails to enhance' social reasoning is not strongly supported."** *Removed. The paper's observation is based on the data in its Table 1, which shows CoT does not provide consistent gains and sometimes hurts performance on their benchmark. This is a factual reporting of their results, not an overgeneralization.*
- **Weakness: "The data collection for the TRM is convoluted and introduces model bias."** *Removed. The method (using GPT-5 to score trajectories) is a valid and common approach for reward model training. The concern about "opaque model bias" is a generic critique of the paradigm, not a specific flaw in this work.*
- **Weakness: "The 'out-of-domain' benchmarks are not truly out-of-domain."** *Removed. This is a subjective critique of benchmark selection. The paper uses standard, accepted benchmarks for social reasoning (SocialIQA, EmoBench, etc.). Demanding they test on "orthogonal social capabilities" is scope creep.*
- **Weakness: "The percentage improvements are exaggerated due to low baselines."** *Removed. Reporting relative improvement from a low baseline is standard practice and not misleading if the absolute numbers are also provided (which they are).*
- **Weakness: "The ablation Social-R1 w/o Hard&TRM may confound dataset difficulty with size."** *Removed. The paper states it uses "training data from ToM-RL" but does not specify size. This is a minor missing detail; the primary conclusion from the ablation—that ToMBench-Hard data is crucial—remains valid even if size is a confounding factor.*
- **Weakness: "Limited validation of benchmark annotation quality."** *Removed. The paper describes a rigorous annotation process with three independent annotators. The cited review about inspecting 0.002% of data is about a different, much larger benchmark and is not applicable here.*
- **Weakness: "Potential overestimation due to synthetic data in SocialReward-3k."** *Removed. The data uses LLM generations refined by humans, a standard and accepted practice for creating preference datasets. The cited critique about "synthetic evaluation sets" does not apply to a training dataset.*
- **Weakness: "Limited model variety (only Qwen3)."** *Removed. Using a single model family is a standard and acceptable experimental choice for a method paper. The cited review about "Gemma" is not relevant.*
- **Weakness: "Lack of comparison with alternative RL approaches."** *Removed. The paper compares against outcome-only rewards and an untrained/general-purpose RM. Demanding comparisons to every possible RL formulation is excessive scope creep.*

## Suggestions
- Reframe the paper to more accurately reflect the primary contribution: the demonstration that **outcome-based RL on a high-quality, challenging social reasoning dataset (ToMBench-Hard) yields dramatic performance gains**. The thinking reward model should be presented as a promising but preliminary extension that requires further validation.
- Conduct the missing **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) baseline** on ToMBench-Hard to cleanly separate the effect of the data from the RL process.
- Perform a **qualitative analysis** of the TRM's scoring behavior. Show examples of reasoning trajectories it assigns high/low rewards to, explicitly linking them to the claimed SIP dimensions (cue perception, logical consistency).
- Strengthen the baseline comparisons by including a **broader set of state-of-the-art open-source models** beyond LLaMA3-70B to contextualize the performance claims.
- Add a brief discussion on the **limitations of the current evaluation**, acknowledging that gains are most pronounced on the in-domain benchmark and that testing on generative or interactive social tasks remains future work.

## Evaluation
- **Novelty:** Moderate. The ToMBench-Hard benchmark is a clear novel contribution. The application of outcome-RL to social reasoning is novel in this domain. The integration of a theory-inspired thinking reward is a novel idea, but its empirical contribution is not firmly established.
- **Technical Soundness:** Good. The methodology is clearly described, the experiments are comprehensive, and the ablation studies are well-designed. The primary weakness is the insufficient validation of the TRM's mechanism.
- **Empirical Support:** Strong but with a critical caveat. The results are extensive and show clear improvements. However, the evidence does not convincingly support the paper's central claim about the necessity or unique benefit of the thinking reward model.
- **Significance:** High. The demonstration that targeted RL on a good dataset enables a small model to outperform much larger models on social reasoning tasks is an important and impactful finding for the field.
- **Clarity:** Very Good. The paper is well-structured and clearly written.
