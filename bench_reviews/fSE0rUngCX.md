## Summary
This paper introduces the novel task of Multimodal Policy Internalization (MPI), which aims to embed complex, reasoning-intensive multimodal policies (containing text and visual instructions) into model parameters, eliminating the need for lengthy in-context prompts during inference. The authors contribute two new datasets (ClevrPolicy and GTAPolicy) and propose TriMPI, a three-stage training framework featuring a novel PolicyRollout RL algorithm. The method demonstrates substantial improvements in policy-following accuracy, efficiency, generalization, and robustness compared to baselines.

## Strengths
1. **Novel and Well-Motivated Task Definition**: The paper clearly identifies a practical efficiency and fidelity bottleneck in deploying multimodal conversational agents with long, complex policies. The MPI task extends prior work on text-only safety alignment and simple prompt compression into the multimodal, reasoning-intensive domain.
2. **Comprehensive Benchmarking**: The creation of two distinct, well-described datasets (ClevrPolicy for controlled reasoning and GTAPolicy for low-data, real-world tool usage) provides a solid foundation for research. The zero-shot in-context results (Table 1) effectively establish the tasks as non-trivial and justify the need for internalization.
3. **Extensive and Multifaceted Evaluation**: The evaluation goes beyond simple accuracy to include efficiency gains (Fig. 6), generalization to policy updates (Policy Override, Table 3), assessment of embedded knowledge (Policy Referral, Table 3), robustness to catastrophic forgetting (referenced in Table 9), and a sanity check where the policy is returned to context (Table 4). This breadth is a significant strength.
4. **Interesting Algorithmic Idea**: The PolicyRollout algorithm is a conceptually neat solution to a core challenge: it uses policy-conditioned responses to guide RL exploration for a model that must operate without the policy at inference, avoiding a train-test mismatch. The three-stage framework is clearly motivated and described.

## Weaknesses
### Major:
1. **Insufficient Isolation of PolicyRollout's Contribution**: The paper's central claim is that PolicyRollout enables "more policy-aware exploration." However, in Table 2, the comparison of "TriMPI w/ PoRo-GRPO" against "VM-CPT + CoT SFT + GRPO" (without PoRo) is confounded. The "TriMPI" configuration involves a full training pipeline, and the reported improvement could be attributed to the extra RL steps taken in that run, the specific hyperparameters, or the sequential combination of stages, rather than the novel rollout augmentation itself. The critical missing baseline is **CoT SFT + GRPO (or VM-CPT + CoT SFT + GRPO) trained for a matched number of RL steps/updates as the TriMPI run**. Without this controlled comparison, the unique benefit of PolicyRollout is not empirically validated. **(§4.3, Table 2)**
2. **Lack of Justification for the Three-Stage Pipeline Design**: While ablations show each stage adds value, the paper does not test simpler, more efficient alternatives to justify the specific *three-stage* sequence. For instance, what is the performance of **RL (or RL with PolicyRollout) starting directly from the base pre-trained model, skipping the SFT stage?** Or, **VM-CPT followed directly by RL, skipping CoT-SFT?** The omission of these ablations leaves open the possibility that the RL stage is the dominant driver and the preceding stages are merely effective initializations. The necessity of the full, computationally heavier pipeline is not fully substantiated. **(§4.2, §5.1)**
3. **Opaque and Potentially Biased CoT Data Generation**: The entire pipeline's success hinges on high-quality Chain-of-Thought data for SFT. The paper states generation methods are detailed in Appendix D, but if this appendix is not provided, it is a critical omission for reproducibility. More importantly, if the CoT data is generated by a much stronger teacher model (e.g., Claude-4) than the base student model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), a significant portion of the gains from SFT+RL could be simple knowledge distillation from a superior model, not a triumph of the internalization method. A more rigorous approach would involve generating CoT data *with the base model itself* in a few-shot setting, or at least analyzing the sensitivity of results to the teacher model's strength. **(§4.1, reference to Appendix D)**

### Minor:
4. **Limited Exploration of Policy Composition and Scalability**: The evaluation tests generalization via "Policy Override" but does not probe the model's ability to internalize *multiple* distinct policies simultaneously or to compose knowledge from them—a key challenge for real-world agents juggling many rules. The listed limitation mentions "mixtures of tasks," but this is a critical aspect that should have been explored to better define the method's boundaries.
5. **Superficial Analysis of the VM-CPT Mechanism**: The justification for the visually-masked continual pretraining stage is empirical ("has shown empirical success despite its simplicity"). A deeper analysis of *what* is learned—for instance, via probing for policy concepts in representations or analyzing attention patterns—would strengthen the claim of "direct policy knowledge injection" and clarify its interaction with visual tokens.

### Trivial:
6. **Efficiency Metrics Limited to Prefill Time**: The efficiency analysis (Fig. 6) reports reductions in prompt tokens and prefill time, but the more relevant metric for a conversational agent is total end-to-end latency or throughput, which includes generation time. This is a minor omission.

## Nice-to-Haves
1. A small-scale case study applying TriMPI to policies from an open-source multimodal agent project could strengthen the real-world applicability narrative beyond synthetic benchmarks.
2. A quantitative analysis measuring the "reasoning-intensity" of the proposed policies (e.g., logical depth, required steps) compared to those in prior prompt compression work would help crystallize the task's novelty.

## Suggestions
1. **Run the critical controlled RL ablation**: Compare "TriMPI w/ PoRo-GRPO" against a "VM-CPT + CoT SFT + GRPO" baseline trained for an exactly matched number of policy gradient update steps (not just "RL steps" which may count rollouts). This will isolate the effect of the PolicyRollout augmentation from the effect of longer RL training.
2. **Test streamlined pipeline variants**: Run experiments for (a) Base Model → RL (with PolicyRollout) and (b) Base Model → VM-CPT → RL (with PolicyRollout). Report results alongside the full TriMPI pipeline. This will clarify the necessity of each stage.
3. **Clarify and analyze CoT data generation**: In the main paper or a provided appendix, detail the exact method for generating CoT data for each dataset. If a strong teacher model was used, discuss the potential distillation effect or include an ablation using a weaker teacher or the base model itself.
4. **Add an experiment on policy mixture**: Design a small-scale test where the model must internalize two distinct policies (e.g., from ClevrPolicy and GTAPolicy) and demonstrate the ability to follow the correct one based on context, or report the interference observed.
