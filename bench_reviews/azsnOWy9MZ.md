## Summary
PLORA is a system for accelerating LoRA hyperparameter tuning by packing multiple heterogeneous LoRA adapter training configurations into a single concurrent fine-tuning job that shares a frozen base model. It addresses the severe GPU underutilization observed when training individual small LoRA adapters (e.g., 16.7% SM occupancy) through an offline packing planner (DTM algorithm) and custom execution kernels. The system demonstrates up to 7.52× reduction in makespan for sweeping a hyperparameter search space and up to 12.8× improvement in job-level throughput across models like QWen-2.5 and Llama-3.

## Strengths
- **Identifies and substantiates a clear, practical problem:** The paper provides an extensive empirical study (1000+ experiments) demonstrating that optimal LoRA hyperparameters vary significantly across tasks and base models (Table 2, Table 4), and that individual LoRA training jobs severely underutilize GPU resources due to small batch sizes (Section 3.1). This establishes a solid, data-driven motivation.
- **Novel and coherent system design:** The core idea of "intra-run" packed LoRA training—where multiple independent adapters are trained concurrently on a shared base model—is a novel systems approach to the underutilization problem. The design is comprehensive, integrating an optimization-based packing planner, job scheduler, and custom kernels into a working prototype (Section 4).
- **Compelling end-to-end performance gains:** The evaluation shows substantial, measured improvements: up to 7.52× makespan reduction for hyperparameter sweeps and up to 12.8× job throughput improvement (Figures 4, 5). The system also finds better-performing adapters, with quality gains up to 23.4% over default configurations (Table 5), validating its practical utility.

## Weaknesses
### Major:
- **Inadequate baseline comparison undermines claimed contribution.** The primary baselines ("Min GPU" and "Max GPU") are naive sequential/parallel schedulers that do not represent standard practice for hyperparameter optimization on a cluster. The paper does not compare against a realistic concurrent HPO scheduler (e.g., one that runs multiple independent tuning jobs in parallel to fill the cluster), making it impossible to determine how much of the speedup is due to the novel packing idea versus simply better concurrent scheduling. This is a fundamental flaw in evaluating the system's advantage.
- **Lack of ablation studies isolates the source of benefits.** The impressive speedups are attributed jointly to the packing concept, the DTM scheduler, and custom kernels. However, there is no ablation to disentangle these: e.g., comparing DTM to a simple greedy packing heuristic, or measuring kernel speedup in isolation (packed vs. sequential execution with the same scheduler). Without this, the necessity and contribution of the algorithmic and kernel components are unsubstantiated.
- **Critical implementation details are omitted, hindering assessment.** The description of the "customized packed LoRA kernels" is minimal (relegated to Appendix C, not provided). How the kernel efficiently manages computation and memory for multiple heterogeneous adapters—a core technical challenge—is not explained. Similarly, the throughput cost model \( T(H_{j,k}, d_j) \), essential for the DTM scheduler, is not detailed (how it is built, profiled, or validated). This lack of detail makes reproduction and full evaluation impossible.

### Minor:
- **Evaluation scale is limited relative to production LLM tuning.** Experiments use up to 8 GPUs and models up to 32B parameters. While the system shows gains here, its behavior and scalability for much larger models (e.g., 70B+) requiring pipeline parallelism or FSDP—common in production—are unexplored, limiting confidence in broader applicability.
- **Hyperparameter search is treated as a static grid.** The system is evaluated on a fixed set of 120 configurations from a predefined grid. It does not demonstrate integration with adaptive HPO methods (e.g., Bayesian optimization) that dynamically generate configurations based on past results—a more realistic and efficient tuning scenario. The performance and interaction in such a dynamic setting remain unknown.
- **Potential quality trade-offs from packing are unexamined.** The paper assumes no degradation in final adapter quality from concurrent training but provides no analysis. Packing multiple adapters could affect convergence dynamics (e.g., gradient interference, effective batch size per adapter). The absence of any quality comparison between packed and isolated training for the same configurations leaves a gap in validating the approach's safety.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Explore integration with an adaptive HPO framework (e.g., Ray Tune) to demonstrate efficiency in a dynamic, iterative tuning loop.
- Provide a visualization or case study showing example "packs" chosen by the planner and the rationale behind them, making the scheduler's decisions more interpretable.
- Briefly discuss the applicability of the packing concept to other parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods (e.g., (IA)³, AdaLoRA).

## Removed Points
*These points are flagged to be removed; treat them with caution.*
- **"Weakness: The empirical study justifying the 'necessity' of LoRA hyperparameter tuning is weak... The claim that this necessitates a *systems* solution like PLORA is not supported."** → **Removed.** The paper's study (Sec 2.3) effectively shows hyperparameters strongly influence quality and vary across tasks/models, motivating the need for tuning. The systems solution is motivated by the subsequent *hardware underutilization* problem (Sec 3.1), not directly by the hyperparameter sensitivity. The critic misrepresents the logical flow.
- **"Weakness: The optimization formulation... connection to practical performance is asserted but not demonstrated."** → **Weakened to Major Weakness #2 (lack of ablation).** The formulation is clearly presented, and the paper states the DTM algorithm solves it with provable bounds (Appendix D). The valid criticism is the *lack of empirical ablation* comparing DTM to simpler strategies, not that the connection is unsubstantiated.
- **"Weakness: The core methodology is conflated with the evaluation and contribution is ambiguous."** → **Removed.** The contribution—a system for packed LoRA hyperparameter tuning—is clearly stated. The methodology (planning + execution) is distinct from the evaluation (makespan, throughput, quality).
- **"Weakness: The 'necessity' of packed training versus simpler alternatives (e.g., gradient accumulation) is not explored."** → **Moved to Major Weakness #1 (inadequate baselines).** This is a valid concern about baseline comparison, but the paper does argue that packing exploits the unique opportunity of sharing a frozen base model across adapters, which gradient accumulation does not. The core issue is the lack of comparison to intelligent concurrent scheduling, which is captured in the major weakness.
- **"Weakness: Missing discussion of related work in concurrent/distributed HPO."** → **Removed as a standalone weakness.** The paper does discuss related work in hyperparameter tuning (Sec 7) and correctly states PLORA is orthogonal to search algorithms. A deeper comparison to systems like Ray Tune is a suggestion for improvement, not a required weakness.

## Suggestions
- **Conduct a fair comparison against a strong baseline:** Implement a baseline that uses a standard concurrent job scheduler (e.g., launching multiple independent LoRA tuning jobs in parallel to saturate the cluster) and compare makespan and throughput. This will clearly demonstrate the unique advantage of the packing approach.
- **Perform essential ablation studies:** (1) Compare the DTM scheduler's makespan against a simple greedy packing heuristic. (2) Profile the custom kernel's speedup by comparing packed execution against a sequential loop of adapters (using the same packing plan). Report these components' individual contributions.
- **Elucidate key implementation details in the main paper:** Add a subsection (or detailed appendix) describing the design of the packed LoRA kernels (how batching, gradient computation, and memory management work for heterogeneous adapters) and the construction of the throughput cost model \( T \). This is critical for reproducibility and assessing the systems contribution.
