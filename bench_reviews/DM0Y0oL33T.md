## Summary
This paper introduces the concept of a Generative Universal Verifier for multimodal reasoning. Its core contributions are: (1) ViVerBench, a comprehensive new benchmark for evaluating visual-outcome verification in MLLMs; (2) OmniVerifier-7B, a model trained on novel automated data pipelines that shows significant gains on this benchmark; and (3) OmniVerifier-TTS, a sequential test-time scaling method that uses the verifier to iteratively refine generated images from a unified multimodal model, outperforming parallel sampling baselines.

## Strengths
- **Substantial and Novel Benchmark:** The creation of ViVerBench is a significant contribution. It is large (3,594 questions), manually curated, spans 16 diverse tasks across 6 categories, and includes human performance baselines. It systematically identifies and quantifies three key weaknesses in current SOTA VLMs (fine-grained alignment, mismatched world knowledge activation, and underdeveloped reasoning critics), providing a strong empirical foundation for the work's motivation.
- **Insightful Analysis of Atomic Capabilities:** The ablation studies in Section 4.2 provide a valuable scientific finding. The paper identifies three atomic capabilities (*Explicit Alignment*, *Relational Verification*, *Integrative Reasoning*) and demonstrates through controlled training that skills in the first two generalize to each other, while the third requires task-specific data. This offers a concrete, minimalist recipe for training verifiers and is a key insight for the community.
- **Effective Application with Clear Empirical Gains:** The proposed OmniVerifier-TTS is a practical and well-evaluated application. It demonstrates clear improvements over strong baselines and parallel TTS (Best-of-N) on established benchmarks (T2I-ReasonBench, GenEval++), providing evidence of the verifier's utility for enhancing image generation through iterative refinement. The sequential method is also shown to be more efficient.

## Weaknesses
### Major:
- **The "Universal" Claim is Not Fully Supported.** The paper's core framing centers on a "Generative Universal Verifier." However, the atomic capability analysis in Section 4.2 explicitly shows that *Integrative Reasoning* tasks (e.g., Maze, Robotics) do **not** benefit from training on other atomic skills and require task-specific data. Since OmniVerifier-7B is trained only on data for *Explicit Alignment* and *Relational Verification*, it is not "universal"—it is a specialized verifier for alignment and relational tasks. The paper does not reconcile this fundamental limitation with its central thesis, significantly overstating its model's generality.
- **Insufficient Causal Link for OmniVerifier-TTS Gains.** The performance improvements of the sequential TTS pipeline (+3.7 on T2I-ReasonBench) are attributed to the "superior and accurate visual verification ability" of OmniVerifier. However, the system’s success depends equally on the base UMM's ability to correctly execute the *edit prompts* generated by the verifier. No ablation is provided to disentangle these factors (e.g., comparing the success rate of edit prompt execution vs. using a simpler verifier). Without this, the claim that OmniVerifier is the key driver is not fully substantiated.
- **Critical Process Details are Omitted or Unexplained.** The operation of OmniVerifier-TTS hinges on converting the verifier's natural language explanation into an actionable "edit prompt." The paper states this is a "rephrased form" but provides **no algorithm, prompt template, or qualitative analysis** of this conversion process. This is a critical methodological gap, as the quality of this step directly determines the success of the entire iterative loop. Furthermore, the quantitative evaluation of the verifier's *explanations* (a key output of a "generative" verifier) is absent; only binary accuracy is reported, leaving its reasoning quality unverified.

### Minor:
- **Limited Analysis of the Verifier's Performance Profile.** While OmniVerifier-7B shows strong gains on its target tasks, a more detailed breakdown of its performance—particularly on the *Integrative Reasoning* tasks it is not trained for—would provide a clearer picture of its true capabilities and limitations. The comparison to larger models (Gemini-2.5-Pro, GPT-5) is presented but not analyzed in depth.
- **The Atomic Capability Definitions Could Be More Operational.** While Figure 4 is helpful, the criteria for when a task's underlying capability "may shift with prompt complexity" are abstract. More concrete examples or decision rules would make the framework easier to apply and validate.

### Trivial:
- Formatting artifacts in some figures (e.g., Figure 3) slightly hinder readability but do not obscure the core findings.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Conducting an ablation to directly compare the proposed atomic-skill training recipe against training on a diverse mixture of all benchmark tasks.
- Providing a more detailed failure case analysis for the OmniVerifier-TTS loop, illustrating where edits fail or degrade the image.
- Including a simple supervised fine-tuning baseline on the same data to help isolate the contribution of the RL algorithm versus the data quality.

## Suggestions
1.  **Reframe the central claim.** The title and narrative should be adjusted to more accurately reflect the findings. OmniVerifier-7B is a powerful **specialized** verifier for core visual alignment and relational skills, not a universal one. The "universal" aspiration can remain as a long-term vision, but the empirical claims must be precise.
2.  **Add a critical methodological detail.** In Section 5.1, include a concise description or example of the process that converts the verifier's explanation into an edit prompt. This is essential for reproducibility and understanding the TTS pipeline's mechanics.
3.  **Strengthen the evidence for the TTS contribution.** Consider a simple but revealing ablation: for a subset of prompts, use a weaker judge (e.g., the untrained base Qwen2.5-VL-7B) in the same TTS loop and compare the final image quality. This would help isolate the value added by the specifically-trained verifier.

## Evaluation
- **Novelty:** High. The focus on *visual-outcome* verification is distinct, the ViVerBench benchmark is new, and the atomic capability analysis provides a novel training insight. The sequential TTS application is a creative use of a verifier.
- **Technical Soundness:** Good, with one significant flaw. The benchmark construction, data pipelines, and ablation studies are sound. However, the omission of details for the edit-prompt conversion in TTS and the overstatement of the "universal" claim detract from the overall technical rigor.
- **Empirical Support:** Strong for the benchmark and verifier training results. Moderate for the TTS application, as the causal link between the verifier and the gains is not fully isolated.
- **Significance:** High for the benchmark and the atomic capability findings, which provide clear guidance for future work. The TTS method is a significant and practical application.
- **Clarity:** Generally clear and well-structured. The writing is good. The main clarity issue is the elision of the edit-prompt conversion step.

**Overall:** This is a **strong paper with important contributions** (especially the benchmark and capability analysis) that is significantly marred by a **central overclaim**. The mismatch between the empirical results (which show the verifier is not universal) and the paper's framing undermines its credibility. If the authors can temper their claims to match their evidence, the core contributions remain valuable and likely publishable. In its current form, the overstatement is a major issue that must be addressed.
