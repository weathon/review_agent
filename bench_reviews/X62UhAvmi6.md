## Summary
This paper introduces Budgeted Broadcast (BB), a biologically-inspired pruning method that limits a neuron's "traffic" (the product of its long-term activity and its fan-out) to a local budget. The authors derive a theoretical selectivity-audience balance from a constrained entropy objective and show the rule improves accuracy at matched sparsity across diverse tasks (ASR, face recognition, change detection, synapse segmentation, and LLMs), with particular robustness on rare or tail events.

## Strengths
- **Novel, Principled Foundation:** The paper successfully grounds a practical pruning heuristic in a first-principles, information-theoretic objective (maximizing coding entropy under a total traffic budget). The derived selectivity-audience balance (log[(1-a_i)/a_i] = β k_i) provides a clear, testable prediction that distinguishes the work from purely empirical methods.
- **Compelling and Diverse Empirical Validation:** The method is demonstrated across five distinct domains and architectures (Transformers, ResNets, 3D U-Nets, LLMs). The most striking result is BB's dramatic superiority over strong baselines like Wanda in protecting rare tokens during LLM pruning (e.g., perplexity of 68.69 vs. 2782 at 70% sparsity on Wikitext-2 rare tokens), providing strong evidence for its core thesis of safeguarding "quiet specialists."
- **Effective Mechanistic Analysis:** Controlled experiments (XOR, DNF) cleanly validate the predicted balance, the safety mechanism for rare features, and the method's ability to break optimization symmetries, convincingly linking the theory to observed behavior.

## Weaknesses
### Major:
- **Incomplete Benchmarking Against State-of-the-Art Methods:** While comparisons to magnitude pruning and Wanda are included, critical contemporary baselines are absent. Most notably, for LLM pruning, there is no comparison to **SparseGPT** (cited in Sec. 2 as "conceptually quite different"), which is a standard and strong baseline. For structured N:M pruning, comparisons are limited to Wanda, excluding other strong methods (e.g., ASP, SR-STE). For the synapse prediction task, the claim of "state-of-the-art F1 and PR-AUC" is made without citing or comparing against prior SOTA methods for that specific dataset. This lack of comprehensive comparison makes it difficult to assess the true competitive standing of BB.
- **Under-Explored Practical Overhead and Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** The method introduces non-trivial overhead from tracking Exponential Moving Averages (EMAs) and periodically refreshing masks. The paper mentions this is "modest, amortized" but provides **no quantification** of the extra memory cost or training time slowdown. Furthermore, the core performance depends on the budget threshold `τ` (or the global `β`), but there is **no sensitivity analysis** showing how robust the results are to this hyperparameter across different tasks and architectures. This omission hinders practical adoption and understanding of the method's operational envelope.
- **Limited Analysis of the Core Mechanism at Scale:** The paper claims BB increases "coding entropy and decorrelation," but this is only discussed qualitatively in the theory. There is **no quantitative analysis** (e.g., measuring pairwise neuron activation correlations) in the large-scale experiments to substantiate that this claimed mechanism is actually occurring and responsible for the gains. Similarly, the striking LLM rare-token result begs for deeper analysis: *which* specific rare tokens or linguistic phenomena are being preserved? Without this, the protection of "quiet specialists" remains an aggregate statistic rather than a grounded understanding.

### Minor
- **Clarity Gaps in Method Description:** The description of the SP-in actuator is less clear than for SP-out. While pseudo-code is deferred to the appendix, a more concise summary of the refresh logic (EMA update, threshold check, pruning/regrowth action) in the main text would improve reproducibility. The parser artifacts in the submitted draft (e.g., in Sec. 4) further obscure details, though this is presumably a submission formatting issue.
- **Missing Ablation on Traffic Metric Components:** The core innovation is the product `a_i * k_i`. An ablation experiment pruning based on `a_i` alone or `k_i` alone on one of the core tasks would help isolate the necessity of the combined metric versus simpler heuristics.

### Trivial
- **Variable Experimental Protocols:** While demonstrating breadth, the experimental protocols (fixed vs. discovered budget, regrowth settings, refresh schedules) differ across domains. This is reasonable for exploring a new principle but requires careful reading to parse.

## Nice-to-Haves
- A preliminary experiment applying a traffic-budget principle to attention layers ("Budgeted Attention"), as suggested in the discussion, would significantly strengthen the claim of architectural generality for Transformer-based models.
- Reporting actual inference speedups or FLOPs reduction, especially for the N:M structured pruning experiments, would bridge the biological "metabolic cost" analogy to concrete engineering benefits.

## Suggestions
- **Strengthen the empirical case:** Add a comparison to **SparseGPT** for the LLM pruning experiments (both unstructured and, if possible, structured). For the synapse prediction task, explicitly compare against previously published state-of-the-art results on the same dataset (SmartEM GT2).
- **Add crucial practical analyses:** 1) Include a table or subsection quantifying the memory and time overhead of BB (EMA storage, mask refresh computation) versus a dense baseline during training. 2) Perform a sensitivity analysis on the key hyperparameter (budget `τ` or `β`) across at least two diverse domains (e.g., ASR and Face ID) to demonstrate robustness.
- **Deepen the mechanism analysis:** Provide a quantitative measure of neuron decorrelation (e.g., mean pairwise correlation of activations on a validation set) for a key model (e.g., the pruned ResNet or Transformer) comparing BB to a dense and magnitude-pruned baseline. For the LLM, include a brief qualitative analysis of *examples* of rare tokens where BB's perplexity remains stable but Wanda's explodes.

## Evaluation
- **Novelty:** High. The traffic budget concept and its derivation from a constrained entropy objective represent a fresh and principled perspective on pruning.
- **Technical Soundness:** Good, with a notable caveat. The theoretical derivation is sound and well-connected to the algorithm, but the empirical validation lacks comparisons against the full suite of relevant state-of-the-art baselines, which is a significant methodological shortcoming.
- **Empirical Support:** Strong in breadth and for the core rare-feature claim in LLMs, but **weakened by incomplete benchmarking**. The evidence across five domains is impressive, yet the omission of key comparisons (SparseGPT) prevents a full assessment of the method's standing.
- **Significance:** Potentially high. The demonstrated ability to protect rare features in LLMs during aggressive pruning is a valuable and distinct property. If the benchmarking gaps are filled, this could be a notable contribution to efficient model design.
- **Clarity:** Generally clear, though the method description could be more self-contained, and the draft suffers from parser artifacts that need correction.

**Overall:** This paper presents a novel, theoretically-grounded pruning principle with compelling results, particularly for protecting rare information in large language models. However, its impact is currently limited by insufficient comparisons against contemporary state-of-the-art methods and a lack of analysis regarding practical overhead and hyperparameter sensitivity. The work is promising but requires these substantive additions to meet the high bar for acceptance.
