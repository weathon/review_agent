## Summary
GEOBPE introduces a novel geometric analog of byte-pair encoding for protein backbone structures. It builds a hierarchical vocabulary of structural motifs by iteratively clustering frequent motif pairs (Geo-Pairs) via k-medoids, quantizing them to prototypes, and correcting geometric drift using differentiable inverse kinematics. The method claims significant improvements in compression, out-of-distribution generalization, data efficiency, downstream task performance, and interpretability compared to existing vector-quantized tokenizers.

## Strengths
- **Innovative and Well-Motivated Algorithm:** The adaptation of BPE to continuous protein backbone geometry is novel and well-formulated. The method elegantly addresses the core difficulty of discretizing continuous, noisy structural variability while preserving global consistency through its drift-correction mechanism (glue-aware refinement via differentiable inverse kinematics). This is a clear conceptual advance over existing VQ-VAE approaches.
- **Strong Empirical Results on Core Tokenizer Metrics:** GEOBPE demonstrably establishes a smooth Pareto front for compression-distortion tradeoffs (Fig. 4), achieving significantly lower bits-per-residue (BPR) than baselines while maintaining good reconstruction quality. Its out-of-distribution generalization is particularly impressive, with test/train RMSD ratios near 1.1-1.3 versus ratios as high as 6.4 for VQ-VAE baselines. These results robustly support the method's primary claims.
- **Provides Hierarchical, Multi-Scale Representation:** The algorithm natively outputs a merge tree of motifs, providing a hierarchical decomposition of the fold. This is a tangible, useful output distinct from the flat token sequences produced by other methods and enables multi-resolution analysis.

## Weaknesses

### Major:
- **Misleading Downstream Task Evaluation Setup:** The downstream transfer results in Table 1 are presented as evidence that GEOBPE's vocabulary provides a "strong inductive bias" and "consistently outperforms leading PSTs." However, GEOBPE-TRANSFER uses **ESM3 features as its base representation (Θ ← ESM3)** and then applies GEOBPE's hierarchical aggregation. The baselines (FoldSeek, ProTokens, ESM3, VanillaVQ, AminoASeed) are evaluated as standalone tokenizers or models. This is an apples-to-oranges comparison; the performance gains are conflated with the power of the ESM3 foundation model. A fair assessment of GEOBPE's standalone contribution requires using the same base feature extractor (or none) for all methods. This flaw undermines a key claim of the paper.
- **Generative Claims Are Under-Supported:** The paper states GEOBPE "paired with a transformer, supports unconditional backbone generation via language modeling" and presents SSLM-Eval results (Table 11). However, the evaluation is preliminary: it uses a very small (~7.3M parameter) transformer, and there is no comparison to state-of-the-art generative models (e.g., RFdiffusion, Chroma). Metrics like "99% unique backbones" and "49% higher scTM" are insufficient to substantiate a claim of viable generation; there is no assessment of physical plausibility, folding stability, or functional coherence. The authors correctly note in the Limitations that these results are "not competitive with state-of-the-art," but the main text overstates the capability.
- **Interpretability Analysis is Descriptive Rather than Predictive:** The paper heavily emphasizes interpretability, showing high agreement between GEOBPE segments and CATH/PFAM domain boundaries (Table 2) and presenting expert case studies. However, this analysis is post-hoc and correlative. High segmentation recall is somewhat expected given the method merges contiguous residues. The critical question—whether the discovered tokens provide *novel, actionable insight* or can *predict* function better than other representations—is not addressed. The case studies are illustrative but not a systematic validation of functional utility.

### Minor
- **Computational Complexity and Practical Efficiency are Underexplored:** The method involves iterative clustering, global IK optimization, and maintaining a priority dictionary. While Appendix J discusses complexity, there is no quantitative benchmark comparing the runtime and memory footprint of GEOBPE to simpler baselines (e.g., ProToken, VQ-VAE) on a standard dataset. Claims of "data efficiency" and practicality remain partially unsupported.
- **Limited Comparison to Non-Learned Structural Alphabets:** The baselines are exclusively VQ-VAE-based and Inverse Folding models. A comparison to standard, non-learned structural alphabets (e.g., Protein Blocks) used as a tokenizer would help contextualize whether the learned, hierarchical vocabulary provides a meaningful advantage over fixed, expert-curated alphabets in compression and downstream tasks.
- **Side-Chain and Sequence Information are Absent:** The method operates solely on backbone atoms (N, CA, C). The authors note this as a limitation, but the claimed "multimodal" utility is currently limited. A discussion or preliminary experiment on integrating amino acid type into the Geo-Pair definition would strengthen the paper's scope.

### Trivial
- **Figure and Table Presentation:** Some labels in Figure 4 (e.g., "BPR") are cut off, and the caption's note about varying vocabulary sizes is essential but could be emphasized more in the main text.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Conduct a fair downstream task evaluation by applying all tokenization/aggregation methods to the same base features (e.g., ESM3 or a simpler backbone encoder) to isolate the contribution of the tokenizer's vocabulary.
- Perform a more rigorous generative evaluation by scaling the transformer model size and comparing against a strong unconditional generation baseline using established metrics for physical plausibility.
- Include a runtime/scalability benchmark table comparing GEOBPE to key baselines on tokenizing a standard dataset.
- Provide an ablation study quantifying the contribution of the "glue-aware refinement" (IK optimization) step to reconstruction and OOD generalization.

## Suggestions
1. **Revise the downstream task evaluation:** Clearly label GEOBPE-TRANSFER as "ESM3 + GEOBPE aggregation" in Table 1 and the main text. Additionally, conduct an ablation where GEOBPE's hierarchical aggregation is applied to the same base features used by other PSTs (or to raw coordinates) to provide a fair, isolated comparison of the tokenizer's inductive bias.
2. **Scale and rigorously evaluate generative capabilities:** To substantiate the claim of supporting unconditional generation, train a larger transformer (e.g., 50M-100M parameters) on GEOBPE tokens and compare against a comparable model trained on VQ-VAE tokens, evaluating on standard metrics for backbone design (scTM, diversity, designability) and, if possible, against a state-of-the-art generative baseline.
3. **Add a runtime/scalability benchmark:** Include a table comparing the wall-clock time and memory usage for tokenizing the standard 48K chain dataset between GEOBPE, ProToken, and a VQ-VAE baseline.
4. **Clarify the "Bits-Per-Residue" calculation:** In Section 3.3 or Appendix, explicitly detail how L(T) (description length of the tokenizer) is calculated for GEOBPE's hierarchical vocabulary and for each baseline to ensure the compression comparison is transparent and fair.

## Evaluation Axes (using language)
**Novelty:** High. GEOBPE is the first principled adaptation of byte-pair encoding to protein backbone geometry, introducing a novel discrete, hierarchical tokenization scheme with geometric drift correction.
**Technical Soundness:** High. The algorithm is well-motivated, mathematically detailed, and includes pseudocode. The empirical results on compression, distortion, and OOD generalization are robust and convincingly support the core technical claims.
**Empirical Support:** Mixed. The empirical support for the core tokenizer performance (compression, generalization) is strong. However, support for downstream utility is compromised by the unfair evaluation setup, and support for generative capability is preliminary.
**Significance:** Potentially high. If the downstream evaluation issues are addressed and generative capabilities are more rigorously established, GEOBPE could provide a powerful, interpretable, and efficient foundational tool for protein structure representation, benefiting protein language models and structural analysis.
**Clarity:** Good. The paper is well-structured, with clear notation, algorithm description, and comprehensive experiments. Some figure labels and comparative setups could be clarified.
