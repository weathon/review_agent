## Summary
This paper introduces DeepCritic, a two-stage framework to enhance the mathematical critique ability of LLMs. The core innovation is a data curation pipeline that generates "deliberate" critiques through an iterative process of initial critique, in-depth critique (for multi-perspective verification and self-correction), and synthesis. The resulting model, trained via SFT on this data and further refined with RL, achieves strong performance on error identification benchmarks and shows utility in assisting LLM generators via verified voting and critique-based refinement.

## Strengths
- **Novel and Well-Motivated Methodology:** The iterative seed data generation process—producing initial critiques followed by in-depth critiques for multi-perspective verification and meta-critiquing—is a creative and well-explained approach to instilling deliberate reasoning. The ablation studies (Table 1) cleanly demonstrate the value of each stage over simple distillation.
- **Strong Empirical Performance:** The final model (DeepCritic-7B-RL-PRM800K) achieves state-of-the-art results on multiple math critique benchmarks (MR-GSM8K, PRM800K, ProcessBench), outperforming much larger generalist models (GPT-4o, Qwen2.5-72B) and specialized reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill). The scale of improvement from the base Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (34.1 to 69.1 avg. F1) is substantial.
- **Demonstrated Practical Utility:** The paper moves beyond static accuracy to show the model's value in downstream applications: it effectively serves as a verifier to improve generator performance via majority voting (Figure 4) and provides feedback that helps generators (including a stronger 72B model) refine incorrect solutions (Table 3), demonstrating promising weak-to-strong supervision potential.
- **Exploration of Automated Supervision:** The RL stage using automatically constructed data (via Monte Carlo sampling) shows a clear performance boost, validating a promising path for scalable oversight without heavy human annotation.

## Weaknesses
### Major:
- **Core Claim of "More Informative" Critiques is Under-Supported:** The paper's primary motivation is to address shallow critiques that offer "limited guidance for the LLM generator to perform targeted refinements." However, the main evaluation (Table 1) relies solely on the F1 score for final error identification *judgment*. While the refinement experiments in Section 5.2.2 are a step toward validating improved feedback quality, they are limited in scope (two small test sets) and crucially **do not include a comparison with the strongest baseline, Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B**, which outperforms DeepCritic on judgment F1. The claim of superior informativeness therefore lacks comprehensive, direct evidence against the state-of-the-art comparator.
- **Limited Analysis of "Deliberate" Reasoning Behavior:** The paper asserts that the model learns to perform "iterative evaluation, multi-perspective verification, and meta-critiquing." The evidence for this behavior during inference is thin. The analysis in Section 4.3 (Figure 3) uses a small sample and a subjective GPT-4o classification to report "self-correction" frequency. There is no quantitative analysis of the critique content (e.g., frequency of genuine perspective-switching, depth of reasoning chains) to substantiate that the model is engaging in the novel reasoning processes it was trained on, rather than simply generating longer, more verbose text that correlates with higher accuracy.

### Minor:
- **Dependence on a Strong Teacher for High-Quality Seed Data:** The success of Stage 1 hinges on using Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct to generate the 4.5K seed critiques. The self-improvement experiment (Table 2) shows significantly more modest gains when the 7B model annotates its own data, suggesting the framework's initial boost is tightly coupled with access to a strong external annotator. The scalability of the method to domains or settings without such a powerful teacher is not thoroughly explored.
- **Computational Cost of Data Curation is Under-Analyzed:** The automatic RL data construction via Monte Carlo sampling (Section 3.2.2) is acknowledged as expensive, and while binary search is mentioned as an accelerant (Appendix J), no concrete analysis of the computational cost versus performance gain is provided. Similarly, the inference-time cost of generating long, deliberate critiques is not compared to that of simpler verification models.

## Nice-to-Haves
- A more systematic, quantitative evaluation of critique *quality* (e.g., using LLM or human evaluation to score depth, actionability, or novelty of perspective) would directly strengthen the core claim.
- Exploring the framework's applicability to a non-mathematical domain (e.g., logical reasoning, code review) would better support the claim of generalizability mentioned in the paper.
- A more detailed ablation on RL reward design, investigating rewards that might incentivize the desired deliberative reasoning style beyond simple binary accuracy, could yield further insights.

## Removed Points
*These points are flagged to be removed, treat them with caution.*
- **Weakness: "The paper does not quantitatively measure the informativeness, depth, or multi-perspective nature of the generated critiques themselves."** (From Harsh Critic, Point 1). **Justification:** While this is a valid high-level concern, its phrasing as a "Critical Issue" is too absolute. The paper *does* provide a qualitative example (Figure 1) and the refinement experiment (Section 5.2.2) serves as an indirect quantitative measure of feedback utility. The point is re-framed as a major weakness above, focusing on the insufficiency of the evidence rather than its complete absence.
- **Weakness: "The comparison to the strongest baseline (Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B) is incomplete and potentially unfair... The paper fails to conclusively show that DeepCritic is better..."** (From Harsh Critic, Point 3). **Justification:** The core of this criticism is valid and is retained as a major weakness. However, the accusation of an "unfair" comparison is removed. The paper openly states the baseline was trained on more data and fairly notes its limitation in providing actionable feedback. The flaw is the lack of a direct comparison on the refinement task, not an unfair setup.
- **Weakness: "Evaluation only on mathematical reasoning tasks."** (From Human Finder, Point 1). **Justification:** This is scope creep. The paper explicitly states its focus is "studying and enhancing the math critique ability of LLMs." Criticizing it for not evaluating other domains is demanding it address problems outside its stated scope.
- **Weakness: "Model family limitations... Experiments are primarily conducted on Qwen models."** (From Human Finder, Point 2). **Justification:** This is a generic, one-size-fits-all weakness. The paper's core contribution is a training methodology, not a model architecture analysis. Results on LLaMA3.1 are provided in the appendix, and there is no reason to believe the method is architecture-specific. Demanding evaluation across numerous model families is not a standard requirement for this type of contribution.
- **Weakness: "Potential limitations of Monte Carlo sampling for correctness estimation."** (From Human Finder, Point 4). **Justification:** The paper already addresses this by discussing label noise in Appendix K. This is a methodological detail that has been reasonably acknowledged and investigated by the authors.

## Suggestions
- Conduct a direct, head-to-head comparison between DeepCritic and Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B on the critique-based refinement task (Section 5.2.2) to provide decisive evidence for the claimed advantage in providing actionable feedback.
- Perform a more rigorous content analysis of the generated critiques (e.g., on a sample of 100+ critiques) to quantify the occurrence of multi-perspective verification and meta-critiquing behaviors, moving beyond the high-level "self-correction" metric.

## Evaluation
- **Novelty:** High. The iterative, two-stage data curation process designed to instill deliberate reasoning (multi-perspective verification, meta-critiquing) is a distinct and thoughtful contribution.
- **Technical Soundness:** Good. The methodology is clearly described, and the ablation studies are well-designed. The reliance on automatic labeling for RL is justified, though the computational cost is under-discussed.
- **Empirical Support:** Good but with a significant caveat. The benchmark results are strong and comprehensive. The major weakness is the incomplete support for the paper's central claim about generating superior, actionable feedback, as the refinement experiments lack a crucial comparison.
- **Significance:** High. Improving the critique ability of smaller models is crucial for scalable oversight. The demonstrated weak-to-strong supervision potential is particularly promising for the field.
- **Clarity:** Very Good. The paper is well-structured, the figures are helpful, and the method is explained step-by-step.
