## Summary
This paper proposes TARS, a framework for dexterous manipulation that integrates visual and tactile sensing via a unified point-cloud representation. It introduces a Visual-Tactile Affordance (VTA) module and a Visual-Tactile Policy (VTP) module, trained with a teacher-student reinforcement learning setup, aiming to handle both contact and non-contact states seamlessly. Evaluation on four simulated manipulation tasks shows improved success rates over several baselines.

## Strengths
*   **Addresses a core, challenging integration problem:** The paper directly tackles the difficult issue of creating a policy that functions continuously across states where tactile feedback is intermittently available (non-contact) and unavailable (contact). The proposed point-cloud fusion and the explicit encoding of modality (visual/tactile) are well-motivated steps toward this goal.
*   **Rigorous experimental ablation:** The paper includes a clear and relevant set of baseline comparisons (Robot Synesthesia, Visual Affordance, PointNet+MLP) designed to isolate the contributions of different components (modality encoding vs. affordance). Tests on generalization to new objects and robustness to point-cloud downsampling provide meaningful validation of the framework's properties.
*   **Practical sim-to-real design:** The method of decoupling tactile perception into contact shape and force components, and aligning simulated forces with real sensor data via a CNN, represents a thoughtful and practical approach to mitigating the simulation-to-reality gap for optical tactile sensors.

## Weaknesses
### Major:
*   **Severe structural inconsistency and missing technical content:** Sections 3.2 and 3.3, and the conclusion (Section 5), contain extensive, detailed derivations for a Finite Element Method (FEM) force estimation model for a *soft-bubble gripper* (e.g., Punyo). This content is completely incongruent with the rest of the paper, which describes a system built around *Gelsight Mini* optical tactile sensors. The intended technical description of the VTA and VTP modules is garbled or absent. For instance, the training loss and specific architecture for the VTA module are not provided, and the "visual-tactile one-hot classification encoding" is not properly defined. This flaw makes the core methodology impossible to evaluate or reproduce. **This is a fundamental issue that undermines the entire paper.**
*   **Evaluation does not substantiate core claims:** The experiments validate the high-level performance of the full TARS pipeline but provide no evidence for several key claims:
    *   **Unvalidated Novel Component:** There is no ablation or direct evaluation of the proposed tactile simulation method or the necessity of its specific design (e.g., the CNN for force prediction, the point-cloud sampling). The performance gains could stem from simply having a two-modality input rather than the novel simulation approach.
    *   **Unsubstantiated "Smooth Transitions":** A central motivation is enabling smooth policy transitions between contact and non-contact states. However, the paper provides no analysis—such as success rates during contact initiation, policy action stability at the moment of contact, or feature space analysis—to demonstrate this capability beyond final task success metrics.
    *   **Unclear Affordance Contribution:** The benefit attributed to the VTA module is conflated with the use of modality encoding in the ablation studies (RS vs. VA vs. TARS). There is no isolated experiment or visualization (e.g., a heatmap of predicted affordance on objects) to show what the VTA learns or that its output is meaningfully different from a simpler heuristic.

### Minor:
*   **Incomplete results presentation:** Key data tables (I, II, III) referenced in Section 4.3 are not included in the provided text, preventing assessment of the magnitude of improvement or statistical significance.
*   **Limited analysis of learned representations:** The paper states that the combined features create a "smooth" feature space but offers no analysis (e.g., t-SNE visualization, probing tasks) to interpret what the affordance or classification features actually represent.

## Nice-to-Haves
*   A visualization of the predicted affordance overlaid on object point clouds throughout a task sequence would greatly improve interpretability.
*   A more detailed failure case analysis would help delineate the method's limitations (e.g., sensitive object types, failure modes).

## Removed Points
*   **"Lack of real-world quantitative results" (from Spark):** The paper explicitly states in the introduction that "we successfully conducted real-world experiments to demonstrate the applicability of our approach." Demanding these results be included in the submission is a request for impractically large artifacts (full real-robot deployment logs and videos). The claim of real-world applicability is noted as a strength, but its quantitative validation is beyond the typical scope of a conference submission.
*   **"Limited object diversity" and "Generalizability to unseen objects not evaluated" (from Human Finder):** **Factually incorrect.** The paper directly addresses generalization in Section 4.3, Variants, reporting results in Tab. II (not included in text) where the policy trained on one object ("Lightbulb") is tested on six other unseen objects. The text notes performance variations and analyzes an outlier ("Apple").
*   **"Marginal performance improvements over simpler baselines" (from Human Finder):** This is an unsubstantiated interpretation. Without the actual results tables, the significance of the improvements cannot be judged. The paper's own analysis suggests the improvements are meaningful and attributes them to specific components.
*   **"Computational complexity concerns" (from Human Finder):** This is a generic, one-size-fits-all criticism not supported by any analysis in the paper. The use of PointNet and an MLP is standard in the field.
*   **"Limited comparison with state-of-the-art tactile manipulation methods" (from Human Finder):** The chosen baselines (RS, VA, PN+MLP) are relevant and directly related to the paper's contributions (point-cloud synesthesia, affordance learning). Demanding comparisons to every SOTA method is scope creep.

## Suggestions
1.  **Resolve the catastrophic inconsistency:** The authors must completely rewrite Sections 3.2, 3.3, and 5 to coherently describe the TARS framework as promised in the abstract. This includes: a clear, detailed description of the VTA module (architecture, loss function, supervision signal); a precise definition of all point features (affordance value, modality one-hot encoding); and a conclusion that summarizes the actual contribution (the TARS framework for visuo-tactile manipulation).
2.  **Strengthen the evaluation to support claims:** Add experiments or analyses that directly probe: (a) the accuracy and necessity of the tactile simulation pipeline via an ablation against ground-truth simulator contact data, and (b) the "smooth transition" capability, perhaps by measuring metrics focused on the contact initiation phase.
3.  **Include all results and improve clarity:** Ensure all referenced tables and figures are present in the manuscript. Consider adding a visualization of the affordance predictions to make the method's operation more interpretable.
