## Summary
This paper introduces the Banded Inverse Square Root (BISR), a new matrix factorization method for differentially private, multi-epoch SGD. By imposing a banded structure on the inverse correlation matrix (C⁻¹) rather than on C itself, the method enables an explicit, convolution-based noise injection algorithm. The authors prove a new lower bound and an upper bound for BISR that match asymptotically, claiming asymptotic optimality for the multi-epoch factorization error. Empirically, BISR performs comparably to existing banded and optimized factorization methods on synthetic error metrics and end-to-end model training tasks.

## Strengths
- **Novel and impactful conceptual shift.** The core idea—banding the inverse correlation matrix C⁻¹ instead of C—is non-obvious and fruitful. It directly enables a simple, streaming-friendly algorithm (Algorithm 1, Fig. 1) where noise injection is a fixed-coefficient convolution, requiring only O(p) memory and amenable to FFT acceleration (Lemma 2).
- **Significant theoretical advance.** The paper provides an explicit upper bound on the factorization error for BISR with clear dependence on bandwidth p, participation parameters k, b, and optimizer hyperparameters α, β (Theorem 4). This addresses a known limitation of prior Banded Square Root (BSR) analysis, where the bound was implicit in p.
- **Comprehensive empirical validation across settings.** Experiments rigorously compare BISR against strong baselines (BSR, BLT, BandMF) across a wide matrix of configurations (varying α, β, k, n, p; see Figs. 2, 3, 4). Results consistently show BISR matches or outperforms BSR and BLT in RMSE and remains viable where optimization-based BandMF becomes computationally prohibitive for large n.

## Weaknesses
### Major:
- **The proof of asymptotic optimality is incomplete and potentially flawed.** The central claim—that BISR is asymptotically optimal—rests on Theorem 3, a new lower bound. The proof sketch is critically insufficient. For the case α=1, it claims a bound of Ω(k log n + k) by combining two bounds (Ω(k log n) and Ω(k)), but provides no justification for the additive form. The sketch references an unproven inequality (`E(B, C) ≥ 1/n ∥A_{1,β}π∥²`) without derivation. Since the full proof is relegated to the appendix, its correctness cannot be verified from the submission. **If Theorem 3 is incorrect, the paper's primary theoretical contribution collapses.**
- **Empirical evaluation does not validate the core theoretical scaling laws.** While experiments show BISR is competitive on fixed-size problems, there is no direct validation of the novel *asymptotic* scaling predicted by Theorems 3 and 4. For example, the paper does not sweep n, k, b, and p over orders of magnitude to plot error growth and confirm the predicted rates (e.g., O(√k) for α<1). The experiments thus confirm practical utility but provide weak evidence for the paper's main theoretical advance.
- **Practical advantage over optimized baselines is unclear and sometimes absent.** The paper claims BISR performs "on par with state-of-the-art" and is "simpler." However, Figure 3 shows the numerically optimized BandMF method frequently achieves lower RMSE, especially at higher participation counts (k=16). The text concedes that "methods based on numerical optimization... may achieve superior performance." The claimed benefits are thus primarily analytical clarity and implementation simplicity, not superior performance. End-to-end model accuracy results (Fig. 5) show BISR and BandInvMF are competitive but do not demonstrate clear superiority.

### Minor
- **The BandInvMF optimization procedure is heuristically justified.** Section 5 proposes optimizing the coefficients of the banded inverse matrix. The justification relies on the assumption that "the optimum is achieved for indices of the form i + kb" and the observation that the resulting C is positive and decreasing. While empirically effective, a more rigorous guarantee for this optimization or a discussion of its convergence properties is lacking.
- **Clarity of the central theoretical argument could be improved.** The paper is dense, and the logical flow between Theorem 4, its supporting lemmas, and Corollary 1 is challenging to follow from the sketch alone. A high-level, intuitive explanation of *why* banding the inverse leads to these explicit rates would significantly improve accessibility.

### Trivial
- None.

## Nice-to-Haves
- A direct comparison of final model accuracy (not just RMSE) between BISR and BandMF for a task where both are computationally feasible (e.g., n=4096) would help clarify whether BandMF's RMSE advantage consistently translates to better utility.
- A brief discussion of a concrete, practical rule for choosing the optimal bandwidth p* (beyond the asymptotic p* = O(b/log b) of Corollary 1) would aid practitioners.

## Removed Points
*These points are flagged to be removed, treat them with caution.*
- **Strength: "The paper is well-written."** Removed as a generic strength.
- **Weakness: "BLT adaptation for momentum/weight decay is hand-waved."** Removed. The paper clearly states BLT has only been described for prefix-sum matrices (footnote 3) and thus appropriately omits it from those plots. Citing concurrent work (Huang et al., 2025) that adapts BLT is sufficient context; the authors are not required to present their own adaptation.
- **Weakness: "Missing comparison with broader DP-SGD variants / modern large-scale benchmarks (e.g., Llama)."** Removed as scope creep. The paper's contribution is a matrix factorization mechanism within a well-defined line of work (BSR, BLT, BandMF). It is not required to benchmark against all DP-SGD variants or demonstrate scaling to the largest possible models to establish its core contribution.
- **Weakness: "Insufficient discussion of hyperparameter sensitivity."** Weakened to a Nice-to-Have. The paper provides empirical guidance (e.g., Fig. 2 shows BISR needs smaller p than BSR) and a theoretical rule for asymptotic p*. Demanding a full hyperparameter sensitivity study is not standard for a theoretical/algorithmic contribution of this type.
- **Weakness: "Theoretical optimality may not translate to practical gains."** This point is partially addressed in the Major weakness on practical advantage. The specific observation about RMSE not correlating with accuracy is a valid insight discussed by the authors in Section 5, not a weakness to be removed.

## Suggestions
- The authors must provide a complete, rigorous proof for Theorem 3 (the lower bound) in the main text or a clearly accessible appendix. The current sketch is insufficient to support the central claim of asymptotic optimality. All steps, including the justification for the additive bound Ω(k log n + k) and the derivation of the inequality `E(B, C) ≥ 1/n ∥A_{1,β}π∥²`, must be explicitly shown and justified.
- To strengthen empirical support for the theory, add experiments that directly test the asymptotic scaling laws. For example, plot log(RMSE) against log(n) or log(k) for BISR and baselines, across a wide range of values, to visually demonstrate that the empirical growth rate matches the predicted theoretical rate (e.g., O(√k)).
