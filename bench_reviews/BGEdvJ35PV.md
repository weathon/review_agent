## Summary

This paper identifies that 3D molecular distributions exhibit a "dense-concentrated" (DC) structure, where valid configurations form sharp, narrow probability peaks separated by near-zero density regions. This structure makes standard diffusion inference fragile, as small errors can push samples into invalid, low-density areas. To address this, the authors propose DIST, a model-agnostic plug-in corrective sampling method that steers intermediate distributions back toward valid peaks, improving generation quality and reducing inference cost.

## Strengths
- **Compelling and formalized problem identification:** The paper provides a clear, intuitive argument, supported by Figure 1, that molecular data presents a unique challenge for diffusion models compared to images due to strict validity constraints. The formal definition of DC-structure (Definition 3.1) and the subsequent analysis linking it to reverse-step overshoot (Eq. 7) offer a solid theoretical grounding for the work.
- **Strong and consistent empirical results:** The proposed DIST method provides significant and consistent improvements in critical metrics (atom stability, molecule stability, validity) across three different, strong backbone models (EDM, GeoLDM, RADM) on two standard benchmarks (QM9 and GEOM-Drugs). The improvements are not marginal; for example, EDM's molecule stability on QM9 rises from 82.0% to 89.9%.
- **Model-agnostic and practical design:** DIST is presented as a plug-in component, requiring no retraining or architectural changes to the backbone models. This demonstrates broad utility across GNN/Transformer-based, equivariant/non-equivariant, and coordinate/latent-space methods. An ablation study on pilot subset size (Table 4) shows a sensible quality-compute trade-off.

## Weaknesses
### Major:
- **The method's practical implementation details are vague and hinder reproducibility.** The core algorithmic steps—how radius-\(r\) batches \(B_j\) are constructed in high-dimensional molecular space, what specific "pilot score" \(s_j\) (e.g., round-trip residual, self-consistency) was used in the experiments, and how the critical threshold \(\tau\) is chosen—are not specified in the main text (relegated to the appendix). This opacity makes it difficult to fully understand, trust, or reproduce the method. *[Justification: The paper states in Sec. 3.2: "Each batch \(j\) is further associated with a model-side pilot score \(s_j \in \mathbb{R}\) (e.g., round-trip residual, self-consistency, ensemble variance, or chemistry-based penalty)." The exact metric used is not specified. The description of batch construction via "duplication and perturbation" is also high-level.]*
- **The efficiency claim is incomplete and potentially misleading.** Table 3 reports a reduction in the average number of timesteps per *accepted* sample. However, it does not account for the computational overhead of the corrective step: the pilot inferences run on all candidate batches (including those later discarded), the scoring computations, and the duplication/perturbation process. A proper efficiency comparison requires quantifying the total computational cost (e.g., FLOPs or wall-clock time) to generate a fixed number of *final* valid samples, relative to the baseline. The claim of "nearly half the computational cost" is therefore not fully substantiated. *[Justification: The paper states in Sec. 4.3: "DIST requires only \(T|B| - t\) expected timesteps per inference... setting \(t = 300\) with \(|B| = 100\), each accepted batch... requires only 307 steps." This calculation ignores the cost of the pilot runs for the *100* initial batches to select the accepted ones.]*

### Minor:
- **The claim of being "first" to highlight the fragility issue is overstated.** While the formalization of DC-structure is novel, the core observation that molecular validity constraints and multi-modal distributions pose challenges for diffusion models has been discussed in prior literature (e.g., cited works like Choi et al., 2025 and Bohde et al., 2025). The paper could more clearly distinguish its specific contribution (the DC-structure formalism and the DIST solution) from this broader context.
- **Critical hyperparameter ablations are relegated to the appendix.** The main paper includes an ablation on pilot size but places studies on other essential parameters—the score threshold \(\tau\), the intermediate correction timestep \(t\), and the perturbation intensity—in Appendix H. This makes it harder for the reader to assess the method's sensitivity and tuning requirements from the primary text.

### Trivial:
- The paper is generally well-written and clearly structured.

## Nice-to-Haves
- A more direct empirical validation of the DC-structure hypothesis (e.g., quantitative analysis of peak widths in molecular vs. image embedding spaces at various noise levels) would strengthen the foundational motivation, though the current theoretical and intuitive argument is reasonably convincing.
- A clearer runtime/FLOP analysis to complement the timestep-based efficiency claim.
- A concise, step-by-step algorithmic pseudocode for DIST in the main text.

## Suggestions
- In the main text, add a dedicated subsection (or significantly expand an existing one) to concretely specify: (1) the exact algorithm for constructing a batch \(B_j\) (e.g., the perturbation distribution and radius \(r\)), (2) the specific pilot scoring function \(s_j\) used to produce the results in Tables 2-4, and (3) the method or criterion for setting the filtering threshold \(\tau\). This is essential for reproducibility and scientific clarity.

**Overall Assessment:**
This is a **strong paper**. It identifies a well-argued, theoretically formalized problem in molecular diffusion, proposes a novel, model-agnostic solution (DIST), and demonstrates significant, consistent empirical improvements across multiple architectures and benchmarks. The core weaknesses are primarily related to the clarity and completeness of the method's implementation details and its efficiency analysis, which are addressable in revision. The paper's **novelty** is high (DC-structure formalization and DIST), its **technical soundness** is good (with a theoretical framework and consistent experiments), its **empirical support** is strong (clear improvements on standard benchmarks), its **significance** is high (addressing a core challenge in molecular generation with a plug-in solution), and its **clarity** is good overall, though it requires more precise algorithmic specification.
