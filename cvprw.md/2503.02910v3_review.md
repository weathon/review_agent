## Critical Issues

1.  **Structural: The evaluation framework is insufficient to support the paper's core claims.** The central claim is that the proposed zero-shot method "significantly outperforms baseline methods" and is "promising" for real-world detection. However, this is primarily validated on a synthetic dataset. The only evaluation on real-world data (GasVid) is qualitative (Section 5.5), with no quantitative metrics reported due to a lack of ground truth. For a paper whose ultimate goal is real-world application, failing to provide any quantitative measure of real-world performance is a critical omission. The claim of promising real-world applicability is thus unsupported by evidence.

2.  **Evidential: The comparison to prior state-of-the-art is absent, making the claimed improvement unclear.** The paper only compares its method to ablations of its own pipeline (BGS-only, VLM-only) on its new synthetic dataset (Table 3). There is no comparison to published gas leak detection methods (e.g., GasFormer [35], the method from Gas-DB [48], or VideoGasNet [46]). This omission is especially critical because Section 2 notes that BGS alone works well on GasVid's simple background, yet the paper does not show how its complex pipeline compares to these simpler, established baselines on the existing real-world or synthetic datasets. Therefore, the claim of significant improvement lacks the necessary context.

3.  **Methodological Gap: The core pipeline is an incremental assembly of existing, standard components without a novel unifying mechanism.** The method sequentially applies: 1) standard background subtraction (MOG2), 2) an off-the-shelf VLM detector (OWL-V2) with an empirically chosen prompt, 3) a simple heuristic temporal filter, and 4) an off-the-shelf segmentation model (SAM 2). While the application to gas leaks is novel, the combination itself is straightforward. The temporal filter is a simple rule-based tracker (Algorithm 1), and the prompt engineering ("white steam") is a pragmatic choice but not a methodological innovation. The paper does not introduce a new model, learning objective, or integration mechanism that fundamentally advances how language interacts with video subtraction for transparent object detection.

## Section-by-Section Notes

*   **Abstract & Introduction:** Claims of "significantly outperforms baseline methods" and "decent results on the real-world dataset" are overstated given the limitations noted above (no SOTA comparison, only qualitative real-world results).
*   **Section 2 (Related Work):** The critique of existing datasets (spatial bias, environmental impact) is well-argued and motivates the synthetic dataset. The description of the "dependency loop" in Zero-shot BGS (ZBS) [2] effectively sets up the rationale for placing BGS before the VLM.
*   **Section 3 (Dataset):** The construction of the SimGas dataset is a clear contribution. However, the paper does not discuss the domain gap between synthetic smoke/steam simulations and real infrared gas plumes. The visual examples (Fig. 2) show opaque white clouds, which may not accurately represent the semi-transparent, faint, and textured appearance of real methane leaks in IR.
*   **Section 4 (Methods):** The method is clearly described. The adaptive enhancement factor (Eq. 1) is a sensible engineering detail. The temporal filtering heuristics, while simple, are justified. However, the pipeline's novelty is limited, as noted in the Critical Issues.
*   **Section 5 (Experiments):**
    *   The ablation study (Table 3) is thorough and shows each component's contribution on the synthetic data.
    *   The prompt analysis (Section 5.4, Table 4, Fig. 4) is valuable and highlights the importance of VLM prompt engineering.
    *   **Major Omission:** As a "new baseline algorithm," the method should be compared to the best-performing methods from the datasets discussed in Section 2 (e.g., Gas-DB's cross-modality network, VideoGasNet) on a common benchmark, even if only on the new synthetic dataset. This comparison is missing.
    *   The qualitative results on GasVid (Section 5.5, Fig. 6) are appreciated but insufficient as the sole real-world evaluation. The statement "the overall performance remains acceptable" is not substantiated with metrics.

## Strengths

*   **Dataset Contribution:** The introduction of the public SimGas dataset, with diverse backgrounds, interfering objects, and precise ground truth, is a valuable resource for the community and addresses a genuine shortage.
*   **Practical Zero-Shot Pipeline:** The paper presents a fully functional, zero-shot pipeline that avoids the need for task-specific training data. The integration of BGS, VLM, and SAM 2 is logical and well-executed.
*   **Comprehensive Ablation and Analysis:** The paper includes a detailed ablation study and an insightful analysis of prompt choices, providing clear evidence of each component's utility and the sensitivity to language input.

## Overall Assessment

This paper makes a useful dataset contribution but falls short of the novelty and rigor expected for ICLR due to fundamental weaknesses in its evaluation framework and methodological innovation. The proposed method is a competent but incremental assembly of existing tools. The lack of quantitative evaluation on real data and the absence of comparisons to prior state-of-the-art methods severely undermine the claims of effectiveness and advancement. The work could form a solid workshop or application-track paper, but in its current form, it does not meet the threshold for acceptance at a top-tier conference like ICLR.

## **WEAKNESSES**

**1. Limited quantitative evaluation on real-world data creates significant sim-to-real gap concerns**

The paper explicitly states "Qualitative (because of the lack of ground truth) tested our performance on GasVid" and "Due to the lack of high-quality mask annotations, results were assessed visually." This reliance on qualitative-only evaluation on the real-world dataset is problematic. Human reviewers frequently criticize similar gaps: "Although the paper claims to... lack a thorough quantification... limiting the interpretability and effectiveness" (from XQED8Nk9mu review). The stark contrast between 69% IoU on synthetic data versus only visual inspection on real data leaves the practical applicability unclear.

**2. Incomplete baseline comparisons and lack of alternative method evaluation**

The method only compares against basic background subtraction techniques (MOG2, Median, k-NN from 2004-2006) and the authors' own pipeline ablations. No quantitative comparison is provided to other potential approaches mentioned in the related work, such as Grounding DINO, GLIP, or other open-vocabulary detection methods. A human reviewer noted similar concerns: "Could the author provide results on other state-of-the-art methods to demonstrate... effectiveness" (from jCNRcHrfLo review), and "the paper only provides experimental results integrated with the [one] method... more state-of-the-art methods should be added in comparison."

**3. Method's strong dependence on prompt selection indicates fragility**

Performance varies substantially across prompts: 0.34 IoU ("white plume") to 0.71 IoU ("white steam"), a >2x difference. While the paper attributes this to VLM training on RGB images, this sensitivity raises concerns about robustness and generalization. The paper acknowledges "white gas" performs poorly because gas is "generally invisible in RGB images," yet the entire approach relies on choosing semantically similar RGB concepts for IR phenomena. No systematic strategy is provided for prompt selection on new domains.

**4. Dataset curation bias from selective video removal**

The paper removed three videos (26, 27, 28) due to "strong, highly localized wind that causes smoke to disperse significantly," deciding these "do not represent typical conditions." This subjective filtering based on what constitutes "realistic" scenarios may introduce bias. Additionally, "Some foreground objects, leakage simulations, and background scenes were reused in different combinations," limiting dataset diversity. These design choices are not thoroughly justified or evaluated for their impact on generalization.

**5. Missing analysis of open-vocabulary detection model choice and parameters**

The paper uses Owl-V2 but provides no justification for this choice over alternatives (Grounding DINO, GroundedSAM, etc.) mentioned in the related work. No ablation compares different VLM backbones. Multiple temporal filtering parameters ($k_1$, $n_1$, $\tau_{tIoU_1}$, $\tau_{tShift}$, $k_2$, $\tau_{tIoU_2}$) are set to fixed values with the statement "These hyperparameters were not tuned extensively to avoid overfitting." The impact of these choices on performance remains unclear.

**6. Ablation study reveals critical dependency on background subtraction not fully explored**

The ablation showing "VLM + temporal filtering + SAM 2 (no BGS)" achieves only 0.31 IoU versus 0.69 with BGS indicates the method is fundamentally limited to scenarios with clean background subtraction. The paper notes this scenario "could lead to difficulties in both correctly identifying leaks and avoiding false positives," but does not analyze when this limitation would be problematic in practice or propose solutions for moving-camera scenarios (acknowledged as future work, but common in real applications like the IIG dataset).

## Balanced Review

### Summary
This paper introduces a synthetic infrared video dataset (SimGas) for gas leak detection, featuring diverse backgrounds, interfering moving objects, and precise segmentation ground truth. It also proposes LangGas, a zero-shot pipeline combining background subtraction, vision-language model (VLM) filtering, temporal filtering, and segmentation to detect and segment semi-transparent gas leaks without task-specific training. Experiments show the method achieves 69% IoU on SimGas and promising qualitative results on the real-world GasVid dataset.

### Strengths
1. **Valuable dataset contribution**: The SimGas dataset addresses a clear gap by providing pixel-level ground truth, diverse leak locations, and controlled interfering objects—features lacking in existing datasets (GasVid, Gas-DB, IIG). The dataset is publicly released, enabling future benchmarking.
2. **Comprehensive ablation and analysis**: The paper includes a thorough ablation study (Table 3) isolating the impact of each pipeline component, along with systematic experiments on prompt engineering (Table 4, Figure 4) and threshold sensitivity (Figure 5). This provides strong empirical evidence for design choices.
3. **Practical zero-shot approach**: The method leverages established models (Owlv2, SAM 2) in a novel pipeline for gas leak detection, eliminating the need for labeled training data. The use of language prompts (“white steam”) to bridge the RGB–IR domain gap is clever and well-justified.
4. **Real-world qualitative validation**: Despite the lack of ground truth, the authors provide qualitative results on GasVid (Figure 6, GitHub videos), demonstrating the method’s potential in real scenarios and acknowledging limitations like over-segmentation.

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited quantitative real-world evaluation**: The absence of pixel-level ground truth for GasVid prevents quantitative assessment of real-world performance. While qualitative results are shown, they are insufficient to gauge generalization robustness or compare against existing trained methods.
2. **Pipeline simplicity and heuristic components**: The core innovation lies in combining off-the-shelf modules; the temporal filtering is heuristic (e.g., fixed history lengths, IoU thresholds) rather than learned. This may limit adaptability to complex temporal dynamics or varying video characteristics.
3. **Incomplete comparison with state-of-the-art**: The paper compares only to BGS-only baselines on SimGas. No comparison is made to recent gas-leak-specific methods (e.g., GasNet, VideoGasNet, Gas-DB’s cross-modality network, Gasformer) even on synthetic data, making it hard to contextualize the 69% IoU achievement.
4. **Computational efficiency concerns**: The reported throughput (1–3 FPS on high-end GPUs) is slow for real-time monitoring, especially since frames are skipped. The paper mentions this but does not explore optimizations (e.g., lighter VLMs, model distillation) to improve deployment feasibility.

### Novelty & Significance
- **Novelty**: The work is novel in (a) creating a synthetic gas-leak dataset with precise ground truth and controlled diversity, and (b) integrating language-guided zero-shot detection with background subtraction for gas leaks—a new application of VLMs in this domain.
- **Significance**: The dataset fills an important void for evaluating segmentation methods. The zero-shot pipeline offers a practical alternative to data-hungry approaches, reducing reliance on hazardous real leaks and manual labeling. However, significance is tempered by the lack of quantitative real-world validation and comparisons to existing trained models.

### Suggestions for Improvement
1. **Enhance real-world evaluation**: If manual annotation is infeasible, consider using proxy metrics (e.g., consistency with background-subtraction masks, correlation with GasVid’s quantification labels) or evaluate on a subset of Gas-DB (images) to provide quantitative real-world insights.
2. **Compare with state-of-the-art methods**: Benchmark LangGas against recent gas-leak detection models (e.g., GasNet, Gasformer) on SimGas and, if possible, on Gas-DB or IIG (using bounding-box metrics for IIG). This would clarify the trade-offs between zero-shot and trained approaches.
3. **Refine temporal filtering**: Replace heuristic temporal filters with a learned module (e.g., a lightweight tracker or recurrent network) to better handle intermittent leaks and complex motions, potentially improving recall and robustness.
4. **Optimize for speed**: Profile the pipeline to identify bottlenecks (likely VLM and SAM 2) and explore faster alternatives (e.g., smaller VLMs, prompt caching, spatial subsampling) to boost FPS for real-time applications.

## How to Improve This Paper

### Missing Experiments (top 3-5 only)
1. **Quantitative evaluation on a real-world dataset with proxy ground truth.** The claim of real-world applicability relies solely on qualitative results on GasVid. To be convincing, the authors must provide quantitative metrics, even if on a small, manually annotated subset of GasVid or on the IIG dataset (which has bounding boxes). Without this, the paper's core contribution of a practical zero-shot method is unsubstantiated.

2. **Comparison to existing state-of-the-art gas leak detection methods.** The baselines are limited to simple BGS and ablations. The paper must compare to established methods like GasNet, VideoGasNet, or the model from Gas-DB (even if they require training on other data) to demonstrate that the proposed zero-shot approach is competitive. A comparison to other zero-shot vision-language pipelines (e.g., Grounding DINO + SAM) is also necessary to validate the novelty of the pipeline design.

3. **Ablation study justifying the temporal filtering design and parameters.** The temporal filtering hyperparameters (e.g., k1=10, τ_tIoU1=0.3) are set without justification. An ablation varying these parameters is needed to show they are optimal and not arbitrarily chosen, as they directly impact the recall and robustness claims.

4. **Evaluation of the synthetic dataset's utility for training.** The paper introduces a new dataset but only uses it to evaluate a zero-shot method. To demonstrate the dataset's value, an experiment training a supervised model on SimGas and evaluating its generalization to real data (e.g., GasVid or IIG) is essential. Otherwise, the dataset's contribution is limited to benchmarking the authors' own method.

5. **Performance on the IIG dataset.** The method is not tested on IIG, which contains real leaks and bounding box annotations. Testing here would provide a critical quantitative assessment of detection performance in real, complex industrial settings, strengthening the claims of generalizability.

### Deeper Analysis Needed (top 3-5 only)
1. **Failure mode analysis on both synthetic and real data.** The paper briefly mentions over-segmentation and missing small leaks in GasVid. A systematic analysis of when and why the method fails (e.g., due to BGS artifacts in dynamic backgrounds, VLM's inability to detect faint leaks, limitations of "white steam" prompt) is needed to understand the method's boundaries and guide future work.

2. **Analysis of the VLM's cross-modal transfer capability.** The method uses an RGB-trained VLM (Owlv2) on IR imagery. An analysis is needed to understand why certain prompts work (e.g., "white steam") and whether the VLM's internal representations are effective for IR. This could involve probing the VLM's performance on IR images of common objects versus RGB.

3. **Justification for the pipeline order (BGS before VLM).** The ablation shows that removing BGS hurts performance, but it is unclear if this is due to the VLM failing on raw IR frames or because the leak signal is too subtle. An analysis comparing VLM confidence scores on raw vs. enhanced difference images would clarify the role of BGS as a necessary pre-processor.

4. **Sensitivity analysis of key hyperparameters beyond τ_VLM.** The method has many hyperparameters (enhancement factor α, BGS history length, morphological kernel sizes). A sensitivity analysis is required to show the method's robustness and to confirm that the reported results are not due to overfitting a specific configuration.

### Visualizations & Case Studies
1. **Visualization of intermediate pipeline outputs for success and failure cases.** Show the enhanced difference image, the VLM bounding boxes (before/after temporal filtering), and the final segmentation mask alongside the input and ground truth. This would reveal where errors are introduced (e.g., poor VLM detection, inadequate temporal filtering).

2. **Case studies on challenging scenarios.** Show examples where BGS fails (e.g., due to moving backgrounds), where the VLM confuses leaks with other objects, and how temporal filtering corrects or fails to correct these errors. This would concretely demonstrate the method's strengths and weaknesses.

3. **Side-by-side qualitative comparison with stronger baselines.** Provide visual comparisons of segmentation masks from the proposed method against those from a state-of-the-art supervised method (if available) or a strong zero-shot baseline (e.g., Grounding SAM with a "smoke" prompt) on the same real-world frames from GasVid or IIG.

### Obvious Next Steps
1. **Implement and test the suggested extension using optical flow for moving cameras.** The paper mentions replacing BGS with optical flow for datasets like IIG (with camera motion). Implementing this and showing results on IIG is a logical next step that should have been included to demonstrate the method's adaptability.

2. **Conduct a thorough efficiency analysis and optimization.** The paper notes a slow frame rate (2-3 FPS). An obvious next step is to profile the computational bottleneck (VLM? SAM2?) and discuss or implement optimizations (e.g., model distillation, frame skipping strategies) for real-time deployment.

3. **Explore automatic prompt engineering or tuning.** The paper relies on a hand-crafted prompt ("white steam"). An obvious next step is to use language models or simple search to optimize the prompt, which could improve performance and robustness.

### Other stuff you can add
1. **Clarify the dataset splits and ensure they are standard.** The paper does not specify if the SimGas dataset has standard train/val/test splits. For reproducibility and future benchmarking, clear, video-level splits must be defined and used in all experiments.
2. **Discuss the environmental impact of the method itself.** While the synthetic dataset avoids methane release, the computational cost of running large VLMs and SAM2 for industrial monitoring should be discussed to provide a complete ethical picture.
3. **Address the limitation of static camera assumption.** The method fundamentally relies on background subtraction, which fails with moving cameras. This major limitation should be explicitly stated in the conclusion or discussion.

## Summary
This paper introduces SimGas, a synthetic infrared video dataset for gas leak segmentation featuring diverse backgrounds, interfering objects, and precise pixel-level ground truth. It also proposes LangGas, a zero-shot pipeline that combines background subtraction, a vision-language model (Owlv2) for filtering, temporal heuristics, and SAM 2 for segmentation to detect semi-transparent gas plumes without task-specific training. The method achieves 69% IoU on the synthetic dataset but is evaluated only qualitatively on real-world data.

## Strengths
- **Valuable public dataset**: SimGas addresses a clear community need by providing a video dataset with precise segmentation masks for a phenomenon (gas leaks) that is notoriously difficult to annotate manually. It includes diverse leak locations, backgrounds, and controlled interfering objects, surpassing the limitations of existing datasets like GasVid and Gas-DB.
- **Thorough ablation and prompt analysis**: The paper includes a comprehensive ablation study (Table 3) that clearly demonstrates the contribution of each pipeline component. The systematic analysis of prompt choices (Table 4, Figure 4) provides insightful, empirical evidence for using "white steam" to bridge the RGB-trained VLM to the IR domain.
- **Practical zero-shot pipeline**: The integration of established, off-the-shelf models (background subtraction, Owlv2, SAM 2) into a functional, training-free pipeline for gas leak detection is well-executed and logically motivated, offering a viable alternative to data-hungry supervised approaches.

## Weaknesses
### Major:
- **Lack of quantitative real-world validation undermines the core claim of applicability**. The paper's evaluation on the real-world GasVid dataset is solely qualitative (Section 5.5, Figure 6), with the authors stating assessment is visual "due to the lack of high-quality mask annotations." For a method whose stated contribution includes real-world performance, the absence of any quantitative metric—even on a small annotated subset or using proxy measures—severely limits the credibility of claims like "decent results" and "promising real-world performance." This sim-to-real gap is a fundamental weakness.
- **Incomplete comparison to relevant state-of-the-art methods**. The experimental comparison is limited to basic background subtraction techniques (MOG2, Median, k-NN) and the authors' own ablations. There is no quantitative comparison to contemporary gas leak detection methods (e.g., GasFormer [35], the cross-modality network from Gas-DB [48], or VideoGasNet [46]), even on the new synthetic dataset. Consequently, the reported 69% IoU lacks necessary context, making it impossible to judge whether the proposed zero-shot approach is competitive with existing trained or alternative zero-shot pipelines (e.g., Grounding DINO + SAM).
- **Methodological novelty is limited to a competent assembly of existing components**. The pipeline sequentially applies standard, off-the-shelf modules (background subtraction, Owlv2, a rule-based temporal filter, SAM 2) without introducing a novel unifying mechanism, learning objective, or architectural innovation. The temporal filter is a simple heuristic (Algorithm 1), and the key insight of using "white steam" is pragmatic prompt engineering rather than a methodological advance. The paper demonstrates a well-integrated application but does not provide a significant technical advance in how language interacts with video for transparent object detection.

### Minor:
- **Heavy dependence on empirical prompt selection and heuristic parameters**. Performance varies dramatically with the prompt (IoU ranges from 0.34 to 0.71), indicating fragility. While analyzed, no strategy is provided for robust prompt selection in new domains. Numerous hyperparameters (e.g., temporal filtering constants $k_1$, $\tau_{tIoU_1}$) are fixed with minimal justification, stating they "were not tuned extensively to avoid overfitting," leaving their sensitivity unexamined.
- **Computational performance is low for real-time monitoring**. The reported throughput is 1-3 FPS on high-end GPUs (with frame skipping), which is borderline for continuous industrial monitoring. The paper acknowledges this but does not profile bottlenecks or discuss optimizations.
- **Pipeline is fundamentally limited to static-camera scenarios**. The method relies on background subtraction, which fails with moving cameras (e.g., the IIG dataset mentioned in the paper). While replacing BGS with optical flow is noted as future work, this limitation significantly restricts the method's applicability to many real-world inspection settings.

### Trivial:
- The paper removed a few synthetic videos (26-28) deemed unrealistic due to strong wind patterns. While this could introduce a curation bias, the videos remain in the released dataset for scrutiny, mitigating this concern.

## Nice-to-Haves
- A sensitivity analysis for the many heuristic hyperparameters (temporal filtering constants, enhancement factor) would strengthen the robustness claims.
- Profiling the pipeline to identify computational bottlenecks and discussing optimization pathways (e.g., lighter VLMs) would enhance the practicality discussion.
- A brief discussion on the environmental trade-off—avoiding methane release for dataset creation versus the carbon cost of running large VLMs for inference—would provide a more complete ethical picture.

## Evaluation
- **Novelty**: Moderate. The application of a VLM-guided zero-shot pipeline to gas leak detection is novel, and the synthetic dataset is a useful contribution. However, the core pipeline is an assembly of existing components without a fundamental technical innovation.
- **Technical Soundness**: Good. The pipeline is logically constructed, the ablation study is sound, and the experiments are internally consistent. The major flaw is the evaluation framework, not the technical execution.
- **Empirical Support**: Weak. While the ablation and prompt studies on the synthetic dataset are thorough, the lack of quantitative real-world validation and comparisons to relevant prior work severely undermines the empirical case for the method's effectiveness and advancement.
- **Significance**: Moderate. The dataset is a valuable resource. The zero-shot pipeline is a reasonable alternative to supervised methods, but its significance is hampered by unproven real-world performance and missing comparative benchmarks.
- **Clarity**: Excellent. The paper is well-structured, figures are informative, and the method is clearly explained.

**Overall**: This paper presents a useful dataset and a competently designed application of existing tools to a new problem. However, it falls short of the bar for a top-tier conference due to two critical, interconnected weaknesses: the absence of quantitative real-world validation and the lack of comparison to state-of-the-art methods, which together leave its core claims of effectiveness and advancement insufficiently supported.
