## Section-by-Section Critical Review

---

### Title & Abstract

The title is descriptively accurate but cumbersome. The phrase "Selective Zero-Shot Background Subtraction" is potentially misleading: the paper does not make background subtraction itself zero-shot, but rather uses a conventional BGS algorithm as a preprocessing step and applies a zero-shot VLM for downstream filtering. "Selective" is never formally defined.

The abstract claims an IoU of 69%, which is measured exclusively on the authors' own synthetic dataset. The real-world (GasVid) evaluation is purely qualitative. This asymmetry is not flagged clearly enough in the abstract, which states results "indicate our approach significantly outperforms baseline methods" in a way that implies broader validation.

---

### Introduction & Motivation

The motivation is well-articulated: existing gas leak datasets are scarce, have labelling noise at plume boundaries, carry environmental costs, and contain spatial biases. The problem is genuine and clearly stated.

However, the concept of "Priori Ground Truth" introduced in the introduction is neither a standard term nor formally defined. It is simply "perfect ground truth by construction in synthetic data," and calling it a novel concept overstates the innovation.

The third contribution — "we tested our method on GasVid and results are promising" — is a notably weak contribution as stated. Qualitative inspection of a handful of videos from a dataset without ground truth is not a scientific contribution; it is a sanity check.

---

### Dataset (Section 3)

This is a central contribution but has several significant weaknesses:

**Scale and diversity.** With a total of 12k frames at ~300 frames per clip and 9 scene variations, the dataset is quite small. After excluding videos 24, 26, 27, and 28, the actual number of usable clips is unclear, but the dataset is not large by any standard. The authors acknowledge inter-video similarity is "medium," raising concern about whether performance reflects genuine generalization.

**Domain gap.** The dataset combines Blender smoke simulations with backgrounds from GasVid (real IR) and DALL-E-2-generated images. The plausibility of Blender smoke as a stand-in for real IR gas signatures is not validated. Real infrared gas signatures are temperature-contrast phenomena and may have very different spatial frequency, temporal dynamics, and edge sharpness than rendered smoke. The paper provides no analysis of this domain gap; the qualitative GasVid results are too sparse to be convincing.

**Exclusion criteria.** Videos 26, 27, and 28 were excluded for "strong, highly localized wind" being deemed unrealistic. This is a judgment call without supporting evidence. Atmospheric turbulence and wind gusts are common in real industrial settings. Retaining challenging cases (even as a separate test split) would have been more scientifically rigorous.

**Hyperparameter tuning on the test set.** The paper states (Section 5.1): "we performed a hyperparameter test on key parameters such as morphological kernel size and threshold." There is no mention of a validation/test split within the synthetic dataset. If the VLM threshold τ_VLM is selected based on performance on the same videos used for reporting results, the reported IoU numbers are optimistic. This is the most significant methodological concern in the paper and is insufficiently addressed.

---

### Method (Section 4)

**Background subtraction and enhancement (§4.1).** The adaptive scaling in Equation 1 is reasonable. The history length of 30 frames is borrowed directly from GasNet without re-justification. The clipping and scaling steps are reasonable but the choices of α=15 and threshold=40 (from §5.2) are stated as manually tuned on MOG2 and then uniformly applied—a reasonable engineering choice but one that could disadvantage other BGS methods in the comparison.

**VLM filtering (§4.2).** OWLv2 is an RGB-trained model applied to grayscale IR imagery. The paper justifies this by noting that gas leaks in IR resemble steam in RGB, which is empirically validated via the prompt ablation. However, the paper never quantifies the performance hit from this modality mismatch directly. Additionally, 4-bit quantization is used for OWLv2 with no ablation on accuracy degradation.

The negative prompt ("white human, car, bird, bike, and other objects") is chosen ad hoc and is never ablated. Its contribution to precision is not isolated anywhere in the experiments, which is a gap given that it is part of the core method.

**Temporal filtering (§4.3).** The module has six hyperparameters (k₁, n₁, τ_tIoU₁, τ_tShift, k₂, τ_tIoU₂). The paper states these "were not tuned extensively to avoid overfitting," which is an unusual rationale—it implies these are defaults, yet no sensitivity analysis is provided. Given the small dataset, modest changes to these could plausibly shift reported numbers. At the same time, τ_VLM is swept across values using the test set, which is asymmetric in rigor.

**Segmentation (§4.4).** The OR combination of SAM 2 masks from multiple boxes is straightforward. The paper does not discuss how overlapping boxes from two genuine leak sources would be handled, nor what happens when a box includes background pixels (which SAM 2 may expand or shrink unpredictably).

---

### Experiments & Results (Section 5)

**BGS-Only Baseline (§5.2).** The baseline is well characterized across BGS methods and morphological settings. The result (50% IoU for best BGS-only using MOG2+Morph) is a fair comparison point. One concern: hyperparameters (scale=15, threshold=40) were manually tuned with MOG2 and applied to all methods. This could unfairly disadvantage kNN, which performs very poorly (21% IoU) — it is unclear if this is a genuine weakness or an artifact of suboptimal thresholding.

**Ablation study (§5.3).** The five-condition ablation (Table 3) is the core experiment and is well-structured. The contribution of each component is clearly shown: BGS→VLM→temporal filtering→SAM 2 each improve performance. A notable finding is that removing BGS entirely (case 3) is catastrophically worse than having it (31% vs 69%), which strongly validates the method design.

However, there is a missing ablation: the paper does not report performance of VLM + BGS with the bounding box output directly thresholded (i.e., no SAM 2, no Otsu — just the raw box masks). This would isolate SAM 2's contribution more cleanly than comparing against Otsu, which introduces a different failure mode.

**Threshold optimization on the test set.** Figure 5 shows τ_VLM sweeps for methods with and without temporal filtering. The reported numbers correspond to the best τ_VLM across this sweep. Since there is no held-out validation set, the reported 69% IoU is the best achievable IoU on the test set over the threshold grid — a form of test-set overfitting. The paper would be significantly strengthened by using cross-video leave-one-out for threshold selection.

**Prompt comparison (§5.4).** The prompt ablation (Table 4) is thorough and well-motivated. The finding that "white steam" and "white smoke" outperform domain-specific prompts like "gas leak" is insightful and provides a useful design principle for adapting RGB-trained VLMs to IR domains.

**Qualitative evaluation on GasVid (§5.5).** The paper acknowledges this cannot be quantitative. The qualitative figures in the supplementary (Figure 6) show reasonable performance in simple cases but over-segmentation in complex ones. This is an honest characterization, but it leaves the key question—how does LangGas compare to, say, plain MOG2 on GasVid?—completely unanswered. Even a human evaluation of frame-level detection rate on GasVid (which has classification labels) could have been reported.

**Missing comparisons.** There is no comparison with GasFormer [35], Gas-DB's cross-modal attention method [48], or any supervised method on any shared dataset. The authors argue their zero-shot approach cannot be fairly compared to supervised methods trained on different datasets, which has some merit for the synthetic dataset. But the absence of any comparison on GasVid (where a BGS-based classification approach from GasNet [45] is available) limits the paper's claims significantly.

**Statistical significance.** No error bars or confidence intervals are reported. With 9 scene variations and each video evaluated independently, variance across videos could be substantial. Table 2 and 3 report only point estimates.

---

### Writing & Clarity

The paper is generally readable and organized. A few issues impede understanding:

- The "FLA" (frame-level accuracy) metric is not compared to a trivial baseline (e.g., always-positive or always-negative classifier). FLA values in the 0.63–0.92 range are hard to interpret without context about the positive/negative frame ratio in the dataset.
- The description of the temporal filter's propagation mechanism (second part of §4.3) is confusing. The pseudocode in Algorithm 1 helps, but the prose description contains a logical ambiguity: if "valid boxes is empty AND past boxes length > 3," the algorithm adds any two mutually-overlapping boxes from the last 3 frames — but the returned valid boxes list contains only first-frame boxes, not necessarily the most recent detection. This asymmetry is not discussed.
- "Priori Ground Truth" (§1) is used as though it is an established concept, but it is not.

---

### Limitations & Broader Impact

The conclusion identifies speed (2-3 FPS) and lack of quantitative GasVid results as limitations. However, several important limitations are unaddressed:

1. **Test-set hyperparameter tuning** is not acknowledged as a limitation, though it is arguably the paper's largest methodological weakness.
2. **Domain shift from synthetic to real IR gas signatures** is not analyzed. The paper simply reports qualitative GasVid results and calls them "promising" without discussing failure modes systematically.
3. **Assumption of static cameras**: the method fundamentally requires a static camera (for BGS). This is not prominently stated as a scope limitation, even though the IIG dataset (handheld camera) is explicitly out of scope.
4. **Sensitivity of negative prompts**: the ad hoc negative prompt list may fail in environments with objects not in the list. This is unexamined.
5. **Single IR modality**: the method is tested on a single type of IR imagery. Performance on different IR cameras or spectral bands is unknown.

---

### Overall Assessment

LangGas proposes a well-motivated modular pipeline for zero-shot IR gas leak detection, combining standard background subtraction with a VLM-based filter, temporal smoothing, and SAM 2 segmentation, along with a small synthetic dataset. The method is clearly ablated, the prompt analysis is practically insightful, and the problem domain is genuinely underserved. However, the contribution falls significantly short of ICLR standards on multiple dimensions. The core technical components are all off-the-shelf; the novelty lies in their combination and application to IR gas leak detection. The evaluation is limited to the authors' own synthetic dataset (12k frames), with no quantitative real-world results and no comparison against any supervised baseline or other zero-shot approach on a shared benchmark. Most critically, the key performance metric (τ_VLM selected by sweeping over the test set without a validation split) is methodologically unsound, meaning the reported 69% IoU is likely optimistic. The dataset itself is small, the domain gap to real IR signatures is unvalidated, and the exclusion of difficult videos weakens generalizability claims. As a workshop-level paper in a specialized domain, the work has merit; for ICLR main track, the contribution is insufficiently deep, and the evaluation is insufficiently rigorous.

## Balanced Review

### Summary
This paper addresses the scarcity of high-quality datasets for gas leak detection by introducing SimGas, a synthetic video dataset with precise segmentation ground truth generated via computer simulation. The authors propose LangGas, a zero-shot pipeline that integrates background subtraction, Vision-Language Model (VLM) filtering (OwlV2), temporal filtering, and SAM 2 segmentation to detect semi-transparent gas leaks without task-specific training. The method achieves a 69% IoU on the synthetic dataset and demonstrates promising qualitative performance on the real-world GasVid dataset.

### Strengths
1.  **Valuable Dataset Contribution:** The SimGas dataset fills a critical gap in the field by providing video data with complex backgrounds, interfering moving objects, and pixel-perfect "Priori Ground Truth." Table 1 clearly delineates how SimGas improves upon existing datasets (GasVid, Gas-DB, IIG) regarding ground truth quality and scene variation, addressing the annotation difficulty caused by gas transparency.
2.  **Rigorous Ablation and Prompt Analysis:** The experimental section is thorough. Table 3 provides a clear ablation study isolating the contributions of BGS, VLM filtering, temporal filtering, and segmentation. Furthermore, Table 4 offers insightful analysis on VLM prompt engineering, revealing that terms like "white steam" outperform "gas leak" due to VLM training biases on RGB data, which is a valuable finding for the broader community using VLMs on IR modalities.
3.  **Logical Pipeline Design:** The proposed method logically combines classical computer vision (background subtraction) with modern foundation models (VLMs, SAM 2). The inclusion of temporal filtering (Section 4.3) specifically addresses the transient false positives common in zero-shot detection, demonstrating a thoughtful adaptation of off-the-shelf components to the specific physics of gas leaks (continuity).
4.  **Reproducibility:** The authors commit to releasing the dataset, code, and full qualitative results on GitHub. Hyperparameters (e.g., history length, thresholds, enhancement factors) are explicitly detailed in Sections 4 and 5, facilitating reproduction.

### Weaknesses
1.  **Limited Real-World Quantitative Evaluation:** The evaluation on the real-world GasVid dataset (Section 5.5) is purely qualitative due to the lack of ground truth. While the authors justify this, ICLR standards typically expect some quantitative benchmark on real data to substantiate claims of generalization. The domain gap between synthetic IR (Blender) and real IR sensors is not quantitatively measured.
2.  **Computational Efficiency:** The conclusion notes the method operates at 1-3 FPS on high-end GPUs. For industrial monitoring applications which often require real-time processing of high-resolution streams, this latency is a significant limitation. The paper does not explore optimization strategies (e.g., model distillation, lighter VLM backbones) to improve throughput.
3.  **Novelty of Method vs. Integration:** While the application is novel, the technical novelty lies primarily in the integration of existing models (OwlV2, SAM 2, MOG2) rather than new algorithmic contributions. For a top-tier venue like ICLR, which often prioritizes representation learning or architectural innovations, the methodological contribution may be perceived as incremental engineering compared to the dataset contribution.
4.  **Baseline Comparisons:** The comparison against GasNet/VideoGasNet is limited to a conceptual discussion or a BGS-only recreation. Running state-of-the-art supervised segmentation models (trained on Gas-DB) on SimGas (and vice versa) to measure cross-dataset generalization would strengthen the claim that zero-shot methods are superior in this low-data regime.

### Novelty & Significance
**Novelty:** Moderate. The primary novelty is the SimGas dataset and the specific "Priori Ground Truth" generation pipeline. The LangGas method is a novel combination of existing tools tailored for this domain, but does not introduce new architectural components.
**Significance:** High for the specific domain of environmental monitoring and industrial safety. The work addresses a tangible bottleneck (data scarcity and labeling difficulty) and provides a practical, training-free solution.
**ICLR Fit:** The paper aligns with ICLR's interest in zero-shot generalization and the application of foundation models. However, it leans heavily towards applied computer vision. The insights on VLM modality mismatches (RGB vs. IR) have broader relevance to representation learning.

### Suggestions for Improvement
1.  **Quantify Real-World Performance:** Even without pixel-level ground truth on GasVid, consider using frame-level labels (leak/no-leak) to report Precision/Recall/F1 scores for detection. This would provide a quantitative metric for the real-world transfer capability.
2.  **Analyze Domain Gap:** Provide a deeper analysis of why the synthetic data transfers well (or fails) to real data. Is it the texture, the noise profile, or the thermal signature? A t-SNE visualization of features between SimGas and GasVid could strengthen the representation learning aspect.
3.  **Efficiency Discussion:** Expand the discussion on computational cost. Suggest specific pathways for real-time deployment (e.g., running VLM only on regions of interest identified by BGS, which you partially do, but could be optimized further).
4.  **Clarify "Zero-Shot" Definition:** Explicitly clarify that hyperparameter tuning (thresholds, alpha) was performed on the synthetic dataset. While the models are frozen, the pipeline is tuned, which is a form of few-shot adaptation. Distinguishing this from pure zero-shot would improve technical precision.

## How to Improve This Paper

### Missing Experiments (top 3-5 only)
1.  **Quantitative Evaluation on Real-World Data:** The claim of "promising real-world performance" is unsupported by metrics on GasVid or IIG. 4.  **VLM Backbone Ablation:** The method relies entirely on OwlV2. Experiment with other open-vocabulary detectors (e.g., Grounding DINO, GLIP) to ensure the performance gains are not specific to one model's idiosyncrasies or training data biases.

### Deeper Analysis Needed (top 3-5 only)
1.  **Sim-to-Real Domain Gap Analysis:** Performance drops on GasVid (over-segmentation, false negatives), but the cause is unanalyzed. You must analyze the physical differences between Blender smoke simulations and real IR gas plumes (thermal properties, turbulence, sensor noise) to justify the dataset's fidelity.
2.  **Computational Latency Breakdown:** The method runs at 1-3 FPS, which is marginal for "industrial monitoring." Provide a per-component latency breakdown (BGS vs. VLM vs. SAM 2) to identify bottlenecks and justify why this speed is acceptable for safety-critical leak detection.
3.  **Failure Mode Taxonomy:** For safety applications, knowing when the model fails is critical. Categorize failure cases systematically (e.g., high wind, low contrast, specific background textures) rather than showing anecdotal examples to define the operational design domain.
4.  **Prompt Semantics Sensitivity:** The success of "white steam" is attributed to "frequent occurrence in natural language," which is superficial. Analyze *why* this prompt works in IR modality (e.g., texture matching vs. semantic understanding) to ensure robustness against prompt variations.

### Visualizations & Case Studies
1.  **VLM Input Visualization:** Display the "enhanced difference" images ($I_i''$) passed to the VLM. If these do not visually resemble "white steam" (an RGB concept), it exposes the fragility of the prompt engineering and suggests the detection may be coincidental.
2.  **Real-World Failure Heatmaps:** Generate heatmaps over GasVid frames showing where the model consistently produces false positives or negatives. This reveals systematic biases (e.g., specific cloud patterns or machinery) that average metrics hide.
3.  **Sim vs. Real Texture Comparison:** Side-by-side frequency domain or texture analysis of SimGas leaks versus GasVid leaks. This visually demonstrates whether the synthetic physics match reality, validating the dataset's core contribution.

### Obvious Next Steps
1.  **Annotate Real Data Subset:** Manually label pixel-level ground truth for a small subset of GasVid (e.g., 100 frames) to enable quantitative real-world evaluation, removing the "lack of ground truth" barrier.
2.  **Optimize Inference Speed:** Replace heavy foundation models (OwlV2, SAM 2) with distilled or lighter variants to meet real-time industrial standards (>10 FPS), or provide a rigorous justification for why 1 FPS suffices for slow-leak detection.
3.  **Evaluate on Moving Camera Data:** Test the method on the IIG dataset (handheld camera) to verify if the background subtraction module can be replaced or adapted for camera motion, addressing a key limitation mentioned in the conclusion.

## Summary
LangGas presents two inter-related contributions: (1) SimGas, a Blender-rendered synthetic IR gas leak dataset with 12k frames, 9 scene variations, and pixel-perfect segmentation ground truth obtained by construction ("Priori Ground Truth"); and (2) a zero-shot detection pipeline (LangGas) that chains classical background subtraction (MOG2), OWLv2-based VLM filtering with carefully engineered text prompts, temporal filtering, and SAM 2 segmentation to detect semi-transparent gas leaks without any task-specific training. The pipeline achieves 69% IoU on SimGas and is qualitatively evaluated on the real-world GasVid dataset.

---

## Strengths

- **Clinically precise dataset construction**: By overlaying Blender-rendered plumes onto real IR backgrounds (GasVid non-leak clips) and DALL-E-2 scenes, the authors obtain ground truth that is definitionally perfect — a genuine solution to the blurry-boundary annotation problem that afflicts all existing gas leak datasets. Table 1 concretely shows that no existing public dataset simultaneously offers segmentation masks, diverse backgrounds, interfering moving objects, and high frame continuity.

- **Insightful prompt-engineering finding with cross-domain relevance**: The ablation in Table 4 — showing that "white steam" and "white smoke" outperform domain-specific prompts including "gas leak," "methane gas leak," and "white plume" — is a non-obvious, reproducible result that illuminates how RGB-trained VLMs process thermally-imaged phenomena. This has genuine transferability to other IR/non-standard modality applications and is one of the most practically useful findings in the paper.

- **Well-structured ablation that validates architectural choices**: Table 3 unambiguously shows each component's contribution. The most striking result — removing background subtraction altogether drops IoU from 69% to 31%, worse than BGS-only at 50% — strongly validates the hybrid pipeline logic and is not trivially predictable. The additional finding that temporal filtering broadens the effective τ_VLM range (Figure 5) quantifies robustness improvements concretely.

- **Reproducibility posture**: Full hyperparameters, pseudocode, quantization details, and committed GitHub release of dataset, code, and qualitative results represent a higher-than-average reproducibility standard for a domain-specific paper.

---

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **τ_VLM selected by sweeping over the test set without a held-out validation split.** Section 5.1 explicitly states that a hyperparameter sweep was performed, and Figure 5 shows the sweep across τ_VLM values with the reported 69% corresponding to the best-performing threshold. With only 9 scene variations and no cross-video validation split, the reported IoU is the maximum achievable on the very videos being evaluated — a form of test-set overfitting. This is the most significant methodological concern. The paper should use leave-one-video-out threshold selection or an explicit val/test split, and should acknowledge this limitation in the current version, which it does not.

- **Absence of any quantitative real-world evaluation.** GasVid provides frame-level classification labels (leak/no-leak). Even without pixel-level masks, reporting Precision/Recall/F1 at the frame level on GasVid is feasible and would give the first quantitative evidence of sim-to-real transfer. Purely qualitative inspection of a handful of clips is insufficient to support the abstract's claim of "significant outperformance" and the conclusion's claim of "promising real-world performance." This leaves the practical utility of both the dataset and the pipeline entirely unvalidated by any objective metric.

- **Synthetic-to-real domain gap is uncharacterized.** Blender smoke simulation produces plumes with RGB-space statistics, while real IR gas signatures are driven by thermal contrast, turbulence dynamics, and sensor noise characteristics. The paper provides no analysis — even qualitative texture comparison or frequency-domain analysis — to justify that SimGas plumes are statistically faithful to real IR gas signatures. The qualitative GasVid figures show over-segmentation and false negatives whose causes are not analyzed. Without this analysis, the dataset's core claim of being a useful proxy for real gas leaks rests on assumption rather than evidence.

- **No comparison with any other method on a shared benchmark.** The paper compares against BGS-only baselines (MOG2, kNN, Median). There is no comparison with GasFormer [35], Gas-DB's cross-modal attention model [48], or any other zero-shot video anomaly detection method on any shared evaluation. The authors argue that supervised methods trained on different data cannot be fairly compared; however, a cross-dataset evaluation (e.g., Gas-DB model evaluated on SimGas) would have quantified the data-efficiency advantage of the zero-shot approach. As currently structured, "outperforms baseline methods" means only "outperforms classical BGS," which is a modest bar.

### Minor

- **Negative prompt composition is ad hoc and unablated.** The negative prompt ("white human, car, bird, bike, and other objects") is part of the core VLM filtering step but receives no isolated evaluation. Its contribution to precision is entirely unquantified, despite being a design choice that would need to be adapted for new deployment environments.

- **4-bit quantization of OWLv2 has no accuracy ablation.** This is used throughout all reported results with no comparison against full-precision inference. Any observed performance ceiling could partially reflect quantization artifacts.

- **FLA metric lacks trivial-baseline context.** Frame-level accuracy values of 0.63–0.92 (Table 2, Table 3) are difficult to interpret without knowing the positive/negative frame ratio in the dataset. An always-positive classifier on a dataset with 80% leak frames would score 0.80 FLA. Without this context, FLA numbers are not informative.

- **The third stated contribution is not a contribution.** "We tested our method on GasVid and the results are promising" is a sanity check, not a scientific contribution. As argued above, a frame-level quantitative result would transform this into a genuine contribution.

### Tiny

- **History length of 30 frames is inherited from GasNet without re-justification** for the different dataset characteristics of SimGas.
- **kNN's low performance (21% IoU)** may partially reflect suboptimal thresholding tuned for MOG2; this asymmetry is noted but not resolved.
- **"Priori Ground Truth" terminology** is introduced as if it were an established concept, but it is simply "ground truth obtained by construction in simulation." The usage is not wrong, but the quasi-formal presentation slightly overstates novelty.

- **Per-component latency breakdown** (BGS vs. OWLv2 vs. SAM 2 vs. temporal filter) to identify the bottleneck and inform optimization pathways toward real-time performance.

- **Texture/frequency-domain comparison** between SimGas plumes and GasVid plumes to quantify dataset fidelity.

## Removed Points

*These points are flagged to be removed or substantially weakened; treat them with caution.*

- **"Scale is too small" (12k frames)**: The harsh critic calls the dataset small, but relative to existing gas leak datasets (Gas-DB has 1.2k frames, IIG has 5k frames — Table 1), 12k frames with diverse backgrounds and interfering objects is not small for this domain. This complaint imposes generic large-dataset standards inappropriate to the field.

- **Exclusion of videos 26/27/28 is scientifically unjustified**: The paper explicitly retains these videos in the released dataset so readers can assess the decision, notes that similar exclusions were made in GasVid and Gas-DB, and provides a reasonable rationale (extreme localized wind is atypical in industrial settings). The decision is defensible and reviewable by the community. Criticizing it as lacking "supporting evidence" overstates the concern.

- **BGS hyperparameters unfairly tuned for MOG2 disadvantage kNN**: The harsh critic says this is an unfair comparison. In practice, cross-method hyperparameter transfer is a standard engineering constraint, is explicitly acknowledged in the paper, and the reported results still allow MOG2 to be identified as the best method — the comparison's purpose is not a fair horse-race between BGS methods but to establish the baseline ceiling. This asymmetry, to the extent it exists, disfavors the baseline rather than the authors' method, making this point appropriately removed.

- **Static camera assumption not prominently disclosed**: The paper's conclusion explicitly mentions replacing BGS with optical flow for moving-camera scenarios (IIG), and the IIG dataset is discussed in the related work section as having camera jitter that makes BGS inapplicable. The scope limitation is adequately disclosed.

- **Missing related work suggestions (Report 4)**: Per instructions, these are not included as the external sources cannot be independently confirmed.

- **Request for confidence intervals / multi-run statistics**: Single-run evaluation is the norm for video segmentation benchmarks in this domain, and with a synthetic dataset where variance can be examined per video (Table 2/3 split into interference/no-interference), the request for error bars is not a standard requirement here.

---

## Novel Insights

The most genuinely novel observation to emerge — beyond the paper's own stated contributions — is the prompt modality bridging finding: that an RGB-trained open-vocabulary detector can be applied to processed IR imagery not by describing the IR phenomenon directly ("gas leak," "methane"), but by exploiting the *visual similarity* between the detector's training distribution (steam/smoke in RGB) and the detector's actual input (enhanced IR difference images). This is not merely a practical tip; it suggests that the "language" component of VLMs implicitly serves as a visual appearance prior, and that IR detection tasks can be bootstrapped by identifying the closest RGB visual analog to the target phenomenon. This principle is likely transferable to other non-standard imaging modalities (e.g., UV fluorescence, depth images, radar imagery) and deserves more explicit theoretical framing than the paper currently provides.

---

## Suggestions

1. **Introduce a leave-one-video-out threshold selection protocol**: For each reported result, select τ_VLM by maximizing IoU on the leave-one-out complement, then evaluate on the held-out video. Average across all held-out videos. This would convert the current in-sample optimal into a genuine generalization estimate with minimal implementation cost.

2. **Report frame-level F1 on GasVid**: GasVid has per-video classification labels. Even approximate frame-level labeling (leak / no-leak) is possible for subset clips. Report precision, recall, and F1 at the detection frame level on GasVid to provide the first quantitative real-world transfer metric.

3. **Add a sim-to-real gap section**: A brief frequency-domain or Gram matrix comparison of synthetic vs. real IR plume patches would quantify dataset fidelity far more rigorously than qualitative inspection, and would directly address the most fundamental assumption underlying the dataset's utility.

4. **Ablate the negative prompt**: Report performance with and without the negative prompt to isolate its precision contribution. Even a single-row addition to Table 3 would complete the ablation.

5. **Clarify the "zero-shot" scope**: Explicitly state in Section 5.1 that the models (OWLv2, SAM 2) are completely frozen but that τ_VLM and morphological parameters are selected via grid search on the evaluation set, distinguishing this from pure zero-shot (no tuning) vs. few-shot (training) approaches.

- **Novelty**: Moderate-low. SimGas's Priori Ground Truth construction is a sensible and useful contribution for the specific domain. LangGas combines entirely off-the-shelf components (MOG2, OWLv2, SAM 2) without algorithmic innovation. The prompt-modality bridging insight is the most generalizable novelty.
- **Technical soundness**: Fair-to-weak. Pipeline design is logical and well-motivated, but the core performance metric is selected in-sample, and several design choices (negative prompt, quantization, temporal filter parameters) lack ablations.
- **Empirical support**: Weak. Quantitative evaluation is confined to a small synthetic dataset with possible test-set leakage through τ_VLM selection. All real-world evidence is qualitative. No comparison to any prior method on a shared benchmark.
- **Significance**: Moderate for the specific application domain; limited for the broader ICLR audience. The prompt-engineering finding has wider relevance but is not developed beyond a table entry.
- **Clarity**: Good. The pipeline is clearly explained, pseudocode is provided, hyperparameters are reported, and the ablation structure is transparent.

Overall, this is a well-motivated, clearly written paper that would be a solid contribution to a domain-specific workshop. The dataset is the most durable contribution. However, the evaluation rigor — particularly the absence of quantitative real-world results and the in-sample threshold selection — and the combination-of-existing-components methodology fall short of ICLR main-track standards.
